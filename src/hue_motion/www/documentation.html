Hue Motion Sensor device

A Hue Motion Sensor is one Philips Hue motion sensor, indoor or outdoor, or one MotionAware area of a Hue Bridge Pro, shown as an ordinary Control4 motion sensor. When Hue sees motion the sensor reads Open and fires the standard senses motion event; when Hue reports that the motion has ended it reads Closed and fires stops sensing motion. It is created by the Hue Bridge device's Add Motion Sensor, Import All New Motion Sensors or Add Motion Area action, already connected to the bridge, so there are no connections to make; it is normally never added by hand. A sensor also brings its light level, temperature and battery, as rows, variables, events and conditionals of this device, and its temperature as an output that a Temperature Display or a thermostat can use. An area senses motion only. The device only reads: it never changes anything on the Hue side, so a sensor or an area is switched off, paused or tuned in the Hue app and nowhere else.

The full manual is on the Hue Bridge device: select the bridge in System Design and open its Documentation tab. Chapter 14 there is about motion sensors and MotionAware areas, chapter 6 about room mappings, chapter 8 about renames, replacements and restores.
A beta. This driver is new in 1.1 beta 1, a prerelease for testers, and was written before it met a real Hue motion sensor: the developer's house has none. Its Control4 side can be tried without a sensor with the two test actions below; what it does with a sensor is what the testers' reports will confirm or correct. To report, run the bridge's Log Motion Diagnostics action and use the Beta feedback form (the manual, chapter 14).

In Control4

To Composer this is a motion sensor like any other, in the Sensors category with Control4's own motion icons. Its Properties tab starts with the rows Control4 gives every contact sensor (Invert Sensor, the debounce settings, State and Verified) and lists the driver's own rows below them, under Advanced Properties. Motion is Open, no motion is Closed. Leave Invert Sensor unticked: ticked, it would turn motion into Closed and the events round. The debounce settings can stay as they are.

In programming it offers what every Control4 motion sensor offers: the events senses motion and stops sensing motion, the conditionals is sensing motion, is not sensing motion and State is Verified, and the variables ContactState, StateVerified and LastActionTime. Control4 counts a closed contact as true, so ContactState is expected to read true while there is no motion (reported by others, not yet seen here); the conditionals say it plainly. The driver adds its own events, variables and conditionals for light level, temperature, battery, Online and Enabled ("Programming" below). In the Control4 app the sensor is expected under Security, Locks & Sensors, with the history of its motion, as Control4's own sensors are; that is one of the things the beta is to confirm.

Verified reads No until the device has reported the sensor's first state since it was added or the controller started. That first state is set without firing an event, unless it differs from the state the device last reported, for example because someone walked in while the controller restarted: then the event fires once, late rather than never. State is Verified lets programming leave a sensor alone until then.

When motion is reported

  • Hue reports the start of motion and its end as two separate reports. The end comes some seconds after the last movement, a time the sensor keeps itself and Control4 cannot set; while someone keeps moving nothing new is reported and the sensor stays Open. How long the end takes on each model is one of the first things the beta measures.
  • The bridge bundles its reports, about one bundle a second, so motion reaches Control4 within a second or two. A movement shorter than that may be reported as its end only, and then changes nothing in Control4.
  • Occupancy Hold Time (s), default 0, keeps the sensor Open for that many seconds after Hue reports the end of motion; new motion meanwhile cancels the wait. The Hue Motion row always shows Hue's own state, before the hold. Change the value, then click the row's Set button; it applies from the next end of motion. A hold interrupted by a restart starts again in full at the next end of motion. A timeout that turns lights off belongs in programming, not here.
  • If the bridge's event feed is lost, or stopped with the bridge's Disconnect Event Stream or Forget Pairing, nothing is assumed: after a minute Status says the motion is not current (table below), the sensor stays as it is, and when the feed is back the device reports what changed meanwhile, so programming catches up late rather than never. A whole visit that began and ended while the feed was down is not replayed (the bridge's Lua output notes it only while the bridge's Debug Mode is On).
  • When the sensor has shown motion for five minutes without a word from Hue, the bridge asks for its state once, and again every five minutes while it lasts. Between full reads it is the only time the driver asks rather than listens.
  • Hue's own automations for the sensor or area keep running beside Control4's programming; neither stops the other.

Temperature, light level and battery

These come from a sensor only; on an area their rows are hidden and their variables stay 0.

  • Temperature shows both scales, for example 21.5 °C (70.7 °F), and sets the variables Temperature C and Temperature F. The device also sends it on its Temperature output: in Composer's Connections view, connect that output to the Temperature In input of Control4's Temperature Display driver (from Composer's online driver database) to show the temperature on Navigators, or to a thermostat that takes a remote temperature sensor. The output sends Celsius and Fahrenheit, with the time the reading reached the controller, whenever the bridge passes on a temperature (a new one, and the last one again at every full read and Refresh From Bridge) and when it is connected; it answers a request with the last reading; and when the sensor goes offline, is switched off, deleted or forgotten, or the device is disconnected, it says once that the value is unavailable, until the bridge passes on a reading again (not merely when the sensor is back). Nothing is sent after a start until the first reading. A Control4 thermostat is reported to ignore a reading older than 15 minutes, and the sensor reports only every few minutes or on a change; whether a thermostat keeps the value through a steady temperature has not been tried. An area's output stays silent.
  • Light Level shows lux, for example 123 lux, or Dark when it is too dark to measure; the variable Light Level holds the lux, rounded, 0 when dark. The event Light Level Changed fires when the level has moved by at least 5 lux or 10 %, whichever is more, since it last fired, so that small changes do not keep programming busy.
  • Battery shows the charge and Hue's word for it, for example 85 % (normal) or 12 % (low); the variables are Battery Level and Battery Low (true while Hue says low or critical), and the events Battery Low and Battery Normal fire when Hue's word changes between normal and low or critical. The bridge's Attention row lists a low or critical battery at once.
  • A reading that is lost keeps its last value, and so does its variable; nothing is ever set to a made-up number. While the sensor is offline the row adds the time it went offline, for example 123 lux (old, 14:02); a reading switched off in the Hue app, or flagged invalid by Hue, keeps its last value without a mark. A sensor without one of the readings shows Not available on that row. After a re-link the old sensor's readings stay until the new one reports.

When the sensor stops reporting

Two kinds of ending are treated differently. A deliberate one, which nothing will undo by itself (the sensor switched off or deleted in the Hue app, an area paused or deleted, the device forgotten by the bridge or disconnected from it), sends "no motion" once, at once and without the hold time, so that programming waiting for stops sensing motion is not left waiting for ever. A failure, which may clear by itself (the sensor out of reach or its battery flat, the bridge away, an area not running), leaves the sensor as it is and says in Status that the motion is not current, so that nothing in the house is switched off on stale information. Programming that must not act on an old state can check Online, and right after a restart State is Verified.

Its conditionals: Online, Enabled in the Hue app and Battery Low (true or false), Light Level (lux) (0 to 100000), Temperature C (-40 to 60) and Battery Level (%) (0 to 100). The numeric ones compare whole numbers.

Actions

ActionWhat it does
Refresh From BridgeAsks the bridge to send this device its sensor's or area's details and state again. Normally not needed: the bridge sends them by itself. Last Action reads Asked the bridge for this device's state; its answer updates the rows, or Not sent: not bound to a Hue Bridge.
Test: MotionMakes the sensor read Open and fire senses motion, as if Hue had seen someone, so programming can be tried without walking past a sensor (or without one at all). At the next motion report from the bridge, or after 30 seconds, whichever comes first, the sensor goes back to Hue's state (hold time included) and fires that state's event if it differs: 30 seconds after the test a sensor with no motion reads Closed again and stops sensing motion fires. Test: No MotionThe same for Closed and stops sensing motion (Test: no motion sent; Hue's state returns within 30 s).

The device has no Forget or rename action of its own: the bridge's Forget Motion Device, Adopt Hue Names and Accept Names As They Are cover it (the manual, chapter 14).

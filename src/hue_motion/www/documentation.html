A motion sensor has no buttons, so this page has no keypad section; keypads and lights follow it through programming.

1. What this device is

A Hue Motion Sensor is one Philips Hue motion sensor, indoor or outdoor, or one MotionAware area of a Hue Bridge Pro, shown as an ordinary Control4 motion sensor. When Hue sees motion the sensor reads Open and fires the standard senses motion event; when Hue reports that the motion has ended it reads Closed and fires stops sensing motion. A sensor also brings its light level, temperature and battery, as rows, variables, events and conditionals of this device, and its temperature as an output that a Temperature Display or a thermostat can use. An area senses motion only.

The device only reads, with one exception: its name. A rename of this device in Composer renames the sensor or area in the Hue app, and a rename there renames the device. Nothing else changes on the Hue side, so a sensor or an area is switched off, paused or tuned in the Hue app and nowhere else. Hue's own automations for the sensor or area keep running beside Control4's programming; neither stops the other.

What it needs. The device does nothing on its own: the Hue Bridge device pairs with the Hue bridge, creates this device, and passes on every report. The project's four drivers, Hue Bridge, Hue Light, Hue Scene and Hue Motion Sensor, come as four .c4z files in each release; install all four from the same release. MotionAware areas need a Hue Bridge Pro; a Hue Bridge v2 offers sensors only.

The full manual is on the Hue Bridge device: select the bridge in System Design and open its Documentation tab. This page calls it "the bridge's manual". Its chapter 14 is about motion sensors and MotionAware areas; chapter 6 is about room mappings, chapter 8 about renames, replacements and restores.

2. Adding and setting up

  • The bridge creates it. The Hue Bridge's Import All New adds every new sensor (not areas), each into the Control4 room its Hue room maps to; Add From Hue adds one sensor or one MotionAware area, into the room you pick or the mapped room (the bridge's manual, 14.3 and 14.4). The device arrives already connected to the bridge, so there are no connections to make. A device added by hand from the Items pane needs a connection and a link: see Not bound to a Hue Bridge in section 6.
  • Control4's rows. The device's Properties tab starts with the rows Control4 gives every contact sensor (Invert Sensor, the debounce settings, State and Verified) and lists the driver's own rows below them, under Advanced Properties. Motion is Open, no motion is Closed. Leave Invert Sensor unticked: ticked, it would turn motion into Closed and the events round. The debounce settings can stay as they are.
  • Rows you may set: Occupancy Hold Time (s) (section 3), and Debug Mode only when asked. A changed value reaches the driver only when you click the row's Set button.
  • The temperature output of a sensor is connected in Connections ("Temperature, light level and battery", section 3).
  • Names follow both ways (the bridge's manual, 8.2 and 14.12). Hue takes names of at most 32 bytes; a name that cannot follow is listed in the bridge's Attention for its Resolve Names.
  • One action: Test Motion Programming (section 5). The device has no Forget or rename action of its own: its name follows the sensor's or area's by itself, and the bridge's Forget Device, Link Device and Resolve Names cover the rest (the bridge's manual, chapter 14).
  • Updating. Install the four new .c4z files at once with Driver » Add or Update Driver or Agent…; every device updates in place and keeps its pairing and links (the bridge's manual, chapter 3). Never delete and re-add the devices. The Hue Motion Sensor driver declares no capabilities, so its own updates never need a controller reboot.

3. Everyday use

When motion is reported

  • Hue reports the start of motion and its end as two separate reports. The end comes some seconds after the last movement (about 10 seconds on a MotionAware area), a time the sensor or area keeps itself and Control4 cannot set; while someone keeps moving nothing new is reported and the sensor stays Open.
  • The bridge bundles its reports, about one bundle a second, so motion reaches Control4 within a second or two. A movement shorter than that may be reported as its end only, and then changes nothing in Control4.
  • When the sensor has shown motion for five minutes without a word from Hue, the bridge asks for its state once, and again every five minutes while it lasts. Between full reads of the bridge it is the only time the driver asks rather than listens.
  • If the bridge's event feed is lost, nothing is assumed: the sensor stays as it is and catches up when the feed is back (section 6).

Occupancy Hold Time

Occupancy Hold Time (s), 0 to 3600 seconds, default 0, keeps the sensor Open for that many seconds after Hue reports the end of motion; new motion meanwhile cancels the wait. The Hue Motion row always shows Hue's own state, before the hold. A new value applies from the next end of motion. A timeout that turns lights off belongs in programming, not here.

A hold interrupted by a restart (a driver update, a Director restart or a controller reboot) goes on afterwards only for the rest of the hold time, as long as the bridge still reports the same end of motion. If that time ran out while the driver was not running, the sensor reads Closed as soon as the bridge answers, and stops sensing motion fires once. If the device did not see the motion end (it ended while the driver was not running, or while the bridge was out of reach), the hold starts again in full.

Temperature, light level and battery

These come from a sensor only; on an area their rows are hidden and their variables stay 0.

  • Temperature shows both scales, for example 21.5 °C (70.7 °F), and sets the variables Temperature C and Temperature F. The device also sends it on its Temperature output: in Composer's Connections view, connect that output to the Temperature In input of Control4's Temperature Display driver (from Composer's online driver database) to show the temperature on Navigators, or to a thermostat that takes a remote temperature sensor. The output sends Celsius and Fahrenheit, with the time the reading reached the controller, whenever the bridge passes on a temperature (a new one, and the last one again at every read of the bridge) and when it is connected; it answers a request with the last reading. When the sensor goes offline (also when the bridge has not answered for 5 minutes), is switched off, deleted or forgotten, or the device is disconnected, it says once that the value is unavailable, until the bridge passes on a reading again (not merely when the sensor is back). Nothing is sent after a start until the first reading. A Control4 thermostat is reported to ignore a reading older than 15 minutes, and the sensor reports only every few minutes or on a change. An area's output stays silent.
  • Light Level shows lux, for example 123 lux, or Dark when it is too dark to measure; the variable Light Level holds the lux, rounded, 0 when dark.
  • Battery shows the charge and Hue's word for it, for example 85 % (normal) or 12 % (low); the variables are Battery Level and Battery Low (true while Hue says low or critical). The bridge's Attention row lists a low or critical battery at once.
  • A reading that is lost keeps its last value, and so does its variable; nothing is ever set to a made-up number. While the sensor is offline the row adds the time it went offline, for example 123 lux (old, 14:02) (while the bridge has been offline for 5 minutes, the time the bridge stopped answering). A reading switched off in the Hue app, or flagged invalid by Hue, keeps its last value without a mark. A sensor without one of the readings shows Not available on that row. After a re-link the old sensor's readings stay until the new one reports.

The events for these readings are in section 5.

5. Programming

What every Control4 motion sensor offers. The events senses motion and stops sensing motion, the conditionals is sensing motion, is not sensing motion and State is Verified, and the variables ContactState, StateVerified and LastActionTime. Control4 counts a closed contact as true, so ContactState is expected to read true while there is no motion; the conditionals say it plainly. Verified reads No until the device has reported the sensor's first state since it was added or the controller started. That first state is set without firing an event, unless it differs from the state the device last reported (someone walked in while the controller restarted, say): then the event fires once, late rather than never. State is Verified lets programming leave a sensor alone until then. In the Control4 app the sensor is expected under Security, Locks & Sensors, with the history of its motion, as Control4's own sensors are.

Test Motion Programming

The device's one action, on its Actions tab. It makes the sensor read the opposite of what it shows and fire the matching event, as if Hue had reported it, so programming can be tried without walking past a sensor, or without one at all: Open and senses motion when it shows Closed, Closed and stops sensing motion when it shows Open. Last Action says which state was sent, for example Test: motion sent; Hue's state returns within 30 s. At the next motion report from the bridge, or after 30 seconds, whichever comes first, the sensor goes back to Hue's state (hold time included) and fires that state's event if it differs: 30 seconds after a test of motion, a sensor with no motion reads Closed again and stops sensing motion fires. Click it twice in a row to test both events. A device that knows no Hue state (no sensor, or not linked) goes back to what it showed before the first test, Closed if it showed nothing.

6. Troubleshooting

When the sensor or the Hue bridge stops reporting

Two kinds of ending are treated differently. A deliberate one, which nothing will undo by itself (the sensor switched off or deleted in the Hue app, an area paused or deleted, the device forgotten by the bridge or disconnected from it), sends "no motion" once, at once and without the hold time, so that programming waiting for stops sensing motion is not left waiting for ever. A failure, which may clear by itself (the sensor out of reach or its battery flat, the bridge away, an area not running), leaves the sensor as it is and says in Status that the motion is not current, so that nothing in the house is switched off on stale information.

When the bridge's event feed is lost, or stopped with the bridge's Diagnostics (Stop the event stream until the next start) or Forget Pairing, nothing is assumed: after a minute Status says the motion is not current, the sensor stays as it is, and when the feed is back the device reports what changed meanwhile, so programming catches up late rather than never. A whole visit that began and ended while the feed was down is not replayed (the bridge's Lua output notes it only while the bridge's Debug Mode is On). When the bridge has not answered, or has refused the pairing, for 5 minutes, Online also turns No and Offline fires, while the sensor still stays as it is; so programming written for a sensor fault, on the Offline event for example, also runs during a long bridge outage.

Not yet seen working

The drivers are meant for Control4 OS 3.x and 4.x (minimum OS 3.3.0) and have been run on OS 3.4. Motion, light level and temperature from Hue motion sensors were seen working on a Hue Bridge v2 by an outside tester, and a MotionAware area's motion on a Hue Bridge Pro, with Control4 programming on it. These parts follow Control4's documentation and the code but had not been confirmed when this page was written:

  • the sensor in the Control4 app under Security, Locks & Sensors, with its motion history;
  • ContactState reading true while there is no motion;
  • a Temperature Display or a thermostat on the temperature output, and whether a thermostat keeps the value through a steady temperature;
  • how long the end of motion takes on each sensor model.

To report how a sensor behaves, run the bridge's Diagnostics action with Log motion diagnostics (10 minutes), which writes what Hue reports about motion to the bridge's Lua output with names, addresses and keys left out, and open an issue on the project's GitHub page (the bridge's manual, 14.15).

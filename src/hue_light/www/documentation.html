Hue Light device

A Hue Light is one Philips Hue light (a bulb, strip or lamp as the Hue app lists it) shown as an ordinary Control4 light: on, off, dimming, and colour or colour temperature when the light supports them. It is created by the Hue Bridge device's Import All New or Add From Hue action, normally never added by hand, and it is driven entirely by the bridge: the bridge tells it what its light can do and what state it is in, and passes your commands on to the light. Changes made in the Hue app or with Hue switches show on this device within a second or two, because the bridge listens to the Hue bridge's event feed.

The same device can stand for a whole Hue zone or room when the bridge's Add From Hue created it from its zones and rooms: a zone light, named after the zone (Bedside Lights), or a room light, named after the room with Lights added (Master Bedroom Lights). Its one tile switches, dims and colours every lamp of the zone or room at once, with a single request to the bridge, and shows what they are doing. Everything on this page applies to it; the differences are noted where they occur.

The full manual, with pictures, is on the Hue Bridge device: select the bridge in System Design and open its Documentation tab. It covers installing, pairing, room mapping, importing, renames, replacements, deletions, restores, scenes, programming and troubleshooting. From release 1.2 this device has no actions of its own: everything you can do to it from Composer is on the bridge (below, "Actions").

In Control4

To Composer this is an ordinary dimmer: programming, keypad buttons, Navigators and Advanced Lighting Scenes use it like any other light. Programming also has two commands of its own for it, Raise Level By and Restore Saved Level (below). A tap on its tile that turns it on, "Turn On" in programming, "Toggle" while it is off and a tap on the top keypad button all turn it on at its Default On Brightness, 100 % unless changed ("Keypad settings" below), not at the level the light had before or the power-on level set in the Hue app. Its on, off and level-changed events fire for changes made in the Hue app, by a Hue dimmer switch or by a Hue automation too. A whole-room command reaches Hue lights at ten a second, so a room with many of them finishes over a few seconds. While the Hue bridge is unreachable, the tile shows the level you asked for a second after the command's ramp time although the light did nothing; the command is retried for up to 30 seconds and delivered if the bridge is back by then, otherwise dropped, and the tile is corrected when the bridge is back.

On a keypad, or on a Control4 Switch V2 or Essential Switch set to Use as 2 Button Keypad, it behaves like a Control4 dimmer once the buttons are bound to its Top Button Link and Bottom Button Link in Connections (listed under its Control Outputs; a single button goes to Toggle Button Link). A tap on the top button turns it on to 100 % over 250 ms, a tap on the bottom button turns it off over 250 ms, and a toggle button switches it the other way. A button held for longer than 0.4 seconds makes Hue brighten the light (top, or toggle while the light is off) or dim it (bottom, or toggle while it is on), the full range in 5 seconds and at that speed from any level (from 50 % to full in 2.5 seconds), until the button is let go, as Hue's own dimmer switch does. Held while the light is off, the top or toggle button turns it on at 1 % and brightens from there (let go within about a second of pressing and it stays at 1 %); the bottom button does nothing. A tap that comes within a second after letting go of a hold on the same button is ignored, because some keypads send one. The keypad's LEDs follow the light's state, including changes made in the Hue app: a top or toggle button is lit blue while the light is on, a bottom button while it is off. The bridge's manual, chapter 11, has the setup steps; "Keypad settings" below says how to change the 100 %, the 250 ms, the 5 seconds and the blue.

A zone light or room light is on while any lamp of its zone or room is lit, and its level is the average brightness of the lit lamps. A tap, the top button, "Turn On" and "Toggle" while it is off set every lamp to the zone or room light's own Default On Brightness (100 % unless changed), as on a single light; the slider, or a level from programming or a lighting scene, sets every lamp to that level, so the differences between the lamps are levelled out; a keypad hold dims the zone and keeps those differences, as Hue's dimmer switch does: smoothly, lamp by lamp, while up to 10 of its lamps are on, and in steps of a second with more (or while a time-of-day or moving scene plays there). Colour and colour temperature go to all the lamps of the zone or room in one request; a lamp that cannot show them should ignore them (not seen on the house system as this is written, where every lamp can show both). The tile has a colour wheel if any lamp has colour and a temperature control if any has colour temperature, and it shows the colour of the first lit lamp showing a colour, else the colour temperature of the first lit lamp showing one, because Hue reports no colour for a zone as a whole. Hue takes about one command a second for a zone or room (ten for single lights), so after a first burst of three a fast slider drag is sent as one request a second while the tile moves smoothly, and the lamps end where the slider ends; a hold's start and stop go out at once. Turning a zone or room light off also ends any Hue scene playing there, so a Hue Scene device of the same zone or room goes off with it (the bridge's manual, 9.4; seen on the house system on 2026-10-25 with a room light).

Keypad settings

The 100 %, the 250 ms, the 5 seconds and the blue LEDs above are the defaults of the settings a Control4 dimmer normally lists on its Properties page, and Composer lists them for this device too, on its Properties tab above the driver's own rows (Advanced Properties):

  • Default On Brightness: the level the light turns on to for a tap on the top button, or on a toggle button while the light is off, and for a tap on its tile and "Turn On" or "Toggle" in programming; 100 unless changed, from 1 to 100.
  • The click rates, up and down: how long the change after a tap takes, turning on and turning off; 250 ms each unless changed.
  • The hold ramp rates, up and down (Hold Ramp Rate Down, for example): how long a held button takes across the whole range, brightening and dimming; 5 seconds each unless changed, never less than 1 second in Composer, and never more than 100 minutes in use (a longer rate is kept and shown, but a hold then crosses the range in 100 minutes).
  • The colours of the keypad buttons' LEDs while the light is on and while it is off: blue unless changed, a top or toggle button lit while the light is on and a bottom button while it is off.

Composer shows the rates in seconds (5.000) and the values the device is using. Type a new level or rate and press Enter: Composer sends it then, and the next button press uses it. The device takes rates from 0 to 86 400 000 ms (24 hours). A value outside these ranges, or one that is not a number, is refused: the device keeps the value it had and reports it back, so Composer shows that value again (a Default On Brightness of 0 would make the top button turn the light off).

Composer also lists a colour on mode for this device, the colour a light should turn on in. The driver does not use it: Control4 hands that setting only to drivers that declare support for colour on modes, and this one does not, because that support would change the device's capabilities and needs Control4's colour presets. So a keypad tap and Control4's ON turn the light on in the colour it had when it went off (or one picked while it was off), whatever the colour on mode says. Whether Control4 applies the setting itself when a Navigator turns the light on had not been checked on the house system as this is written.

Since release 1.3 every way of turning the light on without choosing a level goes to the Default On Brightness: a keypad tap, Control4's ON command, and TOGGLE while the light is off, which is what Control4 describes for a dimmer's On preset. ON also sets a light that is already on to that level, from below or from above, as a tap on the top button does. ON and TOGGLE take effect at once; the click rates apply to keypad taps only. So with the Default On Brightness left at 100 nothing changes from earlier releases, and with it at 40 "Turn On" and "Toggle" in programming and a keypad tap all give 40 %, and so does a tap on the tile as far as Control4's documentation goes: it says a Navigator turns a light on at its On preset, which is this setting (not checked on the house system with a changed Default On Brightness as this is written). A zone or room light has the same settings, its own, and its ON sets every lamp to its Default On Brightness. Showing them changed what this device type can do in Control4, its capabilities, so after the update that brought them they appear only once the controller has been rebooted from System Manager, as after any such update (the bridge's manual, chapter 3); until then the defaults apply.

Raise Level By and Restore Saved Level

This device has two programming commands of its own, for a light that should be brighter for a while and then go back to the level it had, for example while a motion sensor sees someone. Composer's Programming view lists them under the device with its other commands. They act only when programming runs them, never by themselves, and they work the same on a zone or room light.

CommandChoicesWhat it does
Raise Level ByPoints: 1 to 100, default 30.
Ramp (ms): 0 to 60000, default 1000 (one second).		Saves the light's current level (0 when it is off), then sets the light to the saved level plus the points, at most 100 %, fading over the ramp time: 40 % becomes 70 %, 80 % becomes 100 %, and a light that is off turns on at 30 %. While a level is saved, another Raise Level By saves nothing new and does not climb: it sets the light to the saved level plus the points again. If someone has changed the light by hand since the raise, that level becomes the new starting point instead, so a raise never lowers a light someone turned up.
Restore Saved LevelRamp (ms): as above.If the light still stands at the level the last raise set (within 1 point, as Hue reports it), fades it back to the saved level, or turns it off when the saved level was 0. If the light was changed since, to another level or off, leaves it as it is. Either way the saved level is then forgotten. With no saved level it does nothing.

Every outcome is one line in Last Action, for example Raised from 40 % to 70 % (saved 40 %), Restored to 40 %, Not restored: changed to 55 % since the raise; saved level forgotten or Nothing to restore. While the device's light is gone from the bridge, or its connection to the bridge is removed, both commands are refused with a line in Last Action, and a saved level is kept. A saved level outlasts a driver update, a Director restart and a controller reboot. Both commands reach the light the way a slider move does, at the same pace. On a zone or room light the level is the zone's, the average of its lit lamps, and both commands set every lamp to one level, as the slider does. The update that brought the two commands changed no capability, so it needed no controller reboot.

An example. Two lamps, Lamp 1 and Lamp 2, should get 30 points brighter when a Hue Motion Sensor device (a Hue motion sensor or a MotionAware area; the bridge's manual, chapter 14) senses motion, and go back 5 minutes after the last movement:

  1. On the motion device's Properties tab, set Occupancy Hold Time (s) to 300 and click the row's Set button. The device then shows motion until 5 minutes after Hue reports that the motion has ended, which Hue does about 10 seconds after the last movement (measured on a MotionAware area): about 5 minutes and 10 seconds in all, started again by any movement before then.
  2. In Programming, on the motion device's event senses motion: Raise Level By on Lamp 1 and on Lamp 2, with Points 30 and Ramp (ms) 1000.
  3. On its event stops sensing motion: Restore Saved Level on both lamps, with Ramp (ms) 1000.

With Lamp 1 at 40 % and Lamp 2 off, someone walks in: Lamp 1 fades up to 70 % and Lamp 2 comes on at 30 %. About 5 minutes and 10 seconds after the last movement, Lamp 1 fades back to 40 % and Lamp 2 goes off. If someone set Lamp 1 to 55 % in the meantime (in the Hue app, on its tile or with a keypad), it stays at 55 %: it no longer shows the level the raise gave it, so someone chose a new level after the raise, and putting back the old one would undo that choice. A lamp switched off meanwhile stays off for the same reason. Its old level is forgotten, and the next motion raises the lamp from where it now is. The bridge's manual has this example in full in chapter 10, and the motion devices in chapter 14.

The device fills in the rows below itself, most of them from what the bridge tells it; the only one you change is Debug Mode, and only when asked. The keypad settings are described above.

PropertyWhat it shows
Driver VersionThe build number of the installed driver, for example 116. It matches the bridge's after an update; after an update during which Composer asked Restart Director?, only once the controller has been rebooted from System Manager (the bridge's manual, chapter 3).
Debug ModeOff (default) or On. On writes many more lines to this device's Lua output (the lower part of its Lua tab); developer use only. Remember that a changed property reaches the driver only when you click the row's Set button.
StatusIn normal use the Hue name and the light's model, for example Left Bedside Light (Hue color lamp) (Porch (unknown model) until the model is known). A zone light reads Hue zone Bedside Lights (2 lights), a room light Hue room Master Bedroom (9 lights). Other texts are explained below.

Blank until the bridge has linked the device; the long identifier alone for a moment when the device remembers its light but has not heard its number yet. For a zone or room light, zone #2 (5e6f7a8b…) or room #5 (…): the group's number on the bridge and the start of its identifier.
Hue NameThe light's name in the Hue app, as the bridge passes names on: without control characters or spaces at either end, and at most 100 characters long. For a bulb or lamp that is the only light of its Hue device, as most are, that is the name the Hue app shows for it, which Hue keeps on the device, and the name the bridge compares the Composer name with; for one of several lights of one Hue device (a fixture with more than one light in it), that light's own name. Right after the bridge driver restarts, the row keeps the name it showed until the bridge has read its devices, a few seconds later. From 1.2 the device's name in Composer and the light's name in the Hue app are kept the same by the bridge: rename the device in Composer and the light is renamed in the Hue app, rename the light there and the device follows, and this row shows the new name at once (the bridge's manual, 8.2). Hue takes names of at most 32 bytes; a longer Composer name waits for the bridge's Resolve Names. For a zone or room light, the zone's or room's name, which is not kept in step with the device's; a room light's Composer name, Master Bedroom Lights, is not counted as a difference.
ModelThe light's model as Hue reports it (its product name, else its model number), for example Hue color lamp or Hue white lamp, passed on as the Hue Name is. For a zone or room light, Hue zone (2 lights) or Hue room (9 lights); the count follows the lamps in it.
OnlineOnline when the bridge reports the light connected; Offline when it reports the light unreachable (no power, for example), or the device is not bound, gone or forgotten; blank while the bridge has not said either way yet (for ever, harmlessly, for a light whose Hue device reports no Zigbee connection). While Offline, and while the device waits for the bridge, the tile is disabled on Navigators. A zone light is Offline while its zone holds no lights.
Hue RoomThe Hue room the light belongs to, for example Master Bedroom; blank for a light in no Hue room. It follows a move in the Hue app within seconds; moving the device to another Control4 room does not change it. For a zone light, the zone's own label, Bedside Lights (zone); for a room light, the room's name.
Last ActionThe result of the last Raise Level By or Restore Saved Level (above), or Forgotten by the bridge after the bridge released it. For a forgotten zone light it carries the instruction, Delete this device, or bind it again and use Link Device on the Hue Bridge, because that device's Status is shorter.

Actions

From release 1.2 this device has no actions: its Actions tab is empty, and everything is on the Hue Bridge. Its two programming commands, Raise Level By and Restore Saved Level (above), are not actions and stay. Where the two earlier actions went:

Up to 1.1From 1.2
Adopt Hue NameNot needed: the bridge keeps the device's name and the light's name the same by itself. A name that cannot follow (Hue refuses it, or it differed already when 1.2 was installed) is listed in the bridge's Attention and settled with the bridge's Resolve Names.
Forget This LightThe bridge's Forget Device, which asks for confirmation. The device stays in the project for you to delete, and the Hue light is listed under the bridge's Add From Hue again.

When this device cannot act (not bound, waiting, gone or forgotten, or a zone light whose zone has no lights) it drops the commands it receives, and Status says why. Colour and colour temperature controls appear on Navigators only when the light has them; brightness is shown in whole percents, never 0 % while the light is on, and a colour set while the light is off is applied when Control4 next turns it on (a turn-on from elsewhere, the Hue app for example, drops it).

In the System Design tree this device shows a small Hue dimmer switch as its icon if it was created by a build that has the Hue icons; one created by an earlier build keeps Control4's standard bulb. In a room's List View it always shows the standard light icon.

Hue Light device

A Hue Light is one Philips Hue light (a bulb, strip or lamp as the Hue app lists it) shown as an ordinary Control4 light: on, off, dimming, and colour or colour temperature when the light supports them. It is created by the Hue Bridge device's Import All New or Add From Hue action, normally never added by hand, and it is driven entirely by the bridge: the bridge tells it what its light can do and what state it is in, and passes your commands on to the light. Changes made in the Hue app or with Hue switches show on this device within a second or two, because the bridge listens to the Hue bridge's event feed.

The same device can stand for a whole Hue zone or room when the bridge's Add From Hue created it from its zones and rooms: a zone light, named after the zone (Bedside Lights), or a room light, named after the room with Lights added (Master Bedroom Lights). Its one tile switches, dims and colours every lamp of the zone or room at once, with a single request to the bridge, and shows what they are doing. Everything on this page applies to it; the differences are noted where they occur.

The full manual, with pictures, is on the Hue Bridge device: select the bridge in System Design and open its Documentation tab. It covers installing, pairing, room mapping, importing, renames, replacements, deletions, restores, scenes, programming and troubleshooting. From release 1.2 this device has no actions of its own: everything you can do to it from Composer is on the bridge (below, "Actions").

In Control4

To Composer this is an ordinary dimmer: programming, keypad buttons, Navigators and Advanced Lighting Scenes use it like any other light. Programming also has two commands of its own for it, Raise Level By and Restore Saved Level (below). A tap on its tile that turns it on, "Turn On" in programming, "Toggle" while it is off and a tap on the top keypad button all turn it on the way its On Mode says: with Preset, the default, at its Default On Brightness, 100 % unless changed; with Previous, at the level it had when it went off ("On Mode" below). They never use the power-on level set in the Hue app. A light with colour turns on in the colour its colour on mode asks for, normally Control4's On colour ("Colours" below). Its on, off and level-changed events fire for changes made in the Hue app, by a Hue dimmer switch or by a Hue automation too. A whole-room command reaches Hue lights at ten a second, so a room with many of them finishes over a few seconds. A command that does not reach the Hue bridge never shows as done, and after 5 minutes without an answer from the bridge the light goes offline until the bridge answers again ("When the Hue bridge does not answer" below).

On a keypad, or on a Control4 Switch V2 or Essential Switch set to Use as 2 Button Keypad, it behaves like a Control4 dimmer once the buttons are bound to its Top Button Link and Bottom Button Link in Connections (listed under its Control Outputs; a single button goes to Toggle Button Link). A tap on the top button ramps the light to its Default On Brightness, 100 % unless changed: up from off or from a lower level, and down to it from a higher one (a light already at it stays as it is). With On Mode Previous, a tap from off ramps it to the level it had when it went off instead. A tap on the bottom button ramps it to off, and a toggle button ramps it on, as the top button does, while it is off and to off while it is on. Unless the click rates were changed, a ramp across the whole range takes 3 seconds and a shorter one its share: off to 100 % in 3 seconds, 50 % to off in 1.5 seconds, 70 % to 100 % in 0.9 seconds. A tap on any of the light's buttons while it ramps stops it, as on a Control4 dimmer: the lamp halts where it is and settles there within a second, whether you tap the top, the bottom or the toggle button, so the light never turns round, and one stopped just before it reaches off stays on, at 1 %. A second tap on the same button within about 0.6 seconds of the first is part of a double tap: it starts nothing and stops nothing, and the light goes on to where the first tap sent it, so programming on the keypad's double-tap event can run without the light changing course (a double tap on a toggle button turns the light on, or off, as one tap would; a triple tap the same). To stop a ramp, tap a little later than that. A button held for longer than 0.4 seconds makes Hue brighten the light (top, or toggle while the light is off) or dim it (bottom, or toggle while it is on), the full range in 5 seconds and at that speed from any level (from 50 % to full in 2.5 seconds), until the button is let go, as Hue's own dimmer switch does; held while the light ramps, the button first stops the ramp and then brightens or dims from where it stopped. Held while the light is off, the top or toggle button turns it on at 1 % and brightens from there (let go within about a second of pressing and it stays at 1 %); the bottom button does nothing. A tap that comes within a second after letting go of a hold on the same button is ignored, because some keypads send one. The keypad's LEDs follow the light's state, including changes made in the Hue app: a top or toggle button is lit blue while the light is on, a bottom button while it is off. The bridge's manual, chapter 11, has the setup steps; "Keypad settings" below says how to change the 100 %, the 3 seconds, the 5 seconds and the blue, and "Colours" what colour the light turns on and off in.

A tap stops any ramp of the light, not only one a tap started: a level that programming, a Navigator or an Advanced Lighting Scene sets with a ramp time (a slow wake-up ramp from programming, for example) stops where it is at a tap too, as a Control4 dimmer stops whenever it is tapped while it dims. Control4's own stop command, which an Advanced Lighting Scene sends to end its ramp, now stops the lamp the same way, and so does a keypad button bound to the light's Brightness Stop link in Connections, which Control4 provides for stopping a ramp. Up to release 1.3 both only set the tile to the level the device knew while the lamp went on to the end of its ramp. While the light ramps, the device tells Control4 once, at the start, where the ramp goes and how long it takes, and once more when it ends or stops, as Control4 asks its dimmers to do: Navigators move the tile's slider along from the first message and stop it at the second, and the light's level-changed event fires at the end or the stop rather than on the way. At a stop the device works out from how long the ramp ran where the lamp has got to, and sends the lamp that level with a one-second glide, so the lamp halts smoothly rather than with a jump; the tile shows that level at once, and Hue reports the same level straight away, so the tile, the Hue app and the lamp agree. Any other brightness command during a ramp takes over from it: the slider, "Turn On", "Turn Off", "Toggle", a level, Raise Level By or Restore Saved Level. A colour or colour temperature change leaves the ramp going. How the lamps ramp and stop was watched on the house system on 2026-11-09, on a single light and on a zone light; taps from a real keypad, the Brightness Stop link and the tile's moving slider had not been watched as this is written.

A zone light or room light is on while any lamp of its zone or room is lit, and its level is the average brightness of the lit lamps. A tap, the top button, "Turn On" and "Toggle" while it is off set every lamp to the zone or room light's own Default On Brightness (100 % unless changed), as on a single light; with On Mode Previous they bring every lamp back at its own level instead, so lamps set to different levels stay different; the slider, or a level from programming or a lighting scene, sets every lamp to that level, so the differences between the lamps are levelled out; a keypad hold dims the zone and keeps those differences, as Hue's dimmer switch does: smoothly, lamp by lamp, while up to 10 of its lamps are on, and in steps of a second with more (or while a time-of-day or moving scene plays there). Keypad taps ramp and stop a zone or room light as they do a single light, every lamp at once, with one request to the bridge for the start and one for the stop: a tap ramps every lamp to the Default On Brightness or to off over the click rate, and a tap while they ramp stops them, every lamp halting and settling within a second at the level the tile shows. Hue keeps the time of such a ramp to a level for a whole zone or room, and the stop is smooth on every lamp (watched on the house system on 2026-11-09); it ignores the time given for a relative dim of a whole zone or room, which is why a hold goes lamp by lamp. Colour and colour temperature go to all the lamps of the zone or room in one request, and so does the On colour of a turn-on (with colour on mode Previous each lamp keeps its own colour); a lamp that cannot show them should ignore them (not seen on the house system as this is written, where every lamp can show both). The tile has a colour wheel if any lamp has colour and a temperature control if any has colour temperature, and it shows the colour of the first lit lamp showing a colour, else the colour temperature of the first lit lamp showing one, because Hue reports no colour for a zone as a whole. Hue takes about one command a second for a zone or room (ten for single lights), so after a first burst of three a fast slider drag is sent as one request a second while the tile moves smoothly, and the lamps end where the slider ends; a hold's start and stop, and a keypad tap's start and stop, go out at once. Turning a zone or room light off also ends any Hue scene playing there, so a Hue Scene device of the same zone or room goes off with it (the bridge's manual, 9.4; seen on the house system on 2026-10-25 with a room light).

Keypad settings

The 100 %, the 3 seconds of a tap, the 5 seconds of a hold and the blue LEDs above are the defaults of the settings a Control4 dimmer normally lists on its Properties page, and Composer lists them for this device too, on its Properties tab above the driver's own rows (Advanced Properties):

  • Default On Brightness: the level of the light's On preset, 100 unless changed, from 1 to 100. A tap on the top button ramps the light to it, from below or from above. With On Mode Preset it is also the level that a toggle tap, a tap on the tile, "Turn On" and "Toggle" turn the light on to.
  • The On Mode, Preset (the default) or Previous: the level the light turns on at ("On Mode" below).
  • The click rates, up and down: how long a tap's ramp takes across the whole range, up (brightening, and turning on from off) and down (dimming, and turning off); 3 seconds each unless changed, and a shorter ramp takes its share (off to 40 % in 1.2 seconds). A rate of 0 makes a tap change the light at once, with no ramp to stop. Up to release 1.3 a click rate was the time of every tap whatever the distance, 250 ms by default, so a tap was over before a second one could stop it.
  • The hold ramp rates, up and down (Hold Ramp Rate Down, for example): how long a held button takes across the whole range, brightening and dimming; 5 seconds each unless changed, never less than 1 second in Composer.
  • On a light with colour or colour temperature, the On colour, the Dim colour and the colour on mode ("Colours" below).
  • The colours of the keypad buttons' LEDs while the light is on and while it is off: blue unless changed, a top or toggle button lit while the light is on and a bottom button while it is off.

Composer shows the rates in seconds (5.000) and the values the device is using. Type a new level or rate and press Enter: Composer sends it then, and the next button press uses it. The device takes rates from 0 to 6 000 000 ms (100 minutes), the longest fade Hue takes. A value outside these ranges, or one that is not a number, is refused: the device keeps the value it had and reports it back, so Composer shows that value again (a Default On Brightness of 0 would make the top button turn the light off). A device whose click rates were changed in Composer before keeps its values, and they now count for the whole range too: a tap across the whole range takes as long as before and a shorter one less; type 3.000 into each for the new default. Up to release 1.4 the device took rates up to 86 400 000 ms (24 hours), although no hold took longer than 100 minutes. A rate above 6 000 000 ms that an earlier release stored goes back to its default at the update (3 seconds for a click rate, 5 for a hold rate).

A zone or room light has the same settings, its own. Showing them changed what this device type can do in Control4, its capabilities, so after the update that brought them they appear only once the controller has been rebooted from System Manager, as after any such update (the bridge's manual, chapter 3); until then the defaults apply. The update that brought the On Mode, the colours and the 100-minute limit is such an update. Until that reboot Composer shows the old page, and the light turns on at its Default On Brightness as before.

On Mode

The On Mode decides the level for every way of turning the light on without choosing a level: a tap on its tile, "Turn On" in programming, "Toggle" while it is off, and a tap on the top or toggle button of a bound keypad while it is off. Composer lists it with the keypad settings, with two choices:

  • Preset (the default): the light turns on at a brightness preset, normally the On preset, which is the Default On Brightness (100 % unless changed). This is how releases 1.3 and 1.4 turned the light on; up to release 1.2, "Turn On" and "Toggle" always went to 100 %.
  • Previous: the light turns on at the level it had when it last went off. Right after a restart of the driver, before the light has gone off once, it uses the level Hue remembers, else the Default On Brightness. A zone or room light brings every lamp back at its own level, so lamps set to different levels stay different (not seen on the house system as this is written).

While the light is on, a tap on the top button and "Turn On" go to the preset's level with Preset, and to the Default On Brightness with Previous: up from a lower level, down from a higher one. A slider move, a level from programming or a lighting scene, Raise Level By and Restore Saved Level go to the level they name, whatever the On Mode. A held top button still starts a light that is off at 1 %. A light turned on in the Hue app or with a Hue switch comes on as Hue decides, at its last level.

The device tells Control4 which preset the light is going to and which one it sits at, as Control4 asks of its dimmers, so Control4 features that follow presets, such as the Daylight Agent, can follow the light (not tried on the house system as this is written). Control4's documentation says a tap on the tile follows the On Mode too; that had not been checked on the house system as this is written. Control4 added the On Mode in OS 3.3.2; on an older controller the light uses Preset.

Ramp times. "Turn On", "Turn Off" and "Toggle" in programming, a tap on the tile, and a level sent without a ramp time use the default ramp time that Control4 keeps for each light (its default brightness rate, if Composer lists it on the settings page). Until it is set they act at once, as before; keypad taps use the click rates. A ramp time from programming or a lighting scene can be up to 100 minutes; a longer one is cut to 100 minutes.

Colours

Control4 gives every colour light an On colour, a Dim colour and a colour on mode, so that lights of every brand turn on in the same colour. Composer lists them with the keypad settings of a light that has colour or colour temperature. The device uses them; up to release 1.4 it ignored them, and a light turned on in its last colour. Dealers: set each colour light's On and Dim colours and its colour on mode in Composer. Until then a light turns on in the On colour Composer shows, which is Control4's default.

The colour on mode has three choices:

  • Preset (the default): every turn-on from Control4 brings the light on in the On colour. At low levels the colour is blended toward the Dim colour: the On colour at the Default On Brightness and above, the Dim colour at 1 %, a mix in between. With On 4000 K, Dim 2000 K and a Default On Brightness of 80 %, for example, the light shows 3000 K at 40 %. The blend follows the light's level through slider moves, keypad taps and levels from programming, until someone picks a colour, the colour is changed in the Hue app, or the light goes off.
  • Fade: Control4's warm dim. On every level change from Control4 the colour follows the brightness: the Dim colour at 1 %, the On colour at 100 %, a mix in between. A colour someone picks stays until the next level change.
  • Previous: a turn-on sends no colour. The light comes on in the colour it had, as up to release 1.4; a zone or room light keeps each lamp's own colour.

With Preset and Fade, a turn-off that has a fade time fades toward the Dim colour as the light goes off: a tap on the bottom button, for example, or a level of 0 with a ramp time. An instant off sends no colour. Hue keeps the colour of a lamp that is off, so a lamp turned on afterwards in the Hue app or with a Hue switch comes on in the Dim colour. A held button changes only the brightness while you hold it; about half a second after you let go, the colour glides to the blend for the new level over a second.

When the Dim colour's checkbox in Composer is not ticked, Control4 makes the Dim colour the same as the On colour, so the light shows the On colour at every level. A colour picked while the light is off is kept and shown at the next turn-on from Control4, in place of the On colour. A zone or room light sends one colour to all its lamps; a lamp that cannot show it should ignore it. A white-only light never gets a colour, and a light with colour temperature only takes the nearest white. A light turned on in the Hue app, with a Hue switch or by a Hue automation comes on as Hue decides, and the device does not change its colour afterwards. Changing a colour setting in Composer never moves a light by itself: the next turn-on uses it.

Raise Level By and Restore Saved Level

This device has two programming commands of its own, for a light that should be brighter for a while and then go back to the level it had, for example while a motion sensor sees someone. Composer's Programming view lists them under the device with its other commands. They act only when programming runs them, never by themselves, and they work the same on a zone or room light.

CommandChoicesWhat it does
Raise Level ByPoints: 1 to 100, default 30.
Ramp (ms): 0 to 60000, default 1000 (one second).		Saves the light's current level (0 when it is off), then sets the light to the saved level plus the points, at most 100 %, fading over the ramp time: 40 % becomes 70 %, 80 % becomes 100 %, and a light that is off turns on at 30 %. While a level is saved, another Raise Level By saves nothing new and does not climb: it sets the light to the saved level plus the points again. If someone has changed the light by hand since the raise, that level becomes the new starting point instead, so a raise never lowers a light someone turned up.
Restore Saved LevelRamp (ms): as above.If the light still stands at the level the last raise set (within 1 point, as Hue reports it), fades it back to the saved level, or turns it off when the saved level was 0. If the light was changed since, to another level or off, leaves it as it is. Either way the saved level is then forgotten. With no saved level it does nothing.

Every outcome is one line in Last Action, for example Raised from 40 % to 70 % (saved 40 %), Restored to 40 %, Not restored: changed to 55 % since the raise; saved level forgotten or Nothing to restore. While the device's light is gone from the bridge, or its connection to the bridge is removed, both commands are refused with a line in Last Action, and a saved level is kept. A saved level outlasts a driver update, a Director restart and a controller reboot. Both commands reach the light the way a slider move does, at the same pace. On a zone or room light the level is the zone's, the average of its lit lamps, and both commands set every lamp to one level, as the slider does. The update that brought the two commands changed no capability, so it needed no controller reboot.

An example. Two lamps, Lamp 1 and Lamp 2, should get 30 points brighter when a Hue Motion Sensor device (a Hue motion sensor or a MotionAware area; the bridge's manual, chapter 14) senses motion, and go back 5 minutes after the last movement:

  1. On the motion device's Properties tab, set Occupancy Hold Time (s) to 300 and click the row's Set button. The device then shows motion until 5 minutes after Hue reports that the motion has ended, which Hue does about 10 seconds after the last movement (measured on a MotionAware area): about 5 minutes and 10 seconds in all, started again by any movement before then.
  2. In Programming, on the motion device's event senses motion: Raise Level By on Lamp 1 and on Lamp 2, with Points 30 and Ramp (ms) 1000.
  3. On its event stops sensing motion: Restore Saved Level on both lamps, with Ramp (ms) 1000.

With Lamp 1 at 40 % and Lamp 2 off, someone walks in: Lamp 1 fades up to 70 % and Lamp 2 comes on at 30 %. About 5 minutes and 10 seconds after the last movement, Lamp 1 fades back to 40 % and Lamp 2 goes off. If someone set Lamp 1 to 55 % in the meantime (in the Hue app, on its tile or with a keypad), it stays at 55 %: it no longer shows the level the raise gave it, so someone chose a new level after the raise, and putting back the old one would undo that choice. A lamp switched off meanwhile stays off for the same reason. Its old level is forgotten, and the next motion raises the lamp from where it now is. The bridge's manual has this example in full in chapter 10, and the motion devices in chapter 14.

When the Hue bridge does not answer

The light can do only what the Hue bridge passes on. When the bridge does not answer (it is restarting or updating, has no power or network, has moved to another address, or refuses the pairing), the light tells Control4 so, in two steps, and comes back by itself:

  • At once: nothing shows as done unless the bridge took it. A tap, a slider move, a keypad button or a level from programming starts as usual, but when the bridge does not take the command, the tile, the slider and the keypad LEDs go back to the level and colour the light last reported, not to the level you asked for. Once the bridge driver knows that the bridge is away (from a minute into an outage) that takes about a second. Right at the start of an outage, before it knows, the tile can show the new level for 5 to 15 seconds, until the request to the bridge gives up: Hue confirms a command only by answering it, so nothing quicker is possible. The command is still tried again for up to 30 seconds after the last command to the light: a bridge that answers within that time still takes it, and the tile follows when Hue reports the change. After that it is dropped, so no light jumps to a level asked for minutes earlier.
  • After 5 minutes: offline. When the bridge has not answered for 5 minutes, or has refused the pairing for 5 minutes, the light goes offline: Online reads Offline, the tile is disabled (greyed out) on Navigators, keypad buttons bound to the light do nothing and their LEDs keep the last known state, and commands from programming, Advanced Lighting Scenes or anywhere else are dropped, not kept for later. Status says why and since when, for example Hue Bridge not answering since 14:02 (the table at the end has every wording), and the Hue Bridge's Attention reads Hue devices offline since 14:07: the bridge has not answered since 14:02; they come back by themselves when it answers. Every Hue Light, zone light, room light and Hue Scene goes offline at the same moment, and every Hue Motion Sensor's Online turns No.
  • Back at the first answer. The moment the bridge answers again, the light is Online, its tile usable and its buttons working, with nothing to click; within seconds the tile shows what the light really does. A bridge restart, which takes a few minutes, stays under the 5 minutes, so the tiles do not grey for one.

While the bridge is offline the light's own reachability is not known, so Status shows the bridge's text; once the bridge is back, a lamp without power reads Offline again by itself. A controller reboot or a driver update during a long outage does not start the 5 minutes again: the light goes offline at the bridge's first failed check after the start, within a minute, and stays online if the bridge answers. A bridge that loses its power cannot say so: in a quiet house, where nobody uses a Hue device, the driver notices it only when the bridge's event feed has been silent for 10 minutes, so the light goes offline about 15 minutes after the power cut; a command sent meanwhile fails, and the 5 minutes then count from that command (the bridge's manual, chapter 4).

Actions

From release 1.2 this device has no actions: its Actions tab is empty, and everything is on the Hue Bridge. Its two programming commands, Raise Level By and Restore Saved Level (above), are not actions and stay. Where the two earlier actions went:

Up to 1.1From 1.2
Adopt Hue NameNot needed: the bridge keeps the device's name and the light's name the same by itself. A name that cannot follow (Hue refuses it, or it differed already when 1.2 was installed) is listed in the bridge's Attention and settled with the bridge's Resolve Names.
Forget This LightThe bridge's Forget Device, which asks for confirmation. The device stays in the project for you to delete, and the Hue light is listed under the bridge's Add From Hue again.

When this device cannot act (not bound, waiting, gone or forgotten, the Hue bridge offline for 5 minutes, or a zone light whose zone has no lights) it drops the commands it receives, and Status says why. Colour and colour temperature controls appear on Navigators only when the light has them; brightness is shown in whole percents, never 0 % while the light is on, and a colour set while the light is off is applied when Control4 next turns it on, in place of the On colour (a turn-on from elsewhere, the Hue app for example, drops it).

In the System Design tree this device shows a small Hue dimmer switch as its icon if it was created by a build that has the Hue icons; one created by an earlier build keeps Control4's standard bulb. In a room's List View it always shows the standard light icon.

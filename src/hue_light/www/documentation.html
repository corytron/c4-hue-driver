1. What this device is

A Hue Light is one Philips Hue light (a bulb, strip or lamp as the Hue app lists it) shown as an ordinary Control4 light, a dimmer: on, off, dimming, and colour or colour temperature when the light has them. Navigators show the colour and colour temperature controls only for a light that has them.

The same device can stand for a whole Hue zone or room instead: a zone light. Its one tile switches, dims and colours every lamp of the zone or room at once, with one request to the Hue bridge, and shows what they are doing. A zone light is named after its Hue zone (Accent); one made from a Hue room is named after the room with Lights added (Living Room Lights), and this page calls it a room light where the two differ. Everything on this page applies to zone lights; their differences are noted where they occur, most of them under "Zone lights" in section 3.

What it needs. The device does nothing on its own: the Hue Bridge device pairs with the Hue bridge, creates this device, and passes on every command and every change. The project's four drivers, Hue Bridge, Hue Light, Hue Scene and Hue Motion Sensor, come as four .c4z files in each release; install all four from the same release.

The full manual is on the Hue Bridge device: select the bridge in System Design and open its Documentation tab. This page calls it "the bridge's manual". Its chapters cover installing and updating (chapter 3), pairing and the bridge offline (4), room mappings (6), importing lights and zone lights (7), renames, replacements and deletions (8), the Hue Light device (10), using the lights from Control4, keypads included (11), and entertainment areas and syncing with a TV (16).

2. Adding and setting up

  • The bridge creates it. The Hue Bridge's Import All New (every new light at once) or Add From Hue (one light, or one Hue zone or room as a zone light) creates the device, already connected to the bridge and named as in the Hue app, in the Control4 room its Hue room maps to or in the room you pick (the bridge's manual, chapter 7). There is nothing to connect. A device added by hand from the Items pane needs a connection and a link: see Not bound to a Hue Bridge in section 7.
  • No actions of its own. The device's Actions tab is empty. What you do to it from Composer is on the Hue Bridge: Link Device points it at another light, zone or room, Forget Device releases it, and Resolve Names settles a name that could not follow (the bridge's manual, chapter 8; chapter 13 says where the actions of earlier releases went). Its two programming commands are in section 6.
  • Names follow both ways. Rename the device in Composer and the bridge renames the light in the Hue app; rename the light there and the device follows. A zone light's name is not kept in step (Hue Name, section 5).
  • Colour lights: set the colours. Dealers set each colour light's On colour, Dim colour and colour on mode in Composer ("Colours", section 3). Until then the light follows Control4's default colours and colour on mode.
  • Keypads and load groups are set up as for any Control4 dimmer: keypad buttons in Connections and the keypad settings on the Properties tab (section 4), load groups in Composer ("Load groups", section 3).
  • Updating. Install the four new .c4z files at once with Driver » Add or Update Driver or Agent…; every device updates in place and keeps its pairing and links (the bridge's manual, chapter 3). Never delete and re-add the devices. An update that changes what this device type can do in Control4, its capabilities, makes Composer ask Restart Director?: the new settings, events and load groups then appear only once the controller has been rebooted from System Manager, and until then Composer shows the old page and the light works as before. Release 1.5 was such an update (On Mode, the colour settings, the button events, load groups).
  • From an older release. Since 1.4 a click rate is the time of a ramp across the whole range, 3 seconds by default (up to 1.3 it was the time of every tap, 250 ms by default). A device whose click rates were changed in Composer keeps its values; type 3.000 into each for the new default. A click or hold rate above 6 000 000 ms (100 minutes), which releases before 1.5 accepted, goes back to its default (3 seconds for a click rate, 5 for a hold rate) at the update.

3. Everyday use

To Composer this is an ordinary dimmer: Navigators, the Control4 app, programming, keypads, Advanced Lighting Scenes and Load Groups use it like any other Control4 light.

Turning it on: On Mode

Every way of turning the light on without choosing a level (a tap on its tile, "Turn On" in programming, "Toggle" while it is off, a tap on the top or toggle button of a bound keypad while it is off) follows its On Mode, which Composer lists with the keypad settings (section 4):

  • Preset (the default): the light turns on at a brightness preset, normally the On preset, whose level is the Default On Brightness, 100 % unless changed.
  • Previous: the light turns on at the level it had when it last went off. Right after a restart of the driver, before the light has gone off once, it uses the level Hue remembers, else the Default On Brightness. A zone light brings every lamp back at its own level, so lamps set to different levels stay different.

Neither uses the power-on level set in the Hue app. While the light is on, a top tap and "Turn On" go to the preset's level with Preset and to the Default On Brightness with Previous: up from a lower level, down from a higher one. A slider move, a level from programming or a lighting scene, Raise Level By and Restore Saved Level go to the level they name, whatever the On Mode; a held top button starts a light that is off at 1 %. A light turned on in the Hue app or with a Hue switch comes on as Hue decides, at its last level.

The device tells Control4 which preset the light is going to and which one it sits at, as Control4 asks of its dimmers, so Control4 features that follow presets, such as the Daylight Agent, can follow the light. Control4 added the On Mode in OS 3.3.2; on an older controller the light uses Preset.

Colours

On a light with colour or colour temperature, Composer lists Control4's On colour, Dim colour and colour on mode with the keypad settings, so that lights of every brand turn on in the same colour. Which colour on mode a new device starts with is Composer's choice (Dim-To-Warm, the Fade mode below, when this was checked on 2026-11-10). The three modes:

  • Preset: every turn-on from Control4 brings the light on in the On colour, blended toward the Dim colour at low levels: the On colour at the Default On Brightness and above, the Dim colour at 1 %, a mix in between. With On 4000 K, Dim 2000 K and a Default On Brightness of 80 %, the light shows 3000 K at 40 %. The blend follows the level through slider moves, keypad taps and levels from programming until someone picks a colour, the colour is changed in Hue, or the light goes off.
  • Fade: Control4's warm dim. On every level change from Control4 the colour follows the brightness: the Dim colour at 1 %, the On colour at 100 %, a mix in between. A colour someone picks stays until the next level change.
  • Previous: a turn-on sends no colour, so the light comes on in the colour it had; a zone light's lamps each keep their own.

With Preset and Fade, a turn-off that has a fade time (a bottom tap, or a level of 0 with a ramp time) fades toward the Dim colour; an instant off sends no colour. Hue keeps the colour of a lamp that is off, so a lamp turned on afterwards in the Hue app or with a Hue switch comes on in the Dim colour. A held button changes only the brightness; about half a second after you let go, the colour glides to the blend for the new level over a second. When the Dim colour's checkbox in Composer is not ticked, Control4 makes the Dim colour the same as the On colour, so the light shows the On colour at every level.

A colour picked while the light is off is kept and shown at the next turn-on from Control4, in place of the On colour; a turn-on from elsewhere, the Hue app for example, drops it. A white-only light never gets a colour, and a light with colour temperature only takes the nearest white. A light turned on in the Hue app, with a Hue switch or by a Hue automation comes on as Hue decides, and the device does not change its colour afterwards. Changing a colour setting in Composer never moves a light by itself: the next turn-on uses it.

Ramps and stops

"Turn On", "Turn Off" and "Toggle" in programming, a tap on the tile, and a level sent without a ramp time use the default ramp time that Control4 keeps for each light (its default brightness rate, if Composer lists it with the keypad settings); until that is set they act at once. Keypad taps use the click rates (section 4). A ramp time can be up to 100 minutes; a longer one is cut to 100 minutes.

While the light ramps, the device tells Control4 once, at the start, where the ramp goes and how long it takes, and once more when it ends or stops, as Control4 asks of its dimmers: Navigators move the tile's slider along from the first message and stop it at the second, and the light's level-changed event fires at the end or the stop rather than on the way.

Any ramp, also one that programming, a Navigator or an Advanced Lighting Scene started with a ramp time (a slow wake-up ramp, say), stops where it is at a tap on any of the light's keypad buttons, at Control4's stop command (which an Advanced Lighting Scene sends to end its ramp), and at a keypad button bound to the light's Brightness Stop link in Connections, as a Control4 dimmer does. The device works out from how long the ramp ran where the lamp has got to, and sends the lamp that level with a one-second glide, so it halts smoothly rather than with a jump; the tile shows that level at once, and Hue reports the same level straight away. Any other brightness command during a ramp takes over from it: the slider, "Turn On", "Turn Off", "Toggle", a level, Raise Level By or Restore Saved Level. A colour or colour temperature change leaves the ramp going.

Changes made in Hue

A change made outside Control4 (in the Hue app, with a Hue dimmer switch or tap dial, or by a Hue automation) reaches the device within about a second, and its on, off and level events fire for it.

  • Turning the light on or off in Hue shows at once, on the tile and on the keypad LEDs.
  • While such a change moves the level (a slider dragged in the Hue app, a Hue switch held down, a Hue automation fading the light), the device passes the new level on to Control4 at most once a second, as Control4 asks of its dimmers, and the last level it passes on is exact, at most about a second after the light stops. Hue reports where a light is, never where it is going, so the change shows in Control4 as steps rather than one smooth ramp. The device itself knows the exact level at once: a keypad tap, Raise Level By and Restore Saved Level start from it.
  • A level Hue reports when the driver starts, or when the Hue bridge answers again after an outage, goes out at once. A level or ramp from Control4 started while a change made in Hue is still being reported takes over at once, without the tile jumping back on the way.
  • Colour and colour temperature changes made in Hue are passed on as the Hue bridge reports them, up to about two a second.

Pace

Hue takes about ten commands a second for single lights and about one a second for a zone or room. A whole-room command from Control4 reaches single Hue Lights at ten a second, so a room with many of them finishes over a few seconds; a zone light needs one request. A command that does not reach the Hue bridge never shows as done (section 7).

Zone lights

A zone light is on while any lamp of its zone or room is lit, and its level is the average brightness of the lit lamps (0 % while all are off).

  • Turning on. A tap, the top button, "Turn On" and "Toggle" while it is off set every lamp to the zone light's own Default On Brightness, as on a single light; with On Mode Previous every lamp comes back at its own level instead.
  • The slider, or a level from programming or a lighting scene, sets every lamp to that level, so the differences between the lamps are levelled out.
  • Keypad taps ramp and stop every lamp at once, with one request to the bridge for the start and one for the stop; the lamps halt and settle within a second at the level the tile shows.
  • A keypad hold brightens or dims the lamps that are on and keeps the differences between them, as Hue's dimmer switch does: smoothly, lamp by lamp, while up to 10 of its lamps are on, and in steps of a second with more, or while a time-of-day scene or a scene with moving colours plays there. Hue ignores the time given for a relative dim of a whole zone or room, which is why a smooth hold goes lamp by lamp. Lamps that are off stay off.
  • Colour and colour temperature, and the On colour of a turn-on, go to all the lamps in one request (with colour on mode Previous each lamp keeps its own colour); a lamp that cannot show them should ignore them. The tile has a colour wheel if any lamp has colour and a temperature control if any has colour temperature. It shows the colour of the first lit lamp showing a colour, else the colour temperature of the first lit lamp showing one, because Hue reports no colour for a zone as a whole.
  • Pace. After a first burst of three, a fast slider drag is sent as one request a second while the tile moves smoothly, and the lamps end where the slider ends. A hold's start and stop, and a keypad tap's start and stop, go out at once.
  • Scenes. Turning a zone light off also ends any Hue scene playing in its zone or room, so a Hue Scene device of the same zone or room goes off with it (the bridge's manual, 9.4).
  • An empty zone. A zone or room whose lamps were all removed in the Hue app leaves the device Offline (section 7, Hue zone Accent has no lights; nothing to control).

Load groups

The device can be a member of a Control4 Load Group, so a load group switches and dims Hue lights in programming, on keypads and on Navigators as it does other Control4 lights. A single light and a zone light can each join; a Hue Scene device cannot, because a scene is not a load. Add the Load Group in Composer and pick its members as for any Control4 lights; each member's Load Groups row (section 5) then names the group. Composer offers Hue Lights for a load group only after the controller has been rebooted once following the update to 1.5 or later (section 2).

  • Each member does what the group asks: on, off, level, ramp and hold, as it follows its own commands. Its tile, its keypad LEDs and its events follow as usual. A group command names its level, so the member's Default On Brightness does not apply.
  • Its own controls still work. A tap on the member's own keypad stops its ramp, also one the group started. The group's stop does not end a hold on the member's own keypad.
  • Brightness only. A load group has no colour: a colour light in a group dims and switches like a white one, and a lamp the group turns on from off comes on in the colour a level from programming would give it. Set colours on the device or with an Advanced Lighting Scene.
  • What moves together. When the Hue lights of a group make up whole Hue rooms or zones, each such room or zone gets one request and its lamps move together; a zone light in the group is always one request. Other lamps get one request each, a few hundredths of a second apart: a ramp hides that, an instant change can show it, up to about 0.4 seconds across 10 lamps. A held button moves each lamp on its own, because Hue ignores the time of a dim for a whole room or zone; a zone light in the group holds as it does on its own keypad.
  • Keep loads in sync. The device shows this setting of the group in its Load Groups row. Keeping the loads together is the Load Group's job: neither this device nor the bridge passes a change at one member on to the others, also not a change made in the Hue app.

When the device cannot act (not bound, gone or offline) it drops the group's commands as it drops any other, and Status says why. Its memberships outlast a driver update, a Director restart and a controller reboot. The bridge's manual, chapter 11, has the details.

While the TV syncs

A lamp can belong to a Hue entertainment area: lamps that the Hue app sets up for light sync with a TV, a PC screen or music. While a Hue Play HDMI Sync Box or one of Hue's Sync apps syncs to the area, the lamp follows the picture or the music, sent to it directly many times a second. Hue then reports nothing new for it: the Hue bridge goes on reporting the state the lamp had before the sync. The device keeps that state and says what is going on:

  • Status reads Syncing with the TV (another app). The words in brackets say who syncs; until the sync box gets a driver of its own in a later 2.0 beta, every sync, a box's included, reads another app. The tile, the slider and the keypad LEDs keep the state from before the sync.
  • Commands are not sent. A tap, a slider move, "Turn On", "Turn Off", "Toggle", a level or a ramp from programming, a colour or colour temperature, and a keypad tap or hold change nothing: the sync keeps running, and the tile goes back at once to the state it showed. Last Action reads Not changed: the lamp follows the TV while it syncs (another app). Control4 cannot end a sync: stop it in the app or on the device that runs it.
  • It stays online. The device never goes offline because of the sync.
  • Events. Hue reports nothing for the lamp while it syncs, so the device's on, off and level events do not fire for what the TV does. The Hue Bridge has events of its own for a sync starting and stopping (the bridge's manual, chapter 16).
  • A zone light whose lamps all sync behaves the same way, with Last Action Not changed: the lamps follow the TV while they sync (another app). While only some of its lamps sync, Status reads, for example, 2 of 6 lamps syncing with the TV: the tile shows the lamps that do not sync (on while any of them is on, at the average level of those that are lit), and commands go out as usual, so those lamps obey while the others keep following the TV.
  • When the sync ends, Status returns to its usual text and the device follows Hue again. The Hue Bridge then puts the lamp back as it was before the sync, unless its After A Sync Ends reads As Hue decides; the device's events fire for that as for any change. A command sent during the sync is not carried out afterwards.

4. Keypads

On a keypad, or on a Control4 Switch V2 or Essential Switch set to Use as 2 Button Keypad, the light behaves like a Control4 dimmer once the buttons are bound in Connections to its Top Button Link and Bottom Button Link (listed under its Control Outputs), or a single button to its Toggle Button Link. The bridge's manual, chapter 11, has the setup steps.

  • Tap. The top button ramps the light to its Default On Brightness: up from off or from a lower level, down from a higher one (a light already there stays as it is); with On Mode Previous, a tap from off ramps it to the level it had when it went off. The bottom button ramps it to off. A toggle button ramps it on, as the top button does, while it is off, and to off while it is on. A ramp across the whole range takes the click rate, 3 seconds unless changed, and a shorter one its share: off to 100 % in 3 seconds, 50 % to off in 1.5 seconds, 70 % to 100 % in 0.9 seconds.
  • A tap while it ramps on any of the light's buttons stops the ramp, as on a Control4 dimmer: the lamp halts where it is and settles there within a second, so it never turns round, and one stopped just before it reaches off stays on at 1 %. That goes for every ramp ("Ramps and stops", section 3).
  • Double tap. A second tap on the same button within about 0.6 seconds of the first is part of a double tap: it starts and stops nothing, and the light goes on to where the first tap sent it, so programming on the double-tap event can run without the light changing course (a double or triple tap on a toggle button turns the light on, or off, as one tap would). To stop a ramp, tap a little later than that.
  • Hold. A button held for longer than 0.4 seconds makes Hue brighten the light (top, or toggle while the light is off) or dim it (bottom, or toggle while it is on) until you let go, as Hue's own dimmer switch does: the full range in the hold rate, 5 seconds unless changed, at that speed from any level (from 50 % to full in 2.5 seconds). Held while the light ramps, the button first stops the ramp and then brightens or dims from there. Held while the light is off, the top or toggle button turns it on at 1 % and brightens from there (let go within about a second of pressing and it stays at 1 %); the bottom button does nothing. A tap within a second after letting go of a hold on the same button is ignored, because some keypads send one.
  • LEDs. The buttons' LEDs follow the light's state, including changes made in the Hue app: a top or toggle button is lit blue while the light is on, a bottom button while it is off.

6. Programming

In programming the device is a Control4 light: Control4's own light events (on, off, level changed), conditionals and commands work as for any dimmer, and its on, off and level events fire also for changes made in the Hue app, by a Hue switch or by a Hue automation (section 3). The device adds no variables or conditionals of its own. It adds two commands, its buttons' events and Set All LEDs.

Raise Level By and Restore Saved Level

Two commands for a light that should be brighter for a while and then go back to the level it had, for example while a motion sensor sees someone. Composer's Programming view lists them under the device with its other commands. They act only when programming runs them, and they work the same on a zone light.

CommandChoicesWhat it does
Raise Level ByPoints: 1 to 100, default 30.
Ramp (ms): 0 to 60000, default 1000 (one second).		Saves the light's current level (0 when it is off), then sets the light to the saved level plus the points, at most 100 %, fading over the ramp time: 40 % becomes 70 %, 80 % becomes 100 %, and a light that is off turns on at 30 %. While a level is saved, another Raise Level By saves nothing new and does not climb: it sets the light to the saved level plus the points again. If someone has changed the light since the raise, that level becomes the new starting point instead, so a raise never lowers a light someone turned up.
Restore Saved LevelRamp (ms): as above.If the light still stands at the level the last raise set (within 1 point, as Hue reports it), fades it back to the saved level, or turns it off when the saved level was 0. If the light was changed since, to another level or off, leaves it as it is: someone chose a new level, and putting back the old one would undo that. Either way the saved level is then forgotten. With no saved level it does nothing.

Every outcome is one line in Last Action, for example Raised from 40 % to 70 % (saved 40 %), Restored to 40 %, Not restored: changed to 55 % since the raise; saved level forgotten or Nothing to restore. While the device's light is gone from the bridge, or its connection to the bridge is removed, both commands are refused with a line in Last Action, and a saved level is kept. A saved level outlasts a driver update, a Director restart and a controller reboot. Both commands reach the light the way a slider move does, at the same pace. On a zone light the level is the average of its lit lamps, and both commands set every lamp to one level, as the slider does.

An example. Lamp 1 and Lamp 2 should get 30 points brighter when a Hue Motion Sensor device senses motion, and go back 5 minutes after the last movement. On the motion device's Properties tab, set Occupancy Hold Time (s) to 300 and click Set. In Programming, on its event senses motion, add Raise Level By on both lamps with Points 30 and Ramp (ms) 1000; on its event stops sensing motion, add Restore Saved Level on both with Ramp (ms) 1000. With Lamp 1 at 40 % and Lamp 2 off, someone walks in: Lamp 1 fades up to 70 % and Lamp 2 comes on at 30 %. About 5 minutes and 10 seconds after the last movement (Hue reports the end of motion about 10 seconds after it, as measured on a MotionAware area, and the hold adds 5 minutes) they go back to 40 % and off; a lamp someone changed in the meantime stays as it was set. The bridge's manual has this example in full in chapter 10, and the motion devices in chapter 14.

Button events

The device fires the button events of a Control4 dimmer: a press, a release, and a single, double and triple tap for each of its top, bottom and toggle buttons, fifteen events in all, listed among the device's events in Composer's Programming view. A Hue lamp has no buttons of its own, so the presses are those of keypad buttons bound to its Button Links (section 4), and the button presses Control4 sends the device directly.

  • Every press fires them, whatever the light then does. A tap that ramps the light, a tap that stops a ramp, the second tap of a double tap (which changes nothing at the light) and a press while the device cannot act (not bound, gone, forgotten, or offline while the Hue bridge does not answer) all fire them. Programming that should follow what the light did belongs on its on, off and level events.
  • When each one fires. The press fires as a button goes down and the release as it comes up. The single tap fires about 0.6 seconds after a tap's press, once no second press of the same button has come in that time; two taps of the same button within about 0.6 seconds of each other fire the double tap instead, three the triple tap, and a fourth adds nothing. Press and release fire for each tap of a double or triple tap too, as on a Control4 dimmer, which is why Control4 advises against programming a button's press or release and its taps together. A button held for longer than 0.4 seconds fires press, then release when it is let go, and no tap. A tap of the same button just before a hold counts on its own, so it fires its single tap as the hold begins. Taps on different buttons are counted apart.
  • The light does what it did before. The events change nothing in what a press does to the light (section 4): a double tap fires while the light goes on to where the first tap sent it, and two taps about 1.5 seconds apart ramp the light, stop it, and fire two single taps.
  • The keypad fires its own events as well. A keypad button bound to this device also fires its own events, in the keypad's programming. For any one press, put the programming on the keypad's event or on the light's, not on both, or it runs twice.
  • Never let a light's button event press its own buttons. Programming on one of this device's button events must not press a button of the same device, for example a keypad button bound to its Button Links: each press fires the event again, and the programming repeats itself until someone stops it. The driver cannot see programming and cannot prevent this.
  • Hue switches do not fire them. A Hue dimmer switch, tap dial or other Hue switch moves the lamps through the Hue bridge, which reports what the lamps do, not which switch moved them. What such a switch does fires the light's on, off and level events, never its button events.

Set All LEDs

Control4's Set All LEDs command sets the LED colours of all three buttons, lit and unlit, to one colour: the six LED colours of the keypad settings (section 4). The device keeps them as if each had been set on the Properties page, so that page shows them and they outlast a driver update, a Director restart and a controller reboot, and the buttons of bound keypads take them at once, lit or unlit as the light is on or off. A later change of one colour on the Properties page changes that one again. The command also works while the device cannot act. A colour that is not a six-digit hexadecimal RGB value (ff0000 is red) changes nothing, and the colours already set stay.

7. Troubleshooting

When the Hue bridge does not answer

The light can do only what the Hue bridge passes on. When the bridge does not answer (it is restarting or updating, has no power or network, has moved to another address, or refuses the pairing), the light tells Control4 so, in two steps, and comes back by itself:

  • At once: nothing shows as done unless the bridge took it. A tap, a slider move, a keypad button or a level from programming starts as usual, but when the bridge does not take the command, the tile, the slider and the keypad LEDs go back to the level and colour the light last reported. Once the bridge driver knows that the bridge is away (from a minute into an outage) that takes about a second; right at the start of an outage the tile can show the new level for 5 to 15 seconds, until the request gives up, because Hue confirms a command only by answering it. The command is still tried again for up to 30 seconds after the last command to the light: a bridge that answers within that time still takes it, and the tile follows when Hue reports the change. After that it is dropped, so no light jumps to a level asked for minutes earlier.
  • After 5 minutes: offline. When the bridge has not answered, or has refused the pairing, for 5 minutes, the light goes offline: Online reads Offline, the tile is disabled (greyed out) on Navigators, keypad buttons bound to the light do nothing and their LEDs keep the last known state, and commands from programming, Advanced Lighting Scenes or anywhere else are dropped, not kept for later. Status says why and since when, for example Hue Bridge not answering since 14:02 (every wording is in the table below), and the Hue Bridge's Attention reads Hue devices offline since 14:07: the bridge has not answered since 14:02; they come back by themselves when it answers. Every Hue Light, zone light and Hue Scene goes offline at the same moment, and every Hue Motion Sensor's Online turns No.
  • Back at the first answer. The moment the bridge answers again, the light is Online, its tile usable and its buttons working, with nothing to click; within seconds the tile shows what the light really does. A bridge restart takes a few minutes and stays under the 5 minutes, so the tiles do not grey for one.

While the bridge is offline the light's own reachability is not known, so Status shows the bridge's text; once the bridge is back, a lamp without power reads Offline again by itself. A controller reboot or a driver update during a long outage does not start the 5 minutes again: the light goes offline at the bridge's first failed check after the start, within a minute, and stays online if the bridge answers. A bridge that loses its power cannot say so: in a quiet house, where nobody uses a Hue device, the driver notices only when the bridge's event feed has been silent for 10 minutes, so the light goes offline about 15 minutes after the power cut; a command sent meanwhile fails, and the 5 minutes then count from that command (the bridge's manual, chapter 4).

Things that look wrong but are not

  • Brightness is shown in whole percents, and never as 0 % while the light is on.
  • In the System Design tree the device shows a small Hue dimmer switch as its icon if it was created by a build that has the Hue icons; one created by an earlier build keeps Control4's standard bulb. In a room's List View it always shows the standard light icon.
  • Debug Mode is Off again although it was set to On: it turns itself back Off after 8 hours, and Last Action says so (section 5). Set it On again if support still needs it.
  • A tap or a slider move changes nothing and the tile springs back: the lamp syncs with a TV, and Status says so ("While the TV syncs", section 3).
  • The lamp changed by itself a few seconds after a sync with the TV ended: the Hue Bridge put it back as it was before the sync (its After A Sync Ends; the bridge's manual, chapter 16).

1. What this device is

A Hue Scene is one Philips Hue scene (a saved look for a Hue room or zone) shown as a Control4 dimmer: a tile with a slider and a percentage on Navigators and in the Control4 app. Turning it ON plays the scene on the Hue bridge, so every light of that Hue room or zone takes the scene's look; turning it OFF turns that whole Hue room or zone off. The percentage is the room's or zone's brightness while this scene is the one playing, and 0 while it is not.

A Hue Scene can also stand for one of Hue's time-of-day scenes, such as Golden hours or Natural light (Hue calls them smart scenes), which play a different ordinary scene in each time slot of the day. ON then starts that schedule and OFF stops it; "Time-of-day scenes" in section 3 lists what else differs.

What it needs. The device does nothing on its own: the Hue Bridge device pairs with the Hue bridge, creates this device, and passes on every command and every change. The project's four drivers, Hue Bridge, Hue Light, Hue Scene and Hue Motion Sensor, come as four .c4z files in each release; install all four from the same release.

The full manual is on the Hue Bridge device: select the bridge in System Design and open its Documentation tab. This page calls it "the bridge's manual". Its chapters cover installing and updating (chapter 3), room mappings and why scenes stay in their mapped room (6), renames (8), scenes (9; time-of-day scenes in 9.11) and using scenes and keypads from Control4 (11). The Hue Light device's own Documentation tab describes the button events in more detail.

2. Adding and setting up

  • The bridge creates it. The Hue Bridge's Add From Hue creates the device, one scene or every scene of a Hue room or zone at once, already connected to the bridge. A scene goes only into the Control4 room that its Hue room or zone maps to (Set Room Mapping on the bridge); a scene of an unmapped room is refused with a text that names Set Room Mapping (the bridge's manual, chapter 6). A device you later drag to another room keeps working. Never add a Hue Scene by hand.
  • No actions of its own. The device's Actions tab is empty. What you do to it from Composer is on the Hue Bridge: Link Device points it at another scene, Forget Device releases it, and Resolve Names settles a name that could not follow (the bridge's manual, chapters 8 and 9; chapter 13 says where the actions of earlier releases went).
  • Names follow both ways. Rename the device in Composer and the bridge renames the scene in the Hue app; rename the scene there and the device follows, also after the device was pointed at another scene with Link Device (the scene then takes the device's name). A zone's scene carries the zone's name in front on the Composer side only: the device Accent Nightlight is the Hue scene Nightlight.
  • Two rows you set: Recall As, how ON plays the scene, and Keypad Follows, what a bound keypad treats as on (section 5). Pick the value, then click the row's Set button.
  • Keypads are bound in Connections and set on the Properties tab as for any Control4 dimmer (section 4). A scene cannot be a member of a Control4 Load Group: it is not a load.
  • Updating. Install the four new .c4z files at once with Driver » Add or Update Driver or Agent…; every device updates in place and keeps its pairing and links (the bridge's manual, chapter 3). Never delete and re-add the devices. An update that changes what this device type can do in Control4, its capabilities, makes Composer ask Restart Director?: its new settings and events then appear only once the controller has been rebooted from System Manager, and until then the device works as before. Release 1.5 was such an update (the click rates in Composer and the button events).
  • From a release before 1.6. The tile now follows its own scene only (section 3). Up to 1.5 the setting then called On State Follows, at its default, also made the tile read on whenever any light of the room or zone was lit. That setting is now Keypad Follows and decides only what a bound keypad shows; at the first start after the update the device copies the old value into it once and hides the old row, which then does nothing, and a value you set later is never overwritten. A click or hold rate above 6 000 000 ms (100 minutes), which releases before 1.5 accepted, goes back to its default at the update.

3. Everyday use

To Control4 the scene is a dimmer: programming, keypad buttons, Navigators and Advanced Lighting Scenes turn it on and off and set its level like any other light. In an Advanced Lighting Scene it is a dimmer member with a level.

ON, OFF and the slider

  • ON plays the scene, with one request to the bridge, as Recall As says (section 5). Editing the scene's colours or brightness in the Hue app needs nothing here; the next ON plays the new look.
  • OFF turns off every light of the Hue room or zone, imported into Control4 or not, with one request; a zone can span several Control4 rooms. A zone with no lights in it is reported off without any request.
  • TOGGLE on a scene that is not on plays it, also while another scene lights the room, as a Control4 Advanced Lighting scene's toggle does; on a scene that is on it turns the room or zone off.
  • The slider while the scene is on brightens or dims the room or zone relative to where it is, keeping the scene's colours and the differences between its lights (while the bridge does not know the room's level yet, it sets that level on every light instead); 0 % turns the room or zone off.
  • The slider while the scene is not on (the room off, or lit by another scene) switches to this scene at once and plays it at that brightness, all its lights at that level; 100 % plays it exactly as designed.
  • Ramp times. A ramp time from programming or an Advanced Lighting Scene is passed to Hue as the time to fade into the scene, and Hue fades over that time. "Turn On", "Turn Off" and "Toggle" in programming, a tap on the tile, and a level sent without a ramp time use the default ramp time that Control4 keeps for the device (its default brightness rate, if Composer lists it with the keypad settings); until that is set they act at once. Keypad taps use the click rates (section 4). A ramp time can be up to 100 minutes, the longest Hue takes.
  • While the level changes (a keypad hold, a slow fade, a change made in Hue) the percentage on Navigators updates at most once a second and settles on the level Hue reports at the end.

When the tile reads on

The tile follows its own scene and nothing else. The scene reads on from the moment it is played (from Control4, a keypad, the Hue app, a Hue switch or automation, or a time-of-day scene's schedule) until one of these happens:

  • its Hue room or zone is turned off, from anywhere, the Hue app and a Hue dimmer switch included;
  • another scene of the same room or zone is played;
  • a scene of another room or zone that shares a lamp with it is played (a scene of the whole home shares every lamp);
  • this device turns it off.

Dimming or changing its lights afterwards, with the slider, a keypad, the Hue app or a Hue dimmer switch, does not turn it off, even when Hue itself no longer calls the scene active (Hue stops doing so when a single light of the scene is changed; a dim of the whole room or zone leaves the scene active): the tile shows the new percentage instead. An ON tile therefore means "this was the last scene played here, and its room or zone is on". While another scene plays, a scene with no device, or lamps turned on by hand, the tile reads 0, as an inactive Control4 Advanced Lighting scene does. The on state survives a controller restart, and so does the last percentage the bridge reported: until the bridge answers again, the tile shows that percentage, not 100. After a restart or an outage of the bridge its first answer puts the tile right within seconds. A scene deleted in Hue reads 0 at once, and a device the bridge points at another scene shows nothing until the bridge reports that scene. A time-of-day scene follows Hue instead (below). What a bound keypad shows can differ from the tile: see Keypad Follows in section 4.

Time-of-day scenes

Hue's time-of-day scenes are schedules for one room or zone: each time slot of the day plays an ordinary scene (the Living Room's Golden hours plays Arise from 07:00 and Shine from 16:00), and Hue moves on to the next slot's scene by itself. The bridge's Add From Hue lists each one in the folder of its room or zone as, for example, Golden hours (time-based), and picking that folder as a whole creates them too. The device is named without the number the Hue app gives its copies (Golden hours 4 becomes Golden hours), and a zone's carries the zone's name in front (Accent Golden hours). Everything on this page applies to such a device, except:

  • ON starts the schedule: within about a second the room or zone takes the look of the current time slot's scene, and from then on Hue changes the look at the start of each slot. A ramp time is not used.
  • OFF stops the schedule and turns the whole room or zone off.
  • Keypad taps start and stop the schedule at once, with no fade: Hue cannot fade a time-of-day scene in or out, so the click rates do not apply and there is no fade for a later tap to stop. A second tap within about 0.6 seconds is still part of a double tap and does nothing.
  • The slider and a held keypad button brighten or dim the room or zone while the schedule keeps running; while it runs, a hold moves in steps of a second, never lamp by lamp, so that it keeps running (section 4). When the next time slot starts, Hue plays that slot's scene.
  • A level from off. Hue cannot start a time-of-day scene at a chosen brightness. From off, a level below 100 % (the slider, programming, an Advanced Lighting Scene) starts the schedule and a moment later, a second and a half at most, moves the room or zone to that level. A held top or toggle button starts it and then, while you still hold it, brightens from the time slot's own level, not from 1 %. Holding the bottom button while it is off does nothing.
  • The tile and the on and off events follow Hue: on while Hue runs the schedule, off as soon as Hue stops it for any reason, such as its room or zone turned off from anywhere, another scene of it played, or another time-of-day scene started on its lights. A dim of the whole room or zone does not stop it. Turning the room on again does not restart the schedule; ON does. The keypad LEDs follow Keypad Follows as for any scene.
  • An ordinary scene that the schedule plays and that also has a device reads on while its time slot plays and off when the next slot begins.
  • Recall As does not apply and is not shown. Status names the scene playing now and when its time slot began (section 5).
  • If the time-of-day scene is deleted in the Hue app, or one of the ordinary scenes it plays is (Hue then deletes the whole time-of-day scene by itself), the device reads Scene deleted in Hue since …, as for any scene (section 7). One created in the Hue app appears in the bridge's Add From Hue by itself, and changing its time slots in the Hue app needs nothing here.

4. Keypads

On a keypad, or on a Control4 Switch V2 or Essential Switch set to Use as 2 Button Keypad, the scene behaves like a Control4 dimmer once the buttons are bound in Connections to its Top Button Link and Bottom Button Link (listed under its Control Outputs), or a single button to its Toggle Button Link. The bridge's manual, chapter 11, has the setup steps.

Keypad Follows

A bound keypad can show something other than the tile: whether the scene's room or zone is lit at all. The Keypad Follows row decides what the keypad treats as on, which sets its LEDs, which way its toggle button goes and whether a hold dims a lit room. It never changes the tile.

  • Room/zone lights (the default): on while the scene is on or while any light of its room or zone is lit, whatever lit it. While another scene lights the room, the LEDs stay lit, a hold dims or brightens the room from where it is without making this scene the one playing (its tile stays at 0), a bottom tap and the toggle button fade the room out, and a top tap plays this scene.
  • This scene: on only while the tile is. While another scene lights the room, the LEDs read off, the toggle button and a top tap play this scene, a held top button plays it at the room's current level and brightens from there, a held bottom button does nothing, and a bottom tap turns the room off.

A keypad that shows another state than the tile departs from how a Control4 Advanced Lighting scene's keypad tracks; the default was chosen so that a wall switch keeps showing whether the room is lit. This scene gives the device one state again.

Taps, holds and LEDs

  • Tap. The top button fades the scene in over the click rate, 3 seconds for the whole range unless changed, and a shorter fade its share: from off in 3 seconds, from 40 % in 1.8 seconds (a scene already on at 100 % stays as it is). The bottom button fades the Hue room or zone out to off at the same pace (from 100 % in 3 seconds, from 50 % in 1.5 seconds). A toggle button fades the scene in while the keypad reads it off and the room or zone out while it reads it on.
  • A tap while it fades on any of the scene's buttons stops the fade where it is: each lamp keeps the level it has reached, so the scene keeps its look, only dimmer, and a fade out stopped before the end leaves the lamps lit. At once the tile shows the level the device works out from how long the fade ran; about ten seconds after the fade would have ended, it moves to the level Hue reports. Control4's stop command, from programming or an Advanced Lighting Scene, and a keypad button bound to the device's Brightness Stop link in Connections stop a tap's fade the same way.
  • Double tap. A second tap on the same button within about 0.6 seconds of the first is part of a double tap and does nothing: it starts and stops nothing, and the fade goes on to where the first tap sent it, so programming on the keypad's double-tap event can run without the scene changing course (a double tap on a toggle button fades the scene in, or out, as one tap would). To stop a fade, tap a little later than that.
  • Hold. A button held for longer than 0.4 seconds brightens the room or zone (top, or toggle while the keypad reads the scene off) or dims it (bottom, or toggle while it reads it on), keeping the scene's colours and the differences between its lights, until you let go; the full range takes the hold rate, 5 seconds unless changed. Held while the scene fades, the button first stops the fade and then brightens or dims from there. Held while the keypad reads the scene off with the room or zone off, the top or toggle button plays the scene at 1 % and brightens from there (let go within about a second of pressing and it stays at 1 %); the bottom button does nothing. A tap within a second after letting go of a hold on the same button is ignored.
  • How a hold moves the lights. The driver counts the lamps of the room or zone that are on when the hold starts, or all its lamps for a hold from off. With up to 10, it brightens or dims each of them itself at the hold rate, so the change is smooth and stops where you let go. With more than 10, it moves the room or zone as a whole once a second, each step sized so that the whole range still takes the hold rate (20 % a step at the default 5 seconds): the change shows as steps, softened by the short fade Hue gives every change, and no step follows once you let go. While a time-of-day scene runs there, or a scene plays there with moving colours, a hold always moves in steps, however few the lamps, because changing single lamps could end the schedule or stop the colours. Either way the differences between the lamps are kept, and lamps that are off stay off. While the bridge does not yet know the room's or zone's lamps (for a moment after a restart), or when a hold down finds none of them on, the hold runs to full or off in about a second instead, because Hue ignores the time given for a dim of a whole room or zone. A hold that moves the lamps one by one may make Hue stop calling an ordinary scene active; the tile stays on all the same.
  • LEDs. The buttons' LEDs follow what Keypad Follows treats as on, so a dim keeps them lit: a top or toggle button is lit blue while the keypad reads on, a bottom button while it reads off. If they do not follow it, look at the switch's LED Behavior and pick the option that follows the connected device.

6. Programming

In programming the scene is a Control4 light: Control4's own light events, conditionals and commands work as for any dimmer. Its on event runs when it becomes the scene playing, its off event when it stops being that (section 3), and its level event follows the percentage; they fire also for changes made in the Hue app, by a Hue switch or by a Hue automation. The device adds no variables, conditionals or commands of its own.

Button events

The device fires the button events of a Control4 dimmer, as a Hue Light does: a press, a release, and a single, double and triple tap for each of its top, bottom and toggle buttons, fifteen events in all, listed among the device's events in Composer's Programming view. The presses are those of keypad buttons bound to the device's Button Links (section 4), and the button presses Control4 sends the device directly. The Hue Light device's Documentation tab has the details; in short:

  • Every press fires them, whatever the scene then does: a tap that fades the scene in or out, a tap that stops a fade, the second tap of a double tap (which changes nothing), and a press while the device cannot act (not bound, waiting, gone, forgotten, or offline while the Hue bridge does not answer). Programming that should follow the scene belongs on its on, off and level events.
  • The press fires as a button goes down and the release as it comes up. The single tap fires about 0.6 seconds after a tap's press, once no second press of the same button has come in that time; two taps within about 0.6 seconds of each other fire the double tap instead, and three the triple tap. Press and release fire for each tap of a double or triple tap too, so do not program a button's press or release and its taps together. A button held for longer than 0.4 seconds fires press and release and no tap. The scene does what it did before: the second tap of a double tap still starts and stops nothing, and a tap that stops a fade counts as a tap.
  • A keypad button bound to the device also fires its own events, in the keypad's programming. For any one press, program the keypad's event or the scene's, not both, or the programming runs twice. Never let programming on one of the scene's button events press a button of the same scene device: each such press fires the event again, and the programming repeats itself until someone stops it.
  • A Hue dimmer switch or another Hue switch never fires these events; what it does to the room or zone fires the scene's on, off and level events.
  • A time-of-day scene fires the same events.

Set All LEDs

The device takes Control4's Set All LEDs command, which sets the LED colours of all three buttons, lit and unlit, to one colour, and keeps the six colours as if each had been set on the Properties page: the page shows them, they outlast a driver update, a Director restart and a controller reboot, and bound keypads take them at once. Which of its two colours a button shows still follows what Keypad Follows treats as on. A later change of one colour on the Properties page changes that one again, and a colour that is not a six-digit hexadecimal RGB value (ff0000 is red) changes nothing.

7. Troubleshooting

When the Hue bridge does not answer

When the Hue bridge does not answer (it is restarting or updating, has no power or network, has moved to another address, or refuses the pairing), the scene device behaves as a Hue Light does, and comes back by itself:

  • At once: nothing shows as done unless the bridge took it. ON is one request and is not tried again: if the bridge does not confirm within two seconds the tile shows ON anyway, and drops back to off as soon as the request fails, and the scene is not played when the bridge returns. OFF and slider moves while the scene is on are tried again for up to 30 seconds, like a Hue Light's commands: the room or zone follows if the bridge takes the command within that time, unless a later command replaced it, and after that the command is dropped. A command the bridge does not take is not shown as done: the tile and the slider go back to the state the bridge last reported, about a second after the command once the bridge driver knows that the bridge is away (from a minute into an outage), within 5 to 15 seconds right at its start.
  • After 5 minutes: offline. When the bridge has not answered, or has refused the pairing, for 5 minutes, the device goes offline: Online reads Offline, the tile is disabled on Navigators, keypad buttons bound to it do nothing and their LEDs keep the last known state, and commands from programming or Advanced Lighting Scenes are dropped, not kept for later. Status reads, for example, Hue Bridge not answering since 14:02 (every wording is in the table below), and the Hue Bridge's Attention says that the Hue devices are offline and why. Every Hue Light, zone light and Hue Scene goes offline at the same moment, and every Hue Motion Sensor's Online turns No.
  • Back at the first answer. The moment the bridge answers again, the device is Online, with nothing to click, and within seconds the tile shows what the room or zone really does. A bridge restart takes a few minutes and stays under the 5 minutes.

A controller reboot or a driver update during a long outage does not start the 5 minutes again. The bridge's manual, chapter 4 ("When the bridge is offline"), has the details.

Things that look wrong but are not

  • The tile reads 0 while the room is lit: another scene, or lamps turned on by hand, light it (section 3). A keypad with Keypad Follows Room/zone lights stays lit all the same.
  • The Hue app no longer shows the scene as active after one of its lights was changed, but the tile stays on: the tile follows the rule of section 3, not Hue's flag.
  • In the System Design tree the device shows a small Hue dimmer switch with coloured buttons as its icon if it was created by a build that has the Hue icons; one created by an earlier build keeps Control4's standard bulb. In a room's List View it always shows the standard light icon.
  • Debug Mode is Off again although it was set to On: it turns itself back Off after 8 hours, and Last Action says so (section 5). Set it On again if support still needs it.

The drivers are meant for Control4 OS 3.x and 4.x (minimum OS 3.3.0) and have been run on OS 3.4, with a Hue Bridge Pro and, by an outside tester, with a Hue Bridge v2. Ramp times passed to Hue and keypad taps on a scene, with their fades and stops, were watched on 2026-11-09. These parts follow Control4's documentation and the code but had not been watched on a controller when this page was written; if one behaves otherwise, please report it as an issue on the project's GitHub page:

  • the Brightness Stop link stopping a tap's fade;
  • holds on a whole room or zone, smooth and in steps, including a time-of-day scene that keeps running through a hold in steps;
  • whether a time-of-day scene's next time slot also replaces a level dimmed by hand;
  • the button events, and the keypad on a time-of-day scene.

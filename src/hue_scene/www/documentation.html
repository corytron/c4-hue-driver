Hue Scene device

A Hue Scene is one Philips Hue scene (a saved look for a Hue room or zone) shown as a Control4 dimmer: a tile with a slider and a percentage on Navigators and in the Control4 app. Turning it ON plays the scene on the Hue bridge (recalls it, in Hue's word), so every light in that Hue room or zone takes the scene's look; turning it OFF turns that whole Hue room or zone off, with one request to the bridge. The percentage is the brightness of the scene's room or zone as Hue reports it (the average of the lights that are on), 0 while off and 100 while on until Hue has reported a level. Moving the slider while the scene is on brightens or dims the room or zone relative to where it is, keeping the scene's colours and the differences between its lights (while the bridge does not know the room's level yet, it sets that level on every light instead); 0 % turns the room or zone off; moving it while the scene is off plays the scene at that brightness, all its lights at that level, and 100 % from off plays it exactly as designed. It is created by the Hue Bridge device's Add Scene or Add All Scenes Of Room action, normally never added by hand. To Control4 it is a dimmer, so programming, keypad buttons and Advanced Lighting Scenes can turn it on and off or set its level like any other; in an Advanced Lighting Scene it is a dimmer member with a level.

A Hue Scene can also stand for one of Hue's time-of-day scenes, such as Golden hours or Natural light (Hue calls them smart scenes), which play a different ordinary scene in each time slot of the day. ON then starts that schedule, OFF stops it and turns the room or zone off; the section "Time-of-day scenes" below lists what else differs.

The full manual, with pictures, is on the Hue Bridge device: select the bridge in System Design and open its Documentation tab. Chapter 9 there is about scenes, 9.11 about time-of-day scenes, chapter 11 about programming.

When the tile reads off

The scene reads on from the moment it is played, from Control4, a keypad, the Hue app or a time-of-day scene's schedule, until its Hue room or zone is turned off (from anywhere, including the Hue app or a Hue dimmer switch) or another scene of the same room or zone is played. Dimming or changing its lights afterwards, with the slider, a keypad, the Hue app or a Hue dimmer switch, does not turn it off, even when Hue itself no longer calls the scene active (Hue stops doing so when a single light of the scene is changed; a dim of the whole room or zone leaves the scene active): the tile shows the new percentage instead. An ON tile therefore means "this was the last scene played here and its room or zone is on"; programming that fires when the scene turns off fires when the room or zone goes off or another of its scenes is played. The on state survives a controller restart. A time-of-day scene follows Hue instead; see "Time-of-day scenes" below.

Good to know

  • A ramp time given by programming or by an Advanced Lighting Scene is passed to Hue as the time to fade into the scene (Hue accepts it; how long the fade really takes has not been measured on the house system); a tap on a Navigator passed a short one of its own, 750 ms, when this was looked at on the house system (2026-10-21).
  • During a ramp (a keypad hold or a slow fade) the percentage on Navigators updates at most once a second and settles on the level Hue reports at the end.
  • OFF turns off every light of the Hue room or zone, imported into Control4 or not; a zone can span several Control4 rooms. A zone with no lights in it is reported off without any request.
  • ON (and a level set while the scene is off, which plays it) is one request and is not retried: if the Hue bridge does not confirm within two seconds the tile shows ON anyway and drops back if the request fails. OFF, and slider moves while the scene is on, are kept and retried for up to 30 seconds, like a Hue Light's commands: while the bridge is unreachable the tile shows the result at once (OFF; within two seconds for a time-of-day scene) or after the ramp time plus a second (a level), and the room or zone follows if the bridge is back within that time, unless a later command replaced it; after that the command is dropped.
  • Editing the scene's colours or brightness in the Hue app needs nothing here; the next ON plays the new look.
  • On a keypad whose buttons are bound to the device's Top, Bottom or Toggle Button Link in Connections (listed under its Control Outputs), a tap on the top button plays the scene, on the bottom turns the Hue room or zone off, and a toggle button alternates. A button held for longer than 0.4 seconds brightens the room or zone (top, or toggle while the scene is off) or dims it (bottom, or toggle while it is on), keeping the scene's colours and the differences between its lights, until you let go; the full range takes 5 seconds. Held while the scene is off, the top or toggle button plays the scene at 1 % and brightens from there (let go within about a second of pressing and it stays at 1 %); the bottom button does nothing. A tap within a second after letting go of a hold on the same button is ignored. The connected buttons' LEDs follow the on state described above, so a dim keeps them lit: a top or toggle button is lit blue while the scene is on, a bottom button while it is off (if they do not follow it, look at the switch's LED Behavior and pick the option that follows the connected device).
  • In the System Design tree this device shows a small Hue dimmer switch with coloured buttons as its icon if it was created by a build that has the Hue icons; one created by an earlier build keeps Control4's standard bulb. In a room's List View it always shows the standard light icon.

Keypad settings

The 5 seconds of a hold and the blue LEDs above are the defaults of settings a Control4 dimmer normally lists on its Properties page, and Composer lists them for this device too, on its Properties tab above the driver's own rows (Advanced Properties): the hold ramp rates up and down (Hold Ramp Rate Down, for example: how long a held button takes across the whole range, brightening and dimming; 5 seconds each unless changed, shown in seconds as 5.000) and the colours of the keypad buttons' LEDs while the scene is on and while it is off. A scene has no click rates: its taps play the scene or turn the room or zone off without a ramp time of their own, and any other setting Composer lists for it has no effect on a scene. Type a new rate and press Enter: Composer sends it then, and the next hold uses it. The device takes rates from 0 to 86 400 000 ms (24 hours), and Composer keeps them at 1 second or more; a value outside that range is refused, the device keeps the value it had and reports it back, and Composer shows that value again. Showing them changed what this device type can do in Control4, its capabilities, so after the update that brought them they appear only once the controller has been rebooted from System Manager, as after any such update (the bridge's manual, chapter 3); until then the defaults apply.

Time-of-day scenes

Hue's time-of-day scenes, such as Golden hours and Natural light, are schedules for one room or zone: each time slot of the day plays an ordinary scene (the Master Bedroom's Golden hours plays Arise from 07:00 and Shine from 16:00), and Hue moves on to the next slot's scene by itself. The bridge's Add Scene lists each one as, for example, Master Bedroom: Golden hours (time-based), and Add All Scenes Of Room creates them too. The device is named without the number the Hue app gives its copies (Golden hours 4 becomes Golden hours), and a zone's carries the zone's name in front (Bedside Lights Golden hours). Everything on this page applies to such a device, except:

  • ON starts the schedule: within about a second the room or zone takes the look of the current time slot's scene, and from then on Hue changes the look at the start of each slot. A ramp time is not used.
  • OFF stops the schedule and turns the whole room or zone off.
  • The slider and a held keypad button brighten or dim the room or zone while the schedule keeps running; when the next time slot starts, Hue plays that slot's scene (whether it then also replaces the dimmed level had not been watched on the house system as this is written).
  • Hue cannot start a time-of-day scene at a chosen brightness. From off, a level below 100 % (the slider, programming, an Advanced Lighting Scene) starts the schedule and a moment later, a second and a half at most, moves the room or zone to that level; a held top or toggle button starts it and then, while you still hold it, brightens from the time slot's own level, not from 1 %. Holding the bottom button while it is off does nothing.
  • The tile, the keypad LEDs and the on and off events follow Hue: on while Hue runs the schedule, off as soon as Hue stops it for any reason, such as its room or zone turned off from anywhere, another scene of it played, or another time-of-day scene started on its lights. A dim of the whole room or zone does not stop it. Turning the room on again does not restart the schedule; ON does.
  • An ordinary scene that the schedule plays and that you have also imported reads on while its time slot plays and off when the next slot begins.
  • Recall As does not apply and is not shown. Status names the scene playing now and when its time slot began (see the table at the end).
  • If the time-of-day scene is deleted in the Hue app, or one of the ordinary scenes it plays is (Hue then deletes the whole time-of-day scene by itself), this device reads Scene deleted in Hue since …, as for any scene. One created in the Hue app appears in the bridge's Add Scene list by itself, and changing its time slots in the Hue app needs nothing here.

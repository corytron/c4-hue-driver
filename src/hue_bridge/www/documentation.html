1. What this driver does, and what it does by itself

This driver connects one Philips Hue bridge to your Control4 system over your own home network; no cloud account is involved and nothing leaves the house. Each Hue light you choose becomes an ordinary Control4 light that you can switch, dim, colour and program like any other light in the project, and so can a whole Hue zone or room: one light whose tile moves every lamp in it at once (chapter 7). Changes made in the Hue app or with Hue switches and dimmers show up in Control4 within a second or two, because the driver listens to the bridge's event feed (the bridge's running report of everything that changes) rather than asking it over and over. Hue scenes, the saved looks you create for a room in the Hue app, can become Control4 dimmers: turning one on plays the scene, turning it off turns that Hue room or zone off, and its slider brightens or dims the room or zone while keeping the scene's look. Hue's time-of-day scenes, such as Golden hours and Natural light, can become such dimmers too; turning one on starts its schedule (9.11). Adding a device and removing one are always your clicks: the driver never creates, deletes or moves a Control4 device on its own. The rest it looks after by itself, and says so: it reads the bridge again behind the event feed, finds the bridge again when its address changes, keeps the names of lights, scenes and motion sensors the same in Composer and in the Hue app, maps a new Hue room to the Control4 room of the same name, and re-links a bulb that comes back to the bridge with the same radio. The last section of this chapter lists everything it does by itself. What needs you is listed in Attention; the result of each action you run, and of each change the driver made by itself, is in Last Action.

Two words this manual uses throughout: a light is a Hue light, the bulb, strip or lamp as the Hue app lists it; a Hue Light, with a capital L, is the Control4 device that stands for it. A Hue Light can also stand for a whole Hue zone or room; this manual then calls it a zone light, for a Hue room as for a Hue zone. Navigators is Control4's word for everything you control the house from: touch screens, remotes and the Control4 app. A short glossary of the other terms is at the end of this manual.

What the driver does by itself

The Hue Bridge runs a few jobs of its own. They are always on; there is no setting to switch them off. They run one at a time, read from the bridge without changing anything there (the one thing the driver writes to Hue by itself is a name: after a device was renamed in Composer, 8.2, or re-linked, 8.3), and always let your own commands and clicks go first; the safety reads among them also wait five minutes after a controller restart (a minute after a driver update) before they start. None of them reaches the internet: the driver talks only to the bridge and, to find it, to your local network.

What has no counterpart

  • The whole home as one light. Add From Hue offers Hue zones and rooms, not Hue's "all lights" group. For one tile that moves every light in the house, make a zone holding them all in the Hue app and add that zone.
  • Hue buttons, dimmers and switches are not represented. Pressing a Hue dimmer switch still shows its effect on the lights in Control4, because the lights report their state. Hue motion sensors and MotionAware areas are represented (chapter 14); the motion of a whole Hue room or zone, and Hue's cameras, are not.
  • Hue automations (the routines the Hue app runs by itself) and a Hue switch's time-based light are not represented as devices; what they do to the lights shows in Control4 (8.1). The bridge lists the automations on its Hue Automations row, points out the ones that move Control4's lights by themselves, and switches one off in Hue when you pick it (chapter 15). A switch's time-based light is not one of the time-of-day scenes of 9.11.
  • Entertainment areas (light sync with screens and music) are not touched.
  • Several bridges. One Hue Bridge device per project is supported. Adding a second one is not prevented, but every light and scene can be connected to one bridge only; do not add a second bridge device.

3. Installation

Finding your way in Composer

Everything in this manual happens in Composer Pro, connected to your controller. The places it refers to:

  • System Design is the view Composer opens in (the view buttons are at the lower left: System Design, Connections, Media, Agents, Programming). Its left-hand tree lists the project's rooms and, under each room, the devices in it. Click a device to select it.
  • With a device selected, the pane to the right of the tree shows its tabs: Properties (the rows this manual quotes), Actions (the buttons), Documentation (this page) and Lua. The Lua tab's lower part is the driver's Lua output, the log a few texts in this manual ask you to look at; nothing else on that tab is for you.
  • The Items pane at the far right of System Design is where drivers are added to rooms; its Search tab finds a driver by name.
  • An action is run with one click on its button on the Actions tab. An action that needs choices first opens a dialog ("How an action's dialog works", below).
  • To rename a device, select it in the tree, press F2, type the new name and press Enter.
  • To delete a device, select it in the tree and press the Delete key; Composer asks Are you sure you want to delete the item '…'? and deletes it on Yes.
  • To move a device to another room, press and hold the mouse button on it in the tree, drag it onto the room's name and release. Composer asks nothing.
  • The Connections view (its button is below the tree) shows, for the selected device, an upper pane titled Control & Audio Video Connections with the device's inputs and outputs, and a lower pane listing the devices that can be connected to the selected row. A connection is made by dragging a device from the lower pane onto the row in the upper pane.
  • After adding or deleting devices, choose File » Refresh Navigators so the touch screens and the app show the new rooms' contents.

How an action's dialog works

An action that takes choices opens Composer's Action Parameter List dialog: one row per choice, each with a … button that opens a Select list. Click an entry, then that list's OK (the inner OK, greyed out until you have chosen), and when the rows are filled click the dialog's own OK (the outer OK) to run the action. Cancel on either closes it without doing anything.

The lists of Add From Hue, Link Device, Forget Device and Hue Automations are sorted into folders, the way files are: a folder opens with a double-click on its text. Inside a folder the first line is the driver's and says where it leads: Back to all folders in a folder of the top level, Back to Scenes in a room's or zone's folder of scenes; double-click it to go there. Composer offers a Back of its own as well, but it reacts only to a double-click on the word itself, which is easy to miss. A folder can be picked as a whole where that means something, as a room's folder of scenes does in Add From Hue (all its scenes); otherwise pick a line inside it. Each list takes one pick; for many at once there are the All … lines. A list with nothing to offer holds one line that says why, and picking it changes nothing. The rows of one dialog do not see each other's choice while you pick, so the action checks them together when it runs, and refuses a pair that does not fit (a light's device with a scene, say) in Last Action.

A row left empty reaches the driver empty: an empty Room in Add From Hue means (mapped room); any other empty row is refused with a Not … text in Last Action (for example Not added: … or Not forgotten: pick a device under Device first; chapter 12 lists them, and 14.17 those about motion devices). Forget Device and Forget Pairing have a Confirm row, No or Yes, forget: anything but Yes, forget answers Not forgotten: choose Yes, forget and changes nothing. The dialog remembers your previous choices for that action, so always look at every row before clicking OK; a remembered choice is as good as a new one to the driver.

What you need

  • A Control4 controller on OS 3.3.0 or newer, and Composer Pro. The drivers are meant for OS 3.x and 4.x: they were written and tested on OS 3.4.2 with a Hue Bridge Pro, an outside tester ran them on OS 3.4.3 with a Hue Bridge v2 during the 1.1 betas, and OS 4 has not been tried. Where it relies on behaviour measured on OS 3.4.2 it checks at every start and falls back or stops safely: the event feed's network connection (Event Stream Transport, chapter 5) and the TLS check (chapter 12).
  • A Philips Hue Bridge v2 (the square bridge, model BSB002) or a Hue Bridge Pro (BSB003), on the same network as the controller, preferably with a fixed address: a DHCP reservation in your router (a setting that makes the router hand the bridge the same address every time). The driver finds a bridge that moved to a new address on the local network by itself (chapter 4), but a reservation saves that search. The round first-generation bridge (BSB001) has no CLIP v2 API and is refused at pairing, as is any device at the address that is not a Hue bridge; the bridge's software must be 1948086000 or newer (keep the bridge updated in the Hue app; the driver shows the version in Bridge Firmware).
  • The bridge's address is an IPv4 address, four numbers and dots such as 10.0.2.143 (the addresses in this manual are examples), not a name; the driver works with any such address. Only when the event feed runs on Web transfer, the fallback of Event Stream Transport (chapter 5), does an address with a number below 8 still help: it keeps commands to the lights from waiting behind the feed.
  • The driver files of one build: hue_bridge.c4z, hue_light.c4z, hue_scene.c4z and hue_motion.c4z (for Hue motion sensors and MotionAware areas, chapter 14), all four from the same release on the project's GitHub page. Each build has a number that all its files share and that the devices show as Driver Version once installed; the release names it, and so does the changelog at the end of this page. Install them all together.

Only drivers you trust in the project. Control4 does not tell a driver which driver sent it a command. So any driver installed in the same project can run the Hue Bridge's actions as the Actions tab does, Forget Pairing and Hue Automations included, and can send messages to the Hue Light, Hue Scene and Hue Motion Sensor devices. No action gives out the pairing key, a device ignores a message that names another Hue light, scene or sensor than its own, and every action writes Last Action, so a change you did not make shows there. Install only drivers you trust, as for any driver that can command the house. The file SECURITY.md on the project's GitHub page says what is protected and what is not.

Adding the drivers to the project

  1. Copy the four files to the computer that runs Composer Pro, into any folder; Composer copies them into the project.
  2. In Composer, open the Driver menu and choose Add or Update Driver or Agent…. The Select Driver or Agent… file dialog opens; select all four files at once (hold Ctrl while clicking) and open them. Nothing needs to be selected in the tree first. Composer answers with, for example, Update Succeeded: Drivers or agents were successfully added: hue_bridge.c4z, hue_light.c4z, hue_motion.c4z, hue_scene.c4z.
  3. In System Design, select the room that should hold the bridge (a double-click in the Items pane adds to the room that is selected). In the Items pane, open the Search tab and type Hue Bridge. The search also finds Control4's own Hue drivers; the one you want is the Hue Bridge by manufacturer Philips Hue with the model text Hue Bridge (CLIP v2). Double-click it: one Hue Bridge device appears in the room.
  4. Do not add a Hue Light, a Hue Scene or a Hue Motion Sensor from the Items pane. The bridge creates them, already connected and linked, when you use its actions. Their three drivers only need to be in the project's driver list, which step 2 did.

Updating to a newer build

Updates go through the same Driver » Add or Update Driver or Agent… menu: select all four new files at once, as for the first installation. Composer answers Update Succeeded: Drivers or agents were successfully updated: hue_bridge.c4z, hue_light.c4z, hue_motion.c4z, hue_scene.c4z (on an update from 1.0, hue_motion.c4z is listed as added) and pushes the new version to every existing device: the bridge and every Hue Light, Hue Scene and Hue Motion Sensor show the new Driver Version within a minute, the bridge restarts and its event feed is back within seconds, and the pairing, the lights, the scenes and the motion devices are untouched. That is all, unless Composer asks the question below. The first time you open a device's Documentation tab after an update, Composer may still show the page of the build before (seen once on 2026-10-25): select the device again, or another device and back, and the tab shows the new page.

If Composer asks Restart Director?, reboot the controller. Some releases change what a device type can do in Control4, its capabilities; 1.5, for example, let Hue Lights join Control4 load groups (chapter 11), and the changelog at the end of this page says so for each release that needs the reboot. Installing such a build makes Composer ask Restart Director?. Answering Yes does not restart Director: the driver whose capabilities changed keeps running its old code, and its devices keep showing the old Driver Version, until the controller itself is rebooted. To reboot it, open System Manager (in Composer, Tools » System Manager; it needs administrator rights on the computer), right-click the controller and reboot it. Composer loses its connection and is back a few minutes later (about seven and a half minutes once on the house system). Then check Driver Version on the bridge, on one Hue Light, one Hue Scene and one Hue Motion Sensor, and choose File » Refresh Navigators so the touch screens and the app pick up what changed. For up to a minute after the reboot a Hue Light, Hue Scene or Hue Motion Sensor may read Not bound to a Hue Bridge; it corrects itself (8.7). An update during which Composer does not ask the question takes effect at once and needs no reboot. Until the reboot, what the update brought to the device types (keypad settings, a place in load groups, new events) is missing in Composer; chapter 12 has that case among its texts.
Never delete the Hue Bridge device to update or "reset" it. The pairing with the bridge and the list of which device is which light are stored on that device. A deleted and re-added bridge starts empty: you would have to pair again and every Hue Light and Hue Scene would read Not bound to a Hue Bridge until connected and linked again by hand (8.6). Updating in place keeps everything.

If you ever need to reload the same version, right-click the bridge and choose Update Driver; Composer asks Versions are equal. Do you want to overwrite…? and reloads it on Yes. For anything newer, use the menu route above. Neither route helps a device that kept its old code after a Restart Director? question; only the reboot does.

Going back to an older build uses the same menu with the older files. This has not been tested; expect the devices to keep their pairing and links as they do on an update, and read Status and Lights afterwards rather than assume.

Release and development builds. The developer sometimes makes development builds that fetch their code from a server on the home network; they carry two extra properties (Dev Server, Diagnostic Server) and four extra entries in the list of the Diagnostics action, each starting with (dev) (two connection experiments, reloading the credentials and checking the CA pinning). A release build shows none of them, and a development build's Driver Version, on every device, reads its build number followed by -dev; so a device whose Driver Version ends in -dev, or a bridge whose Properties page has a Dev Server row, is running a development build and should be replaced with the release files through the menu above.

4. Pairing with the bridge

Step 1: let the driver find the bridge

A Hue Bridge device that is not paired looks for Hue bridges on your network by itself: a few seconds after it is added (and after every restart of the controller, and after Forget Pairing), again after a minute and after five minutes, then every 15 minutes until it is paired. It asks the way the Hue app does on a home network (mDNS); it never asks the internet. Each bridge that answers is checked before it is listed (it must be a Hue bridge, with Signify's certificate, and it must report its identifier), and the bridges found appear in the Bridges Found row, right below Bridge IP Address. The row shows a summary; open its list to see the bridges:

The address must be four numbers from 0 to 255 separated by dots, written without leading zeros (10.0.2.143, not 10.000.002.143), and not a name. Checking the connection

The driver checks the connection by itself: a rejected key 5 minutes, 15 minutes and an hour later and then every hour (8.8), and the lists behind the feed (chapter 1). To check at once, run Diagnostics and pick Check the connection now (chapter 12): Last Action reads Check the connection now: started (the result shows in Status), and the driver asks the bridge one question and writes the answer to Status, briefly Testing: GET /clip/v2/resource/bridge via 10.0.2.143, then Test OK: HTTP 200 via 10.0.2.143 in 0 s, bridge 001788fffe123abc. The three-digit numbers in these texts are the bridge's reply codes: 200 means the bridge answered properly, 401 or 403 that it refused the pairing key, 404 that the thing asked for no longer exists. The question carries the pairing, so it goes only to a bridge that has passed two checks: the driver asks the bridge at the address for its identifier, a question without the pairing, and then makes the TLS check (chapter 12, "The TLS check"). The driver makes both at every start and after a change of Bridge IP Address, and asks for the identifier again once the feed has been down for a minute (the TLS check of that start stands; "When the bridge is offline" below); if they have not passed since, the check makes them first (Status reads Testing: checking the bridge identity first meanwhile). A different bridge at the address ends it with Bridge at 10.0.2.143 reports ID 001788fffe456def, expected 001788fffe123abc; nothing is sent until the address is corrected (it receives nothing that carries the pairing), and a failed TLS check with TLS check failed: nothing is sent (see Attention); a check asked for after that sends nothing and says Test refused: TLS check failed; nothing is sent (see Attention). A device at the address that is no bridge this driver can use ends it with Bridge at 10.0.2.143 is a …: no CLIP v2 API; nothing is sent (chapter 12). When nothing answers, Status reads Bridge at 10.0.2.143 not reachable (…); checking again in 30 s, or TLS check inconclusive (…): nothing is sent; checking again in 30 s when only the TLS check went unanswered (chapter 12); the driver asks again every 30 seconds, and the check goes ahead by itself once both pass. Once the bridge has passed, three failures are possible: Test failed: key rejected (HTTP 403). Re-pair with Pair Bridge (the bridge no longer accepts this pairing, see 8.8; a bridge that answers 401 shows that number instead), Test failed: bridge reports ID …, expected … (the answer names another bridge) and Test failed: … with the reason: a network error when nothing answers, or another reply code such as HTTP 503. When not paired it says Not paired. If the check succeeds while the driver had stopped because the bridge refused the pairing (Key rejected by the bridge …, 8.8), the bridge accepts the pairing again: the stop ends and the feed reconnects, as it does when the driver's own re-check succeeds. Otherwise the check leaves the normal connection alone.

Forget Pairing

Forget Pairing removes the stored pairing from the controller and stops the event feed. It asks first: its dialog has one row, Confirm, with No and Yes, forget; only Yes, forget goes ahead, and anything else answers in Last Action Not forgotten: choose Yes, forget and changes nothing. (The dialog remembers your last choice, as every dialog does, so look at the row before clicking OK.) Once forgotten, Status returns to Not paired: press the link button, then run Pair Bridge (the address is kept), Event Stream reads Stopped, Bridge ID, Bridge Model, Bridge Name, Bridge Firmware, Stream Address and Last Event are cleared and Events Received returns to 0, and Last Action reads Pairing forgotten: the key is gone and the event stream stopped; devices and the address are kept. No pairing window opens. The Bridges Found row drops its Paired with … summary at once and reads No Hue bridge answered: type its address in Bridge IP Address, or 2 Hue bridges found: pick one and click Set to pair when its last search found some, and the automatic searches of step 1 start again: three seconds later the row reads Looking for Hue bridges on the network..., and the searches follow after a minute, after five minutes and then every 15 minutes until a bridge is paired. Attention drops its items about the paired bridge (below). The Hue Light, Hue Scene and Hue Motion Sensor devices stay in the project and keep their links; they simply stop following the bridge until you pair again (after a minute a motion device's Status says so, Bridge not answering since 14:02; motion not current), a command given to a light or scene meanwhile is dropped, not kept for later, and after 5 minutes every one of them goes offline, reading Hue Bridge not paired since 14:02, until you pair again ("When the bridge is offline" below). Pair with the same bridge and everything resumes (the links are expected to survive; this has not been exercised on the house system, so read Lights afterwards). Forget Pairing is needed only to pair with a different bridge. Pair Bridge compares the identifier of the bridge at the address with the stored one before it asks for a key: the same bridge pairs again at any time (after a reset of the bridge, say, or at a new address) and keeps every link, while a different bridge is refused with Pairing failed: Bridge at 10.0.2.143 reports ID 001788fffe456def, expected 001788fffe123abc; run Forget Pairing first to pair with another bridge and receives nothing, because the links of your devices belong to the paired bridge. Nothing else changes: the pairing, the feed and the rows stay as they were. After Forget Pairing the other bridge pairs, and then every Hue Light and Hue Scene whose light or scene that bridge does not have reads Gone until re-linked, by itself for a bulb with the same radio or with Link Device (8.8); a Hue Motion Sensor whose sensor or area it lacks reads as deleted in Hue (14.12).

When the bridge is offline

If the bridge is unplugged, rebooting or unreachable then the feed ends. The driver notices when the bridge closes it, or when Stream Watchdog (s) passes without a word from the bridge (Disconnected: watchdog: no data for 600 s. Reconnecting in 1 s; in a quiet house it can take until the connection's Stream Timeout (s), chapter 5). While the bridge does not answer, Event Stream reads Disconnected: transfer error 28: …. Reconnecting in 2 s or similar (the number is the controller's error code, 28 meaning "no answer in time"; the reason after it varies. Error 28 is also how a connection that reached Stream Timeout (s) ends, once an hour by default, so one such line followed by a fresh Connected since is normal), and the driver retries with a growing pause, doubling from 1 second up to 60 seconds between attempts. As soon as the bridge is back, Event Stream reads Connected since … again and the driver does a full read of every light and scene, so Control4 catches up with whatever happened while it was blind. Once the feed has been down for a minute, the driver checks the bridge's identity again before anything that carries the pairing goes out (another device could have taken the address meanwhile): it asks the bridge at the address for its identifier, without the pairing, as at a start. While it asks, Status reads Checking the bridge at 10.0.2.143 before anything is sent and Event Stream Not connected: bridge identity not verified yet, and with Debug Mode On the Lua output says event stream down for 60 s: the bridge's identity is checked again before the key goes out. If the bridge answers as itself, Status returns to Paired and the feed reconnects. If nothing answers, Status reads Bridge at 10.0.2.143 not reachable (…); checking again in 30 s, the driver asks again every 30 seconds, and the feed reconnects once the bridge answers; another bridge at the address reads Bridge at 10.0.2.143 reports ID 001788fffe456def, expected 001788fffe123abc; nothing is sent until the address is corrected. Once the feed has been down for 5 minutes, the driver reads the lights from the bridge every 10 minutes instead (a quick read, without the scene list), which keeps the rows current while the bridge still answers questions but its feed does not. You do nothing. When the bridge has not answered for 5 minutes the driver takes every Hue device offline (below) and starts looking for the bridge on the network, and after an hour Attention says so (next section). If instead the bridge answers but its feed stays down for an hour, Attention reads Event stream down since 14:02 while the bridge answers (lists read every 10 minutes): set Event Stream Transport to Web transfer if it stays (on a Web transfer you chose, …: set Event Stream Transport to Network connection if it stays; when the TLS check of the network connection moved the feed to Web transfer, …: restart the bridge if it stays; chapter 5), but not while you stopped the feed yourself or the bridge refuses the key. Each such item goes by itself once its cause ends. While the feed is down:

  • Hue Light and Hue Scene devices keep showing their last known state, and their tiles stay usable, for the first 5 minutes: a bridge restart takes a few minutes, and until then the driver cannot tell a bridge that is restarting from one that is gone. A feed that stays down while the bridge still answers questions (above) never takes them offline.
  • A command the bridge does not take never shows as done: the tile, the slider and the keypad LEDs go back to what the light, or the scene's room or zone, last reported. From a minute into the outage, while the driver checks the bridge's identity, a command is refused at once and the tile goes back within about a second. Right at the start, before the driver has noticed the outage, the tile can show the new level for 5 to 15 seconds, until the request to the bridge gives up (5 seconds to connect, 15 in all): Hue confirms a command only by answering it, so nothing quicker is possible. The command itself is kept as "the latest wanted value" for that light and retried with a growing pause (half a second, doubling up to eight seconds) for at most 30 seconds after the last command to that light, then dropped (a line in the Lua output says so). A bridge that is back within those 30 seconds still takes the last level asked for, and the tile follows when Hue reports it; after a longer outage nothing is replayed, so no light jumps to a level asked for minutes earlier. The full read after reconnecting then shows on every tile what the lights really do.
  • A Hue Scene's ON (playing the scene) is one request and is not retried: while the request still waits for an answer the tile shows ON after two seconds, and it drops back to off when the request fails (from a minute into the outage at once, without showing ON); the scene is not played when the bridge returns. Its OFF, and a slider move while the scene is on, are kept and retried like a light's commands, for at most 30 seconds, and the tile goes back to the room or zone's last known state when a try fails; a bridge that is back within that time turns the room or zone off, or changes its level, unless a later command replaced it; after that the command is dropped.
  • Hue Motion Sensor devices keep showing their last state too. After a minute their Status reads Bridge not answering since 14:02; motion not current, and once the bridge is back they report what changed meanwhile (14.10).

After 5 minutes the Hue devices go offline. When the bridge has not answered for 5 minutes, or has refused the pairing for 5 minutes (a reset bridge, for example, 8.8), or the driver has not been paired for 5 minutes (after Forget Pairing), the driver tells every Hue device at once:

  • Each Hue Light, zone light and Hue Scene reads Online Offline. Its tile is disabled (greyed out) on Navigators, keypad buttons bound to it do nothing and their LEDs keep the last known state, and commands from programming, Advanced Lighting Scenes or anywhere else are dropped, not kept for later. Its Status says what is wrong with the bridge and since when: Hue Bridge not answering since 14:02, Hue Bridge not at its address since 14:02 (another bridge answers there), Hue Bridge refuses the pairing since 14:02, Hue Bridge not usable since 14:02 (see its Status) (a failed TLS check, or a device at the address that this driver cannot use) or Hue Bridge not paired since 14:02. The time is when the trouble began, 14:02 for today and 10-26 14:02 for another day.
  • Each Hue Motion Sensor reads Online No, fires its Offline event and reads the same text followed by ; motion not current; its motion state stays as it was (14.10).
  • The bridge's Attention reads Hue devices offline since 14:07: the bridge has not answered since 14:02; they come back by themselves when it answers (the other causes are in chapter 12), and with Debug Mode On the Lua output says, for example, Hue devices offline: the bridge has not answered since 14:02 (not_answering); 12 devices told.

They come back at the bridge's first answer, without any wait: the feed connecting, a request the bridge accepts, or its identity check passing while it accepts the pairing. Every device is Online again at once (a lamp that has no power reads Offline again after the full read), the Attention item goes, the full read that follows the reconnection shows on every tile what its lights really do, and a motion device's Online returns to the sensor's own value (firing Online if the sensor is reachable). With Debug Mode On the Lua output says, for example, Hue devices online: the bridge answers again after 20 min; 12 devices told. A new spell of trouble needs another 5 minutes, so the devices never flicker: at most once every 5 minutes offline and back. A controller reboot or a driver update during a long outage does not start the 5 minutes again: the devices go offline at the bridge's first failed check after the start, within a minute, and stay online if the bridge answers. A bridge that loses its power cannot say so: in a quiet house the driver notices only when the feed has been silent for Stream Watchdog (s) (10 minutes unless changed), so the devices go offline about 15 minutes after the power cut; a command sent meanwhile fails, and the 5 minutes then count from that command. A feed stopped with Diagnostics » Stop the event stream until the next start is no outage: the devices stay online and their commands still reach the bridge.

When the bridge's address changes

If the router gives the bridge a new address then the feed drops and keeps failing: for the first minute Event Stream reads Disconnected: …. Reconnecting in … s and Connecting, then, once the identity check of the previous section finds nothing at the old address, Not connected: bridge identity not verified yet, while Status reads Bridge at 10.0.2.143 not reachable (…); checking again in 30 s (at once after a restart of the controller, because the driver checks the bridge at every start). The driver then finds the bridge again by itself:

  1. When the bridge has not answered for 5 minutes at the address where it last answered (so never in the first 5 minutes after a start, since a power cut restarts the bridge too), or another bridge answers there, the driver asks the network for Hue bridges, as at the first load (step 1), and looks for the one with its own bridge's identifier. It asks again every few minutes while the bridge stays away.
  2. If that finds nothing, it asks each address of up to two blocks of 254 addresses, the block of the bridge's last address and the controller's own, whether the paired bridge is there, a few at a time; at most once an hour, and only while the bridge is missing.
  3. Whatever it finds has to pass the same checks as at pairing: a Hue bridge with Signify's certificate that reports the paired bridge's identifier, then the TLS check. Only then does the driver write the new address into Bridge IP Address, and only then does anything that carries the pairing go to it. The feed reconnects within seconds.
  4. Last Action reads, for example, The bridge moved from 10.0.2.143 to 198.51.100.20 (found by mDNS): Bridge IP Address updated; reserve its address in the router (found by a search of 10.0.2.x and 198.51.100.x when the address search found it). Attention shows the same text for a day, also across a restart, while Bridge IP Address is the new address, as a reminder to reserve it; Forget Pairing removes it.

The driver never takes another bridge in this way: a bridge with a different identifier stays a deliberate step (8.8). It never opens the pairing window by itself, and it never overwrites an address you typed that never worked. If you typed a wrong address, the driver asks the network once, by mDNS, where the paired bridge is, and only offers what it finds: Attention reads Bridge 123ABC answers at 192.0.2.57, not at the typed 192.0.2.75: pick it in Bridges Found (123ABC being the last six characters of the bridge's identifier), and Bridges Found lists it as its (paired) line. Pick that line and Set: Last Action reads Bridge IP Address set to 192.0.2.57, where the paired bridge answers, and the item goes. (Search again in the same list asks once more at any time.) If the search for a moved bridge finds nothing, the search by mDNS is repeated every few minutes and the address search hourly, with Debug Mode On each run is a line in the Lua output (chapter 1), and once the bridge has been away for an hour Attention says so: Bridge 123ABC missing since 14:02: not found on the network (searched 15:05); check its power, or type its address in Bridge IP Address when the search found it nowhere, or Bridge 123ABC not answering at 192.0.2.75 since 14:02: check its power, or type its address in Bridge IP Address where no search applies (at an address you typed, as here). The times read 14:02 for today and 10-26 14:02 for another day, and the item goes by itself once the bridge answers. The search does not cross into another part of a network that is split into segments (VLANs): a bridge moved there is found only by its typed address.

You can always type the new address yourself: look it up in the Hue app, type it into Bridge IP Address and click Set. The pairing is kept, because it belongs to the bridge and not to the address: Status reads Checking the bridge at … before anything is sent (with the new address) while the driver confirms that the bridge there is the paired one, then Paired again, and the feed reconnects within seconds, also when it had stopped because the bridge refused the pairing. Then reserve the address in the router: with an address that stays put, the search never runs.

What the follow-up trusts. The driver follows the bridge only to a device that has Signify's certificate and reports the paired bridge's identifier. The identifier is no secret, and the controller cannot compare it with the name in the certificate. So a device on your network that holds a real Hue bridge's certificate together with its private key, in practice a Hue bridge whose software someone has broken into, could pass for your bridge while yours is away (a power cut, a router restart, a long update), and the driver would send it the pairing, which controls every Hue light. An ordinary Hue bridge cannot. The owner chose to keep the follow-up: without it every Hue light stops whenever the router gives the bridge a new address, and such a device could take the bridge's address anyway. A reserved address means the follow-up never runs. If Last Action reports a move you cannot explain, look up the bridge's address in the Hue app and in the router, and type the right one into Bridge IP Address if they differ (SECURITY.md on the project's GitHub page has the details).

6. Rooms first

Before importing anything, tell the driver which Control4 room each Hue room stands for. The mapping has one job: when a device is created, it goes into the Control4 room its Hue room maps to. A light, a zone light and a motion device go there unless you pick a room yourself; a scene goes only there, and a scene whose Hue room or zone has no mapping cannot be added until it has one (below, "Scenes stay in their room"). The mapping never moves an existing device, and changing a mapping later changes only where future additions land. That is deliberate: Control4 offers no way for a driver to move a device, and a device you dragged somewhere on purpose should stay there.

  • (unmapped): no Control4 room chosen yet. Lights in that Hue room are skipped by Import All New and need a room picked in Add From Hue. A room or zone you create in the Hue app appears here by itself: mapped at once when exactly one Control4 room has its name (below), (unmapped) otherwise; map it with Set Room Mapping. (unmapped; Control4 room Porch matches) means a Control4 room has its name but the driver did not map it by itself (a Hue room left unmapped by release 1.1 or earlier, or a Control4 room made after the Hue room appeared): Set Room Mapping maps it with one choice.
  • How a mapping was made: (by name) the driver mapped it by name; (by hand) you chose it in Set Room Mapping; (before 1.2) it was made by release 1.1 or earlier; (no mapping, by hand) you removed it ((no mapping) in Set Room Mapping). The driver never changes the last three.
  • (Control4 room gone): the mapping points at a Control4 room that has since been deleted from the project, for example Kitchen → (Control4 room gone) (by hand).   • A line can also say what the driver found, so that you can decide with one Set Room Mapping click. Each is only an offer: nothing is mapped until you click.
    • Garden → (unmapped; its 3 scene devices are in Den): every scene device of this unmapped Hue room sits in one Control4 room, here the Den.
    • Great Room → (unmapped; the deleted Great Room was mapped to Living): a Hue room or zone was deleted and made again under its old name and type (after a reset of the bridge, say), and the old one was mapped. Map the new one the same way if that is still right.
    • Garage → (unmapped; 2 Control4 rooms are named Garage): the name matches two Control4 rooms, so the driver did not choose; pick one.

    • TV Area (zone) → (no mapping, by hand; its scenes cannot be added): you removed the mapping, so the scenes of this zone have no Control4 room to go to.

Mapping by name, by itself

The driver maps a Hue room or zone to the Control4 room with exactly the same name (capitals and spaces at the ends do not matter) by itself: when it first reads the bridge's rooms on a new installation, and whenever a Hue room or zone appears later that it has not seen before. Last Action names what it mapped, for example Mapped 4 Hue rooms and zones by name: Bedroom → Bedroom, Kitchen → Kitchen, …. It maps only where exactly one Control4 room has that name, and it never touches a mapping that exists, one you set by hand, one made by release 1.1 or earlier, or one you removed: a Hue room you set to (no mapping) stays unmapped until you map it again yourself. A Hue room it has seen once is not looked at again, so a Control4 room you create later with its name is offered on the Hue room's line ((unmapped; Control4 room … matches)) and not mapped by itself. An installation updated from 1.1 or earlier keeps its mappings, marked (before 1.2), and its unmapped Hue rooms stay unmapped, offered where a Control4 room matches: the update maps nothing by itself. A name that two Control4 rooms share is left for you to map with Set Room Mapping, and so is every Hue room whose name no Control4 room has: Garden, say, or the zone Accent. When the driver mapped something, its Last Action names the shared names after it, …; not mapped, the name is ambiguous (Set Room Mapping): Garage (2 Control4 rooms are named Garage); when it mapped nothing, Last Action is left alone and only the Lua output names them, lights: rooms: not mapped, the name is ambiguous (Set Room Mapping): ….

Zones. A Hue zone can be mapped like a room. A light's default room always comes from its Hue room (a light belongs to exactly one room in Hue), so a zone mapping is used for scenes, zone lights and MotionAware areas: Add From Hue puts a zone's scenes into the zone's mapped room, its zone light there (chapter 7), and a MotionAware area that covers the zone (14.4). The whole home. Hue keeps a group of all the lights, and the Hue app can save scenes for it. Set Room Mapping can map that group like a room: pick (whole home) under Hue Room, which is listed when the bridge has such scenes, and the Control4 room its scenes should go to. The mapping is used only to place those scenes and to mark them on their lines; a light's Hue room is never the whole home, so lights never use it.

A device in another room than its Hue room

The driver points out the devices that sit in another Control4 room than the one their Hue room maps to, on the device's own line in the Lights, Scenes, Zone Lights and Motion Sensors lists: a light whose Hue room Kitchen maps to the Kitchen but that sits in the Den reads Kitchen: Lamp 3 → device 1209 (in Den instead of Kitchen), and one whose mapped Control4 room was deleted … (in Den; mapped room gone). Nothing is moved for you; if you agree, drag the device to the room in the System Design tree and the mark goes at the next refresh of the rows; if you placed it there on purpose, leave it. A scene, or a zone light, is compared with the room its Hue room or zone maps to. Only devices whose Hue room or zone is mapped are compared.

If you move a light to another Hue room in the Hue app, its Hue Room property follows within seconds; the Control4 device stays where it is, and its line in Lights names the room it sits in until you drag it or decide not to.

Why the driver never moves a device. Control4 gives drivers no way to move a device between rooms, and a move would also be the kind of surprise this driver avoids: you may well want a Hue light that Hue files under "Kitchen" to live in the Control4 room "Den". The mark on the list is there so you can decide, light by light.

7. Importing lights

Two actions create Hue Light devices: Import All New takes every new light at once, Add From Hue one at a time. Both create each device already connected to the bridge, named as in the Hue app, with its capabilities (dimming, colour, colour temperature) set from what the light reports, and with its current state. Both refuse to create a second device for a light that already has one, so clicking twice is safe. Add From Hue also creates a Hue Light that stands for a whole Hue zone or room; the last section of this chapter describes it.

Import All New

Import All New has no dialog: one click adds every new light and every new motion sensor (14.3), each into the Control4 room its Hue room maps to. It first reads the bridge's lights afresh, then adds every light that has no device yet, one at a time (under a second each on the house system, so a large import takes a while). Before it adds anything it re-links every light that came back with the same radio to its old device (8.3); those are no longer new, so they are not added, and the result names them, re-linked 1 by radio: Garage White (no Re-linked … text of their own is written). Three kinds of light are skipped on purpose and named in the result: lights that look like the replacement of a gone or unbound device (the same name in the same Hue room, which is the same room or, after a reset of the bridge, a room of the same name), which you link to that device instead of adding a second one, skipped 1 that may be a replacement (Link Device or Add From Hue): Lamp 5 #9 (looks like Lamp 5 #5, device 1213; re-link?); lights with no mapped room (their Hue room is not mapped, is mapped to a Control4 room that no longer exists, or they are in no Hue room at all); and lights whose device you deleted in Composer (8.4 explains why). Zones, rooms, scenes and MotionAware areas are never imported in bulk: each is a choice in Add From Hue. Last Action reads, for example:

Added 4 new lights: Lamp 2 #2 to Living Room (device 1207), Lamp 3 #3 to Kitchen (device 1209), Lamp 4 #4 to Kitchen (device 1211), Lamp 5 #5 to Bedroom (device 1213); skipped 2 with no mapped room (Set Room Mapping or Add From Hue): Lamp 6 #6 (Hue room Garden not mapped), Lamp 7 #7 (no Hue room); skipped 1 deleted in Composer (Add From Hue re-creates one): Lamp 8 #8 (deleted 2026-10-22)

followed by the motion sensors' part (14.3). A light whose Hue room is mapped to a deleted Control4 room is listed as … (Hue room … mapped to a Control4 room that no longer exists). The whole result is the lights' part and the motion part joined by | Motion: , for example No new lights on the bridge (8 on bridge, 8 imported) | Motion: …. When there is nothing to do the lights' part reads No new lights on the bridge (8 on bridge, 8 imported), or No new lights on the bridge (4 on bridge, 4 imported); re-linked 1 by radio: Garage White when the only news was a bulb back with its radio; when every new light was skipped, Added no lights; skipped …. If the bridge could not be read it reads Import failed: … with the reason; wait for Event Stream to say Connected since and click again. An import that stopped while creating devices ends in …; stopped early: … with the error that stopped it (the Lua output has the details); the devices created up to then are kept, so click again and it carries on where it stopped. AddDevice failed for 2: … names lights whose device Composer could not create (chapter 12). While an import or a full read of the lights is running, the other actions on lights are refused with Busy: importing; try again in a moment or Busy: resyncing; try again in a moment (resyncing is the driver's word for a full read); wait a few seconds, longer during a large import, and click again. A busy state that was never ended clears itself after 90 seconds.

Add From Hue

Add From Hue adds one thing from the bridge into a room you choose. Its dialog has two rows:

  • What: everything on the bridge that has no device yet, in folders (chapter 3 says how folders work), each with its count: Lights (6); Zones and rooms (6) (as zone lights, below); Scenes (43 in 5 rooms), inside it one folder per Hue room or zone, Bedroom → Bedroom: 8 scenes (chapter 9); and Motion sensors and areas (3) (chapter 14). In the lights' folder the first line, All 6 new lights, each into its mapped room, adds every new light at once, as Import All New does for the lights, with the same result in Last Action; then every light without a device, as Name [Hue room] #number, for example Lamp 3 [Kitchen] #3. The number is the light's number on the bridge (a light without one shows the first eight characters of its Hue identity instead); it tells two lights of the same name apart and appears in every text about that light. A light in no Hue room has no bracket: Lamp 7 #7. A light that looks like the replacement of a missing one is listed first with a hint, Lamp 5 [Bedroom] #9 (looks like Lamp 5 #5, device 1213; re-link?); lights whose device you deleted in Composer come last, as Lamp 8 [Bedroom] #8 (deleted from Composer 2026-10-22).   • Room: (mapped room) first, then every Control4 room as Floor / Room, for example Main Floor / Kitchen. Pick (mapped room), like any other entry, to use the Room Mappings entry of the light's Hue room; pick a room to put the light there regardless of the mapping. A line that adds several things at once (every new light, every scene of a room) always uses the mapped rooms. A scene is the exception: it goes only into the room its Hue room or zone maps to, and another room picked here is refused (chapter 6, "Scenes stay in their room").

Click OK. Last Action reads Added Lamp 1 #1 to Living Room (device 1201) and the device appears in that room in the System Design tree within a second. Where the device lands and what it is called

The new device goes into the room you picked (or the mapped room) and takes the light's name from the Hue app, for example Lamp 1; for a fixture with several lights, each device gets the name of its own light as the Hue app shows it under the fixture. From that moment the names on both sides stay the same: rename the device in Composer and the light is renamed in the Hue app, rename the light in the Hue app and the device follows (8.2). Move it to another room as you like; neither a rename nor a move touches the link to the Hue light. Its Connections view shows the light's Hue Bridge input already connected to the bridge's Hue Lights output; you never have to make that connection for a device the bridge created. Adding a zone or room as a light

A Hue zone is a group of lights you made in the Hue app, often to run a few lamps together: the Accent zone, say, holds two of the lamps of the larger Living Room. Add From Hue makes such a zone, or a whole Hue room, into one Hue Light device, a zone light: one tile in a Control4 room whose on, off, slider, colour and colour temperature go to every lamp of the zone or room with a single request to the bridge, and that shows what the zone or room is doing. To everything else in this manual it is an ordinary Hue Light: the same Properties (chapter 10), the same keypad behaviour (chapter 11), the same place in programming and Advanced Lighting Scenes. Zones and rooms are never added by themselves and there is no "add all zones": one click per zone or room, so that the project holds only the ones you want.

In Add From Hue:

  • What: open the folder of zones and rooms. It lists every zone on the bridge that has no device yet, then every such room, each with the number of lights it holds: Accent (zone, 2 lights), Living Room (room, 2 lights). A zone or room whose device you deleted in Composer comes after the others, its entry ending in (deleted from Composer 2026-10-24); one that holds no lights reads Reading Corner (zone, no lights) and is refused. When every zone and room has a device the folder is left out. Hue's whole-home group ("all lights") is not offered (chapter 2).
  • Room: (mapped room) first, then every Control4 room as Floor / Room, exactly as for a light. (mapped room) uses the Room Mappings entry of that zone or room itself: a Hue room is usually mapped already by name; a zone is mapped when the driver found a Control4 room of exactly its name, or when you mapped it with Set Room Mapping (chapter 6).

Click OK. Last Action reads Added Accent (zone) to Living Room (device 1203) (a room reads, for example, Kitchen (room) in these texts) and the device appears in that room in the System Design tree within a second; choose File » Refresh Navigators afterwards. If you pick (mapped room) for a zone that is not mapped then nothing is created: Not added: TV Area (zone): Hue room TV Area (zone) not mapped; pick a room, or Set Room Mapping first; pick the room in the dialog, or map the zone first. A zone or room mapped to a Control4 room that has since been deleted is refused as a light would be (mapped to a Control4 room that no longer exists). A zone without lights answers Not added: Reading Corner (zone) has no lights; a remembered choice that already has a device, Not added: Accent (zone) is already imported (device 1203). The dialog remembers your last choices, as every dialog does: open both lists each time.

Names and properties. A zone light takes the zone's name as it is, Accent. A Hue room's zone light is named Living Room Lights, the room's name with Lights added, so that the Living Room's Lighting page does not list a light called "Living Room". Unlike a light's, a zone light's name is not kept in step with Hue: it stands for the name of a Hue room or zone, which the driver never renames, so renaming the device in Composer changes nothing in Hue, and a renamed Hue zone is only listed for Resolve Names (8.10). A zone light called Living Room Lights is never reported as differing from its Hue name. The device's Status reads Hue zone Accent (2 lights) or Hue room Living Room (2 lights); its Hue Light ID zone #1 (5e6f7a8b…), the zone's number on the bridge and the start of its Hue identity; Hue Name the zone's or room's name; Model Hue zone (2 lights); Hue Room the zone's own label, Accent (zone), or the room's name; Online Online. Composer shows the device's driver as Hue Light, with the Hue Light icon.

The Properties page of a zone light for the zone Accent: Status Hue zone Accent (2 lights), Hue Light ID zone #1 (5e6f7a8b…), Model Hue zone (2 lights) and Hue Room Accent (zone).

What the tile does. The zone light is on while any lamp of the zone is lit, and its slider shows the brightness of the lit lamps as Hue reports it, their average in whole percents; 0 % while all are off.

  • ON ("Turn On" in programming, a tap on the tile, "Toggle" while the zone is off, a tap on the top keypad button) turns every lamp on the way the zone light's On Mode says, exactly as on a Hue Light (chapter 11): with Preset, the default, every lamp at the zone light's On preset, its Default On Brightness, 100 % unless changed; with Previous, every lamp back at its own last level, as the Hue app does (not seen on the house system as this is written).
  • The slider, or a level from programming or a lighting scene, sets every lamp to that level: 40 % means every lamp at 40 %, and the tile then shows 40. Differences between the lamps are levelled out; 0 % turns them all off. (A Hue Scene's slider moves its room or zone relative to where it is, keeping those differences, 9.4; a zone light is a light and keeps a light's promise.)
  • A keypad hold brightens or dims the lamps of the zone that are on, together, and keeps the differences between them, as with Hue's dimmer switch; lamps that are off stay off. With up to 10 lamps on it is smooth and stops where you let go; with more, or while a time-of-day scene or a scene with moving colours plays in the zone, it moves in steps, one a second (chapter 11, "How a hold moves a room or zone"). Held while the zone is off, the top button turns it on at 1 % and brightens from there; the bottom button does nothing. Taps, the toggle button and the LEDs behave as on a Hue Light (chapter 11): a tap ramps every lamp to the On preset or to off over the click rate, and a tap while they ramp stops them, every lamp halting and settling within a second at the level the tile shows. Hue keeps the time of such a ramp for a whole zone or room, and the stop is smooth on every lamp (watched on the house system on 2026-11-09). The LEDs show on while any lamp is lit.
  • Colour and colour temperature go to every lamp that can follow: a white-only lamp in a colour zone should ignore the colour and keep its level (not seen on the house system as this is written, where every lamp has colour). A colour set while the zone is off is kept and applied at the next turn-on, as on a light. A turn-on from Control4 brings every lamp on in the zone light's On colour, unless its colour on mode is Previous, which leaves each lamp its own colour (chapter 11, "On Mode and colours").
  • Which controls the tile has depends on all the lamps together: the colour wheel appears if any lamp has colour, the temperature control if any lamp has colour temperature, and the temperature range is the one those lamps share (where they share none, the first lamp's). When lamps are added to or removed from the zone in the Hue app, the controls and the light count in Status follow within seconds (as this is written, a change of controls on a live tile had not yet been tried on the house system; look rather than assume).
  • The colour the tile shows. Hue reports on and brightness for a zone but no colour, so the driver shows the colour of the first lit lamp that has one (in the zone's own order), else the colour temperature of the first lit lamp that has one, and nothing while every lamp is off. The tile therefore follows colours set in the Hue app or by a scene; in a zone whose lamps differ it shows one lamp's colour, not a mixture.
  • One request a second. Hue's guidance is about one command a second for a zone or room as a whole (ten for single lights), so the driver sends a zone light's commands at that pace after a first three at once, keeping only the latest value: a quick slider drag is sent as one request a second while the percentage on the tile moves smoothly, and the lamps catch up and end where the slider ends. A hold is not held back: when it moves the lamps one by one, its start and its stop go to each lamp at once, and when it moves in steps they go out one a second (chapter 11); a keypad tap's start and its stop go out at once too, and so does a tap right after a hold.
  • A zone without lights. If every lamp is removed from the zone in the Hue app then the device reads Status Hue zone Accent has no lights; nothing to control with Online Offline, its tile is disabled and commands are dropped; put a lamp back and it is Online again, nothing to click. The zone light is not counted as missing, because the zone still exists (8.10).

A zone light is the one-request way to switch a whole Hue room (chapter 11, "Whole-room and whole-house commands"), and the natural thing to put on a wall keypad. A zone light and the Hue Scene devices of the same zone work side by side; 9.4 says what each tile shows. The bridge's Zone Lights row counts the zone lights the way Lights counts lights: 2 zones + 4 rooms on bridge, 1 imported, 5 new. New counts the zones and rooms Add From Hue would add: those with lights and without a device, not those whose device you deleted in Composer; Lights itself does not count them. Renames, a zone deleted in Hue, a device deleted in Composer: 8.10.

8. Living with it: renames, replacements, deletions, restores

Over the years lights get replaced, names change on one side or the other, devices get deleted by mistake, bridges get reset and projects get restored. This chapter walks through each case. The rule behind all of them: the Control4 device is the thing your programming refers to, so the driver keeps devices and re-points their links instead of deleting and re-creating, and it does nothing on its own that would make a later fix harder. Two cases it settles by itself: names, which it keeps the same on both sides (8.2), and a bulb or sensor that comes back to the bridge with the same radio (8.3). What needs you is listed in Attention until you deal with it; nothing is lost by waiting.

8.1 Two-way state

Turn a light on in the Hue app, press a Hue dimmer switch, or let a Hue automation run: the Control4 light follows within one to two seconds (the bridge bundles its reports, about one bundle a second), and Events Received on the bridge ticks up. The other way round, a tap in Control4 reaches the light within about a second and the tile then shows the level Hue confirms. If you switch a light off at its wall switch (cutting its power) then Hue needs a while, sometimes minutes, to notice; the device's Online then reads Offline and the tile is disabled on Navigators (greyed out, it cannot be used) until the light has power again. When power returns the light comes back on its own and Online returns to Online. A light offline for more than a day is mentioned in Attention: 1 light offline for more than 24 h: Lamp 3 #3; that day is counted from the moment the driver, since its last start, first saw the light offline, so after a controller restart or a driver update it starts over. A light that connects to the bridge over Wi-Fi or Matter rather than Zigbee never reports reachability, so its Online stays blank; that is harmless.

8.2 A light renamed in Hue, or a device renamed in Composer

The driver keeps the names of lights, scenes and motion sensors the same on both sides, so the two never drift apart:

  • Renamed in Composer (select the device, F2, type, Enter): within seconds the driver gives the light, scene or sensor the same name in the Hue app. Last Action reads, for example, Name sent to Hue: Reading Lamp (was Desk lamp in Hue).
  • Renamed in the Hue app: the device follows in Composer, and its Hue Name row shows the new name at once. Last Action reads, for example, Name taken from Hue: Office Lamp (device 1213, was Desk Lamp). The driver renames one device every 2 seconds at most, because each rename makes Composer refresh the project.
  • Renamed on both sides before the driver saw either (while the feed was down, say, or the controller restarting): Control4 is the source of truth, so the Composer name wins and goes to Hue, and Last Action names the Hue name it replaced: Both renamed: kept the Composer name Reading Lamp and sent it to Hue (Hue had Office lamp).

What "the same name" means on each side: for a light, the name of the lamp as the Hue app shows it, which its Hue Name row shows too; for a scene, the scene's name, with a zone scene's zone name in front on the Composer side only (Accent Nightlight in Composer, Nightlight in Hue), and a time-of-day scene without the number the Hue app adds (9.11); for a motion sensor, the sensor's name (14.12). A device whose Composer name already carries a word in front of or after its Hue name when the driver first pairs the two names (after an update from 1.1 or earlier, say: Kitchen Pendant for the Hue lamp Pendant) keeps that word on the Composer side: Hue keeps Pendant, and a rename to Island in the Hue app gives Kitchen Island. Not kept in step: Hue rooms and zones and the zone lights that stand for them (8.10), the bridge itself, and Control4 rooms. The driver never renames a Hue room or zone.

What cannot follow by itself waits, and Attention says so, each item ending in the action that settles it:

  • A name Hue refuses. Hue takes names of at most 32 bytes: 32 plain letters and digits, fewer when the name has accented letters or symbols. A longer Composer name is not cut short behind your back; it waits, with an item such as 1 name Hue refused: Kitchen Under-Cabinet Left Light Strip (38 characters, Hue takes 32) (Resolve Names) (a name with accented letters says … bytes, Hue takes 32 (accented letters count twice)). Shorten it in Composer (the new name then goes to Hue at once), or settle it in Resolve Names.
  • Renames while the bridge is away. A rename in Composer while the bridge is offline, or while it refuses the key, waits and is sent when the bridge is back; after 10 minutes Attention says, for example, 2 renames wait for the bridge: Reading Lamp, Relax.
  • Many at once. When the driver finds more than five names changed at once that nobody renamed while it watched (a Hue backup restored, a bridge reset, a Control4 project restored), it changes none of them and lists them: 12 names changed while the driver was not watching (Resolve Names). More than ten renames from the Hue app within a minute are held the same way.
  • A name that keeps changing. If the two sides keep answering each other for one device (two Control4 projects on one bridge, say), the driver stops after a few rounds and lists the device: Names of Lamp 3 keep changing; their sync is paused (Resolve Names).
  • Two devices for one Hue name. Two devices whose names both come from the same Hue name (two devices claiming one light, say) do not sync: 2 devices share one Hue name and do not sync: … (Forget Device on one).
  • After an update from 1.1 or earlier. Names that already differ between Composer and Hue when two-way names start are not changed by themselves either. Last Action says so once, Names: 3 names differ between Composer and Hue (Resolve Names: "Composer names to Hue" sends the Composer names); other renames now sync both ways (or Names: every Composer name agrees with Hue; renames now sync both ways), Attention lists them (3 names differ between Composer and Hue (Resolve Names)), and Resolve Names settles them, normally with its first line, which sends the Composer names to Hue. From then on every rename follows by itself.

A device that waits for Resolve Names is settled too when you rename it in Composer (Control4 wins); a rename of it in the Hue app only updates its line until you answer.

Resolve Names has one row, Names, listing only what waits: first Composer names to Hue (all 3) and Hue names to Composer (all 3), each for all the waiting names at once; then two lines per waiting device, one for each direction, Reading Lamp: send it to Hue (Hue has Office lamp) and Reading Lamp: rename it to Office lamp from Hue; for a name Hue refused, a line that shortens it to fit in Hue, Kitchen Under-Cabinet Left Light Strip: cut to Kitchen Under-Cabinet Left Light in Hue; and last, when zone lights differ from their Hue names, Zone lights: rename to the Hue names (all 2) and Zone lights: keep the Composer names (all 2) (8.10). Pick one and click OK; Last Action says what was renamed where, for example Sent to Hue 3 names: … or Renamed from Hue 1 name: …, followed by ; not done 1: … for what could not be done. With nothing waiting the list holds (every name agrees between Composer and Hue). An empty row answers Nothing renamed: pick a row under Names first, and a remembered row that is no longer offered Nothing renamed: that row is not offered any more; open Names again.

8.3 A light replaced or deleted in Hue

If a light is deleted from the bridge in the Hue app, or replaced by a new one (which Hue treats as a new light), then its Hue Light device stays in the project and reads Status Gone from the Hue bridge since 2026-10-22 09:15; Link or Forget Device with Online Offline; its tile is disabled, the bridge's Lights counts it as 1 missing and Attention reads 1 light missing from the bridge: Lamp 5 #5 (device 1213, since 2026-10-22) (Link or Forget Device). Programming, Advanced Lighting Scenes and Navigator pages still refer to the device, so nothing of yours is lost.

The same bulb back under a new identity. A bulb deleted in the Hue app and added again, or every bulb after a reset of the bridge, comes back with a new Hue identity but with the same radio: the address of its Zigbee radio is fixed in the bulb's hardware. The driver recognises it by that radio at its next read of the lights and re-links the old device to it by itself: Last Action reads Re-linked Lamp 5 to its bulb (same radio, new Hue id) (when it happens inside Import All New, the import's result names it instead, chapter 7), and the device keeps its name, room, connections and programming, its tile works again, and the bulb takes the device's Composer name in the Hue app (8.2). It does so only where the match is certain: exactly one gone device of the same kind had that radio, the old device was not forgotten or deleted in Composer, and the new light has no device yet. For a fixture with several lights on one radio, the light's place in the fixture has to match too. Anything less certain is only suggested, as below.

A new bulb in place of the old one. A replacement bulb has its own radio, so it shows up as a new light; when it carries the old one's name in the same Hue room (the same room or, after a reset of the bridge, a room of the same name) the driver points that out in an Attention item of its own: 1 new light on the bridge may be a replacement: Lamp 5 [Bedroom] #9 (looks like Lamp 5 #5, device 1213; re-link?) (Link Device) (2 new lights on the bridge may be replacements: … for more). Import All New leaves such a light out and says so. Do not add it as a new device; point the old one at it with Link Device:

  1. Open Link Device. In Device, open the Lights (1) folder (when only one kind of device is listed, its devices are listed directly) and pick the gone device. A device with a match the driver is sure enough to suggest carries it at the end of its line, after an arrow, with the driver's reason: Lamp 5 (device 1213, gone since 10-22) → Lamp 5 [Bedroom] #9 (suggested: same name in the same Hue room) (a device whose connection was removed reads …, unbound since 10-21); lists use the short date). The reasons are same radio (a light or sensor whose radio matches but that the driver did not re-link by itself), same name in the same Hue room (lights and motion devices), same name in the same group (scenes) and same name (zone lights); a suggestion without a reason ends in (suggested). In To, leave (its suggested match), or pick the new light in the lights' folder, which lists every light that has no device at the moment.
  2. Click OK. Last Action reads Re-linked device 1213 (was Lamp 5 #5) to Lamp 5 [Bedroom] #9; Hue gets the Composer name Lamp 5 (the part after the semicolon only when the names differ). The device keeps its name, room, connections and programming, and the new light takes its name in the Hue app; its Status, Hue Light ID and capabilities now describe the new light and its tile works again. If the new light can do less than the old one (a white light in place of a colour one) the colour controls disappear from the tile, and programming that sets a colour on it does nothing.

To re-link every device that has a suggested match in one go, pick the first line of the Device list, Every device with a suggested match (3) (it counts the suggestions of every kind, zone lights included), leave To at (its suggested match) and click OK; Last Action lists each one, Suggested matches: Re-linked device 1213 …; … | …, followed by what the same click did for bound devices the bridge did not know (8.6). Any other choice under To answers Not linked: every device with a suggested match goes to its own match; set To to (its suggested match).

If the light comes back under its old identity (for example you restored a Hue backup) then the driver notices and the device is simply linked again; you do nothing. If the light is gone for good and you do not want the device either, delete the device in Composer (8.4). A gone device is never dropped by the driver itself. Refusals: Not linked: pick a device under Device first (an empty row); Not linked: device 1213 has no suggested match; under To pick a light (the Lights folder) (To left at its suggestion where there is none); Not linked: Lights under To is a folder; open it and pick one inside; Not linked: device 1213 is a light; under To pick a light (the Lights folder) (the two rows do not fit each other, a light's device and a scene, say; chapter 3, "How an action's dialog works"); Not linked: device 1213 is not a device of this bridge any more; open Device again; Not re-linked: Lamp 5 #9 is already linked to device 1217 (the light you picked already has a device); Not re-linked: device 1213 is already linked to Lamp 5 #9 (the device you picked was re-linked meanwhile, usually a remembered choice; open the Device list again); and Not re-linked: light … is not on the bridge (Diagnostics ▸ Read everything from the bridge now, then try again) (the list was stale). A folder with nothing in it holds one line that says so, (no light device is gone or unbound), and the To folders (nothing of this kind is free on the bridge).

8.4 A device deleted in Composer

If you delete a Hue Light device in Composer then the bridge is told at once: Lights ends in …, 1 deleted (after deleting most of them it reads, for example, 8 lights on bridge, 1 imported, 0 new, 7 deleted) and Attention stays None, because deleting in Composer is a deliberate act. The driver remembers the deletion:

  • Import All New skips that light on purpose and says so: skipped 1 deleted in Composer (Add From Hue re-creates one): Lamp 8 #8 (deleted 2026-10-22). A bulk import will therefore never quietly bring back a device you removed, and the driver never re-links a deleted device by its radio either.
  • Add From Hue lists it at the end of its lights as Lamp 8 [Bedroom] #8 (deleted from Composer 2026-10-22). Picking it creates a fresh device (a new device for Control4, so programming that pointed at the old one has to be redone) and clears the memory of the deletion.

If you deleted a device while the controller was off or the driver was not running then the driver finds out at its next start and treats it the same way, with one rare exception: when no Hue Light at all is left in the project at that moment the driver cannot tell a deletion from a missing answer and lists the device as 1 unbound device: … instead; Forget Device on it clears that.

8.5 Forget Device

Forget Device is the deliberate way to release a device from the bridge without deleting anything, for every kind of device: a light, a zone light, a scene or a motion device. Its Device row lists every device the bridge manages, sorted into folders by kind (Lights (8), Zone lights (1), Scenes (12), Motion sensors and areas (2)): Lamp 1 #1 (device 1201), and with its state after the bracket when it is not in its normal state, Lamp 5 #5 (device 1213) gone or Lamp 4 #4 (device 1211) unbound. Its Confirm row reads No until you pick Yes, forget; anything else answers Not forgotten: choose Yes, forget. On OK, Last Action reads Forgot Lamp 1 #1 (device 1201): unbound and left in the project for you to delete; the Hue light is listed under Add From Hue again. The device stays in the project with Status Forgotten by the bridge (was Hue light Lamp 1 #1). Delete this device, or bind it again and use Link Device on the Hue Bridge, its connection to the bridge removed and its tile disabled; delete it in Composer when you are ready. The Hue light is listed in Add From Hue and counted as new again. An empty Device answers Not forgotten: pick a device under Device first, and a remembered device that was forgotten meanwhile Not forgotten: device 1201 is not linked to this bridge any more. With nothing to forget the list holds (no device is linked to this bridge).

Which to use: delete the device without Forget when you want the light gone from Control4 for good (a bulk import will not bring it back); Forget and then delete when you want to import the light afresh, for example into another room; Link Device when you are replacing the light and want to keep the device.

8.6 Link Device and the bare "Hue Light"

Occasionally a Hue Light device exists that the bridge does not manage: one added by hand from the Items pane, one that was Forgotten and that you want back, or every light after the bridge device itself was deleted and re-added (which this manual tells you never to do). Such a device reads Status Not bound to a Hue Bridge, or, if you forgot it, Forgotten by the bridge (…). (A device that is connected can read the same for up to a minute after a Director restart or a controller reboot; wait that minute first, 8.7.) The way in is a connection, and sometimes one action:

  1. Open the Connections view, select the device, and connect its Hue Bridge input (in the upper pane) to the bridge's Hue Lights output by dragging the bridge from the lower pane, HUE_BRIDGE Output Devices, onto that row. Scene and motion devices use the same Hue Lights output.
  2. What happens next depends on whether the device remembers its light. A device that remembers which Hue light it was (one that was Forgotten, one left over from a deleted bridge, or one restored with a project) announces that light the moment it is connected, and the bridge takes it over on the spot, as it does at every start: its Status returns to Lamp 1 (Hue color lamp) and nothing needs clicking. If that light was meanwhile imported as another device, the bridge takes over neither and Attention reads 2 devices claim Hue light Lamp 1 #1: 1201 and 1219 (Forget Device on one of them): forget one of the two and delete it. If the remembered light is no longer on the bridge, the device is taken over anyway and reads Gone; 8.3 applies. (A scene or motion device you forgot is different: it stays released when it is connected again, until you link it with Link Device, 9.9 and 14.11.)
  3. A bare device, added by hand, remembers nothing and reads Bound; waiting for the bridge after the connection. The driver looks for a Hue light without a device whose name is the device's Composer name (the light's name, or the name of its Hue device; capitals and spaces at the ends do not matter), and when exactly one has it, links it with the first line of Link Device: a bare device is not listed on a line of its own. Open Link Device, pick the first line of Device, Every bound device the bridge does not know yet, by its own id or name (it reads Every device with a suggested match (…) while there are suggestions, and does both), leave To at (its suggested match) and click OK. Last Action reads, for example, Linked 1 device: Lamp 3 (device 1221) to Lamp 3 [Kitchen] #3 by name | Scenes: No scene devices are bound to this bridge | …, one part per kind. Rename the device in the tree to the exact Hue name first if nothing is linked. Linking never moves the device: it stays in whatever room it is in.

Link Device, in short: its Device row lists the devices of every kind that are gone or unbound, each with the driver's suggestion where it has one, and first the line for every device with a suggested match, which also links the connected devices the bridge does not know yet; its To row lists every light, zone, room, scene, motion sensor and MotionAware area that has no device, sorted into folders by kind. A device is linked only to a Hue thing of its own kind: a light's device to a light, a zone light to a zone or room, a scene device to a scene, a sensor's device to a sensor and an area's to an area.

The Connections view of a Hue Light, upper pane Control & Audio Video Connections. The row to look at is the device's Hue Bridge input under Control Inputs, whose Connected To column reads Hue Bridge->Hue Lights: it is connected to the bridge's Hue Lights output. Devices the bridge creates come connected like this; a hand-added one gets this row filled by dragging the bridge from the lower pane, HUE_BRIDGE Output Devices, onto it. The picture predates the keypad feature: today the Control Outputs also list Top Button Link, Bottom Button Link and Toggle Button Link (chapter 11).

If you remove that connection on a managed device then the bridge counts it as 1 unbound, Attention reads 1 unbound device: Lamp 4 #4 (device 1211) (bind it to this bridge in Composer, or Forget Device) and the device reads Not bound to a Hue Bridge; connect it again and it resumes by itself, no click needed. A device connected to a second Hue Bridge device by mistake never hears from the bridge it belongs to (a bare one reads Bound; waiting for the bridge for ever); delete that second bridge rather than link anything to it.

If two devices both claim the same Hue light (possible after copying devices around) then neither is changed and Attention reads 2 devices claim Hue light Lamp 4 #4: 1211 and 1223 (Forget Device on one of them): Forget one of the two and delete it.

8.7 The bridge offline and back; a controller restart

Covered in chapter 4: the driver reconnects by itself and does a full read when the bridge returns. Expect Event Stream to read Disconnected: …. Reconnecting in … s meanwhile; nothing is needed from you unless the bridge's address changed. When the bridge itself ends the feed, Event Stream reads Disconnected: server closed the stream (HTTP 200). Reconnecting in 1 s and the driver reconnects when the bridge is back; a bridge that restarts or updates its firmware is the expected cause (not yet observed on the house system). A light that updates its firmware needs nothing either; a capability it gains (colour, say) is picked up at the next full read. Besides the reads at every connection, the driver reads the bridge again by itself when its last read is 6 hours old (a quick read) or 24 hours old (a full read), and every 10 minutes once the feed has been down for 5 minutes (chapter 1). A bridge that has not answered for 5 minutes takes every Hue device offline until it answers again; a shorter outage, such as a restart, changes nothing on the tiles (chapter 4).

After a controller restart everything reconnects by itself: the lights, scenes and motion devices announce themselves to the bridge, Lights reads 5 imported alone until the first full read, and within seconds of Connected since every tile and colour control is back. Nothing to click. Right after a Director restart or a reboot the controller can be late in telling a device about its connection to the bridge, so a Hue Light, Hue Scene or Hue Motion Sensor may read Not bound to a Hue Bridge at first although Composer shows it connected. The device looks again 5, 15, 30 and 60 seconds after it starts and greets the bridge as soon as it sees the connection, so the text corrects itself within a minute. Only a device that still reads it after that needs its connection checked (8.6); a motion device then also acts as disconnected (14.12). A restart during a long bridge outage keeps the devices offline: they go offline again at the bridge's first failed check, within a minute of the start (chapter 4).

8.8 The bridge factory reset, restored or replaced

  • Factory reset. If the bridge is reset to factory settings then it forgets the pairing, and it refuses the next request the driver makes with it (the event feed reconnecting after the bridge's restart, or a command; after a refused command the driver asks the bridge once more to be sure): Status reads Key rejected by the bridge (HTTP 403): checked again by itself; re-pair with Pair Bridge if it stays and Event Stream Stopped: key rejected (HTTP 403) (the number is the bridge's reply code: the Hue Bridge Pro answered 403 when this was tested on 2026-10-25; the driver treats a 401 the same way). From then on the driver sends nothing with the rejected pairing except its own check of the key, 5 minutes, 15 minutes and an hour after the refusal and then every hour, in case the bridge refused it by mistake (a bridge that accepts it again ends the stop by itself; Diagnostics » Check the connection now checks at once and says Test failed: key rejected (HTTP 403). Re-pair with Pair Bridge). The feed stays stopped (Diagnostics » Reconnect the event stream does not reopen it) and commands from Control4 are dropped, not kept for later; after 5 minutes every Hue device goes offline, reading Hue Bridge refuses the pairing since …, until the bridge accepts the pairing again (chapter 4). Press the link button and click Pair Bridge (it is the same bridge, so Forget Pairing is not needed); Status returns to Paired and the feed reconnects. The lights and motion sensors you then add back in the Hue app come with new identities but with their own radios, so after the first read the driver re-links their devices by itself and sums it up in Last Action, for example Re-linked 47 devices by radio: Lamp 1, Lamp 2, Lamp 3 +44 more; their Hue names then follow the Composer names, one after another (8.2). A light or sensor whose radio does not decide (two devices that had one radio, say) is listed in Attention as a likely replacement, left out by Import All New and suggested in Link Device by its name and room. Scenes, zones, rooms and MotionAware areas made again in the Hue app have new identities and no radio: their devices read Gone or deleted in Hue. Link Device suggests a match by name within the same Hue room or zone, and a room or zone made again under its old name counts as the same one (a room and a zone of one name stay apart): a scene made again under its old name in its old room or zone, a zone or room made again under its old name, and an area made again under its old name over a room or zone of its old name are suggested, and the first line of Link Device, Every device with a suggested match (…), links them all at once. Point the rest at their new scene, zone or area under To, one by one. Your Control4 programming survives. The rooms and zones made again are new to the driver and start unmapped: Room Mappings offers each its old mapping, for example Great Room → (unmapped; the deleted Great Room was mapped to Living), and one Set Room Mapping click per room or zone takes it; nothing is mapped by itself. Re-linking a scene device to a scene of a room or zone made again under its old name is not held up by that, because it counts as the same one, but adding its other scenes waits for the mapping (chapter 6, "Scenes stay in their room").
  • Restore from a Hue backup, or a bridge moved to new hardware with the Hue app's transfer. The bridge's identifier belongs to its hardware. Restored onto the same bridge, it keeps its identifier: if the restore brings the same lights back under their old identities then the driver sees nothing wrong and links them again by itself; only devices still reading Gone from the Hue bridge … need Link Device. If the restored bridge no longer accepts the pairing (Status reads Key rejected by the bridge …), press the link button and Pair Bridge. Moved to new hardware, it is a different bridge to the driver, as in the next case: Forget Pairing, then press the new bridge's link button and click Pair Bridge; lights and scenes that kept their identities in the transfer keep their links, and the first full read shows which did not. Expect few re-links, possibly none; read Lights and Attention rather than assume. Note that a Composer project backup does not back up the Hue bridge; use the Hue app's backup for that.
  • A different bridge at the same address. If a replacement bridge answers at the old address, the driver finds another identifier there at its next start, after a change of Bridge IP Address, or a minute after the event feed dropped: Status reads Bridge at 10.0.2.143 reports ID 001788fffe456def, expected 001788fffe123abc; nothing is sent until the address is corrected, and the replacement receives nothing that carries the pairing. A replacement swapped in while the driver runs that answers within that minute refuses the pairing first, when the feed reconnects: Status reads Key rejected by the bridge (HTTP 403): checked again by itself; re-pair with Pair Bridge if it stays, as after a factory reset. Either way, Pair Bridge refuses the replacement until you have clicked Forget Pairing: Pairing failed: Bridge at 10.0.2.143 reports ID 001788fffe456def, expected 001788fffe123abc; run Forget Pairing first to pair with another bridge, without asking it for a key. Click Forget Pairing (with Yes, forget), press the replacement's link button and click Pair Bridge; lights and sensors moved to it keep their radios and are re-linked by themselves, and the rest read Gone until you link them as above. Taking a replacement bridge over automatically is not supported in this version.

8.9 A Control4 project restored from a backup, or a new controller

Where things are kept: the pairing key, the list of which device is which light or scene, the memory of deleted devices and the room mappings are all stored on the Hue Bridge device on the controller. Each Hue Light, Hue Scene and Hue Motion Sensor device also remembers its own Hue identity, name and model. Whether the bridge's store comes back when a project is restored from a Composer backup, or moved to a replacement controller, has not been verified; the devices' own memories travel with the project. So after a restore or a controller replacement:

  1. Read the bridge's Status. If it says Not paired: press the link button, then run Pair Bridge, press the link button and click Pair Bridge. If it says Paired, nothing was lost on that side.
  2. Wait for Event Stream to say Connected since and read Lights. If it shows all your devices as imported, you are done.
  3. Otherwise wait a minute: every connected device that remembers its light, scene or sensor is taken over by that identity as the bridge starts, by itself. What is left is linked with the first line of Link Device (8.6). A device reading Not bound to a Hue Bridge needs its connection made again first.
  4. Check Room Mappings: rooms named alike on both sides are mapped again by themselves; map the rest with Set Room Mapping, the whole home too if it had a mapping, because a scene can only be added in its mapped room (chapter 6, "Scenes stay in their room"). Scene devices that come back with the project are taken over by the scene they remember wherever they sit, and are never refused for their room. Programming, rooms and names are Composer's and come back with the project. If the restored names differ from the Hue names in more than five places, nothing is renamed by itself: Attention lists them for Resolve Names (8.2).

8.10 A zone or room renamed, emptied or deleted in Hue

A zone light (chapter 7) lives through the same cases as a light, with the zone or room in place of the light. The rule is the same: the device stays, its link is re-pointed, and nothing is dropped by itself.

  • Renamed in the Hue app. The device's Hue Name and Status follow at once; its Composer name is not changed by itself, because a zone light's name stands for the name of a Hue zone or room, which the driver keeps out of the name sync (8.2). Attention lists the difference instead, for example after the zone Accent was renamed Sofa in the Hue app 1 zone light name differs from the Composer names: Accent is Sofa in Hue (Resolve Names), and Resolve Names offers Zone lights: rename to the Hue names (all 1) (for a Hue room's zone light, the Hue name is the new room name with Lights added) and Zone lights: keep the Composer names (all 1), for all the zone lights that differ at once. Renamed in Composer, the device keeps its new name and nothing is ever written to Hue: the driver never renames a Hue zone or room.
  • Lamps added or removed. Nothing to do: the light count in Status, the tile's controls and the colour it shows follow the new membership (chapter 7). A lamp replaced, renamed or deleted in Hue changes nothing for the zone light; the lamp's own Hue Light device, if it has one, follows 8.2 and 8.3.
  • Emptied, then filled again. A zone with no lamps left reads Hue zone Accent has no lights; nothing to control with Online Offline and a disabled tile; it is not counted as missing, because the zone still exists. Put a lamp back and the device is Online again by itself.
  • Deleted in the Hue app. The device stays and reads Status Zone deleted in Hue since 2026-10-24; Link or Forget Device (for a Hue room, Room deleted in Hue since …) with Online Offline and a disabled tile; the bridge's Zone Lights row gains , 1 missing and Attention reads 1 zone light missing from the bridge: Accent (device 1203, since 2026-10-24) (Link or Forget Device). A zone deleted and made again in the Hue app has a new identity and no radio, so the device still reads deleted and the new zone is listed in Add From Hue; Link Device suggests a zone or room of the device's name and type (a room and a zone of one name stay apart), Accent (zone light, device 1203, gone since 10-24) → Accent (zone) (suggested: same name), and its first line, Every device with a suggested match (…), links it with the others. Re-link the device rather than add a second one. Two ways to answer:
    • Link Device points the device at another zone or room, keeping its name, room, connections and programming. Device lists it as Accent (zone light, device 1203, gone since 10-24), with its suggestion after it when there is one; in To, leave (its suggested match), or pick the zone or room in the folder of zones and rooms. Last Action reads Re-linked device 1203 (was Accent (zone)) to TV Area (zone); the device then describes the new zone, and a name difference is listed for Resolve Names. A zone light cannot be pointed at a single light, nor a light's device at a zone: the action refuses such a pair.
    • Forget Device, whose Device list holds the zone lights too, releases the device: Forgot Accent (zone, device 1203): unbound and left in the project for you to delete. The device reads Forgotten by the bridge (was Hue zone Accent), with the usual instruction in its Last Action (Delete this device, or bind it again and use Link Device on the Hue Bridge); delete it in Composer. The zone, if it still exists, is listed under Add From Hue again.
  • Deleted in Composer. The bridge remembers it, as for a light (8.4): Zone Lights gains , 1 deleted and Add From Hue lists the zone with (deleted from Composer 2026-10-24); picking it creates a fresh device (programming that pointed at the old one has to be redone) and clears the memory. Nothing re-creates it by itself.
  • Moved to another Control4 room, or its connection removed. Moving changes nothing; its line in Zone Lights names the room it sits in, like a light's, when its zone or room is mapped to a different Control4 room (chapter 6). A device whose connection to the bridge was removed is counted as unbound in Zone Lights and Attention; connect it again in Connections and it resumes by itself (8.6), because the device remembers which zone it stands for. That memory also brings it back after a project restore or a replacement controller (8.9): once connected it is taken over by itself, and two devices remembering the same zone are reported in Attention for you to Forget one of them.
  • The bridge reset, restored or replaced (8.8): a zone light whose zone comes back under its old identity is linked again by itself; one whose zone has a new identity reads deleted and is re-pointed with Link Device, which suggests a zone or room made again under its old name.

9. Scenes

A Hue scene is a saved look for one room or zone. The driver can show any scene as a Hue Scene device, a dimmer in a Control4 room: ON plays (recalls, in Hue's word) the scene on the bridge, OFF turns the scene's Hue room or zone off, the slider brightens or dims the room or zone while keeping the scene's look, and the device follows what happens in Hue. Each scene device shows its own scene: its tile reads on only while that scene is the one playing (9.4). Hue creates about seven default scenes per room, so scenes are never imported automatically; you pick the ones you want, and each goes into the Control4 room its Hue room or zone maps to. Hue's time-of-day scenes, which switch between ordinary scenes through the day, can become Hue Scene devices too; 9.11 says how they differ.

9.4 What the tile and slider do

  • ON (a tap on the tile, or programming turning it on) plays the scene on the bridge, the way the Recall As property says. The member lights take the scene within about a second and the tile shows ON with the room or zone's brightness. If the bridge does not confirm within two seconds the tile shows ON anyway; if the request then fails, the tile drops back, and the next full read corrects it in any case. A ramp time given by programming (or by an Advanced Lighting Scene) is passed to Hue as the time to fade into the scene, and Hue fades the scene in over that time (seen on the house system on 2026-11-09); a tap on a Navigator uses a short fade, and a keypad tap fades the scene in over the device's click rate (chapter 11).
  • OFF turns the scene's whole Hue room or zone off, every light in it, imported or not, with one request to the bridge. The tile shows OFF. Remember that a zone can span several Control4 rooms, so OFF on a zone scene can turn off lights in rooms other than the one the scene device sits in. A zone that has no lights in it is reported off without any request.
  • The slider shows the brightness of the scene's Hue room or zone as Hue reports it (the average of the lights that are on, in whole percents) while this scene is on, and 0 % while it is not, also while another scene lights the room. Moving it while the scene is on brightens or dims the room or zone relative to where it is, keeping the scene's colours and the differences between its lights, as Hue's own dimmer switch does; 0 % turns the room or zone off. Moving it while the scene is not on switches to it at once, whether the room or zone is off or another scene is playing there: the scene plays at that brightness, all its lights at that one level, and its tile goes on while the other scene's goes to 0. 100 % plays the scene exactly as designed, each light at its own level, as ON does. A second move right after such a switch is measured from the level just set. During a ramp (a keypad hold or a slow fade) the percentage on Navigators updates at most once a second.
  • TOGGLE (from programming, or a Navigator tap that arrives as one) turns the room or zone off while the scene is on, and plays the scene while it is not, also while another scene lights the room, as a Control4 Advanced Lighting scene's toggle does.
  • When the tile reads on. The tile follows its own scene and nothing else. The scene reads on from the moment it is played, from Control4, a keypad, the Hue app, a Hue switch or automation, or a time-of-day scene's schedule (9.11), until one of these happens:
    • its room or zone is turned off, from anywhere, including the Hue app or a Hue dimmer switch;
    • another scene of the same room or zone is played;
    • a scene of another room or zone that shares a lamp with it is played, such as a scene of the Bedside Lights zone over a Master Bedroom scene; a scene of the whole home shares every lamp;
    • this device turns it off.
    Dimming or changing its lights afterwards (the slider, a keypad, a light's own tile, the Hue app, a Hue dimmer switch) does not turn it off, even when Hue itself no longer calls the scene active (Hue stops when a single light of the scene is changed; a dim of the whole room or zone leaves the scene active); the tile shows the new percentage instead. An ON tile therefore means "this was the last scene played here and its room or zone is on". While a scene that has no device plays, or lamps were turned on by hand, every scene tile of the room reads 0, as an inactive Advanced Lighting scene does. Programming that fires "when this scene turns on" fires when it becomes the scene playing, and "when this scene turns off" when it stops being that (9.10). The on state survives a controller restart; after a restart or an outage of the bridge, the bridge's first answer puts every scene tile right within seconds, also when another scene was played in the meantime.
  • The keypad is separate. Whatever the tile shows, a keypad bound to the scene can keep showing whether its room or zone is lit: that is the device's Keypad Follows setting (9.5, chapter 11), which decides only the keypad, never the tile.

A time-of-day scene's device differs in a few points: it reads on exactly while Hue runs its schedule, and a level while it is not running starts the schedule before moving to the level (9.11). A scene that was deleted in Hue reads 0 at once (9.8), and a device pointed at another scene shows nothing until the bridge reports that scene (9.7).

With a zone light of the same zone. If the zone also has a zone light (chapter 7), the two kinds of tile sit side by side and both follow the same reports from the bridge. Move the zone light's slider, or hold its keypad button, while a scene plays: both tiles show the new level and the scene stays on (its rule above). Turn the zone light off: Hue ends the scene, so the scene tile goes off and the zone light goes off. Turn the zone light on again: it comes on at its On preset (100 % unless changed) on every lamp and the scene tile stays off, because on is a level, not a look. Play a scene, from anywhere: the zone light's tile goes on at the scene's average brightness and shows the colour of the first lamp the scene lit. A zone light of a Hue room and the scenes of that room behave the same way.

A device can be re-linked from one kind of scene to the other and then behaves as the new kind, its Recall As row appearing or disappearing. The re-linked device starts afresh: it shows nothing of the old scene's state and takes the new scene's as soon as the bridge reports it.
  • The room check (chapter 6, "Scenes stay in their room"). A scene of an unmapped Hue room or zone, or of one mapped to a deleted Control4 room, is refused as in 9.2 (Not linked: …). Not checked: a scene of the same Hue room or zone as the device's previous scene (also one made again under its old name), and a device taken over by the scene it remembers. While the device's room is not known yet, the check is skipped and the Lua output says so.
  • Other refusals mirror the light's (8.3): an empty row, a device or scene that is linked already, a device the bridge no longer manages, a stale scene, or a scene picked for a light's device (Not linked: device 1213 is a light; under To pick a light (the Lights folder)). Inside the To list the room folders of scenes cannot be picked as a whole.
  • Forget Device releases a scene device (8.5): its Device list holds the scene devices as Living Room: Relax (device 1205); with Yes, forget the result reads Forgot Relax (device 1205): unbound and left in the project for you to delete; the Hue scene is listed under Add From Hue again. Connected to the bridge again, it stays released until the first line of Link Device links it again by the scene it remembers.
  • Reading the scenes again. New, renamed and deleted scenes arrive through the event feed, and the driver reads the scenes again by itself at the full reads of chapter 1; to read everything at once, run Diagnostics and pick Read everything from the bridge now (chapter 12).

9.8 A scene deleted, renamed, edited or copied in Hue

If a scene is deleted in the Hue app then its device stays and reads Status Scene deleted in Hue since 2026-10-21 21:35; ON does nothing; Link or Forget Device with Online Offline; its tile reads 0 at once, also when the scene was playing, and tapping ON leaves the tile off (the bridge's Lua output notes Recall failed: HTTP 404 (scene gone?) if the deletion had not yet reached the driver); Attention reads 1 scene missing from the bridge: Concentrate (device 1241, since 2026-10-21) (Link or Forget Device). Re-link it to another scene, or Forget it and delete the device; a scene deleted and made again in the Hue app under the same name in the same room or zone (or in a room or zone made again under its old name) is suggested in Link Device. If a scene is renamed in Hue then its device follows (8.2): the zone scene Nightlight renamed to Night Light in the Hue app makes the device Accent Night Light. Editing a scene's colours, brightness or dynamics in the Hue app needs nothing in Control4; the next ON plays the new look. Copying a scene to another room in the Hue app creates a new scene; the device keeps following the original. A scene created in the Hue app appears in Add From Hue by itself. All of this holds for time-of-day scenes too; in addition, deleting one of the ordinary scenes a time-of-day scene plays makes Hue delete the whole time-of-day scene (9.11).

9.9 A scene device unbound, or added by hand

A scene device that lost its connection reads Not bound to a Hue Bridge and is counted as 1 unbound, with Attention 1 unbound scene device: Relax (device 1205) (bind it to this bridge in Composer, or Forget Device); connect it again in Connections (to the bridge's Hue Lights output, the same as a light) and it resumes by itself. A device that remembers its scene is taken over the moment it is connected, exactly as in 8.6, in whatever room it sits, unless Forget Device released it: the bridge then tells it again that it is forgotten, until the first line of Link Device links it. Two devices remembering the same scene give 2 devices claim Hue scene Relax: 1205 and 1243 (Forget Device on one of them). A bare scene device reads Bound; waiting for the bridge, and the first line of Link Device (8.6) links it to the one scene without a device whose name it carries: the scene's own name (Nightlight), the name the bridge would give the device (Accent Nightlight for a zone scene, Natural light for a time-of-day scene) or the Hue app's name with its number, capitals and spaces at the ends aside. The match looks only among the scenes of the Hue rooms and zones that map to the Control4 room the device sits in, since a name such as Relax exists in many rooms; while the device's room is not known, it looks among all of them.

9.10 Programming with scenes

To Control4 a Hue Scene device is a dimmer. In programming, turn it on to play the scene, off to turn the room or zone off, or set a level to brighten or dim the room or zone (while the scene is not on, also while another scene plays, a level plays it at that brightness, and 100 % plays it as designed); schedule it, put it behind a keypad button, or include it in an Advanced Lighting Scene next to other lights (the Hue lights themselves can be members too). Its on state follows the rule in 9.4, so a Hue scene played from the Hue app is visible to Control4 as the scene being on, and a dim in the Hue app as a new level. Its on event fires when the scene becomes the one playing, and its off event when it stops being that: its room or zone turned off, or another scene of it, or of a room or zone that shares a lamp with it, played. Updating from 1.5 or earlier: these events used to follow the room's lights by default, so programming written for that, "when the scene turns on" meaning "when the room is lit", say, now runs only for this scene; use a zone light's events for the room (chapter 7). A time-of-day scene is a dimmer too: on starts its schedule, off stops it and turns the room or zone off, and its on and off events follow Hue's own word on whether the schedule is running (9.11). Chapter 11 has the details.

9.11 Time-of-day scenes

Besides ordinary scenes, Hue has scenes that follow the time of day, such as Golden hours and Natural light; Hue calls them smart scenes. Each belongs to one room or zone and is a schedule rather than a look: the day is divided into time slots, each slot plays an ordinary scene, and Hue moves on to the next slot's scene by itself, fading over about a minute. The Bedroom's Natural light, for example, may play Energize from 07:00 and Relax from 19:00; a slot can also begin at sunset. Once started, Hue keeps the schedule running, slot after slot, until something stops it. The driver lists time-of-day scenes next to the ordinary ones and makes each into a Hue Scene device; the rest of this chapter applies to them, with the differences below.

Adding one. Add From Hue lists every time-of-day scene that has no device in the folder of its room or zone, marked (time-based) and placed before the ordinary scenes: Natural light (time-based) in the Bedroom's folder, Golden hours (time-based) in Accent's. An ordinary scene that a time-of-day scene plays in one of its time slots carries that scene's name in brackets, Relax (in Natural light), in Add From Hue and in Link Device; it need not be imported for the time-of-day scene to work. Picking the room's folder as a whole creates its time-of-day scenes too, after its ordinary scenes (9.3). The bridge's Scenes line counts them as well (9.1).

Names. On the bridge the Hue app can give a time-of-day scene a number after its name, Natural light 2, say. The driver leaves one such trailing number out, in its lists and in the device's name: the Bedroom's device is named Natural light, and a zone's carries the zone's name in front, as ordinary zone scenes do: Accent Golden hours. The name sync of 8.2 follows the same rule, so the missing number never counts as a difference: Golden hours 4 in the Hue app is Golden hours in Composer, and a device renamed Evening in Composer sends Evening to Hue.

Raise Level By and Restore Saved Level

Besides the standard commands of a Control4 dimmer (chapter 11), a Hue Light has two programming commands of its own, for a light that should be brighter for a while and then go back to the level it had: Raise Level By and Restore Saved Level. Composer's Programming view lists them under the device with its other commands. They act only when programming runs them, never by themselves, and they work the same on a light, a zone light.

CommandChoicesWhat it does
Raise Level ByPoints: 1 to 100, default 30.
Ramp (ms): 0 to 60000, default 1000 (one second).		Saves the light's current level (0 when it is off), then sets the light to the saved level plus the points, at most 100 %, fading over the ramp time. Points are added to the percentage: 40 % becomes 70 %, 80 % becomes 100 %, and a light that is off turns on at 30 %. While a level is saved, another Raise Level By saves nothing new and does not climb: it sets the light to the saved level plus the points again. Every outcome is one line in the light's Last Action, for example Raised from 40 % to 70 % (saved 40 %), Restored to 40 %, Not restored: changed to 55 % since the raise; saved level forgotten or Nothing to restore. A device whose light is gone from the bridge, or whose connection to the bridge was removed (chapter 8), refuses both commands, says so in Last Action and keeps its saved level. A saved level outlasts a driver update, a Director restart and a controller reboot, so a restore after one still works. Both commands go to the light as a slider move does, with the same pacing (chapter 11, "Whole-room and whole-house commands"). On a zone light the level is the zone's, the average of its lit lamps, and both commands set every lamp to one level, as the slider does.

An example: two lamps brighter while someone is there

The wish: when someone comes into the hall, its two lamps get 30 points brighter than they are, and 5 minutes after the last movement they go back to what they were. The motion comes from a Hue Motion Sensor device, which stands for a Hue motion sensor or a MotionAware area alike (chapter 14); here it is named Hallway sensor, and the lamps Lamp 1 and Lamp 2.

  1. The delay. Select Hallway sensor, open its Properties tab, set Occupancy Hold Time (s) to 300 and click the row's Set button. The device then shows motion until 5 minutes after Hue reports that the motion has ended (14.7), and Hue reports that itself about 10 seconds after the last movement (measured on a MotionAware area on 2026-10-26; a sensor keeps a time of its own, 14.6). So stops sensing motion fires about 5 minutes and 10 seconds after the last movement, and a movement before then starts the wait again.
  2. Brighter. In Programming, select the event senses motion of Hallway sensor. Among the actions select Lamp 1, choose Raise Level By, set Points to 30 and Ramp (ms) to 1000, and drag it into the event's script. Do the same for Lamp 2.
  3. Back. Select the event stops sensing motion of Hallway sensor and add Restore Saved Level, Ramp (ms) 1000, for Lamp 1 and for Lamp 2 the same way.

Then, with Lamp 1 at 40 % and Lamp 2 off: someone walks in, and within a second or two Lamp 1 fades up to 70 % and Lamp 2 comes on at 30 %. While they move about, nothing more happens: the sensor keeps showing motion, so the raise is not repeated. When they have left, Lamp 1 fades back to 40 % and Lamp 2 goes off, about 5 minutes and 10 seconds after the last movement. Each lamp's Last Action says what it did.

A lamp someone changed meanwhile is left alone. Say someone sets Lamp 1 to 55 % during those minutes, in the Hue app, on its tile or with a keypad. When the motion stops, Lamp 1 stays at 55 % (its Last Action reads Not restored: changed to 55 % since the raise; saved level forgotten) and Lamp 2 goes off as usual. The reason: the lamp no longer shows the level the raise gave it, so someone chose a new level after the raise, and putting back the old one would undo that choice. A lamp switched off meanwhile stays off for the same reason. Its old level is forgotten, and the next motion raises the lamp from where it now is, from 55 % to 85 %.

Two things to keep in mind. The hold time belongs to the motion device, so every program that uses Hallway sensor sees motion until those 5 minutes are over; when only these lamps should wait, a Timer agent can make the delay instead (14.7). And Hue's own automation for the sensor or area keeps running beside this program (14.1): if it moves the same lamps, the restore counts that as a change and leaves them where Hue put them. For a MotionAware area, 14.13 says how to make its automation do nothing, so that Control4 alone moves the lamps.

11. Using the lights from Control4

Once imported, a Hue Light is an ordinary Control4 dimmer to everything in the project, and a Hue Scene an ordinary dimmer whose level is its room or zone's brightness. This chapter says what that means in practice.

On Navigators

Each Hue Light and each Hue Scene appears on its room's Lighting page with a tile and a slider. For a Hue Scene a tap plays the scene or turns its room or zone off and the slider dims the whole room or zone (9.4); a time-of-day scene's tap starts or stops its schedule (9.11). The rest of this paragraph is about lights. Tap the tile for on and off; the slider sets brightness, and the light responds within about a second. A tap that turns a light on, and "Turn On" in programming, follow the light's On Mode (one of the keypad settings below): with Preset, the default, the light goes to its On preset, its Default On Brightness, 100 % unless changed; with Previous, to the level it had when it went off. Neither uses the power-on level set in the Hue app; use the slider or a programmed level for anything else. The driver does the same, as a keypad tap does, for Control4's ON command and for TOGGLE while the light is off, but over the light's default ramp time (at once unless it is set) rather than over the click rate. ON on a light that is already on sets it to the On preset too. Control4 says a tap on the tile follows the On Mode; that it does with a changed On preset or with Previous had not been checked on the house system as this is written. The slider settles on the level Hue reports back, in whole percents; 0 % is off and any other level is on. A tile is disabled while the light is unreachable (chapter 10, Online), and every Hue tile while the bridge has not answered for 5 minutes (chapter 4). Changes from the Hue app or a Hue dimmer switch show on the tile within a second or two.

In programming and on keypads

In Composer's Programming view a Hue Light offers the standard actions and events of any Control4 dimmer: turn on, turn off, toggle, set or ramp to a level, and events when it turns on or off or its level changes. It also offers two commands of its own, Raise Level By and Restore Saved Level, for a light that should be brighter for a while and then go back to the level it had (chapter 10, with an example for a motion sensor). The events fire for changes made in the Hue app, by a Hue dimmer switch or by a Hue automation too, because the device follows the bridge; a "when Lamp 3 turns on" program runs however the light was turned on. A keypad button connected to a Hue Light works as with any Control4 dimmer: a tap ramps it on or off, a tap while it ramps stops it, a hold dims it, and the LED follows the light's state, including changes made on the Hue side; the next section has the setup. A Hue Scene is a dimmer: on plays the scene, off turns the room or zone off, and a level brightens or dims the room or zone (or, while the scene is not on, also while another scene plays, plays the scene at that level, 100 % as designed); its on event fires when the scene becomes the one playing, from Control4, a keypad, the Hue app or a Hue switch, its off event when its room or zone turns off or another scene of it, or of a room or zone sharing a lamp with it, is played, and its level events follow the room or zone's brightness while the scene is on, including dims made in the Hue app (9.4, 9.10). A ramp time given to a scene is passed to Hue as the time to fade into the scene, and Hue fades the scene in over that time (seen on the house system on 2026-11-09). A time-of-day scene's on event fires when Hue starts its schedule and its off event when Hue stops it, and a ramp time does not apply to starting one (9.11).

Physical switches and keypads

A Hue bulb has to stay powered, so the wall switch that feeds it cannot switch its load. A Control4 Switch V2 or Essential Switch can instead run as a two-button keypad whose buttons drive the Hue Light:

  1. In the switch's Properties tick Use as 2 Button Keypad and leave Buttons Control Load unticked. The switch's own load is then set by programming only; for a Hue bulb set it to 100 % (a "when the project loads" program, for example) so the bulb keeps power. Hide From Nav hides the switch's own light from Navigators, leaving the Hue Light as the one tile for that lamp.
  2. In Connections, select the Hue Light (or Hue Scene) device: its Top Button Link, Bottom Button Link and Toggle Button Link are listed under its Control Outputs. Bind the switch's Top button to Top Button Link and its Bottom button to Bottom Button Link (a drag between the two lists). A single-button keypad goes to the Toggle Button Link instead.
  3. Tap and hold the buttons; the Hue Light answers within about a second, like a tap on its tile.

Keypad settings. A Control4 dimmer normally lists its own keypad settings on its Properties page: click and hold rates, an On preset and the colours of its keypad button LEDs. Composer lists them for the Hue Light and Hue Scene devices too, on a settings page of Control4's own above the driver's Advanced Properties (on a Hue Light also an On Mode, and on a Hue Light with colour the On and Dim colours and a colour on mode; see "On Mode and colours" below), with these defaults: on a Hue Light the On preset (default on brightness) is 100 % and the On Mode Preset; on both, a tap's ramp or fade takes 3 seconds across the whole range, a shorter one its share (the click rate), a hold covers the full range in 5 seconds (the hold rate) and the LEDs are blue, a top button lit while the device is on and a bottom button lit while it is off. A Hue Scene lists no On preset, no On Mode and no colours; a time-of-day scene does not fade (below). These fields take a change when you press Enter, unlike the driver's own rows with their Set button; the next button press uses it. The driver accepts an On preset of 1 to 100 % and rates of 0 to 6 000 000 ms, 100 minutes, the longest fade Hue takes (Composer allows no hold rate under one second), and refuses a number outside these ranges or a value that is not a number, and Composer then shows the value in use again. If Composer does not list them after an update, the controller has not been rebooted since an update that changed what these device types can do, their capabilities (chapter 3, "Updating to a newer build"); until then the defaults apply. Control4 added the On Mode in OS 3.3.2; on an older controller a light uses Preset.

On Mode and colours. A Hue Light's On Mode decides the level of every turn-on that names no level (a tap on the tile, "Turn On", "Toggle" from off, a top or toggle tap from off). Preset, the default, turns the light on at its On preset. Previous turns it on at the level it had when it went off; a zone light brings every lamp back at its own level. While the light is on, a top tap and "Turn On" go to the preset. A Hue Light with colour also follows Control4's colour settings. With colour on mode Preset, the default, every turn-on from Control4 brings the light on in the On colour, blended toward the Dim colour at low levels (the On colour at the Default On Brightness and above, the Dim colour at 1 %), and the blend follows the level until someone picks a colour. Fade is Control4's warm dim: the colour follows every level change, from the Dim colour at 1 % to the On colour at 100 %. Previous sends no colour, so the light comes on in its last colour. With Preset and Fade, a turn-off with a fade time fades toward the Dim colour; an instant off and a held button do not, and after a hold the colour glides to the blend about half a second after you let go. Dealers should set each colour light's On and Dim colours and its colour on mode; until then a light turns on in the On colour Composer shows, Control4's default. Turn-ons from the Hue app, a Hue switch or a Hue automation are Hue's own, and the driver never corrects them. The Hue Light's own Documentation tab has the details. A Hue Scene has neither: ON always plays the scene as designed.

A Hue Scene device on a keypad is the usual way to run a Hue room or zone from a wall switch. What its buttons do depends on whether the keypad reads the scene on, which the device's Keypad Follows decides (below): with Room/zone lights, the default, on while the scene is on or while any light of its room or zone is lit; with This scene, on only while the scene is the one playing, as the tile is.

A tap while a scene fades stops it. A tap on any of the scene's three buttons while a tap's fade runs stops the fade where it is, the other button and the toggle included: each lamp keeps the level it has reached, so the scene keeps its look, only dimmer, and a fade out stopped before the end leaves the lamps lit. The driver stops the room or zone as a whole rather than setting a level, which would bring every lamp to one level and lose the look. At once the tile shows the level the device works out from how long the fade ran; about ten seconds after the fade would have ended, it moves to the level Hue reports. A button held while the scene fades stops the fade and then brightens or dims from there. Control4's stop command, from programming or an Advanced Lighting Scene, and a button bound to the scene's Brightness Stop link stop a tap's fade the same way. A second tap on the same button within about 0.6 seconds of the first is part of a double tap and does nothing: the fade goes on to where the first tap sent it, and double-tap programming on the keypad runs (a double tap on a toggle button fades the scene in, or out, as one tap would); to stop the fade, tap a little later than that. A click rate of 0 makes a tap play the scene or turn the room or zone off at once, with nothing to stop. The fades and their stops were watched on the house system on 2026-11-09, and a keypad's taps on a scene worked the same day.

How a hold moves a room or zone. A hold on a Hue Scene device, a zone light brightens or dims the lamps of the room or zone that are on when the hold starts; lamps that are off stay off, and the differences between the lamps are kept. A hold from off (the top or toggle button) starts the lamps at 1 % and then brightens all of them. Hue ignores the time given for a dim of a whole room or zone and covers the whole range in about a second, so the driver does the timing itself:

  • Up to 10 lamps (on, or in the room or zone for a hold from off): the driver brightens or dims each lamp on its own at the hold rate, as on a single Hue Light, so the room or zone moves smoothly and stops where you let go. Each lamp takes one request when the hold starts and one when you let go.
  • More than 10 lamps, or a time-of-day scene running or a scene playing with moving colours in the room or zone, however few its lamps: the driver moves the room or zone as a whole once a second, Hue's pace for a whole room or zone, each step sized so that the whole range still takes the hold rate: 20 % a step at the default 5 seconds, 10 % at 10 seconds. The change shows as steps, softened by the short fade Hue gives every change, and no step follows once you let go. Changing single lamps could end the schedule or stop the moving colours, while a dim of the whole room or zone leaves them running (9.4, 9.11).
  • Lamps not known yet: while the bridge does not yet know which lamps the room or zone holds (for a moment after a restart, say), or when a hold down finds none of them on, the room or zone is dimmed as a whole, which runs to full or off in about a second.

A hold that moves the lamps one by one may make Hue stop calling an ordinary scene active, as after any change to a single light (9.4); the scene's tile stays on all the same. Neither kind of hold on a whole room or zone had been watched on the house system as this is written.

Keypad Follows. The scene's tile always follows the scene (9.4); its keypad follows Keypad Follows, a row on the device's Properties page (9.5). With Room/zone lights, the default, the LEDs show on while the scene is on or while any light of its room or zone is on, whatever turned it on, and off once they are all off: a wall switch shows whether the room is lit, even while the scene's own tile reads 0 because another scene plays. With This scene they follow the scene itself, as the tile does: a dim from its buttons, its slider or the Hue app keeps them on, and so does a change to one of its lights, after which Hue itself no longer calls the scene active (a dim of the whole room or zone leaves it active); they go off when its room or zone is turned off (from anywhere, including the Hue app), or when another scene of the same room or zone, or of one sharing a lamp with it, is played. Pick This scene where a keypad should show which scene is playing. A keypad that shows something other than the tile is a deliberate departure from how a Control4 Advanced Lighting scene tracks (the default keeps a wall switch showing whether its room is lit); This scene gives the device one state again. As on a Hue Light, a tap within a second after a hold on the same button is ignored, and the click and hold rates and the LED colours are the device's keypad settings (the note above).

A time-of-day scene on a keypad (9.11): a tap on top starts its schedule with the current time slot's look, a tap on bottom stops it and turns the room or zone off, and a toggle button alternates, each at once: Hue cannot fade a time-of-day scene in or out, so its taps have no fade for a later tap to stop and the click rates do not apply. A second tap within about 0.6 seconds is still part of a double tap and does nothing. A hold while it runs brightens or dims the room or zone in steps of a second, as described above, and the schedule keeps running; when the next time slot starts, Hue plays that slot's scene (whether it then also replaces the dimmed level had not been watched on the house system as this is written). Held while the keypad reads it off, the top or toggle button starts the schedule and then brightens from the time slot's own level rather than from 1 %, because Hue cannot start one at a chosen brightness; the bottom button does nothing. Its LEDs follow Keypad Follows as for any scene: with This scene they show the scene on while Hue runs the schedule and off as soon as Hue stops it, for whatever reason; with Room/zone lights they also show on while any light of its room or zone is lit.

The keypad's LEDs: the driver tells every connected keypad button whether the light is on, or for a scene whether the keypad reads it on (Keypad Follows), whenever that changes, including changes made in the Hue app. A top button is lit blue while the device is on, a bottom button is lit blue while it is off, and a toggle button is lit blue while it is on; blue is the default, and the colours are among the keypad settings (see the note above). The switch's own LED Behavior decides whether the button follows the device: Push/Release only lights it while pressed, so pick the switch's option that follows the connected device.

Advanced Lighting Scenes

Hue Lights and Hue Scene devices can be members of Control4's own Advanced Lighting Scenes (the Lighting Scenes agent). A lighting scene sets a Hue Light's level, and its colour or colour temperature where the light supports them, with the scene's ramp time; a Hue Scene is a dimmer member with a level: while the scene is not on, also while another scene lights its room, a level above 0 plays the Hue scene at that brightness (100 % plays it as designed); while it is on, a level brightens or dims the room or zone to it, keeping the scene's look; 0 turns the Hue room or zone off. A time-of-day scene as a member starts its schedule for a level above 0 while it is off, and then moves the room or zone to the level (100 % from off simply starts it); 0 stops it and turns the room or zone off (9.11). A "stop ramp" from a lighting scene stops a Hue Light in mid-fade where it is, as a keypad tap does (above; not watched on the house system as this is written). It stops a Hue Scene in mid-fade the same way when a keypad tap started the fade, each lamp keeping its own level; during a fade that the lighting scene itself started, the scene device reports the last level it knew, and the next report from the bridge corrects the tile.

Load groups

Hue Lights can be members of a Control4 Load Group, Control4's own way to switch and dim several lights as one in programming, on keypads and on Navigators. A single light and a zone light can each join. A Hue Scene device cannot: a scene is not a load, and Advanced Lighting Scenes are the way to combine scenes. A load group can mix Hue Lights with other Control4 lights.

  1. After an update from 1.4 or earlier, reboot the controller from System Manager once (chapter 3): the update changes what a Hue Light can do in Control4, its capabilities, and Composer offers Hue Lights in a load group only after that reboot.
  2. In Composer, add a Load Group to a room and pick its members, as for any Control4 lights.
  3. Each member's Load Groups row (chapter 10) then names the group.

What each member does. Every member follows the group's on, off, level, ramp and hold commands the way it follows its own. Each reports its own level, so its tile, its own keypad LEDs and its events follow it as usual; the Load Group's own level and LEDs are Control4's. A tap on a member's own keypad stops that member's ramp, also one the group started, as on a Control4 dimmer. The group's stop does not end a hold on the member's own keypad. A group command names its level, so a member's own Default On Brightness does not apply to it. A member that cannot act (not bound, gone or offline) drops the group's command, as it drops any other, and its Status says why.

Brightness only. Control4 has no colour command for a load group, so a load group sets levels only. A colour light in a load group dims and switches like a white one, and the group leaves its colour alone. A lamp the group turns on from off comes on in the same colour as when a level from programming turns it on. Set colours on the devices themselves or with an Advanced Lighting Scene.

What moves together. The bridge collects the requests of a group's Hue members for one command and sends them together, within about 0.15 seconds of the first. Then:

  • Lamps that make up a whole Hue room or zone, every lamp of it in the group and all asked for the same change, get one request for that room or zone. They move together. While commands come faster than Hue takes them for a room or zone, about one a second (a slider dragged on the group's tile, say), those lamps get one request each instead.
  • A zone light in the group is always one request for its whole zone or room. Its own lamps, when they are members too, ride along in that request.
  • Every other lamp gets a request of its own. These leave one after another, a few hundredths of a second apart, because the controller sends one request at a time to the bridge and Hue has no request for an arbitrary set of lamps. A ramp hides it. An instant change can show it: up to about 0.4 seconds across 10 lamps, about 1.3 seconds across 20.
  • A held button always moves the lamps one by one, each at the hold rate, because Hue ignores the time of a dim for a whole room or zone ("How a hold moves a room or zone", above). The first 10 lamps start and stop at once; more lamps may wait their turn at Hue's pace for single lights, ten requests a second. A zone light in the group holds as it does on its own keypad.

For lamps that should always move as one, make a Hue zone of exactly those lamps in the Hue app. The lamps in the load group then make up that whole zone, so they get one request; or add the zone as a zone light (chapter 7). The driver never makes Hue zones by itself.

Keep loads in sync. A Load Group can keep its loads in sync. The Hue Light records that setting and shows (in sync) after the group's name in its Load Groups row. Keeping the loads together is the Load Group's job: neither the Hue Light nor the bridge passes a change at one member on to the others, also not a change made in the Hue app, so Control4 stays in charge.

Removing and deleting. Removing a light from a load group, or deleting the group, clears that group from the light's Load Groups row. A group that is gone but still listed reads device 1234 (not found); it does no harm. The memberships outlast a driver update, a Director restart and a controller reboot.

With Debug Mode on, the bridge's Lua output shows one line per group command, for example load group 1234: 6 lights in 40 ms: Kitchen (room) as 1 request, 2 lights alone. On the house system Composer offered the Hue Lights, and not the Hue Scenes, to a load group (2026-11-10); a group's commands had not been watched there as this is written.

Whole-room and whole-house commands

A room Off, a house Off or a lighting scene sends one request to the bridge per Hue light, and the driver paces them as Hue asks of every app: the first ten at once, then ten a second. A room with many Hue lights therefore takes a moment to finish, about a tenth of a second for every light beyond the first ten, the lights going off one after another rather than all at once; a room with a handful of lights is done within a second. A Control4 load group does better: the bridge sends each whole Hue room or zone among its lamps as one request, and the rest one per light ("Load groups", above). The one-request way to switch a whole Hue room or zone is a zone light (chapter 7): its on, off, level and colour each reach the bridge as one request for the whole room or zone, however many lamps it holds, so in programming, in Advanced Lighting Scenes and on keypads address the zone light rather than the lamps one by one. (A Control4 room Off still sends its command to every light device in the room, the zone light included, so it saves nothing there.) A Hue Scene device's OFF is one request for the whole room or zone as well (9.4). Several zone lights or scene devices turned off at once send one request each: the first three at once, then about one a second.

After adding devices

After importing lights or adding scenes, choose File » Refresh Navigators in Composer so the touch screens and the app show the new tiles. Renaming or moving a device needs the same refresh to show on Navigators.

Things that look wrong but are not

  • Event Stream shows a new "Connected since" time now and then, and its reconnect count rises. The watchdog (after ten quiet minutes) and, on Web transfer, the stream timeout (once an hour) reopen the feed on purpose; every reopening is a full read. Every minute instead: the feed is on Web transfer and shares the commands' queue (Stream Address, chapter 5).
  • Lights shows "5 imported" with nothing else right after a restart. The bridge has not been read yet; the full line returns within seconds of Connected since.
  • Bridge IP Address changed by itself. The driver found the paired bridge at a new address and followed it; Last Action, and for a day Attention, say from where to where (chapter 4). Reserve the address in the router. A move you cannot explain: check the address in the Hue app (chapter 4, "What the follow-up trusts").
  • Debug Mode is Off again although it was set On. It turns itself back Off 8 hours after it was set to On, on each of the four drivers, and that device's Last Action says so (chapter 5). Set it On again if support still needs it.
  • A device was renamed by itself in Composer, or a lamp in the Hue app. Someone renamed it on the other side; the driver keeps the names the same (8.2). Last Action says which way it went.
  • A gone device works again without a click. Its bulb or sensor came back with the same radio and the driver re-linked it (8.3).
  • Attention lists dozens of new lights after pairing. Those are the lights you have not imported yet. Import the ones you want and the rest stay listed; that is a reminder, not an error.
  • A property you changed jumped back. You did not click the row's Set button (chapter 4).
  • Events Received climbs once a second. Something on the Hue side keeps changing (a dynamic scene playing, for example), and every bundle of changes the bridge reports counts.
  • A scene tile stays ON after its lights were dimmed or changed in the Hue app. A scene reads on until its room or zone turns off or another of its scenes is played; the tile shows the new percentage instead (9.4).
  • A time-of-day scene's tile went off although its lights are still on. Hue stopped the schedule without turning the lights off, for example because another scene of its room or zone was played; the lights keep that scene's look (9.11).
  • An ordinary scene's tile turns on and off by itself during the day. A running time-of-day scene plays that scene in one of its time slots; its tile reads on while that slot plays (9.11).
  • A room dimmed during a time-of-day scene changed brightness by itself. Check whether a new time slot began then: Hue plays that slot's scene as it starts (9.11), and whether that also replaces a dimmed level had not been watched on the house system as this is written.
  • A tile showed the level you tapped for a few seconds, then went back. The bridge did not take the command: it had stopped answering a moment before (Event Stream reads Disconnected), and the driver had not noticed yet. The command is tried again for 30 seconds; if the bridge takes it, the tile follows (chapter 4).
  • Every Hue tile is greyed out at once. The bridge has not answered, or has refused the pairing, for 5 minutes, and every Hue device is offline: Attention says why. The tiles come back by themselves at the bridge's first answer (chapter 4).
  • Online is blank on a working light. A light connected over Wi-Fi or Matter never reports reachability.
  • A zone light shows a colour that only one of its lamps has. Hue reports no colour for a zone, so the tile shows the first lit lamp's colour (chapter 7).
  • A zone light's lamps lag a fast slider drag. Hue takes about one command a second for a zone or room; the lamps end where the slider ends (chapter 7).
  • ON on a zone light set every lamp to one level instead of their previous levels. That is what ON means on a Hue Light with On Mode Preset, the default: the On preset, the zone light's Default On Brightness (100 % unless changed). Set the zone light's On Mode to Previous to bring every lamp back at its own level; use the slider, a scene or the Hue app for other levels (chapters 7 and 11).
  • A Hue Light, Hue Scene or Hue Motion Sensor reads Not bound to a Hue Bridge right after a restart. After a Director restart or a controller reboot this can last up to a minute while the device looks for its connection again (8.7).
  • A Hue Light or Hue Scene shows Control4's standard bulb icon. In a room's List View they always do; in the tree, a device created by a build without the Hue icons keeps the bulb (chapter 1).

The TLS check

The driver talks to the bridge over an encrypted connection (TLS) and tells the controller to accept only a bridge whose certificate comes from Signify, the maker of Hue: Signify's two root certificates ship with the driver. That is what keeps the pairing from being handed to a device that merely pretends to be your bridge. At every start of the driver and after a change of Bridge IP Address, once the bridge has confirmed its identity and before the first request that carries the pairing, the driver checks that the controller really applies this rule: it asks the bridge one question while telling the controller to trust only a decoy certificate made for this check, which no bridge's certificate matches, so the controller has to refuse the connection. While all is well you see nothing of it.

A refusal proves two things: the controller reads the driver's certificates, and it does not accept the bridge without them. It cannot prove that the controller has also stopped trusting the public certificates it keeps for websites, because those refuse the bridge's certificate too. On OS 3.4.2 the developer verified that it has, with a test against a public website that only a development build can make (the CA pinning check in its Diagnostics list), and repeats that test after a controller OS upgrade.

If the controller accepts the connection anyway, its software is not applying the driver's certificates, so that protection is not in effect. The driver then stops sending anything that carries the pairing, Status reads TLS check failed: nothing is sent (see Attention) and Attention reads TLS check failed: the controller accepted the bridge without its Signify roots; keyed requests stopped (see the manual): the lights and scenes no longer follow the bridge and their commands do not reach it, but nothing in the project is changed or lost. The likeliest cause is a controller OS version the driver has not been tested on (it was written and tested on OS 3.4.2). There is nothing to set: the check has no property and cannot be switched off. Report it as an issue at github.com/corytron/c4-hue-driver/issues, with the controller's OS version and the bridge's Lua output from the driver's start (with your own addresses replaced). The stop holds until the next start of the driver or a change of Bridge IP Address, when the check runs again; no check in between lifts it, and a connection check sends nothing meanwhile.

If the check gets no answer at all (a timeout or a connection error), it proves nothing either way, and a device on the network that wanted to get past the check could always make it go unanswered. Whether the controller applies the driver's certificates depends on its software, not on the network, so the driver remembers on which controller software version (the Director version) the check last passed. On that version an unanswered check blocks nothing: the Lua output notes it and the driver checks again the next time it confirms the bridge's identity. On a version that has not passed it yet, as at the first start after an OS update, nothing that carries the pairing is sent: Status reads TLS check inconclusive (…): nothing is sent; checking again in 30 s with the reason in the brackets, the lights and scenes do not follow the bridge, and the driver asks again every 30 seconds. As soon as the bridge answers the check, everything resumes by itself. If it lasts, check that the bridge is on and reachable (chapter 4).

The Diagnostics action (normally not needed)

Diagnostics gathers what support may ask you to run. Its one row, What, lists, harmless first (the dialog remembers the last pick). Each entry first writes Last Action …: started, naming the row its result shows in where that is another row: Check the connection now: started (the result shows in Status).

  • Check the connection now: asks the bridge one question over the normal connection and reports in Status (chapter 4, "Checking the connection"). The first thing to run when lights do not respond: a Test OK means the controller reaches the bridge and the pairing is good. It sends the pairing only to a bridge whose identity is confirmed and only when the TLS check allows it, and makes those checks first when needed; a check that succeeds also ends a Key rejected stop (8.8).
  • Read everything from the bridge now: a full read of the lights, scenes, zone lights, motion sensors and Hue automations, whether or not the event feed is connected, with the four list summaries in Last Action: Refreshed from the bridge: Lights: 8 lights on bridge, 8 imported, 0 new | Scenes: … | Zone lights: … | Motion: …, or Read everything from the bridge: … with what failed. The driver does this by itself behind the feed (chapter 1).
  • Reconnect the event stream: closes the feed and opens it again (Event Stream shows it). It does not reopen the feed while Status reads Key rejected or TLS check failed; Event Stream then reads Not connected: key rejected or Not connected: TLS check failed.
  • Stop the event stream until the next start: leaves Event Stream at Stopped and the lights frozen at their last state until you reconnect it or the driver starts again.
  • Log motion diagnostics (10 minutes): writes what the bridge reports about motion to the Lua output, for a report about motion sensors (14.15).

An empty What answers Nothing run: pick what to run under What first, and a remembered entry this build lacks Nothing run: "…" is not a diagnostic of this build. A release build lists exactly these five entries; a development build adds a few starting with (dev), which mark it as one (chapter 3, "Release and development builds").

Debug Mode and Stream Address are the developer's; leave them as they are unless asked (Debug Mode turns itself back Off after 8 hours, chapter 5). With Debug Mode On, every background job of chapter 1 also writes a line to the Lua output when it runs, for example housekeeping: stream_down_read (timer): OK: quick resync; 15 requests, 2.1 s: the job, what started it, its result, the requests it sent to the bridge and how long it took. A run that failed is written whatever Debug Mode says, ending in ; next try in … s when it is tried again.

13. Limits, the ten actions, and where the old ones went

Limits and what is not supported

  • One Hue bridge per project. A second bridge is not supported; a second Hue Bridge device should not be added (chapter 2).
  • A Hue zone or room becomes a controllable device only when you add it with Add From Hue, one at a time; there is no "add all zones", and Hue's whole-home group ("all lights") is not offered (chapter 7). A zone light shows one lamp's colour, not a mixture, because Hue reports no colour for a group; its ON sets every lamp to its On preset (100 % unless changed); a lamp that cannot do colour should ignore the colour it is sent (not seen on the house system as this is written, where every lamp has colour).
  • Hue automations are listed and can be switched off or on with a click, never deleted or changed otherwise; the set-ups of switches, motion sensors and MotionAware areas are changed in the Hue app only, and other apps' v1 rules, Hue Labs and voice-assistant routines are not seen (chapter 15).
  • Hue buttons, dimmers and switches and entertainment areas are not represented. Hue motion sensors and MotionAware areas are (chapter 14), with the limits of 14.16.
  • Hue cannot start a time-of-day scene at a chosen brightness or with a ramp time, so its keypad taps start and stop it at once, with no fade for a later tap to stop. A level below 100 % from off (the slider, programming, an Advanced Lighting Scene) therefore starts it with the current time slot's look and the driver then moves the room or zone to the level, so for a moment the lights show the slot's own brightness; a top button held while it is off starts it and brightens from there. A dim while it runs keeps the schedule running; when the next time slot starts, Hue plays that slot's scene (9.11), and whether it then also replaces the dimmed level had not been watched on the house system as this is written.
  • The bridge is found on the local network only: by mDNS, then by asking up to two blocks of 254 addresses (around its last address and around the controller's), never through the internet. A bridge on another part of a segmented network is found only by its typed address. IPv4 only. A DHCP reservation saves the search (chapter 4).
  • Names follow each other for lights, scenes and motion sensors only; Hue rooms, zones and zone lights keep their names on each side. Hue takes names of at most 32 bytes; a longer one waits for you (8.2).
  • Each list in an action's dialog takes one pick. Bulk adds are the All … lines and a room's folder of scenes in Add From Hue, and Import All New.
  • Hue asks apps for no more than about ten light commands a second. Dragging a slider quickly sends many requests; the driver keeps only the latest value per light and sends at most ten a second across all lights (after a first ten at once), so the light lags the slider slightly during fast moves and ends where the slider ends. A whole-room command takes about a tenth of a second for every Hue light beyond the first ten (chapter 11); a whole-room OFF through a Hue Scene device or a zone light counts as one request. For a zone or room as a whole Hue asks for about one command a second, so the driver sends the requests of zone lights, and a scene device's OFF and slider moves, at most one a second after a first three, keeping only the latest value: the tile moves smoothly meanwhile and a quick slider drag ends where the slider ends; a scene's ON is a request of its own and goes out at once (further ONs of the same scene within 0.3 s leave as one); a hold's start and stop go out at once, to each lamp when it moves them one by one, and a hold that moves in steps sends them one a second (chapter 11).
  • A keypad hold on a Hue Scene device, a zone light moves each lamp by itself while up to 10 lamps of the room or zone are on (all of its lamps, for a hold from off), and the room or zone as a whole in steps of a second while more are, or while a time-of-day scene or a scene with moving colours plays there, because Hue ignores the time given for a dim of a whole room or zone (chapter 11, "How a hold moves a room or zone").
  • A Control4 load group can hold Hue Lights, zone lights, not Hue Scene devices, and sets brightness only. Its Hue lamps move as one only where they make up whole Hue rooms or zones; other lamps get one request each, a few hundredths of a second apart (chapter 11, "Load groups").
  • Changes from Hue reach Control4 within one to two seconds: the bridge bundles its reports, about one bundle a second.
  • A command that cannot reach the bridge is retried for 30 seconds at most, then dropped; after a longer outage nothing old is replayed (chapter 4). It is not shown as done meanwhile, except one sent in the first seconds of an outage, before the driver has noticed it: that one can show its level for 5 to 15 seconds, until its request gives up, because Hue confirms a command only by answering it. After 5 minutes without an answer every Hue device goes offline until the bridge answers again; a bridge that lost its power while nothing was sent is noticed only once the event feed has been silent for Stream Watchdog (s), so in a quiet house that takes about 15 minutes (chapter 4).
  • The driver never creates, moves or deletes a Control4 device by itself: importing, adding and forgetting are clicks. What it does by itself, renaming a device to follow the Hue app, re-linking a device whose bulb or sensor came back with the same radio, linking a connected device that remembers its Hue identity and mapping rooms by name, is listed in chapter 1.
  • Devices are never moved between rooms by the driver; the Lights, Scenes, Zone Lights and Motion Sensors lists name the room of a device that sits elsewhere than its Hue room's (chapter 6).
  • Replacing the bridge itself is handled by pairing again; lights and motion sensors moved to the new bridge are re-linked by their radio, the rest through Link Device (8.8).
  • Needs Control4 OS 3.3.0 or newer; run on OS 3.4.2 and 3.4.3, not yet on OS 4 (chapter 3).
  • An update that changes a driver's capabilities takes effect only after a controller reboot from System Manager (chapter 3).
  • In a room's List View, Hue Lights and Hue Scenes show Control4's standard light icon; the Hue icons appear in the tree only (chapter 1).

14. Motion sensors and MotionAware areas

A Hue motion sensor, and a MotionAware area of a Hue Bridge Pro, can each become a Hue Motion Sensor device: an ordinary Control4 motion sensor in a room, with Control4's own motion programming, fed by the bridge. It reads Open while Hue reports motion and Closed when Hue reports that the motion has ended, and it fires Control4's standard senses motion and stops sensing motion events as it changes. A sensor also brings its light level, temperature and battery. The bridge creates each device from an action you click, as it does for lights and scenes: one sensor or area at a time with Add From Hue, or every new sensor at once with Import All New (14.3).

What has been seen with real hardware. The developer's house has a Hue Bridge Pro with a MotionAware area and no Hue motion sensor; the area has been watched reporting motion to its device (14.13). An outside tester's Hue motion sensors, on a Hue Bridge v2, reported motion, light level and temperature through these drivers during the 1.1 betas. Beyond that, this chapter describes what the drivers are built to do: where it says expected, it has not been seen yet, and a report helps (14.15). The Control4 side can be tried without a sensor (14.6, Test Motion Programming).

14.1 What a motion device is

Each Hue motion sensor (indoor or outdoor) and each MotionAware area you choose becomes one Hue Motion Sensor device in a Control4 room. The device is the Control4 motion sensor: Control4 gives it the standard motion sensor's icons, its contact rows on the Properties tab (Invert Sensor, the debounce settings, State and Verified), its programming, and a place in the Control4 app under Security, Locks & Sensors (expected, with the history of its motion). There is nothing to connect: Control4 joins the motion sensor part to the driver by itself, and the bridge's action connects the device to the bridge. The driver's own rows follow Control4's on the Properties tab, under Advanced Properties.

Besides motion, a sensor's device shows the sensor's light level, temperature and battery, whether Hue can reach the sensor (Online) and whether it is switched on in the Hue app (Enabled), each as a row, a variable, events and a conditional of its own; its temperature also leaves on an output that a Temperature Display or a thermostat can use (14.8). An area senses motion only: its device hides those rows and leaves their variables at 0.

The driver only reads, with one exception: names. A rename of the device in Composer renames the sensor or the area in the Hue app, and a rename there renames the device (14.12, 8.2). It never switches a sensor or an area on or off, never changes a sensitivity or an area, and never touches a Hue automation: all of that stays in the Hue app. Hue's own automations for a sensor or an area keep running beside Control4's programming; neither stops the other.

14.2 Installing the motion driver

Motion devices run the fourth driver file, hue_motion.c4z, which comes with the other three in every release. Install all four at once through Driver » Add or Update Driver or Agent…, in place, as chapter 3 describes. The motion driver does not change what any device type can do in Control4, so Composer is not expected to ask Restart Director? (if it does, reboot the controller as chapter 3 says). As with lights and scenes, do not add a Hue Motion Sensor from the Items pane: the bridge creates each one, connected and linked (a device added by hand: 14.12).

After the install the bridge shows a new row, Motion Sensors (14.5). Motion devices need no actions of their own on the bridge: Import All New, Add From Hue, Link Device, Forget Device and Diagnostics cover them as they cover lights and scenes (chapter 13).

14.6 What programming gets

From Control4's motion sensor, the same as on any other:

  • The events senses motion (the sensor turned Open) and stops sensing motion (it turned Closed).
  • The conditionals is sensing motion, is not sensing motion and State is Verified, and the loops built on them.
  • The variables ContactState, StateVerified and LastActionTime. Control4 counts a closed contact as true, so ContactState is expected to read true while there is no motion (reported by others, not yet seen here); the conditionals say it plainly.
Leave Invert Sensor unticked. The driver reports motion as Open, as Control4's own motion sensors do; ticking Invert Sensor on the device's Properties tab would turn motion into Closed and the two events round. The debounce settings next to it can stay as they are.

From the driver, on the same device:

Variables: Online and Enabled (true or false), Light Level (lux, a whole number, 0 when dark), Temperature C and Temperature F, Battery Level (percent) and Battery Low (true while Hue calls the battery low or critical). On an area the sensor-only ones stay 0, and Battery Low false. A variable keeps its last value when a reading is lost and is never set to a made-up one; until the bridge first reports, a new device's Online and Enabled are false and its readings 0. Conditionals: Online, Enabled in the Hue app and Battery Low (true or false), Light Level (lux) (0 to 100000), Temperature C (-40 to 60) and Battery Level (%) (0 to 100); the numeric ones compare whole numbers. Once released, none of these is removed, renamed or renumbered, so programming built on them keeps working through updates.

Timing. Hue reports the start of motion and its end separately. The end comes some seconds after the last movement, a time the sensor keeps itself (how long, per model, has not been measured here); while someone keeps moving nothing new is reported and the sensor stays Open. The bridge bundles its reports, about one bundle a second, so motion reaches Control4 within a second or two, and a movement shorter than that may be reported as its end only, changing nothing.

After a restart. Right after a controller or driver start the device shows Waiting for the bridge, sends nothing, and Verified is expected to read No. When the bridge reports, the state is set quietly, without an event, if it is the one the device last reported; if it changed while nobody was watching (someone walked in during the restart), the event fires once, late rather than never. State is Verified lets programming leave a sensor alone until then. After a lost event feed the same rule applies: what changed meanwhile is reported when the feed is back, while a whole visit that began and ended in between is never replayed (the bridge's Lua output notes it only while the bridge's Debug Mode is On).

Trying it without a sensor. The device's one action, Test Motion Programming, makes it read the opposite of what it shows and fire the matching event as if Hue had reported it: Open and senses motion when it shows Closed, Closed and stops sensing motion when it shows Open. Programming can so be tried without walking past a sensor, or without a sensor at all; Last Action says which state was sent. At the next motion report from the bridge, or after 30 seconds, whichever comes first, the sensor goes back to Hue's state, hold time included, and fires that state's event when it differs from the test's: 30 seconds after a test of motion on a quiet sensor it reads Closed again and stops sensing motion fires. Click it twice in a row to test both events. A device that knows no Hue state (no sensor, or not linked) goes back to what it showed before the first test, Closed if it showed nothing.

14.7 Occupancy Hold Time

Many installations want a room to count as occupied for a while after each movement, longer than Hue's own few seconds, which Control4 cannot change. Occupancy Hold Time (s), a property of each motion device, 0 to 3600 seconds and 0 by default, keeps the sensor Open that long after Hue reports the end of motion, and only then reports Closed and fires stops sensing motion. New motion during the hold cancels it; the next end of motion starts it again in full. The device's Hue Motion row always shows Hue's own state, so during a hold it reads, for example, No motion since 14:05 while the sensor still reads Open. Set the value and click the row's Set button; it applies from the next end of motion. A hold interrupted by a restart (a driver update, a Director restart or a controller reboot) goes on afterwards only for the rest of the hold time, as long as the bridge still reports the same end of motion; one that ran out meanwhile closes as soon as the bridge answers, and stops sensing motion still fires once. If the motion ended while the driver was not running, the hold starts again in full at the first report. A deliberate end (14.10) closes the sensor at once, without the hold. A failure (14.10) does not stop a hold that is running: Hue had already reported the end of motion, so the sensor still closes when the hold time is over. The default 0 reports exactly what Hue reports. A lights-off delay is better made in programming, with a Timer agent, so it can differ per room and per time of day; the hold is for a sensor that every program should see as occupied for a while.

14.8 The temperature output

A sensor's device has an output named Temperature in Composer's Connections view, of the kind Control4 thermostats use for remote temperature sensors. Nothing needs it: the Temperature row, the two temperature variables and Temperature Changed work without it. Two uses:

  • On Navigators. Add Control4's Temperature Display driver to the room (find it with the Items pane's Search tab; it comes from Control4's online driver database), then in the Connections view connect its Temperature In input to the motion device's Temperature output, by dragging as for any connection (chapter 3). The display is then expected to show the sensor's temperature on Navigators.
  • In a thermostat. Connect the output to a thermostat's remote temperature input where the thermostat offers one; Control4's own thermostat is reported to accept such a sensor. A Control4 thermostat is also reported to ignore a reading older than 15 minutes, and the device sends a reading when Hue reports a new one (every few minutes, or on a change) and again at every full read (chapter 1), so whether a thermostat keeps the value through a steady temperature has not been tried. Treat it as an experiment and report what you see (14.15).

What the output sends: the temperature in Celsius and in Fahrenheit, with the time the reading reached the controller, on every reading the bridge passes on (a new temperature, and the last one again at every read of the bridge) and when something is connected to it; once, when the sensor goes offline (also when the bridge has been offline for 5 minutes, 14.10), is switched off, deleted or forgotten, or the device is disconnected from the bridge, that the value is unavailable, which lasts until the bridge passes on a reading again (a changed temperature, or a read of the bridge), not merely until the sensor is back; and to a connected device that asks, the last reading or that it is unavailable. After a start nothing is sent until the first reading. An area's device never sends on it. Connecting and disconnecting are yours; the driver never changes a connection.

14.9 Light level and battery

  • Light Level reads, for example, 123 lux, or Dark when it is too dark for the sensor to measure; the variable Light Level holds the lux, rounded, and 0 when dark. Hue is expected to report the light level every few minutes, and sooner on a large change. Light Level Changed fires only after a move of at least 5 lux or 10 %, whichever is more, so that small changes do not keep programming busy; for a threshold ("when it gets darker than 20 lux"), test the Light Level conditional in a program run by that event.
  • Battery reads, for example, 85 % (normal) or 12 % (low): the charge and Hue's own word for it, normal, low or critical (where Hue's "low" begins is not published). The variable Battery Low is true while Hue says low or critical, the events Battery Low and Battery Normal fire when Hue's word changes between normal and low or critical (14.6), and the bridge's Attention lists a low battery at once: 1 motion sensor battery low: Hallway sensor 12 % (1 motion sensor battery critical: … when Hue says critical).
  • A reading that is lost keeps its last value, and so does its variable. While the sensor is offline (not reachable, deleted, forgotten or disconnected from the bridge, or the bridge offline for 5 minutes, 14.10) the row adds the time it went offline, for example 123 lux (old, 14:02) (for the bridge, the time its trouble began); a reading switched off in the Hue app, or flagged invalid by Hue, keeps its last value without a mark. A sensor without one of the three readings shows Not available on that row. After a re-link the old sensor's readings stay until the new one reports.

14.10 When a sensor or area stops reporting

A motion state is not like a light's level: a "no motion" that never arrives can leave programming waiting for hours. The driver therefore tells two kinds of ending apart.

  • Deliberate ends are someone's choice and nothing undoes them by itself: the sensor switched off, or the area paused, in the Hue app; the sensor or area deleted in the Hue app; the device forgotten by the bridge or disconnected from it. The device sends "no motion" once, at once and without the hold time, so stops sensing motion fires if the sensor was Open, and then reports nothing more.
  • Failures may clear by themselves: the sensor out of reach or its battery flat, the bridge away, an area not running. The device leaves the sensor as it is and says in Status that the motion is not current, so nothing in the house is switched off on stale information. Programming that must not act on an old state can check Online, and right after a restart State is Verified. Online also turns No when the bridge has been away for 5 minutes, so programming written for a sensor fault, on the Offline event for example, runs during a long bridge outage too.

14.11 A sensor replaced, deleted or forgotten

The rule is the one of chapter 8: the Control4 device is what your programming refers to, so it is kept and re-pointed, never deleted and re-created to fix something. Deleting a motion device deletes the programming that uses it, and Add From Hue then makes a new device with none.

A sensor replaced. A new sensor is new to Hue, with a new identity; the old one is usually deleted in the Hue app, or left there silent with a flat battery. The same sensor back (one reset and paired again, or every sensor after a reset of the bridge) keeps its radio, and the driver re-links the old device to it by itself, as for a bulb (8.3): Last Action says so, and the device keeps its name, room, connections and programming. A new sensor in place of the old one has its own radio; when it carries the old one's name in the same Hue room (the same room or zone or, after a reset of the bridge, one of the same name) the bridge points that out in an item of its own: Attention reads 1 new motion sensor on the bridge may be a replacement: Hallway sensor [Hallway] #31 (looks like Hallway sensor, device 1251; re-link?) (Link Device) ((same radio as … when the radio matches but the driver did not re-link by itself; 2 new motion sensors on the bridge may be replacements: … for more). Do not add it as a new device (Import All New skips it for that reason); point the old one at it:

  1. Open Link Device. A sensor's device can be re-linked only to a sensor, and an area's device only to an area: Not re-linked: device 1251 is a motion sensor; pick a sensor (… is a motion area; pick an area). Nothing re-creates it by itself, the driver never re-links it by its radio, and Import All New skips it and says so.

14.12 Names, rooms, a device added by hand, restarts and restores

  • Names. A device is named after its sensor or area when it is created, and from then on the two names follow each other as for lights (8.2): rename the device in Composer and the sensor or area is renamed in the Hue app, rename it there and the device follows. A sensor's name is the name of its Hue device; its light level and temperature carry none. What cannot follow is listed in Attention and settled with Resolve Names, as for lights.
  • Rooms. Move a motion device to any Control4 room; nothing changes for the driver. Its Hue Room row follows the Hue app.
  • A device added by hand. A Hue Motion Sensor added from the Items pane is bare: it reads Not bound to a Hue Bridge, its test action works, and nothing else does. Connected in the Connections view (its Hue Bridge input to the bridge's Hue Lights output, which lights, scenes and motion devices share), it reads Bound; waiting for the bridge; rename it to the exact name of a sensor or area that has no device, and the first line of Link Device links it to that sensor or area when exactly one has the name (8.6). A device that remembers its sensor (after a restore, say) is linked the moment it is connected. Two devices that claim one sensor are listed in Attention, 2 devices claim Hue motion sensor Hallway sensor: 1251 and 1257 (Forget Device on one of them) (Hue motion area … for an area): forget one of them and delete it.
  • A Director restart or a controller reboot. Each device reads Waiting for the bridge until the bridge reports, then goes on as 14.6 describes; one may read Not bound to a Hue Bridge for up to a minute first, as lights do (8.7). Nothing to click. During a long bridge outage a device keeps Online No across a restart without firing Offline a second time, and fires Online when the bridge answers (14.10).
  • A project restored from a backup, or a new controller (8.9). Each device remembers its sensor or area and is linked again by itself when the bridge starts, or else by the first line of Link Device; the first report then sets the true state, firing the event once where it changed since the backup.
  • The bridge factory reset or replaced (8.8). After a factory reset the sensors have to be paired again in the Hue app and come back with new identities, and the areas have to be set up again: a sensor paired again keeps its radio, so its device is re-linked by itself, while the devices of areas set up again read deleted until Link Device points each at its area; an area set up again under its old name over a room or zone of its old name is suggested there (8.8). A bridge replaced through the Hue app's transfer may keep the identities; read Attention rather than assume.
  • The motion driver updated in place: every device keeps its sensor or area, its hold time and its programming, and reads Waiting for the bridge for a moment; a hold that was running goes on for the rest of its time (14.7).

14.13 MotionAware areas

MotionAware is the Hue Bridge Pro's way of sensing motion with Hue lights instead of a sensor. An area is set up in the Hue app, for a room, a zone or the whole home, with three or four compatible lights, and needs the room empty for a short calibration; a bridge has at most four. The driver cannot create, change, pause or resume an area; it only reports the area's motion, through a device made with Add From Hue (14.4), and keeps the area's name the same as its device's (14.12).

  • An area's device reads Kind MotionAware area and Model MotionAware area, and its Status names the area and its room or zone, for example MotionAware area Kitchen motion area in Kitchen, MotionAware area Upstairs motion in Upstairs (zone) or MotionAware area Home motion (whole home) (a sensor's reads, for example, Hue motion sensor Hallway sensor in Hallway). It has no light level, temperature or battery, and its Temperature output stays silent.
  • The area's lights must keep their power: one switched off at the wall leaves the area degraded (Status counts the lights that do not respond, for example Area degraded: 1 of its 4 lights not responding), and an area that stops running needs its lights powered and the area saved again in the Hue app (14.10).
  • Hue keeps two motion reports for an area, one for when it switches lights and one for when it is used for security, and both follow the same walk a few seconds apart; the device shows motion while either reports it, and no motion only when both report none, so changing the area's use in the Hue app needs nothing here. None of Hue's security features (alarms, alerts) is used.
  • Hue's own reaction to the area (lights that come on with motion, time slots) keeps running unless you stop it, and then Control4's programming acts beside it. To let Control4 alone decide what the lights do, make the area's Hue automation do nothing; the area keeps reporting motion to Control4 all the same (checked on a Hue Bridge Pro, 2026-10-26: the lights stayed as they were and the device showed motion within about a second). In the Hue app, open the area's automation (the app asks for one when the area is created); keep a single time slot for the whole day; set what happens on motion to Do nothing, and what happens when motion stops to Do nothing as well, since its default turns the room off after ten minutes; and save: the change reaches the bridge only once it is saved. Do not switch the area itself off, and do not remove its automation: either stops the area reporting motion. The bridge's Hue Automations row shows an area set up this way as Does nothing: Kitchen motion area (MotionAware), Control4 decides, and lists one whose automation still moves Control4 lights in Attention; the Hue Automations action then gives these steps (chapter 15).
  • A square Hue Bridge v2 has no MotionAware: the Motion Sensors row says MotionAware not supported by this bridge, Add From Hue lists no area, and an area's device on such a bridge (after a change of bridge) reads This bridge offers no MotionAware areas; Forget Device removes it and Online No, and Attention lists it: 1 motion area device on a bridge without MotionAware: Kitchen motion area (Forget Device). It cannot be re-linked on such a bridge (there is no area to point it at): Forget Device and delete it, or keep it for a later Bridge Pro.
  • A Bridge Pro with older software may report an area only in part, without its name, room or health: the device reads Hue reports this area only in part; update the bridge and still reports motion.

14.15 Reporting what a sensor does: the motion diagnostics

When a sensor or area does something unexpected, a report of what Hue really sends is the fastest way to a fix. The bridge's Diagnostics action, with Log motion diagnostics (10 minutes) picked, writes what the bridge reports about motion to the bridge's Lua output, whatever Debug Mode says: first one pass over every motion resource on the bridge (the resource types the bridge serves; every motion sensor with its light level, temperature, battery, radio and software update; room and zone motion and light level, and sensor groups; the MotionAware areas and their candidate lights; one line per room or zone with motion; the Hue automations; and the counts of cameras, contact sensors and tamper reports, which the driver does not use), then, for ten minutes, every report the bridge sends about them, at most 200 lines. 14.16 What is not supported

  • No changes to Hue but names. Switching a sensor or an area on or off, its sensitivity, creating or pausing areas and the Hue automations of sensors and areas stay in the Hue app; only a name follows a rename in Composer (14.12). The bridge can switch other Hue automations off or on with a click (chapter 15), never a sensor's or an area's.
  • No room, zone or home motion (the motion Hue reports for a whole room from all its sensors), no Hue sensor groups: one device per sensor or area only. The diagnostics log them.
  • No Hue Secure cameras, contact sensors, buttons, dimmers or switches as devices. The diagnostics count cameras and contact sensors.
  • No "add all" for areas: Import All New adds sensors only; areas come one at a time (14.4).
  • No re-link from a sensor to an area or back.
  • No history entries for battery or Online in the Control4 app; motion history, if the app shows one, is Control4's own.
  • Like the other drivers, the motion driver needs OS 3.3.0 or newer and has run on OS 3.4.2 and 3.4.3, not yet on OS 4 (chapter 3).

15. Hue automations

Control4 is meant to decide what the lights do. The Hue bridge keeps automations of its own, though: Wake up, Go to sleep, Lighting schedules, Timers, Coming home, Leaving home, Mimic presence, and what each Hue switch, motion sensor and MotionAware area does. When one of them moves a light that Control4 also programs, the two fight: Control4 turns a lamp off and a Hue schedule turns it on again. The Hue Bridge finds these automations, lists them, and says which ones move Control4's lights by themselves. Switching one off is always your click, it can always be undone, and the driver never deletes anything in Hue.

15.1 What the driver does by itself, and what it never does

  • It reads. At every full read of the bridge (chapter 1) the driver reads the automations, and the event feed keeps the list current between reads: a schedule made, changed or deleted in the Hue app shows on the row within seconds. It does not ask the bridge over and over.
  • It works out which automations move Control4 lights. A Control4 light here is a light with a Hue Light device, every lamp of a zone or room that has a zone light, and every lamp of the room or zone of a Hue Scene device. Devices that are not bound count; forgotten and deleted ones do not. A Hue Motion Sensor device changes no lights. The driver cannot read Control4 programming, so "has a device" stands for "Control4 controls it".
  • It tells you. Every automation is a line on the Hue Automations row (15.2); one that moves Control4 lights by itself is also on Attention (15.3).
  • It writes only on your click. The one change it can make in Hue is an automation's own on/off switch, the one the Hue app shows, and only when you pick that automation in the Hue Automations action (15.4), or another driver in the project runs that action (chapter 3, "Only drivers you trust in the project"). Never from an event, a read, a start, a timer or the removal of the driver; never a change to a sensor, an area, a switch or a scene; never a delete. It never switches an automation off again by itself, and never switches one on by itself, not even when it stops moving Control4 lights.

The automation stays on the bridge with all its settings and shows as off in the Hue app, where anyone can switch it on again. An automation that also moves lights Control4 does not control stops moving them too, and Last Action names them. With Every one … the driver goes one at a time; those that worked stay off, and the others are named.
  • Restore turns the automation on again as it is now, with any edits made in the Hue app meanwhile; the driver never writes old settings back. If it still moves Control4 lights, it is kept at once, so that Attention does not list it right after your own click; Stop keeping lists it again.
  • Keep on the Hue side is the driver's own note and is never sent to Hue. A kept automation leaves Attention until it starts moving other Control4 lights; it is then listed again, naming only the new lights. A Hue switch or a timer can be kept too, though they never go on Attention.
  • The record. What Control4 switched off, and what you keep, is stored in the Hue Bridge device. It survives an update of the driver in place, a Director restart and a controller reboot. It is lost when the bridge device is deleted and added again: the automations Control4 had switched off then show as Disabled outside Control4, and Restore still turns them on.
  • When the bridge says no. If the bridge refuses the switch-off, or accepts it and leaves the automation on, the driver notes that this automation can only be changed in the Hue app on this bridge software, and gives the Hue app steps from then on. After an update of the bridge's software the next click tries again. The driver never retries by itself.
  • When the driver is busy or the bridge is away. A pick while the driver is reading the bridge or running another action is refused: Busy: reading the bridge; try again in a moment. A pick while the bridge does not answer or rejects the pairing is refused too, and says that nothing was changed. Nothing is ever queued for later.

15.5 Hue app steps, where the bridge says no

The bridge lets other apps switch off the automations made in the Hue app's automations list (Wake up, Go to sleep, Lighting schedules, Coming home, Leaving home). It does not let them switch off what a Hue switch, a motion sensor or a MotionAware area does, and Mimic presence is started and stopped rather than switched off. For those the Hue Automations action writes nothing and puts the steps in Last Action instead. The Hue app's labels move between versions, so look for the nearest match:

  • A MotionAware area: Kitchen motion area is a MotionAware set-up the bridge does not let other apps switch off. In the Hue app open the area's automation, keep one time slot for the whole day, set On motion to Do nothing and When motion stops to Do nothing, and save. Do not switch the area off or remove its automation: the area would stop reporting motion to Control4. This is the recipe of 14.13, checked on a Hue Bridge Pro: the area keeps reporting motion to Control4, and the row then reads Does nothing.
  • A motion or contact sensor: Hallway sensor's behaviour is a sensor set-up. In the Hue app open the sensor, set every time slot to Do nothing on motion and when motion stops, and save. Leave the sensor on: Control4 reads its motion.
  • A Hue switch: Bedroom dimmer is a Hue switch. To stop it moving Control4 lights, open it in the Hue app and point its buttons elsewhere, or reset it and set it up in another app. Or keep it (Hue Automations ▸ Keep on the Hue side).
  • Mimic presence: Away lights is started and stopped in the Hue app: Automations, Away lights, stop.
  • A kind the driver does not know: Evening routine is a kind of Hue automation this driver does not switch off. Change or switch it off in the Hue app, where it is listed under its room.

Never switch a MotionAware area off, never remove its automation, never switch its light control off and never switch a motion sensor off to quiet Hue: each of these stops motion reaching Control4's Hue Motion Sensor device.

15.6 Living with it

  • Edited in the Hue app while Control4 has it switched off: the row adds ; changed in Hue since, and Restore turns it on as it is now and says what changed.
  • Renamed in Hue: the row and the list show the new name; Last Action may name both, for example Hall timer (was Hallway timer).
  • Switched on again outside Control4: On again outside Control4 and Attention (15.3). The driver never fights a person in the house.
  • Deleted in Hue, or deleted by the bridge with the room, scene or switch it needed: dropped from the record after the next full read that works, with a line in the Lua output. A read that failed marks nothing deleted. Restore never re-creates an automation.
  • A device added, imported, linked or forgotten in Control4: the automations are checked again at once, so a new conflict reaches Attention without a read of the bridge.
  • The bridge offline or rebooting: the summary ends in (as of 14:02), picks are refused, nothing is marked deleted.
  • Forget Pairing keeps the record, and its Last Action names what Control4 still has switched off, for example Control4 still has 2 Hue automations disabled on this bridge: …; pair again to restore them, or turn them on in the Hue app.
  • Before you delete the Hue Bridge device, restore what Control4 switched off (Restore ▸ Everything Control4 disabled). If it is deleted anyway, those automations stay off on the bridge, where the Hue app shows them and can switch them on; the Lua output names them at the removal.
  • A new bridge (a bridge replaced, or moved to through the Hue app): the old bridge's record is set aside, shown as one line, and offered for forgetting in Restore; automations that arrive switched off on the new bridge show as Disabled outside Control4. A bridge factory reset, or a pairing the bridge no longer accepts, keeps the record until the bridge is paired again.
  • A square Hue Bridge v2 has everything of this chapter except MotionAware, which it lacks.

15.7 What the driver cannot see

The driver reads what the bridge's current interface shows. Rules and schedules that older or other apps made in the bridge's earlier interface, Hue Labs formulas, and the routines of Alexa, Google Home, Apple Home and Home Assistant are not visible to it, which is why the row's last line names them; an empty list does not prove that nothing else moves the lights. Nor does the bridge say which automation changed a light: the row says what could move Control4's lights, never what did.

Glossary

Advanced Lighting Scene
Control4's own kind of lighting scene, made in the Lighting Scenes agent: a saved set of levels for several Control4 lights. Hue Lights and Hue Scene devices can be members (chapter 11). Not to be confused with a Hue scene.
Bound, bind, unbound
A device is bound when its Hue Bridge input is connected to the bridge's Hue Lights output in Composer's Connections view. The bridge's texts say bind it to this bridge in Composer for making that connection and unbound for a device whose connection was removed. Devices the bridge creates are bound from the start.
Button Link
The three connections of a Hue Light and a Hue Scene listed under the device's Control Outputs in Composer's Connections view, Top Button Link, Bottom Button Link and Toggle Button Link, to which the buttons of a Control4 switch or keypad are bound by a drag (chapter 11). The device then answers the buttons like a Control4 dimmer: tap for on and off (a ramp on a Hue Light, a fade on a Hue Scene, which a later tap stops; at once on a time-of-day scene), hold to dim.
DHCP reservation
A setting in your router that makes it hand the bridge the same address every time it asks for one, so Bridge IP Address stays valid and the driver never has to look for the bridge (chapter 4). The router's manual explains where; it is usually under the DHCP or LAN settings and needs the bridge's hardware (MAC) address.
Director, System Manager
Director is the program on the controller that runs the project and every driver in it. System Manager is Control4's maintenance tool for the controller, opened from Composer's Tools menu with administrator rights; the controller is rebooted from it after an update during which Composer asked Restart Director? (chapter 3).
Event feed
The bridge's running report of everything that changes, which the driver keeps open all the time; the Event Stream property shows its state and the bridge's texts call it the event stream. Each report is a bundle the driver's texts call a container.
Folder (in a list)
A line in an action's Select list that holds other lines, as a folder holds files: double-click its text to open it, and double-click its first line, Back to all folders or Back to Scenes, to go back (Composer's own Back works too, with a double-click on the word itself). Add From Hue, Link Device, Forget Device and Hue Automations sort their lists into folders (chapter 3, "How an action's dialog works").
Full read
The driver reading every light, room, zone, scene and motion sensor, and the Hue automations, from the bridge in one go: right after the feed connects and after an error reconnect or a long outage, and by itself behind the feed (chapter 1); a quick read leaves out the scenes. Import All New reads the lights afresh, and Diagnostics » Read everything from the bridge now everything. The Busy texts call it resyncing.
Gone, missing
The same state: a device whose light, scene, zone, room, motion sensor or MotionAware area is no longer on the bridge. The device's Status says Gone from the Hue bridge (or Scene deleted in Hue, Zone deleted in Hue); the bridge's Lights, Scenes, Zone Lights, Motion Sensors and Attention count it as missing.
Hue automation
Something the Hue bridge does to the lights by itself or at a press: Wake up, Go to sleep, a Lighting schedule, a Timer, Coming home, Leaving home, Mimic presence, and what a Hue switch, motion sensor or MotionAware area does. They are made in the Hue app and stored on the bridge. The Hue Automations row lists them and the action of the same name can switch one off (chapter 15).
Hue identity
The long code the bridge gives every light and scene; the driver links devices by it, so names and rooms can change freely. Hue Light ID and Hue Scene ID show its start. A light deleted and re-added in the Hue app is expected to get a new one, which is why such a device reads Gone and has to be re-linked.
Linked, link, re-link
A device is linked when the bridge knows which Hue light or scene it stands for. Linking is the bridge's bookkeeping, binding is Composer's connection; a device needs both. The driver links a bound device that remembers its Hue identity by itself, and a device whose bulb or sensor came back with the same radio; Link Device links the others and points a device at a different light, scene or sensor. Not to be confused with the bridge's link button, which is pressed for pairing.
Load Group
Control4's own grouping of lights, added in Composer, whose on, off, level and hold commands go to all its members. Hue Lights, zone lights can be members; Hue Scene devices cannot (chapter 11). Not to be confused with a Hue room or zone.
Lua output
The driver's log, shown in the lower part of the device's Lua tab in Composer. A few texts in this manual say "see Lua output"; it is for the developer, you only need to look when asked.
Mapping
The entry in Room Mappings that says which Control4 room a Hue room or zone stands for. It decides where newly created devices land and nothing else (chapter 6).
Navigator
Control4's word for the things you control the house from: touch screens, remotes and the Control4 app. A device appears there as a tile. File » Refresh Navigators in Composer makes them show newly added devices.
On colour, Dim colour, colour on mode
Control4's colour settings for every colour light: the colour a light turns on in, the colour it dims toward, and how the two are used (Preset, Fade or Previous). Chapter 11, "On Mode and colours".
On Mode
A Hue Light's keypad setting for the level it turns on at when no level is given: Preset (the default, the On preset) or Previous (the level it had when it went off). Chapter 11, "On Mode and colours".
On preset
The level a Hue Light, zone light goes to when its top keypad button is tapped, and, with On Mode Preset, when its toggle button is tapped while it is off, for Control4's ON command ("Turn On" in programming) and for TOGGLE while it is off: 100 % unless changed among the device's keypad settings, where Composer calls it the Default On Brightness (chapter 11; for a tap on the tile see chapter 11, "On Navigators").
Online, offline
A device's Online row: whether Control4 can use it now. A Hue Light or Hue Scene that is offline (its light without power, the device not bound, gone or forgotten, or the bridge not answering for 5 minutes) has its tile disabled on Navigators and ignores commands; a Hue Motion Sensor's Online reads No and its Offline event fires. Each comes back by itself when the cause ends (chapter 4, 8.1, 14.10).
Pairing
The one-time handshake in which the bridge issues a key to this controller after its link button was pressed (chapter 4). The driver stores the key on the Hue Bridge device and never shows it.
Radio
The Zigbee radio inside every Hue bulb and sensor, whose address (its MAC) is fixed in the hardware. A bulb deleted and added again in the Hue app, or after a reset of the bridge, gets a new Hue identity but keeps its radio, which is how the driver recognises it and re-links its old device (8.3).
Room, zone
In Hue a light belongs to exactly one room; a zone is a second grouping that may span rooms, used mostly for scenes. In Control4 only rooms exist; a zone's scenes go to the room the zone is mapped to. Either can also be added as a single Hue Light, a zone light (chapter 7).
Scene
A Hue scene is a saved look (levels and colours) for one Hue room or zone, created in the Hue app; Hue adds about seven defaults per room. The driver shows a scene as a Hue Scene device, a dimmer for its room or zone; playing it is what Hue calls recalling.
Set button
The button that appears to the right of a property row the moment you change its value; only it hands the change to the driver (chapter 4). The keypad settings of chapter 11 take a change with Enter instead.
Time-of-day scene
One of Hue's scenes that follow the time of day, such as Golden hours and Natural light; Hue calls them smart scenes. It belongs to one room or zone and plays an ordinary scene in each time slot of the day, moving on to the next slot's scene by itself. Add From Hue lists it with (time-based) after its name, for example Natural light (time-based); its Hue Scene device starts and stops the schedule (9.11).
Zone light
A Hue Light device created by Add From Hue from its zones and rooms that stands for a whole Hue zone or room rather than one light: one tile whose on, off, slider and colour go to every lamp of the zone or room in a single request; on while any lamp is lit, showing their average brightness and the first lit lamp's colour (chapter 7). A zone light is named after the zone, or after the Hue room with Lights added (Living Room Lights); the bridge's Zone Lights row counts them, and Link Device re-points one whose zone was deleted (8.10).

Glossary

1. What this driver does

This driver connects one Philips Hue bridge to your Control4 system over your own home network; no cloud account is involved and nothing leaves the house. Each Hue light you choose becomes an ordinary Control4 light that you can switch, dim, colour and program like any other light in the project, and so can a whole Hue zone or room: one light whose tile moves every lamp in it at once (chapter 7). Changes made in the Hue app or with Hue switches and dimmers show up in Control4 within a second or two, because the driver listens to the bridge's event feed (the bridge's running report of everything that changes) rather than asking it over and over. Hue scenes, the saved looks you create for a room in the Hue app, can become Control4 dimmers: turning one on plays the scene, turning it off turns that Hue room or zone off, and its slider brightens or dims the room or zone while keeping the scene's look. Hue's time-of-day scenes, such as Golden hours and Natural light, can become such dimmers too; turning one on starts its schedule (9.11). The driver never adds, renames, moves or removes a Control4 device on its own, with two small exceptions it tells you about: the one-time Map Rooms By Name it runs when it first sees the bridge's rooms (chapter 6), and the re-adoption of a device that announces a light the bridge already knows (8.6). Every other change is a button you click in Composer, and the driver reports in plain words what it did.

Two words this manual uses throughout: a light is a Hue light, the bulb, strip or lamp as the Hue app lists it; a Hue Light, with a capital L, is the Control4 device that stands for it. A Hue Light can also stand for a whole Hue zone or room; this manual then calls it a zone light or a room light. Navigators is Control4's word for everything you control the house from: touch screens, remotes and the Control4 app. A short glossary of the other terms is at the end of this manual.

What has no counterpart

  • The whole home as one light. Add Zone Light offers Hue zones and rooms, not Hue's "all lights" group. For one tile that moves every light in the house, make a zone holding them all in the Hue app and add that zone.
  • Hue motion sensors, buttons, dimmers and switches are not represented. Pressing a Hue dimmer switch still shows its effect on the lights in Control4, because the lights report their state.
  • Hue automations (the routines the Hue app runs by itself) and a Hue switch's time-based light are not represented; what they do to the lights shows in Control4 (8.1). A switch's time-based light is not one of the time-of-day scenes of 9.11.
  • Entertainment areas (light sync with screens and music) are not touched.
  • Several bridges. One Hue Bridge device per project is supported. Adding a second one is not prevented, but every light and scene can be connected to one bridge only; do not add a second bridge device.

3. Installation

Finding your way in Composer

Everything in this manual happens in Composer Pro, connected to your controller. The places it refers to:

  • System Design is the view Composer opens in (the view buttons are at the lower left: System Design, Connections, Media, Agents, Programming). Its left-hand tree lists the project's rooms and, under each room, the devices in it. Click a device to select it.
  • With a device selected, the pane to the right of the tree shows its tabs: Properties (the rows this manual quotes), Actions (the buttons), Documentation (this page) and Lua. The Lua tab's lower part is the driver's Lua output, the log a few texts in this manual ask you to look at; nothing else on that tab is for you.
  • The Items pane at the far right of System Design is where drivers are added to rooms; its Search tab finds a driver by name.
  • An action is run with one click on its button on the Actions tab. An action that needs choices first opens the Action Parameter List dialog (chapter 5): each row has a … button that opens a Select list; click an entry, click that list's OK (the inner OK, greyed out until you have chosen), and when every row is filled click the dialog's own OK (the outer OK) to run the action. Cancel on either closes it without doing anything.
  • To rename a device, select it in the tree, press F2, type the new name and press Enter.
  • To delete a device, select it in the tree and press the Delete key; Composer asks Are you sure you want to delete the item '…'? and deletes it on Yes.
  • To move a device to another room, press and hold the mouse button on it in the tree, drag it onto the room's name and release. Composer asks nothing.
  • The Connections view (its button is below the tree) shows, for the selected device, an upper pane titled Control & Audio Video Connections with the device's inputs and outputs, and a lower pane listing the devices that can be connected to the selected row. A connection is made by dragging a device from the lower pane onto the row in the upper pane.
  • After adding or deleting devices, choose File » Refresh Navigators so the touch screens and the app show the new rooms' contents.

What you need

  • A Control4 controller on OS 3.3.0 or newer, and Composer Pro. The driver was written and tested on OS 3.4.2; it uses nothing that is specific to an OS version, but OS 4 has not been tried.
  • A Philips Hue Bridge (the square version 2 bridge) or a Hue Bridge Pro, on the same network as the controller, with a fixed address: give it a DHCP reservation in your router (a setting that makes the router hand the bridge the same address every time). The driver does not search for a bridge that moved.
  • The bridge's address should contain a number below 8, such as 192.168.1.50 or 192.168.5.20. The controller keeps one connection queue per spelling of an address, and the driver gives the event feed a queue of its own by writing such a number with leading zeros (192.168.001.50), so that commands to the lights never wait behind the feed. On an address with no number below 8, such as 192.168.10.50 or 192.168.100.5, the driver still works, but the feed shares the commands' queue: Stream Address says shared queue, Stream Timeout (s) is capped at 60 and a command can wait up to a minute behind the feed. If your bridge has such an address, give it another one in the router. Addresses are IPv4 only: four numbers and dots, no name.
  • The three driver files: hue_bridge.c4z, hue_light.c4z and hue_scene.c4z from the same build. Each build has a number (116, 125, …) that the three files share and that the devices show as Driver Version once installed; the developer tells you the number with the files.

Adding the drivers to the project

  1. Copy the three files to the computer that runs Composer Pro, into any folder; Composer copies them into the project.
  2. In Composer, open the Driver menu and choose Add or Update Driver or Agent…. The Select Driver or Agent… file dialog opens; select all three files at once (hold Ctrl while clicking) and open them. Nothing needs to be selected in the tree first. Composer answers with, for example, Update Succeeded: Drivers or agents were successfully added: hue_bridge.c4z, hue_light.c4z, hue_scene.c4z.
  3. In System Design, select the room that should hold the bridge (a double-click in the Items pane adds to the room that is selected). In the Items pane, open the Search tab and type Hue Bridge. The search also finds Control4's own Hue drivers; the one you want is the Hue Bridge by manufacturer Philips Hue with the model text Hue Bridge (CLIP v2). Double-click it: one Hue Bridge device appears in the room.
  4. Do not add a Hue Light or a Hue Scene from the Items pane. The bridge creates them, already connected and linked, when you use its actions. The two drivers only need to be in the project's driver list, which step 2 did.

Updating to a newer build

Updates go through the same Driver » Add or Update Driver or Agent… menu: select all three new files at once, as for the first installation. Composer answers Update Succeeded: Drivers or agents were successfully updated: hue_bridge.c4z, hue_light.c4z, hue_scene.c4z and pushes the new version to every existing device: the bridge and every Hue Light and Hue Scene show the new Driver Version within a minute, the bridge restarts and its event feed is back within seconds, and the pairing, the lights and the scenes are untouched. That is all, unless Composer asks the question below.

If Composer asks Restart Director?, reboot the controller. Some builds change what a device type can do in Control4, its capabilities; build 160, for example, turned the Hue Scene from an on/off switch into a dimmer. Installing such a build makes Composer ask Restart Director?. Answering Yes does not restart Director: the driver whose capabilities changed keeps running its old code, and its devices keep showing the old Driver Version, until the controller itself is rebooted. To reboot it, open System Manager (in Composer, Tools » System Manager; it needs administrator rights on the computer), right-click the controller and reboot it. Composer loses its connection and is back a few minutes later (between two and eight minutes on the house system). Then check Driver Version on the bridge, on one Hue Light and on one Hue Scene, and choose File » Refresh Navigators so the touch screens and the app pick up what changed. For up to a minute after the reboot a Hue Light or Hue Scene may read Not bound to a Hue Bridge; it corrects itself (8.7). An update during which Composer does not ask the question takes effect at once and needs no reboot.
Never delete the Hue Bridge device to update or "reset" it. The pairing with the bridge and the list of which device is which light are stored on that device. A deleted and re-added bridge starts empty: you would have to pair again and every Hue Light and Hue Scene would read Not bound to a Hue Bridge until connected and linked again by hand (8.6). Updating in place keeps everything.

If you ever need to reload the same version, right-click the bridge and choose Update Driver; Composer asks Versions are equal. Do you want to overwrite…? and reloads it on Yes. For anything newer, use the menu route above. Neither route helps a device that kept its old code after a Restart Director? question; only the reboot does.

Going back to an older build uses the same menu with the older files. This has not been tested; expect the devices to keep their pairing and links as they do on an update, and read Status and Lights afterwards rather than assume.

Release and development builds. The developer sometimes makes development builds that fetch their code from a server on the home network; they carry two extra properties (Dev Server, Diagnostic Server) and three extra actions (Connection Test, Reload Credentials, Check CA Pinning). A release build shows none of them, so a bridge whose Properties page has a Dev Server row is running a development build and should be replaced with the release files through the menu above.

4. Pairing with the bridge

Step 1: find the bridge's address

Open the Hue app on your phone: Settings » My Hue system » tap the bridge. Its IP address is listed there, for example 192.168.1.50. Reserve that address for the bridge in your router if you have not done so; the driver has to be told by hand when the address changes. If the address has no number below 8, change it in the router first (chapter 3, "What you need").

Step 2: enter it in Composer

On the bridge's Properties page, click in the value cell of Bridge IP Address, type the address, for example 192.168.1.50, and click the row's Set button. Status changes to Not paired: press the link button, then run Pair Bridge.

The address must be four numbers from 0 to 255 separated by dots, written without leading zeros (192.168.1.50, not 192.168.001.050), and not a name. Before pairing, an invalid entry is simply ignored: Status keeps saying Enter the bridge IP address; correct the text and click Set again. While paired, an invalid entry is not harmless: the driver drops the stored address, Status returns to Enter the bridge IP address, the event feed stops (Event Stream reads Stopped) and the lights stop following the bridge until a valid address is set again. The pairing itself is kept, so a corrected address brings everything back within seconds.

The Set button rule. Composer sends a changed property to the driver only when you click the Set button that appears to the right of the value (together with Cancel) the moment the value differs. Pressing Enter or clicking somewhere else leaves the new value in the grid only, and the grid later jumps back to the driver's value. This applies to every editable property in this manual: Bridge IP Address, Debug Mode, the two stream settings, and a scene's Recall As.

A property row after editing: the Set and Cancel buttons at the right end of the row, and only Set hands the change to the driver.

Step 3: press the link button, then Pair Bridge

  1. Have Composer open on the bridge's Actions tab, then walk to the bridge and press the large round button on top once, briefly (the link button). The bridge accepts a new pairing for about 30 seconds after the press.
  2. Within those 30 seconds, click Pair Bridge on the Actions tab.
  3. Watch Status: it reads Pairing: contacting bridge, then Pairing: found BSB003 001788fffe123abc, requesting key (the model and the bridge's identifier; a square version 2 bridge reports BSB002), then Paired. Bridge ID and Bridge Model fill in. The pairing is a key the bridge issued to this controller; the driver stores it and never shows it.

If you clicked before pressing the button, or the 30 seconds had passed, then Status reads Press the link button on the bridge, then run Pair Bridge again; press the button and click Pair Bridge once more. If Status reads Enter the bridge IP address first, step 2 was skipped. Three failures start with Pairing failed: followed by the reason: Pairing failed: Bridge not reachable: … means nothing answered at that address (check the address in the Hue app and that bridge and controller are on the same network); Pairing failed: Pairing request failed: … means the bridge answered the first question but not the pairing request itself (try again; if it persists, check that the address really is a Hue bridge); Pairing failed: bridge error … means the bridge refused with an error of its own (try again after a moment, and after a restart of the bridge if it persists).

Right after Paired the driver opens the bridge's event feed. Event Stream shows Connecting, then Connected since 14:03:12, and from the first event onwards Connected since 14:03:12, 12 containers, last 14:03:40; a container is one bundle of changes the bridge sent, and Events Received counts the same bundles. Within a few seconds the driver has done its first full read of the bridge's lights, rooms, zones and scenes:

  • Lights reads, for example, 47 on bridge, 0 imported, 47 new, 0 missing, 0 unbound, 0 deleted and Scenes 125 scenes and 15 time-based on bridge, 0 imported, 0 missing, 0 unbound, 0 deleted.
  • Attention lists the new lights: 47 new lights on the bridge: Bar Bottom [Kitchen] #80, Bar Left [Kitchen] #81, Bar Right [Kitchen] #82 +44 more (Add Light or Import All New Lights).
  • Room Mappings fills in, because the driver runs Map Rooms By Name once by itself the first time it sees the Hue rooms (chapter 6).

Nothing has been added to the project yet. Chapters 6 and 7 are the next steps.

The Hue Bridge Properties page after pairing. Look at Status Paired, Bridge ID and Bridge Model filled in, Event Stream Connected since …, and the Lights and Scenes count lines. The exact Stream Address text depends on your bridge's address (chapter 5). Chapter 5 explains every row.

Test Connection

Test Connection on the Actions tab asks the bridge one question and writes the answer to Status: briefly Testing: GET /clip/v2/resource/bridge via 192.168.1.50, then Test OK: HTTP 200 via 192.168.1.50 in 0 s, bridge 001788fffe123abc. The three-digit numbers in these texts are the bridge's reply codes: 200 means the bridge answered properly, 401 or 403 that it refused the pairing key, 404 that the thing asked for no longer exists. Three failures are possible: Test failed: key rejected (HTTP 401 or 403). Re-pair with Pair Bridge (the bridge no longer accepts this pairing, see 8.8; the text shows whichever of the two codes the bridge answered), Test failed: bridge reports ID 001788fffe456def, expected 001788fffe123abc (a different bridge answers at that address) and Test failed: … with the network error (nothing answers). When not paired it says Not paired. The test leaves the normal connection alone.

Forget Pairing

Forget Pairing removes the stored pairing from the controller and stops the event feed. Status returns to Not paired: press the link button, then run Pair Bridge (the address is kept), Event Stream reads Stopped, Bridge ID, Bridge Model, Stream Address and Last Event are cleared and Events Received returns to 0. The Hue Light and Hue Scene devices stay in the project and keep their links; they simply stop following the bridge until you pair again. Pair with the same bridge and everything resumes (the links are expected to survive; this has not been exercised on the house system, so read Lights afterwards). Forget Pairing is never required: Pair Bridge simply replaces the stored pairing, with the same bridge or a different one, because the driver does not check the bridge's identity when pairing. It is a tidy way to make the bridge's rows blank before pairing with a replacement bridge; remember that after pairing with a different bridge every Hue Light and Hue Scene reads Gone until re-linked (8.8).

When the bridge is offline

If the bridge is unplugged, rebooting or unreachable then Event Stream reads Disconnected: transfer error 28: …. Reconnecting in 1 s (transfer error 28 is the controller's "no answer in time"; the reason varies) and the driver retries with a growing pause, doubling up to 60 seconds between attempts. As soon as the bridge is back, Event Stream reads Connected since … again and the driver does a full read of every light and scene, so Control4 catches up with whatever happened while it was blind. You do nothing. Meanwhile:

  • Hue Light and Hue Scene devices keep showing their last known state, and their tiles stay usable, because the driver cannot tell an unreachable bridge from a quiet one until it tries.
  • If you tap a Hue Light during the outage, the tile shows the level you asked for after about a second (the ramp time plus one second) although the light did nothing. The command itself is kept as "the latest wanted value" for that light and retried with a growing pause (half a second, doubling up to eight seconds) until the bridge answers, even hours later: when the bridge returns, the light goes to the last level asked for during the outage, unless a later command replaced it. The full read after reconnecting then corrects every tile.
  • A Hue Scene's ON (playing the scene) is one request and is not retried: the tile shows ON after two seconds and drops back to off a few seconds later, when the request fails; the scene is not played when the bridge returns. Its OFF, and a slider move while the scene is on, are kept and retried like a light's commands: the tile shows OFF at once, or the new level after the ramp time plus a second, and when the bridge returns the room or zone goes off, or changes level, unless a later command replaced it.

When the bridge's address changes

If the bridge receives a new address from the router then the feed drops and keeps failing: Event Stream cycles through Disconnected: …. Reconnecting in 60 s and Connecting, and Test Connection fails with a network error. Look up the new address in the Hue app, type it into Bridge IP Address and click Set. The pairing is kept, because it belongs to the bridge and not to the address: Status stays Paired and the feed reconnects within seconds. Then reserve the address in the router so it does not happen again.

Composer shows the device's driver as Hue Light, with the Hue Light icon.

What the tile does. The zone light is on while any lamp of the zone is lit, and its slider shows the brightness of the lit lamps as Hue reports it, their average in whole percents; 0 % while all are off.

  • ON (a tap on the tile, the top keypad button, "Turn On" in programming) turns every lamp on at 100 %, exactly as on a Hue Light; the lamps' own last levels are not restored (the Hue app does that).
  • The slider, or a level from programming or a lighting scene, sets every lamp to that level: 40 % means every lamp at 40 %, and the tile then shows 40. Differences between the lamps are levelled out; 0 % turns them all off. (A Hue Scene's slider moves its room or zone relative to where it is, keeping those differences, 9.4; a zone light is a light and keeps a light's promise.)
  • A keypad hold is Hue's own dimming of the whole zone: every lamp brightens or dims together and the differences between them are kept, as with Hue's dimmer switch; the hold stops where you let go. Held while the zone is off, the top button turns it on at 1 % and brightens from there; the bottom button does nothing. Taps, the toggle button and the LEDs behave as on a Hue Light (chapter 11); the LEDs show on while any lamp is lit.
  • Colour and colour temperature go to every lamp that can follow: a white-only lamp in a colour zone ignores the colour and keeps its level. A colour set while the zone is off is kept and applied at the next turn-on, as on a light.
  • Which controls the tile has depends on all the lamps together: the colour wheel appears if any lamp has colour, the temperature control if any lamp has colour temperature, and the temperature range is the one those lamps share (where they share none, the first lamp's). When lamps are added to or removed from the zone in the Hue app, the controls and the light count in Status follow within seconds (as this is written, a change of controls on a live tile had not yet been tried on the house system; look rather than assume).
  • The colour the tile shows. Hue reports on and brightness for a zone but no colour, so the driver shows the colour of the first lit lamp that has one (in the zone's own order), else the colour temperature of the first lit lamp that has one, and nothing while every lamp is off. The tile therefore follows colours set in the Hue app or by a scene; in a zone whose lamps differ it shows one lamp's colour, not a mixture.
  • One request a second. Hue allows about one command a second for a zone or room as a whole (ten for single lights), so the driver sends a zone light's commands at that pace, keeping only the latest value: a quick slider drag is sent as one request a second while the percentage on the tile moves smoothly, and the lamps catch up and end where the slider ends. A hold's start and its stop, and a tap right after a hold, go out at once.
  • A zone without lights. If every lamp is removed from the zone in the Hue app then the device reads Status Hue zone Bedside Lights has no lights; nothing to control with Online Offline, its tile is disabled and commands are dropped; put a lamp back and it is Online again, nothing to click. The zone light is not counted as missing, because the zone still exists (8.10).

A room light is the one-request way to switch a whole Hue room (chapter 11, "Whole-room and whole-house commands"), and the natural thing to put on a wall keypad. A zone light and the Hue Scene devices of the same zone work side by side; 9.4 says what each tile shows. 8. Living with it: renames, replacements, deletions, restores

Over the years lights get replaced, names change on one side or the other, devices get deleted by mistake, bridges get reset and projects get restored. This chapter walks through each case. The rule behind all of them: the Control4 device is the thing your programming refers to, so the driver keeps devices and re-points their links instead of deleting and re-creating, and it does nothing on its own that would make a later fix harder. What needs you is listed in Attention until you deal with it; nothing is lost by waiting.

8.1 Two-way state

Turn a light on in the Hue app, press a Hue dimmer switch, or let a Hue automation run: the Control4 light follows within one to two seconds (the bridge bundles its reports to one per second), and Events Received on the bridge ticks up. The other way round, a tap in Control4 reaches the light within about a second and the tile then shows the level Hue confirms. If you switch a light off at its wall switch (cutting its power) then Hue needs a while, sometimes minutes, to notice; the device's Online then reads Offline and the tile is disabled on Navigators (greyed out, it cannot be used) until the light has power again. When power returns the light comes back on its own and Online returns to Online. A light offline for more than a day is mentioned in Attention: 1 light offline for more than 24 h: Porch #53; that day is counted from the driver's start, so after a controller restart or a driver update it starts over. A light that connects to the bridge over Wi-Fi or Matter rather than Zigbee never reports reachability, so its Online stays blank; that is harmless.

8.2 A light renamed in Hue, or a device renamed in Composer

Names are never changed behind your back on either side. When the Hue name and the Composer name of a device differ, Attention says so. A device renamed in Composer is reported within a few seconds, because the bridge listens for Composer's rename. A light renamed in the Hue app is reported at the bridge's next full read (the event feed reopens every few minutes when the house is quiet, see Stream Watchdog (s)) or as soon as you run any action on the bridge's Actions tab; Room Audit is a harmless one to click for that. For a device renamed in Composer from Left Bedside Light to Left Bedside Lamp the line reads:

1 Hue name differs from the Composer names: Left Bedside Lamp is Left Bedside Light in Hue (Adopt Hue Names or Accept Names As They Are)

(With several differences it reads 2 Hue names differ from the Composer names: ….) You have three ways to answer:

  • Adopt Hue Names renames every differing device in Composer to its Hue name; Last Action reads Renamed 1 device: Left Bedside Lamp → Left Bedside Light (device 1142) | Scenes: No Composer names differ from the Hue scene names, or No Composer names differ from the Hue names when there is nothing to do. A device it could not rename is listed after a semicolon as not renamed 1: Left Bedside Lamp (device 1142): no proxy id known or …: rename failed: …; try again after a minute, and after a controller restart if it persists.
  • Accept Names As They Are keeps the Composer names and stops Attention from mentioning these particular differences: Accepted 1 name difference: Left Bedside Lamp (Hue: Left Bedside Light). The driver remembers the Hue name it accepted: if that light is renamed again in the Hue app the difference is reported again, but a further rename of the device in Composer is not.
  • Adopt Hue Name on one Hue Light device (its own Actions tab) renames just that device: its Last Action reads Renamed device 1143 to Left Bedside Light (the device's own actions use Composer's number for it, one higher than the bridge's, see chapter 5).

Scene devices join in: Adopt Hue Names, Accept Names As They Are and Link Devices apply to lights and scenes alike, so their Last Action always has two halves separated by | Scenes: , the lights' result first and the scenes' result after it, for example No Composer names differ from the Hue names | Scenes: Renamed 1 scene device: Read → Bedside Lights Read (device 1141). The driver never renames anything in Hue.

Refusals: Not re-linked: pick a device and a light (an empty row), Not re-linked: Porch #53 is already linked to device 2060 (the light you picked already has a device), Not re-linked: device 2051 is already linked to Porch #53 (the device you picked was re-linked meanwhile, usually a remembered choice; open the Device list again), Not re-linked: device 2051 is not a registered light (the device is not one the bridge manages) and Not re-linked: light … is not on the bridge (connect the event stream and try again) (the list was stale). The Device list says (no gone or unbound devices) when there is nothing to re-link.

8.4 A device deleted in Composer

If you delete a Hue Light device in Composer then the bridge is told at once: Lights ends in …, 1 deleted (after deleting most of them it reads, for example, 47 on bridge, 1 imported, 0 new, 0 missing, 0 unbound, 46 deleted) and Attention stays None, because deleting in Composer is a deliberate act. The driver remembers the deletion:

  • Import All New Lights skips that light on purpose and says so: skipped 1 deleted in Composer (Add Light re-creates one): Bar Left #58 (deleted 2026-10-22). A bulk import will therefore never quietly bring back a device you removed.
  • Add Light lists it at the end of its Light list as Bar Left [Kitchen] #58 (deleted from Composer 2026-10-22). Picking it creates a fresh device (a new device for Control4, so programming that pointed at the old one has to be redone) and clears the memory of the deletion.

If you deleted a device while the controller was off or the driver was not running then the driver finds out at its next start and treats it the same way, with one rare exception: when no Hue Light at all is left in the project at that moment the driver cannot tell a deletion from a missing answer and lists the device as 1 unbound device: … instead; Forget Light on it clears that.

8.5 Forget Light

Forget Light is the deliberate way to release a device from its Hue light without deleting anything. Its Device list holds every managed device as Vanity 3 #92 (device 1130); a device that is not in its normal state carries its state after the bracket, Porch #12 (device 2051) gone or Desk Lamp #12 (device 2043) unbound. On OK, Last Action reads Forgot Vanity 3 #92 (device 1130): unbound and left in the project for you to delete; the Hue light is listed under Add Light again. The device stays in the project with Status Forgotten by the bridge (was Hue light Vanity 3 #92). Delete this device in Composer, or bind it again and use Link Devices, its connection to the bridge removed and its tile disabled; delete it in Composer when you are ready. The Hue light is available in Add Light and counted as new again. The same can be started from the device itself with its Forget This Light action. An empty choice answers Not forgotten: pick a device first.

Which to use: delete the device without Forget when you want the light gone from Control4 for good (a bulk import will not bring it back); Forget and then delete when you want to import the light afresh, for example into another room; Re-link when you are replacing the light and want to keep the device.

The Forget Light dialog: every managed device as Name #number (device number); a gone or unbound device carries that word at the end of its entry.

8.6 Link Devices and the bare "Hue Light"

Occasionally a Hue Light device exists that the bridge does not manage: one added by hand from the Items pane, one that was Forgotten and that you want back, or every light after the bridge device itself was deleted and re-added (which this manual tells you never to do). Such a device reads Status Not bound to a Hue Bridge. (A device that is connected can read the same for up to a minute after a Director restart or a controller reboot; wait that minute first, 8.7.) The way in is a connection, and sometimes one click:

  1. Open the Connections view, select the device, and connect its Hue Bridge input (in the upper pane) to the bridge's Hue Lights output by dragging the bridge from the lower pane, HUE_BRIDGE Output Devices, onto that row. Scene devices use the same Hue Lights output.
  2. What happens next depends on whether the device remembers its light. A device that remembers which Hue light it was (one that was Forgotten, or one left over from a deleted bridge) announces that light the moment it is connected, and the bridge adopts it on the spot: its Status returns to Vanity 3 (Hue color lamp) and nothing needs clicking; Link Devices then only answers All 47 bound devices are registered, which is success. If that light was meanwhile imported as another device, the bridge adopts nothing and Attention reads 2 devices claim Hue light Vanity 3 #92: 1130 and 1144 (Forget Light on one of them); Link Devices says the same in its own words, … announces Vanity 3 #92, which device 1144 holds (Forget Light on one of them). Forget one of the two and delete it. If the remembered light is no longer on the bridge, Link Devices adopts the device anyway and ends its line with (not on the bridge: marked gone); it then reads Gone and 8.3 applies.
  3. A bare device, added by hand, remembers nothing and reads Bound; waiting for the bridge after the connection. On the bridge, click Link Devices: the device is matched by name to a Hue light that has no device yet, and the name must be exactly the same and unique. Last Action reads Linked 1 device: Vanity 3 (device 1034) to Vanity 3 [Master Bathroom] #92 by name | Scenes: All 2 bound scene devices are registered; with nothing to do, All 47 bound devices are registered, or No devices are bound to this bridge. A device adopted by its remembered identity is reported in the same way, with by its and the driver's word for the Hue identity in place of by name.

If the name matches nothing then Last Action reads not linked 1: Hue Light (device 1034): no unimported Hue light has that name; rename the device in Composer to the Hue light's exact name and run Link Devices again: rename the device in the tree to the exact Hue name and click again. If two Hue lights share that name, … 2 unimported Hue lights have that name; rename the device in Composer to one Hue light's exact name … (rename one of them in the Hue app first). If two bound devices carry the same name, … 2 bound devices share that name; rename one in Composer and run Link Devices again. Not linked: the bound-device lookup failed (see the Lua output) means Composer's connection list was not available at that moment; try again. Linking never moves the device: it stays in whatever room it is in.

The Connections view of a Hue Light, upper pane Control & Audio Video Connections. The row to look at is the device's Hue Bridge input under Control Inputs, whose Connected To column reads Hue Bridge->Hue Lights: it is connected to the bridge's Hue Lights output. Devices the bridge creates come connected like this; a hand-added one gets this row filled by dragging the bridge from the lower pane, HUE_BRIDGE Output Devices, onto it.

If you remove that connection on a managed device then the bridge counts it as 1 unbound, Attention reads 1 unbound device: Desk Lamp #12 (device 2043) (bind it to this bridge in Composer, or Forget Light) and the device reads Not bound to a Hue Bridge; connect it again and it resumes by itself, no click needed. A device connected to a second Hue Bridge device by mistake reads Bound; waiting for the bridge for ever; delete that second bridge rather than link anything to it.

If two devices both claim the same Hue light (possible after copying devices around) then neither is changed and Attention reads 2 devices claim Hue light Desk Lamp #12: 2043 and 2051 (Forget Light on one of them): Forget one of the two and delete it.

8.7 The bridge offline and back; a controller restart

Covered in chapter 4: the driver reconnects by itself and does a full read when the bridge returns. Expect Event Stream to read Disconnected: …. Reconnecting in … s meanwhile; nothing is needed from you unless the bridge's address changed. A bridge that reboots or updates its firmware closes the feed itself: Event Stream reads Disconnected: server closed the stream (HTTP 200). Reconnecting in 1 s and reconnects when the bridge is back. A light that updates its firmware needs nothing either; a capability it gains (colour, say) is picked up at the next full read.

After a controller restart everything reconnects by itself: the lights and scenes announce themselves to the bridge, Lights reads 47 imported alone until the first full read, and within seconds of Connected since every tile and colour control is back. Nothing to click. Right after a Director restart or a reboot the controller can be late in telling a device about its connection to the bridge, so a Hue Light or Hue Scene may read Not bound to a Hue Bridge at first although Composer shows it connected. The device looks again 5, 15, 30 and 60 seconds after it starts and greets the bridge as soon as it sees the connection, so the text corrects itself within a minute. Only a device that still reads it after that needs its connection checked (8.6).

8.8 The bridge factory reset, restored or replaced

  • Factory reset. If the bridge is reset to factory settings then it forgets the pairing: Event Stream reads Disconnected: HTTP 401 or 403. Reconnecting in … s over and over (one of the two codes, whichever the bridge answers) and Test Connection says Test failed: key rejected (HTTP 401 or 403). Re-pair with Pair Bridge. Press the link button and click Pair Bridge (Forget Pairing first is optional). The lights and scenes you then add back in the Hue app normally come with new identities, so after the first full read every Hue Light device reads Gone from the Hue bridge since … and you re-point each with Re-link Device To Light (the hints by name make each one quick, but it is one dialog per device; for 47 lights that is 47 dialogs) and each scene device with Re-link Scene. Your Control4 programming survives.
  • Restore from a Hue backup, or a bridge moved to new hardware with the Hue app's transfer. If the restore or transfer brings the same lights back under their old identities then the driver sees nothing wrong and links them again by itself; only devices reading Gone from the Hue bridge … need Re-link Device To Light. If the restored bridge no longer accepts the pairing, press the link button and Pair Bridge. Expect few re-links, possibly none; read Lights and Attention rather than assume. Note that a Composer project backup does not back up the Hue bridge; use the Hue app's backup for that.
  • A different bridge at the same address. If a replacement bridge answers at the old address then Test Connection reads Test failed: bridge reports ID 001788fffe456def, expected 001788fffe123abc. Press the link button and click Pair Bridge (Forget Pairing first is optional; it only blanks the rows); the devices then read Gone and you re-link each to its light as above. Taking a replacement bridge over automatically is not supported in this version.

8.9 A Control4 project restored from a backup, or a new controller

Where things are kept: the pairing key, the list of which device is which light or scene, the memory of deleted devices and the room mappings are all stored on the Hue Bridge device on the controller. Each Hue Light and Hue Scene device also remembers its own Hue identity, name and model. Whether the bridge's store comes back when a project is restored from a Composer backup, or moved to a replacement controller, has not been verified; the devices' own memories travel with the project. So after a restore or a controller replacement:

  1. Read the bridge's Status. If it says Not paired: press the link button, then run Pair Bridge, press the link button and click Pair Bridge. If it says Paired, nothing was lost on that side.
  2. Wait for Event Stream to say Connected since and read Lights. If it shows all your devices as imported, you are done.
  3. Otherwise click Link Devices: every connected device that remembers its light or scene is adopted by that identity (most are adopted by themselves the moment the bridge starts, so expect All 47 bound devices are registered or a Linked … line). A device reading Not bound to a Hue Bridge needs its connection made again first (8.6).
  4. Check Room Mappings; if it reads None; use Map Rooms By Name, click Map Rooms By Name and fix the rest with Set Room Mapping. Programming, rooms and names are Composer's and come back with the project.

8.10 A zone or room renamed, emptied or deleted in Hue

A zone light or room light (chapter 7) lives through the same cases as a light, with the zone or room in place of the light. The rule is the same: the device stays, its link is re-pointed, and nothing is dropped by itself.

  • Renamed in the Hue app. The device's Hue Name and Status follow; its Composer name stays yours, and Attention counts the difference together with the lights' (1 Hue name differs from the Composer names: …, 8.2). Adopt Hue Names, Accept Names As They Are and the device's Adopt Hue Name answer it as for a light; a room light's adopted name is the new room name with Lights added. Renamed in Composer, the device is reported the same way, and nothing is ever written to Hue.
  • Lamps added or removed. Nothing to do: the light count in Status, the tile's controls and the colour it shows follow the new membership (chapter 7). A lamp replaced, renamed or deleted in Hue changes nothing for the zone light; the lamp's own Hue Light device, if it has one, follows 8.2 and 8.3.
  • Emptied, then filled again. A zone with no lamps left reads Hue zone Bedside Lights has no lights; nothing to control with Online Offline and a disabled tile; it is not counted as missing, because the zone still exists. Put a lamp back and the device is Online again by itself.
  • Deleted in the Hue app. The device stays and reads Status Zone deleted in Hue since 2026-10-24; re-link or Forget (a room light Room deleted in Hue since …) with Online Offline and a disabled tile; the bridge's Zone Lights row gains , 1 missing and Attention reads 1 zone light missing from the bridge: Bedside Lights (device 1180, since 2026-10-24) (Re-link Zone Light or Forget Light). A zone deleted and made again in the Hue app has a new identity, so the device still reads deleted and the new zone is listed in Add Zone Light: re-link the device rather than add a second one. Two ways to answer:
    • Re-link Zone Light points the device at another zone or room, keeping its name, room, connections and programming. Device lists the zone and room lights, as Bedside Lights (zone light, device 1180, gone since …); Group is the same list of zones and rooms as in Add Zone Light. Last Action reads Re-linked device 1180 (was Bedside Lights) to zone Reading Nook; the device then describes the new zone, and Attention mentions the name difference until Adopt Hue Name or a rename settles it. A zone light cannot be pointed at a single light, nor a light's device at a zone: Re-link Device To Light is for lights only and does not list zone lights.
    • Forget Light, whose Device list holds the zone and room lights after the lights, releases the device: Forgot Bedside Lights (zone, device 1180): unbound and left in the project for you to delete. The device reads Forgotten by the bridge (was Hue zone Bedside Lights), with the usual instruction in its Last Action (Delete this device in Composer, or bind it again and use Link Devices); delete it in Composer. The device's own Forget This Light does the same. The zone, if it still exists, is listed under Add Zone Light again.
  • Deleted in Composer. The bridge remembers it, as for a light (8.4): Zone Lights gains , 1 deleted and Add Zone Light lists the zone with (deleted from Composer 2026-10-24); picking it creates a fresh device (programming that pointed at the old one has to be redone) and clears the memory. Nothing re-creates it by itself.
  • Moved to another Control4 room, or its connection removed. Moving changes nothing; Room Audit lists the device, like a light, when its zone or room is mapped to a different Control4 room than the one it sits in. A device whose connection to the bridge was removed is counted as unbound in Zone Lights and Attention; connect it again in Connections and it resumes by itself (8.6), because the device remembers which zone it stands for. That memory also brings it back after a project restore or a replacement controller (8.9): once connected it is adopted, Link Devices confirms it, and two devices remembering the same zone are reported in Attention for you to Forget one of them.
  • The bridge reset, restored or replaced (8.8): a zone light whose zone comes back under its old identity is linked again by itself; one whose zone has a new identity reads deleted and is re-pointed with Re-link Zone Light.

9.4 What the tile and slider do

  • ON (a tap on the tile, or programming turning it on) plays the scene on the bridge, the way the Recall As property says. The member lights take the scene within about a second and the tile shows ON with the room or zone's brightness. If the bridge does not confirm within two seconds the tile shows ON anyway; if the request then fails, the tile drops back, and the next full read corrects it in any case. A ramp time given by programming (or by an Advanced Lighting Scene) becomes the transition Hue uses to fade into the scene; a tap on a Navigator uses a short fade.
  • OFF turns the scene's whole Hue room or zone off, every light in it, imported or not, with one request to the bridge. The tile shows OFF. Remember that a zone can span several Control4 rooms, so OFF on a zone scene can turn off lights in rooms other than the one the scene device sits in. A zone that has no lights in it is reported off without any request.
  • The slider shows the brightness of the scene's Hue room or zone as Hue reports it (the average of the lights that are on, in whole percents), 0 % while the scene is off. Moving it while the scene is on brightens or dims the room or zone relative to where it is, keeping the scene's colours and the differences between its lights, as Hue's own dimmer switch does; 0 % turns the room or zone off. Moving it while the scene is off plays the scene at that brightness, all its lights at that one level; 100 % from off plays the scene exactly as designed, each light at its own level, as ON does. During a ramp (a keypad hold or a slow fade) the percentage on Navigators updates at most once a second.
  • When the tile reads on. The scene reads on from the moment it is played, from Control4, a keypad, the Hue app or a time-of-day scene's schedule (9.11), until its room or zone is turned off (from anywhere, including the Hue app or a Hue dimmer switch) or another scene of the same room or zone is played. Dimming or changing its lights afterwards (the slider, a keypad, a light's own tile, the Hue app, a Hue dimmer switch) does not turn it off, although Hue itself no longer calls the scene active; the tile shows the new percentage instead. An ON tile therefore means "this was the last scene played here and its room or zone is on", and programming that fires "when this scene turns off" fires when the room or zone goes off or another of its scenes is played. The on state survives a controller restart.

A time-of-day scene's device differs in a few points: it reads on exactly while Hue runs its schedule, and a level from off starts the schedule before moving to the level (9.11).

With a zone light of the same zone. If the zone also has a zone light (chapter 7), the two kinds of tile sit side by side and both follow the same reports from the bridge. Move the zone light's slider, or hold its keypad button, while a scene plays: both tiles show the new level and the scene stays on (its rule above). Turn the zone light off: Hue ends the scene, so the scene tile goes off and the zone light goes off. Turn the zone light on again: it comes on at 100 % on every lamp and the scene tile stays off, because on is a level, not a look. Play a scene, from anywhere: the zone light's tile goes on at the scene's average brightness and shows the colour of the first lamp the scene lit. A room light and the scenes of its Hue room behave the same way.

Editing a scene's colours, brightness or dynamics in the Hue app needs nothing in Control4; the next ON plays the new look. Copying a scene to another room in the Hue app creates a new scene; the device keeps following the original. A scene created in the Hue app appears in the Add Scene list by itself. All of this holds for time-of-day scenes too; in addition, deleting one of the ordinary scenes a time-of-day scene plays makes Hue delete the whole time-of-day scene (9.11).

9.9 Scene devices and Link Devices

A scene device that lost its connection reads Not bound to a Hue Bridge and is counted as 1 unbound, with Attention 1 unbound scene device: Relax (device 2061) (bind it to this bridge in Composer, or Forget Scene); connect it again in Connections (to the bridge's Hue Lights output, the same as a light) and it resumes by itself. A device that remembers its scene is adopted the moment it is connected, exactly as in 8.6; two devices remembering the same scene give 2 devices claim Hue scene Relax: 2061 and 2070 (Forget Scene on one of them). A bare scene device reads Bound; waiting for the bridge and is matched by Link Devices by the scene's plain name, Nightlight, not the zone-prefixed Bedside Lights Nightlight; after linking, Adopt Hue Name on the device adds the zone's name. The scenes' half of the Link Devices result reads Linked 1 device: … by name, All 2 bound scene devices are registered or No scene devices are bound to this bridge.

9.10 Programming with scenes

To Control4 a Hue Scene device is a dimmer. In programming, turn it on to play the scene, off to turn the room or zone off, or set a level to brighten or dim the room or zone (while the scene is off, a level plays it at that brightness, and 100 % plays it as designed); schedule it, put it behind a keypad button, or include it in an Advanced Lighting Scene next to other lights (the Hue lights themselves can be members too). Its on state follows the rule in 9.4, so a Hue scene played from the Hue app is visible to Control4 as the scene being on, and a dim in the Hue app as a new level. A time-of-day scene is a dimmer too: on starts its schedule, off stops it and turns the room or zone off, and its on and off events follow Hue's own word on whether the schedule is running (9.11). Chapter 11 has the details.

9.11 Time-of-day scenes

Besides ordinary scenes, Hue has scenes that follow the time of day, such as Golden hours and Natural light; Hue calls them smart scenes. Each belongs to one room or zone and is a schedule rather than a look: the day is divided into time slots, each slot plays an ordinary scene, and Hue moves on to the next slot's scene by itself, fading over about a minute. The Master Bedroom's Golden hours, for example, plays Arise from 07:00 and Shine from 16:00; a slot can also begin at sunset. Once started, Hue keeps the schedule running, slot after slot, until something stops it. The driver lists time-of-day scenes next to the ordinary ones and makes each into a Hue Scene device; the rest of this chapter applies to them, with the differences below.

Adding one. Add Scene lists every time-of-day scene that has no device, marked (time-based) and placed before the ordinary scenes of its room or zone: Master Bedroom: Golden hours (time-based), Bedside Lights (zone): Natural light (time-based). An ordinary scene that a time-of-day scene plays in one of its time slots carries that scene's name in brackets, Master Bedroom: Shine (in Golden hours), in Add Scene and in Re-link Scene; it need not be imported for the time-of-day scene to work. Add All Scenes Of Room creates a room's or zone's time-of-day scenes too, after its ordinary scenes, and its Hue Room list counts them: Downstairs (zone) → (unmapped) (16 scenes, 2 time-based). The bridge's Scenes line counts them as well (9.1).

Names. The Hue app numbers its copies of a time-of-day scene, so on the bridge the Master Bedroom's is called Golden hours 4 and the Bedside Lights zone's Golden hours 11. The driver leaves the number out, in its lists and in the device's name: the Master Bedroom's device is named Golden hours, and a zone's carries the zone's name in front, as ordinary zone scenes do: Bedside Lights Golden hours, Bedside Lights Natural light. Adopt Hue Name, Adopt Hue Names and the name differences in Attention follow the same rule, so the missing number never counts as a difference.

11. Using the lights from Control4

Once imported, a Hue Light is an ordinary Control4 dimmer to everything in the project, and a Hue Scene an ordinary dimmer whose level is its room or zone's brightness. This chapter says what that means in practice.

On Navigators

Each Hue Light and each Hue Scene appears on its room's Lighting page with a tile and a slider. For a Hue Scene a tap plays the scene or turns its room or zone off and the slider dims the whole room or zone (9.4); a time-of-day scene's tap starts or stops its schedule (9.11). The rest of this paragraph is about lights. Tap the tile for on and off; the slider sets brightness, and the light responds within about a second. A tap that turns a light on, and "Turn On" in programming, always mean 100 %, not the level the light had before or the power-on level set in the Hue app; use the slider or a programmed level for anything else. The slider settles on the level Hue reports back, in whole percents; 0 % is off and any other level is on. A tile is disabled while the light is unreachable (chapter 10, Online). Changes from the Hue app or a Hue dimmer switch show on the tile within a second or two.

In programming and on keypads

In Composer's Programming view a Hue Light offers the standard actions and events of any Control4 dimmer: turn on, turn off, toggle, set or ramp to a level, and events when it turns on or off or its level changes. The events fire for changes made in the Hue app, by a Hue dimmer switch or by a Hue automation too, because the device follows the bridge; a "when the Porch light turns on" program runs however the light was turned on. A keypad button connected to a Hue Light works as with any Control4 dimmer: a tap switches it, a hold dims it, and the LED follows the light's state, including changes made on the Hue side; the next section has the setup. A Hue Scene is a dimmer: on plays the scene, off turns the room or zone off, and a level brightens or dims the room or zone (or, while the scene is off, plays the scene at that level, 100 % as designed); its on event fires when the scene is played, from Control4, a keypad or the Hue app, its off event when its room or zone turns off or another scene of it is played, and its level events follow the room or zone's brightness, including dims made in the Hue app (9.4). A ramp time given to a scene becomes the fade Hue uses to play it. A time-of-day scene's on event fires when Hue starts its schedule and its off event when Hue stops it, and a ramp time does not apply to starting one (9.11).

Physical switches and keypads

A Hue bulb has to stay powered, so the wall switch that feeds it cannot switch its load. A Control4 Switch V2 or Essential Switch can instead run as a two-button keypad whose buttons drive the Hue Light, the way the stock Philips driver did:

  1. In the switch's Properties tick Use as 2 Button Keypad and leave Buttons Control Load unticked. The switch's own load is then set by programming only; for a Hue bulb set it to 100 % (a "when the project loads" program, for example) so the bulb keeps power. Hide From Nav hides the switch's own light from Navigators, leaving the Hue Light as the one tile for that lamp.
  2. In Connections, select the Hue Light (or Hue Scene) device: its Top Button Link, Bottom Button Link and Toggle Button Link are listed under its Control Outputs. Bind the switch's Top button to Top Button Link and its Bottom button to Bottom Button Link (a drag between the two lists). A single-button keypad goes to the Toggle Button Link instead.
  3. Tap and hold the buttons; the Hue Light answers within about a second, like a tap on its tile.

Advanced Lighting Scenes

Hue Lights and Hue Scene devices can be members of Control4's own Advanced Lighting Scenes (the Lighting Scenes agent). A lighting scene sets a Hue Light's level, and its colour or colour temperature where the light supports them, with the scene's ramp time; a Hue Scene is a dimmer member with a level: while the scene is off, a level above 0 plays the Hue scene at that brightness (100 % plays it as designed); while it is on, a level brightens or dims the room or zone to it, keeping the scene's look; 0 turns the Hue room or zone off. A time-of-day scene as a member starts its schedule for a level above 0 while it is off, and then moves the room or zone to the level (100 % from off simply starts it); 0 stops it and turns the room or zone off (9.11). A "stop ramp" from a lighting scene cannot stop a Hue light or a scene's room in mid-fade, because Hue cannot stop a fade to a set level; the driver reports the last level it knew, and the next report from the bridge corrects the tile.

Whole-room and whole-house commands

A room Off, a house Off or a lighting scene sends one request to the bridge per Hue light, and the driver paces them at ten a second, the most the bridge allows across everything that talks to it. A room with 47 Hue lights therefore takes about five seconds to finish, the lights going off one after another in the bridge's order rather than all at once; a room with a handful of lights is done within a second. The one-request way to switch a whole Hue room or zone is a room light or zone light (chapter 7): its on, off, level and colour each reach the bridge as one request for the whole room or zone, however many lamps it holds, so in programming, in Advanced Lighting Scenes and on keypads address the room light rather than the lamps one by one. (A Control4 room Off still sends its command to every light device in the room, the room light included, so it saves nothing there.) A Hue Scene device's OFF is one request for the whole room or zone as well (9.4). Several room lights or scene devices turned off at once send one request each, about one a second, one after another.

After adding devices

After importing lights or adding scenes, choose File » Refresh Navigators in Composer so the touch screens and the app show the new tiles. Renaming or moving a device needs the same refresh to show on Navigators.

Things that look wrong but are not

  • Event Stream shows a new "Connected since" time every five or ten minutes. The watchdog and the stream timeout reopen the feed on purpose; every reopening is a full read. Every minute instead: the feed shares the commands' queue (chapter 3).
  • Lights shows "47 imported" with nothing else right after a restart. The bridge has not been read yet; the full line returns within seconds of Connected since.
  • Attention lists dozens of new lights after pairing. Those are the lights you have not imported yet. Import the ones you want and the rest stay listed; that is a reminder, not an error.
  • A property you changed jumped back. You did not click the row's Set button (chapter 4).
  • Events Received climbs once a second. A dynamic scene is playing somewhere; each colour step is an event.
  • A scene tile stays ON after its lights were dimmed or changed in the Hue app. A scene reads on until its room or zone turns off or another of its scenes is played; the tile shows the new percentage instead (9.4).
  • A time-of-day scene's tile went off although its lights are still on. Hue stopped the schedule without turning the lights off, for example because another scene of its room or zone was played; the lights keep that scene's look (9.11).
  • An ordinary scene's tile turns on and off by itself during the day. A running time-of-day scene plays that scene in one of its time slots; its tile reads on while that slot plays (9.11).
  • A room dimmed during a time-of-day scene changed brightness by itself. The next time slot began, and Hue set that slot's look, brightness included (9.11).
  • The tile shows the level you tapped but the light did not move. The bridge is unreachable (Event Stream reads Disconnected); the tile corrects itself when the feed returns, and the command is delivered then (chapter 4).
  • Online is blank on a working light. A light connected over Wi-Fi or Matter never reports reachability.
  • A zone light shows a colour that only one of its lamps has. Hue reports no colour for a zone, so the tile shows the first lit lamp's colour (chapter 7).
  • A zone light's lamps lag a fast slider drag. Hue takes about one command a second for a zone or room; the lamps end where the slider ends (chapter 7).
  • ON on a zone light set every lamp to 100 % instead of their previous levels. That is what ON means on a Hue Light; use the slider, a scene or the Hue app for other levels (chapter 7).
  • A Hue Light or Hue Scene reads Not bound to a Hue Bridge right after a restart. After a Director restart or a controller reboot this can last up to a minute while the device looks for its connection again (8.7).
  • A Hue Light or Hue Scene shows Control4's standard bulb icon. In a room's List View they always do; in the tree, a device created by a build without the Hue icons keeps the bulb (chapter 1).

Diagnostics actions (normally not needed)

Test Connection asks the bridge one question over the normal connection and reports in Status (chapter 4). It is the first thing to click when lights do not respond: a Test OK means the controller reaches the bridge and the pairing is good.

Connect Event Stream and Disconnect Event Stream start and stop the event feed by hand. Disconnect leaves Event Stream at Stopped and the lights frozen at their last state until you connect again; the driver reconnects by itself at its next start in any case. Normally not needed.

Development builds add three diagnostic actions (Connection Test, Reload Credentials, Check CA Pinning) and two properties (Dev Server, Diagnostic Server); a release build carries none of them.

Debug Mode and Stream Address are the developer's; leave them as they are unless asked.

These are all the diagnostics a release build has. The developer's other diagnostic commands exist only in development builds; a release build removes them.

13. Limits, and a quick reference of all actions

Limits and what is not supported

  • One Hue bridge per project. A second bridge is not supported; a second Hue Bridge device should not be added (chapter 2).
  • A Hue zone or room becomes a controllable device only when you add it with Add Zone Light, one at a time; there is no "add all zones", and Hue's whole-home group ("all lights") is not offered (chapter 7). A zone light shows one lamp's colour, not a mixture, because Hue reports no colour for a group; its ON is 100 % on every lamp; a lamp that cannot do colour ignores the colour it is sent.
  • Hue motion sensors, buttons, dimmers and switches and entertainment areas are not represented.
  • Hue cannot start a time-of-day scene at a chosen brightness or with a ramp time. A level below 100 % from off (the slider, programming, an Advanced Lighting Scene) therefore starts it with the current time slot's look and the driver then moves the room or zone to the level, so for a moment the lights show the slot's own brightness; a top button held while it is off starts it and brightens from there. A dim while it runs lasts until the next time slot, whose look Hue sets in full (9.11).
  • The bridge is found by its address only, IPv4 only. If the address changes you type the new one (chapter 4); a DHCP reservation avoids that. An address with no number below 8 works, but with the feed sharing the commands' queue (chapter 3).
  • Hue allows about ten light commands a second across everything that talks to the bridge. Dragging a slider quickly sends many requests; the driver keeps only the latest value per light and sends at most ten a second, so the light lags the slider slightly during fast moves and ends where the slider ends. A whole-room command takes a tenth of a second per Hue light (chapter 11); a whole-room OFF through a Hue Scene device or a room light counts as one request. Hue allows about one command a second for a zone or room as a whole, so a zone or room light sends at most one request a second, keeping only the latest value: the tile moves smoothly meanwhile and a quick slider drag ends where the slider ends; a hold's start and stop go out at once.
  • Changes from Hue reach Control4 within one to two seconds, never faster: the bridge bundles its reports once a second.
  • The driver never creates, renames, moves or deletes a Control4 device by itself. Importing, re-linking, forgetting, renaming and room mapping are all clicks; the only automatic housekeeping is the one-time Map Rooms By Name after installation, and linking a device again when its light or its connection comes back or when a connected device announces a light the bridge already knows (8.6).
  • Devices are never moved between rooms by the driver; Room Audit tells you what to drag.
  • Replacing the bridge itself is handled by pairing again and re-linking every device by hand (8.8).
  • Needs Control4 OS 3.3.0 or newer; tested on OS 3.4.2.
  • The proxy settings of the Hue Light and Hue Scene devices (click and hold rates, On preset, button LED colours) are hidden in Composer; the defaults apply (chapter 11).
  • An update that changes a driver's capabilities takes effect only after a controller reboot from System Manager (chapter 3).
  • In a room's List View, Hue Lights and Hue Scenes show Control4's standard light icon; the Hue icons appear in the tree only (chapter 1).

Glossary

Advanced Lighting Scene
Control4's own kind of lighting scene, made in the Lighting Scenes agent: a saved set of levels for several Control4 lights. Hue Lights and Hue Scene devices can be members (chapter 11). Not to be confused with a Hue scene.
Bound, bind, unbound
A device is bound when its Hue Bridge input is connected to the bridge's Hue Lights output in Composer's Connections view. The bridge's texts say bind it to this bridge in Composer for making that connection and unbound for a device whose connection was removed. Devices the bridge creates are bound from the start.
Linked, link, re-link
A device is linked when the bridge knows which Hue light or scene it stands for. Linking is the bridge's bookkeeping, binding is Composer's connection; a device needs both. Link Devices links bound devices the bridge does not manage yet; Re-link Device To Light and Re-link Scene point a linked device at a different light or scene. Not to be confused with the bridge's link button, which is pressed for pairing.
Button Link
The three connections of a Hue Light and a Hue Scene listed under the device's Control Outputs in Composer's Connections view, Top Button Link, Bottom Button Link and Toggle Button Link, to which the buttons of a Control4 switch or keypad are bound by a drag (chapter 11). The device then answers the buttons like a Control4 dimmer: tap for on and off, hold to dim.
On preset
The level a Hue Light goes to when its top keypad button is tapped, or its toggle button while the light is off: 100 %, fixed for now because Composer does not show the setting (chapter 11). It applies to keypad buttons only; a tap on the tile and "Turn On" in programming always mean 100 %.
Pairing
The one-time handshake in which the bridge issues a key to this controller after its link button was pressed (chapter 4). The driver stores the key on the Hue Bridge device and never shows it.
Hue identity
The long code the bridge gives every light and scene; the driver links devices by it, so names and rooms can change freely. Hue Light ID and Hue Scene ID show its start. A light deleted and re-added in the Hue app gets a new one, which is why such a device reads Gone and has to be re-linked.
Gone, missing
The same state: a device whose light, scene, zone or room is no longer on the bridge. The device's Status says Gone from the Hue bridge (or Scene deleted in Hue, Zone deleted in Hue); the bridge's Lights, Scenes, Zone Lights and Attention count it as missing.
Mapping
The entry in Room Mappings that says which Control4 room a Hue room or zone stands for. It decides where newly created devices land and nothing else (chapter 6).
Room, zone
In Hue a light belongs to exactly one room; a zone is a second grouping that may span rooms, used mostly for scenes. In Control4 only rooms exist; a zone's scenes go to the room the zone is mapped to. Either can also be added as a single Hue Light, a zone light or room light (chapter 7).
Zone light, room light
A Hue Light device created by Add Zone Light that stands for a whole Hue zone or room rather than one light: one tile whose on, off, slider and colour go to every lamp of the zone or room in a single request; on while any lamp is lit, showing their average brightness and the first lit lamp's colour (chapter 7). A zone light is named after the zone, a room light Master Bedroom Lights; the bridge's Zone Lights row counts them, and Re-link Zone Light re-points one whose zone was deleted (8.10).
Scene
A Hue scene is a saved look (levels and colours) for one Hue room or zone, created in the Hue app; Hue adds about seven defaults per room. The driver shows a scene as a Hue Scene device, a dimmer for its room or zone; playing it is what Hue calls recalling.
Time-of-day scene
One of Hue's scenes that follow the time of day, such as Golden hours and Natural light; Hue calls them smart scenes. It belongs to one room or zone and plays an ordinary scene in each time slot of the day, moving on to the next slot's scene by itself. Add Scene lists it as, for example, Master Bedroom: Golden hours (time-based); its Hue Scene device starts and stops the schedule (9.11).
Navigator
Control4's word for the things you control the house from: touch screens, remotes and the Control4 app. A device appears there as a tile. File » Refresh Navigators in Composer makes them show newly added devices.
Director, System Manager
Director is the program on the controller that runs the project and every driver in it. System Manager is Control4's maintenance tool for the controller, opened from Composer's Tools menu with administrator rights; the controller is rebooted from it after an update during which Composer asked Restart Director? (chapter 3).
Event feed
The bridge's running report of everything that changes, which the driver keeps open all the time; the Event Stream property shows its state and the bridge's texts call it the event stream. Each report is a bundle the driver's texts call a container.
Full read
The driver reading every light, room, zone and scene from the bridge in one go: right after the feed connects, after every reconnection and on Refresh Scenes. The Busy texts call it resyncing.
DHCP reservation
A setting in your router that makes it hand the bridge the same address every time it asks for one, so Bridge IP Address stays valid. The router's manual explains where; it is usually under the DHCP or LAN settings and needs the bridge's hardware (MAC) address, which the Hue app shows next to its IP address.
Lua output
The driver's log, shown in the lower part of the device's Lua tab in Composer. A few texts in this manual say "see Lua output"; it is for the developer, you only need to look when asked.
Set button
The button that appears to the right of a property row the moment you change its value; only it hands the change to the driver (chapter 4).

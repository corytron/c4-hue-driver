Glossary

1. What this driver does

This driver connects one Philips Hue bridge to your Control4 system over your own home network; no cloud account is involved and nothing leaves the house. Each Hue light you choose becomes an ordinary Control4 light that you can switch, dim, colour and program like any other light in the project, and so can a whole Hue zone or room: one light whose tile moves every lamp in it at once (chapter 7). Changes made in the Hue app or with Hue switches and dimmers show up in Control4 within a second or two, because the driver listens to the bridge's event feed (the bridge's running report of everything that changes) rather than asking it over and over. Hue scenes, the saved looks you create for a room in the Hue app, can become Control4 dimmers: turning one on plays the scene, turning it off turns that Hue room or zone off, and its slider brightens or dims the room or zone while keeping the scene's look. Hue's time-of-day scenes, such as Golden hours and Natural light, can become such dimmers too; turning one on starts its schedule (9.11). The driver never adds, renames, moves or removes a Control4 device on its own, with two small exceptions it tells you about: the one-time Map Rooms By Name it runs when it first sees the bridge's rooms (chapter 6), and the re-adoption of a device that announces a light the bridge already knows (8.6). Every other change is a button you click in Composer, and the driver reports in plain words what it did.

Two words this manual uses throughout: a light is a Hue light, the bulb, strip or lamp as the Hue app lists it; a Hue Light, with a capital L, is the Control4 device that stands for it. A Hue Light can also stand for a whole Hue zone or room; this manual then calls it a zone light or a room light. Navigators is Control4's word for everything you control the house from: touch screens, remotes and the Control4 app. A short glossary of the other terms is at the end of this manual.

What has no counterpart

  • The whole home as one light. Add Zone Light offers Hue zones and rooms, not Hue's "all lights" group. For one tile that moves every light in the house, make a zone holding them all in the Hue app and add that zone.
  • Hue buttons, dimmers and switches are not represented. Pressing a Hue dimmer switch still shows its effect on the lights in Control4, because the lights report their state. Hue motion sensors and MotionAware areas are, from 1.1 beta 1 (chapter 14); the motion of a whole Hue room or zone, and Hue's cameras, are not.
  • Hue automations (the routines the Hue app runs by itself) and a Hue switch's time-based light are not represented; what they do to the lights shows in Control4 (8.1). A switch's time-based light is not one of the time-of-day scenes of 9.11.
  • Entertainment areas (light sync with screens and music) are not touched.
  • Several bridges. One Hue Bridge device per project is supported. Adding a second one is not prevented, but every light and scene can be connected to one bridge only; do not add a second bridge device.

3. Installation

Finding your way in Composer

Everything in this manual happens in Composer Pro, connected to your controller. The places it refers to:

  • System Design is the view Composer opens in (the view buttons are at the lower left: System Design, Connections, Media, Agents, Programming). Its left-hand tree lists the project's rooms and, under each room, the devices in it. Click a device to select it.
  • With a device selected, the pane to the right of the tree shows its tabs: Properties (the rows this manual quotes), Actions (the buttons), Documentation (this page) and Lua. The Lua tab's lower part is the driver's Lua output, the log a few texts in this manual ask you to look at; nothing else on that tab is for you.
  • The Items pane at the far right of System Design is where drivers are added to rooms; its Search tab finds a driver by name.
  • An action is run with one click on its button on the Actions tab. An action that needs choices first opens the Action Parameter List dialog (chapter 5): each row has a … button that opens a Select list; click an entry, click that list's OK (the inner OK, greyed out until you have chosen), and when the rows are filled click the dialog's own OK (the outer OK) to run the action. Cancel on either closes it without doing anything.
  • To rename a device, select it in the tree, press F2, type the new name and press Enter.
  • To delete a device, select it in the tree and press the Delete key; Composer asks Are you sure you want to delete the item '…'? and deletes it on Yes.
  • To move a device to another room, press and hold the mouse button on it in the tree, drag it onto the room's name and release. Composer asks nothing.
  • The Connections view (its button is below the tree) shows, for the selected device, an upper pane titled Control & Audio Video Connections with the device's inputs and outputs, and a lower pane listing the devices that can be connected to the selected row. A connection is made by dragging a device from the lower pane onto the row in the upper pane.
  • After adding or deleting devices, choose File » Refresh Navigators so the touch screens and the app show the new rooms' contents.

What you need

  • A Control4 controller on OS 3.3.0 or newer, and Composer Pro. The driver was written and tested on OS 3.4.2; OS 4 has not been tried. Where it relies on behaviour measured on OS 3.4.2 it checks at every start and falls back or stops safely: the event feed's own connection queue (Stream Address, chapter 5) and the TLS check (chapter 12).
  • A Philips Hue Bridge v2 (the square bridge, model BSB002) or a Hue Bridge Pro (BSB003), on the same network as the controller, with a fixed address: give it a DHCP reservation in your router (a setting that makes the router hand the bridge the same address every time). The driver does not search for a bridge that moved. The round first-generation bridge (BSB001) has no CLIP v2 API and is refused at pairing, as is any device at the address that is not a Hue bridge; the bridge's software must be 1948086000 or newer (keep the bridge updated in the Hue app; the driver shows the version in Bridge Firmware).
  • The bridge's address should contain a number below 8, such as 10.0.2.143 or 198.51.100.5 (the addresses in this manual are examples). The controller keeps one connection queue per spelling of an address, and the driver gives the event feed a queue of its own by writing such a number with leading zeros (10.000.002.143), so that commands to the lights never wait behind the feed. On an address with no number below 8, such as 198.51.100.50 or 198.51.100.200, the driver still works, but the feed shares the commands' queue: Stream Address says shared queue, Stream Timeout (s) is capped at 60 and a command can wait up to a minute behind the feed. If your bridge has such an address, give it another one in the router. Addresses are IPv4 only: four numbers and dots, no name.
  • The driver files of one build: hue_bridge.c4z, hue_light.c4z, hue_scene.c4z and, from 1.1 beta 1, hue_motion.c4z for Hue motion sensors and MotionAware areas (chapter 14). Each build has a number (116, 125, …) that all its files share and that the devices show as Driver Version once installed; the developer tells you the number with the files. Install them all together.

Adding the drivers to the project

  1. Copy the four files (three before 1.1 beta 1) to the computer that runs Composer Pro, into any folder; Composer copies them into the project.
  2. In Composer, open the Driver menu and choose Add or Update Driver or Agent…. The Select Driver or Agent… file dialog opens; select all four files at once (hold Ctrl while clicking) and open them. Nothing needs to be selected in the tree first. Composer answers with, for example, Update Succeeded: Drivers or agents were successfully added: hue_bridge.c4z, hue_light.c4z, hue_motion.c4z, hue_scene.c4z.
  3. In System Design, select the room that should hold the bridge (a double-click in the Items pane adds to the room that is selected). In the Items pane, open the Search tab and type Hue Bridge. The search also finds Control4's own Hue drivers; the one you want is the Hue Bridge by manufacturer Philips Hue with the model text Hue Bridge (CLIP v2). Double-click it: one Hue Bridge device appears in the room.
  4. Do not add a Hue Light, a Hue Scene or a Hue Motion Sensor from the Items pane. The bridge creates them, already connected and linked, when you use its actions. The three drivers only need to be in the project's driver list, which step 2 did.

Composer's Driver menu with Add or Update Driver or Agent… highlighted: the one route used both for installing and for updating.

Updating to a newer build

Updates go through the same Driver » Add or Update Driver or Agent… menu: select all four new files at once, as for the first installation. Composer answers Update Succeeded: Drivers or agents were successfully updated: hue_bridge.c4z, hue_light.c4z, hue_motion.c4z, hue_scene.c4z (the first time, hue_motion.c4z may be listed as added) and pushes the new version to every existing device: the bridge and every Hue Light, Hue Scene and Hue Motion Sensor show the new Driver Version within a minute, the bridge restarts and its event feed is back within seconds, and the pairing, the lights, the scenes and the motion devices are untouched. That is all, unless Composer asks the question below. The first time you open a device's Documentation tab after an update, Composer may still show the page of the build before (seen once on 2026-10-25): select the device again, or another device and back, and the tab shows the new page.

If Composer asks Restart Director?, reboot the controller. Some builds change what a device type can do in Control4, its capabilities; build 160, for example, turned the Hue Scene from an on/off switch into a dimmer. Installing such a build makes Composer ask Restart Director?. Answering Yes does not restart Director: the driver whose capabilities changed keeps running its old code, and its devices keep showing the old Driver Version, until the controller itself is rebooted. To reboot it, open System Manager (in Composer, Tools » System Manager; it needs administrator rights on the computer), right-click the controller and reboot it. Composer loses its connection and is back a few minutes later (about seven and a half minutes once on the house system). Then check Driver Version on the bridge, on one Hue Light, one Hue Scene and one Hue Motion Sensor, and choose File » Refresh Navigators so the touch screens and the app pick up what changed. For up to a minute after the reboot a Hue Light, Hue Scene or Hue Motion Sensor may read Not bound to a Hue Bridge; it corrects itself (8.7). An update during which Composer does not ask the question takes effect at once and needs no reboot.
Never delete the Hue Bridge device to update or "reset" it. The pairing with the bridge and the list of which device is which light are stored on that device. A deleted and re-added bridge starts empty: you would have to pair again and every Hue Light and Hue Scene would read Not bound to a Hue Bridge until connected and linked again by hand (8.6). Updating in place keeps everything.

If you ever need to reload the same version, right-click the bridge and choose Update Driver; Composer asks Versions are equal. Do you want to overwrite…? and reloads it on Yes. For anything newer, use the menu route above. Neither route helps a device that kept its old code after a Restart Director? question; only the reboot does.

Going back to an older build uses the same menu with the older files. This has not been tested; expect the devices to keep their pairing and links as they do on an update, and read Status and Lights afterwards rather than assume.

Release and development builds. The developer sometimes makes development builds that fetch their code from a server on the home network; they carry two extra properties (Dev Server, Diagnostic Server) and three extra actions (Connection Test, Reload Credentials, Check CA Pinning). A release build shows none of them, so a bridge whose Properties page has a Dev Server row is running a development build and should be replaced with the release files through the menu above.

4. Pairing with the bridge

Step 1: find the bridge's address

Open the Hue app on your phone: Settings » My Hue system » tap the bridge. Its IP address is listed there, for example 10.0.2.143. Reserve that address for the bridge in your router if you have not done so; the driver has to be told by hand when the address changes. If the address has no number below 8, change it in the router first (chapter 3, "What you need").

Step 2: enter it in Composer

On the bridge's Properties page, click in the value cell of Bridge IP Address, type the address, for example 10.0.2.143, and click the row's Set button. Status changes to Not paired: press the link button, then run Pair Bridge.

The address must be four numbers from 0 to 255 separated by dots, written without leading zeros (10.0.2.143, not 10.000.002.143), and not a name. Before pairing, an invalid entry only makes Status read Enter the bridge IP address (an address entered earlier is dropped); correct the text and click Set again. While paired, an invalid entry is not harmless: the driver drops the stored address, Status returns to Enter the bridge IP address, the event feed stops (Event Stream reads Stopped) and the lights stop following the bridge until a valid address is set again. The pairing itself is kept, so once a valid address is set again the feed reconnects by itself within seconds and everything is back.

The Set button rule. Composer sends a changed property to the driver only when you click the Set button that appears to the right of the value (together with Cancel) the moment the value differs. Pressing Enter or clicking somewhere else leaves the new value in the grid only, and the grid later jumps back to the driver's value. This applies to every editable property of the drivers in this manual: Bridge IP Address, Debug Mode, the two stream settings, and a scene's Recall As. The keypad settings of chapter 11, which Control4 adds, are the exception: they take a change when you press Enter.

A property row after editing: the Set and Cancel buttons at the right end of the row, and only Set hands the change to the driver.

Step 3: press the link button, then Pair Bridge

  1. Have Composer open on the bridge's Actions tab, then walk to the bridge and press the large round button on top once, briefly (the link button). The bridge accepts a new pairing only for a short while after the press (about 30 seconds, by Hue's own account; the driver does not time it).
  2. Right away, click Pair Bridge on the Actions tab.
  3. Watch Status: it reads Pairing: contacting bridge, then Pairing: found Hue Bridge Pro (BSB003) 001788fffe123abc, requesting key (the model with its id, then the bridge's identifier; a square version 2 bridge reads Hue Bridge v2, square (BSB002)), then for a moment Checking the bridge at 10.0.2.143 before anything is sent while the driver confirms the bridge's identifier and makes the TLS check (chapter 12, "The TLS check"), then Paired. Bridge ID, Bridge Model, Bridge Name (the bridge's own name from the Hue app) and Bridge Firmware fill in. The pairing is a key the bridge issued to this controller; the driver stores it and never shows it.

If you clicked before pressing the button, or too long after it, then Status reads Press the link button on the bridge, then run Pair Bridge again; press the button and click Pair Bridge once more. If Status reads Enter the bridge IP address first, step 2 was skipped. Three failures start with Pairing failed: followed by the reason: Pairing failed: No Hue bridge answered at … means nothing usable answered at that address (check the address in the Hue app and that bridge and controller are on the same network); Pairing failed: No usable answer to the key request: … means the bridge answered the first question but not the pairing request itself (try again; if it persists, check that the address really is a Hue bridge); Pairing failed: bridge error … means the bridge refused with an error of its own (try again after a moment, and after a restart of the bridge if it persists). Rarer answers are Pairing failed: the bridge answered without a usable application key, Pairing failed: unexpected response and Pairing failed: empty response; try again. Four more refusals come before any key is asked for: Pairing failed: Bridge at 10.0.2.143 reports ID 001788fffe456def, expected 001788fffe123abc; run Forget Pairing first to pair with another bridge (the bridge at the address is not the paired one; see Forget Pairing below), Pairing failed: This is a Hue Bridge v1, round (BSB001): … (the round first-generation bridge, which this driver cannot use), Pairing failed: The device at … is not a Hue bridge (model …) (the address belongs to something else), and Pairing failed: Bridge firmware … is older than 1948086000, … (update the bridge in the Hue app, then pair again).

Right after Paired the driver opens the bridge's event feed. Event Stream shows Connecting, then Connected since 14:03:12, and from the first event onwards Connected since 14:03:12, 12 containers, last 14:03:40; a container is one bundle of changes the bridge sent, and Events Received counts the same bundles. Within a few seconds the driver has done its first full read of the bridge's lights, rooms, zones, scenes and motion sensors:

  • Lights reads, for example, 8 on bridge, 0 imported, 8 new, 0 missing, 0 unbound, 0 deleted, Scenes 43 + 1 time-based on bridge, 0 imported, 0 deleted, Zone Lights 2 zones, 4 rooms on bridge; 0 imported and Motion Sensors 2 sensors and 1 area on the bridge; 0 added (chapter 14).
  • Attention lists the new lights: 8 new lights on the bridge: Lamp 1 [Living Room] #1, Lamp 2 [Living Room] #2, Lamp 3 [Kitchen] #3 +5 more (Add Light or Import All New Lights).
  • Room Mappings fills in for every Hue room or zone named like a Control4 room, because the driver runs Map Rooms By Name once by itself the first time it sees the Hue rooms (chapter 6).

Nothing has been added to the project yet. Chapters 6 and 7 are the next steps.

Test Connection

Test Connection on the Actions tab asks the bridge one question and writes the answer to Status: briefly Testing: GET /clip/v2/resource/bridge via 10.0.2.143, then Test OK: HTTP 200 via 10.0.2.143 in 0 s, bridge 001788fffe123abc. The three-digit numbers in these texts are the bridge's reply codes: 200 means the bridge answered properly, 401 or 403 that it refused the pairing key, 404 that the thing asked for no longer exists. The question carries the pairing, so it goes only to a bridge that has passed two checks: the driver asks the bridge at the address for its identifier, a question without the pairing, and then makes the TLS check (chapter 12, "The TLS check"). The driver makes both at every start and after a change of Bridge IP Address; if they have not passed since, the test makes them first (Status reads Testing: checking the bridge identity first meanwhile). A different bridge at the address ends the test with Bridge at 10.0.2.143 reports ID 001788fffe456def, expected 001788fffe123abc; nothing is sent until the address is corrected (it receives nothing that carries the pairing), and a failed TLS check with TLS check failed: nothing is sent (see Attention); a test clicked after that sends nothing and says Test refused: TLS check failed; nothing is sent (see Attention). A device at the address that is no bridge this driver can use ends it with Bridge at 10.0.2.143 is a …: no CLIP v2 API; nothing is sent (chapter 12). When nothing answers, Status reads Bridge at 10.0.2.143 not reachable (…); checking again in 30 s, or TLS check inconclusive (…): nothing is sent; checking again in 30 s when only the TLS check went unanswered (chapter 12); the driver asks again every 30 seconds, and the test goes ahead by itself once both checks pass. Once the bridge has passed, three failures are possible: Test failed: key rejected (HTTP 403). Re-pair with Pair Bridge (the bridge no longer accepts this pairing, see 8.8; a bridge that answers 401 shows that number instead), Test failed: bridge reports ID …, expected … (the answer names another bridge) and Test failed: … with the reason: a network error when nothing answers, or another reply code such as HTTP 503. When not paired it says Not paired. If the test succeeds while the driver had stopped because the bridge refused the pairing (Key rejected by the bridge …, 8.8), the bridge accepts the pairing again: the driver ends that stop by itself and the feed reconnects. Otherwise the test leaves the normal connection alone.

Forget Pairing

Forget Pairing removes the stored pairing from the controller and stops the event feed. Status returns to Not paired: press the link button, then run Pair Bridge (the address is kept), Event Stream reads Stopped, Bridge ID, Bridge Model, Bridge Name, Bridge Firmware, Stream Address and Last Event are cleared and Events Received returns to 0. The Hue Light, Hue Scene and Hue Motion Sensor devices stay in the project and keep their links; they simply stop following the bridge until you pair again (after a minute a motion device's Status says so, Bridge not answering since 14:02; motion not current), and a command given to a light or scene meanwhile is dropped, not kept for later. Pair with the same bridge and everything resumes (the links are expected to survive; this has not been exercised on the house system, so read Lights afterwards). Forget Pairing is needed only to pair with a different bridge. Pair Bridge compares the identifier of the bridge at the address with the stored one before it asks for a key: the same bridge pairs again at any time (after a reset of the bridge, say, or at a new address) and keeps every link, while a different bridge is refused with Pairing failed: Bridge at 10.0.2.143 reports ID 001788fffe456def, expected 001788fffe123abc; run Forget Pairing first to pair with another bridge and receives nothing, because the links of your devices belong to the paired bridge. Nothing else changes: the pairing, the feed and the rows stay as they were. After Forget Pairing the other bridge pairs, and then every Hue Light and Hue Scene whose light or scene that bridge does not have reads Gone until re-linked (8.8); a Hue Motion Sensor whose sensor or area it lacks reads as deleted in Hue (14.12).

When the bridge is offline

If the bridge is unplugged, rebooting or unreachable then the feed ends. The driver notices when the bridge closes it, or when Stream Watchdog (s) passes without a word from the bridge (Disconnected: watchdog: no data for 600 s. Reconnecting in 1 s; in a quiet house it can take until the connection's Stream Timeout (s), chapter 5). While the bridge does not answer, Event Stream reads Disconnected: transfer error 28: …. Reconnecting in 2 s or similar (the number is the controller's error code, 28 meaning "no answer in time"; the reason after it varies. Error 28 is also how a connection that reached Stream Timeout (s) ends, once an hour by default, so one such line followed by a fresh Connected since is normal), and the driver retries with a growing pause, doubling from 1 second up to 60 seconds between attempts. As soon as the bridge is back, Event Stream reads Connected since … again and the driver does a full read of every light and scene, so Control4 catches up with whatever happened while it was blind. You do nothing. Meanwhile:

  • Hue Light and Hue Scene devices keep showing their last known state, and their tiles stay usable, because the driver cannot tell an unreachable bridge from a quiet one until it tries.
  • Hue Motion Sensor devices keep showing their last state too. After a minute their Status reads Bridge not answering since 14:02; motion not current, and once the bridge is back they report what changed meanwhile (14.10).
  • If you tap a Hue Light during the outage, the tile shows the level you asked for after about a second (the ramp time plus one second) although the light did nothing. The command itself is kept as "the latest wanted value" for that light and retried with a growing pause (half a second, doubling up to eight seconds) for at most 30 seconds after the last command to that light, then dropped (a line in the Lua output says so). A bridge that is back within those 30 seconds still takes the last level asked for; after a longer outage nothing is replayed, so no light jumps to a level asked for minutes earlier. The full read after reconnecting then corrects every tile to what the lights really do.
  • A Hue Scene's ON (playing the scene) is one request and is not retried: the tile shows ON after two seconds and drops back to off a few seconds later, when the request fails; the scene is not played when the bridge returns. Its OFF, and a slider move while the scene is on, are kept and retried like a light's commands, for at most 30 seconds: the tile shows OFF at once, or the new level after the ramp time plus a second, and a bridge that is back within that time turns the room or zone off, or changes its level, unless a later command replaced it; after that the command is dropped.

When the bridge's address changes

If the bridge receives a new address from the router then the feed drops and keeps failing: Event Stream cycles through Disconnected: …. Reconnecting in 60 s and Connecting, and Test Connection fails with a network error (Test failed: …; once the driver has restarted, Status reads Bridge at … not reachable (…); checking again in 30 s instead). Look up the new address in the Hue app, type it into Bridge IP Address and click Set. The pairing is kept, because it belongs to the bridge and not to the address: Status reads Checking the bridge at … before anything is sent (with the new address) while the driver confirms that the bridge there is the paired one, then Paired again, and the feed reconnects within seconds, also when it had stopped because the bridge refused the pairing. Then reserve the address in the router so it does not happen again.

6. Rooms first

Before importing anything, tell the driver which Control4 room each Hue room stands for. The mapping has one job: when a light or scene is created, it goes into the Control4 room its Hue room maps to, unless you pick a room yourself. The mapping never moves an existing device, and changing a mapping later changes only where future additions land. That is deliberate: Control4 offers no way for a driver to move a device, and a device you dragged somewhere on purpose should stay there.

The Room Mappings property

Room Mappings is a drop-down list. Its row shows a summary, for example 6 Hue rooms and zones; 2 unmapped (open the list to read). Open the list and every Hue room, then every Hue zone (marked (zone)), is a line of its own with an arrow to its Control4 room:

Bedroom → Bedroom
Garden → (unmapped)
Kitchen → Kitchen
Living Room → Living Room
Accent (zone) → Living Room
TV Area (zone) → (unmapped)

Picking a line does nothing: the list is there to be read, and the row goes back to the summary. (Composer shows a property value on one line of about 75 characters and clips the rest, which is why the mappings are a list and not one long line.)

  • (unmapped): no Control4 room chosen yet. Lights in that Hue room are skipped by Import All New Lights and need a room picked in Add Light. A room or zone you create in the Hue app appears here as (unmapped) by itself; map it with Set Room Mapping or click Map Rooms By Name.
  • (Control4 room gone): the mapping points at a Control4 room that has since been deleted from the project, for example Kitchen → (Control4 room gone). Attention says 1 room mapping points at a Control4 room that no longer exists: Kitchen (Set Room Mapping); fix it with Set Room Mapping.
  • A Hue room that was deleted in the Hue app but still has a mapping is listed last as Playroom (deleted in Hue) → Den; remove it with Set Room Mapping and (no mapping), or leave it, it does no harm.
  • None; use Map Rooms By Name: nothing is mapped yet.

Mappings are kept by identity, not by name: renaming a Hue room in the Hue app or a Control4 room in Composer leaves its mapping in place, and the line simply shows the new name.

The Room Mappings drop-down, open: the summary on the row, then one line per Hue room or zone, Hue on the left of each arrow, the Control4 room, (unmapped) or (Control4 room gone) on the right.

To read all of a long value, widen the Properties pane, or click Map Rooms By Name and read its result in Last Action.

Map Rooms By Name

Map Rooms By Name maps every Hue room or zone to the Control4 room with exactly the same name (capitals and spaces at the ends do not matter) and leaves everything else alone: it never changes a mapping that exists, and it skips a name that two Control4 rooms share. The driver runs it once by itself the first time it reads the bridge's rooms after installation; you can click it again whenever you have created or renamed rooms on either side. Last Action reports, for example:

Mapped 3: Bedroom → Bedroom, Kitchen → Kitchen, Living Room → Living Room; no Control4 room named: Garden, Accent (zone), TV Area (zone)

and on a later click, with nothing changed, Nothing new to map; 3 already mapped (kept); no Control4 room named: Garden, Accent (zone), TV Area (zone). A name that two Control4 rooms share is left alone and reported as, for example, ambiguous: Bedroom (2 Control4 rooms are named Bedroom). If it reports Nothing mapped: no Hue rooms fetched yet (connect the event stream and try again), the bridge has not been read yet: wait for Event Stream to say Connected since. Nothing mapped: no Control4 rooms found in the project means Composer's room list was not available at that moment; try again.

  • Room: (no mapping) first, then every Control4 room as Floor / Room, for example Main Floor / Den. Where two rooms share a name the room's number is appended so you can tell them apart.

Zones. A Hue zone can be mapped like a room. A light's default room always comes from its Hue room (a light belongs to exactly one room in Hue), so a zone mapping is used for scenes, zone lights and MotionAware areas: Add Scene and Add All Scenes Of Room put a zone's scenes into the zone's mapped room, Add Zone Light puts its zone light there (chapter 7), and Add Motion Area an area that covers the zone (14.4). Room Audit

Room Audit compares, for every imported light, the Control4 room it is in with the room its Hue room maps to (for a zone or room light, the room its zone or room maps to), and writes the differences to Last Action as a to-do list:

2 of 5 are not in the room their Hue room maps to: Lamp 3 is in Den; Hue says Kitchen → drag it to Kitchen in Composer if you want it there; Lamp 5 is in Living Room; Hue says Bedroom → drag it to Bedroom in Composer if you want it there

Nothing is moved for you; if you agree with a line, drag the device to that room in the System Design tree, then run the audit again and the line disappears. When everything agrees it reads All 5 linked lights in mapped Hue rooms are in the rooms their Hue rooms map to (the number is how many lights were checked, zone and room lights included; with a single light, All 1 linked light in a mapped Hue room is in the room its Hue room maps to). A line ending in mapped to a Control4 room that no longer exists (Set Room Mapping) means the mapping, not the light, needs fixing. A tail of ; 1 not found in the project means Composer's device list did not include one of the lights at that moment (usually a device being deleted); run it again later. Room Audit unavailable: the project lookup returned nothing (see the Lua output) means Composer's project information was not available; try again later. Running the audit changes nothing, so it is also a harmless way to make the bridge refresh its Attention line (see 8.2).

If you move a light to another Hue room in the Hue app, its Hue Room property follows within seconds; the Control4 device stays where it is, and the next Room Audit lists it until you drag it or decide not to.

Last Action after a Room Audit with nothing to drag: every checked light is in the room its Hue room maps to. With differences the row holds one line per light, each ending in the room to drag it to; the driver only reports, you drag.

Why the driver never moves a device. Control4 gives drivers no way to move a device between rooms, and a move would also be the kind of surprise this driver avoids: you may well want a Hue light that Hue files under "Kitchen" to live in the Control4 room "Den". The audit is there so you can decide, light by light.

7. Importing lights

Two actions create Hue Light devices. Both create each device already connected to the bridge, named as in the Hue app, with its capabilities (dimming, colour, colour temperature) set from what the light reports, and with its current state. Both refuse to create a second device for a light that already has one, so clicking twice is safe. A third action, Add Zone Light, creates a Hue Light that stands for a whole Hue zone or room; the last section of this chapter describes it.

Two kinds of light are skipped on purpose and named in the result: lights with no mapped room (their Hue room is not mapped, is mapped to a Control4 room that no longer exists, or they are in no Hue room at all), and lights whose device you deleted in Composer (8.4 explains why). Adding a zone or room as a light

A Hue zone is a group of lights you made in the Hue app, often to run a few lamps together: the Accent zone, say, holds two of the lamps of the larger Living Room. Add Zone Light makes such a zone, or a whole Hue room, into one Hue Light device, a zone light or room light: one tile in a Control4 room whose on, off, slider, colour and colour temperature go to every lamp of the zone or room with a single request to the bridge, and that shows what the zone or room is doing. To everything else in this manual it is an ordinary Hue Light: the same Properties and the same two actions (chapter 10), the same keypad behaviour (chapter 11), the same place in programming and Advanced Lighting Scenes. Zones and rooms are never added by themselves and there is no "add all zones": one click per zone or room, so that the project holds only the ones you want.

Its dialog has two rows:

  • Group: every zone on the bridge that has no device yet, then every such room, each with the number of lights it holds: Accent (zone, 2 lights), Living Room (room, 2 lights). A zone or room whose device you deleted in Composer comes after the others, its entry ending in (deleted from Composer 2026-10-24); one that holds no lights reads Reading Corner (zone, no lights) and is refused. When every zone and room has a device the list holds only (every zone and room on the bridge is imported); with no zone or room on the bridge at all, (no zones or rooms on the bridge). Hue's whole-home group ("all lights") is not offered (chapter 2).
  • Room: (mapped room) first, then every Control4 room as Floor / Room, exactly as in Add Light (pictured above). (mapped room) uses the Room Mappings entry of that zone or room itself: a Hue room is usually mapped already by Map Rooms By Name; a zone is mapped when Map Rooms By Name found a Control4 room of exactly its name, or when you mapped it with Set Room Mapping (chapter 6).

The Add Zone Light dialog with the Group list open: the zones first, then the rooms, each with the number of lights it holds.

Click OK. Last Action reads Added Accent (zone) to Living Room (device 1203) (a room reads, for example, Kitchen (room) in these texts) and the device appears in that room in the System Design tree within a second; choose File » Refresh Navigators afterwards. Names and properties. A zone light takes the zone's name as it is, Accent. A room light is named Living Room Lights, the room's name with Lights added, so that the Living Room's Lighting page does not list a light called "Living Room". As with lights the name is set once and is then yours; Attention and Adopt Hue Name apply the same rule, so a room light called Living Room Lights is never reported as differing from its Hue name. The device's Status reads Hue zone Accent (2 lights) or Hue room Living Room (2 lights); its Hue Light ID zone #1 (5e6f7a8b…), the zone's number on the bridge and the start of its Hue identity; Hue Name the zone's or room's name; Model Hue zone (2 lights); Hue Room the zone's own label, Accent (zone), or the room's name; Online Online. Composer shows the device's driver as Hue Light, with the Hue Light icon.

The Properties page of a zone light for the zone Accent: Status Hue zone Accent (2 lights), Hue Light ID zone #1 (5e6f7a8b…), Model Hue zone (2 lights) and Hue Room Accent (zone).

What the tile does. The zone light is on while any lamp of the zone is lit, and its slider shows the brightness of the lit lamps as Hue reports it, their average in whole percents; 0 % while all are off.

  • ON ("Turn On" in programming, a tap on the tile, a tap on the top keypad button while the On preset is at its default) turns every lamp on at 100 %, exactly as on a Hue Light (chapter 11); the lamps' own last levels are not restored (the Hue app does that).
  • The slider, or a level from programming or a lighting scene, sets every lamp to that level: 40 % means every lamp at 40 %, and the tile then shows 40. Differences between the lamps are levelled out; 0 % turns them all off. (A Hue Scene's slider moves its room or zone relative to where it is, keeping those differences, 9.4; a zone light is a light and keeps a light's promise.)
  • A keypad hold is Hue's own dimming of the whole zone: every lamp brightens or dims together and the differences between them are kept, as with Hue's dimmer switch; the hold stops where you let go. Held while the zone is off, the top button turns it on at 1 % and brightens from there; the bottom button does nothing. Taps, the toggle button and the LEDs behave as on a Hue Light (chapter 11); the LEDs show on while any lamp is lit.
  • Colour and colour temperature go to every lamp that can follow: a white-only lamp in a colour zone should ignore the colour and keep its level (not seen on the house system as this is written, where every lamp has colour). A colour set while the zone is off is kept and applied at the next turn-on, as on a light.
  • Which controls the tile has depends on all the lamps together: the colour wheel appears if any lamp has colour, the temperature control if any lamp has colour temperature, and the temperature range is the one those lamps share (where they share none, the first lamp's). When lamps are added to or removed from the zone in the Hue app, the controls and the light count in Status follow within seconds (as this is written, a change of controls on a live tile had not yet been tried on the house system; look rather than assume).
  • The colour the tile shows. Hue reports on and brightness for a zone but no colour, so the driver shows the colour of the first lit lamp that has one (in the zone's own order), else the colour temperature of the first lit lamp that has one, and nothing while every lamp is off. The tile therefore follows colours set in the Hue app or by a scene; in a zone whose lamps differ it shows one lamp's colour, not a mixture.
  • One request a second. Hue's guidance is about one command a second for a zone or room as a whole (ten for single lights), so the driver sends a zone light's commands at that pace after a first three at once, keeping only the latest value: a quick slider drag is sent as one request a second while the percentage on the tile moves smoothly, and the lamps catch up and end where the slider ends. A hold's start and its stop, and a tap right after a hold, go out at once.
  • A zone without lights. If every lamp is removed from the zone in the Hue app then the device reads Status Hue zone Accent has no lights; nothing to control with Online Offline, its tile is disabled and commands are dropped; put a lamp back and it is Online again, nothing to click. The zone light is not counted as missing, because the zone still exists (8.10).

A room light is the one-request way to switch a whole Hue room (chapter 11, "Whole-room and whole-house commands"), and the natural thing to put on a wall keypad. A zone light and the Hue Scene devices of the same zone work side by side; 9.4 says what each tile shows. The bridge's Zone Lights row counts the zone and room lights the way Lights counts lights: 2 zones, 4 rooms on bridge; 1 imported, with , 1 missing, , 1 unbound or , 1 deleted added only when not zero; Lights itself does not count them. Renames, a zone deleted in Hue, a device deleted in Composer: 8.10.

8. Living with it: renames, replacements, deletions, restores

Over the years lights get replaced, names change on one side or the other, devices get deleted by mistake, bridges get reset and projects get restored. This chapter walks through each case. The rule behind all of them: the Control4 device is the thing your programming refers to, so the driver keeps devices and re-points their links instead of deleting and re-creating, and it does nothing on its own that would make a later fix harder. What needs you is listed in Attention until you deal with it; nothing is lost by waiting.

8.1 Two-way state

Turn a light on in the Hue app, press a Hue dimmer switch, or let a Hue automation run: the Control4 light follows within one to two seconds (the bridge bundles its reports, about one bundle a second), and Events Received on the bridge ticks up. The other way round, a tap in Control4 reaches the light within about a second and the tile then shows the level Hue confirms. If you switch a light off at its wall switch (cutting its power) then Hue needs a while, sometimes minutes, to notice; the device's Online then reads Offline and the tile is disabled on Navigators (greyed out, it cannot be used) until the light has power again. When power returns the light comes back on its own and Online returns to Online. A light offline for more than a day is mentioned in Attention: 1 light offline for more than 24 h: Lamp 3 #3; that day is counted from the moment the driver, since its last start, first saw the light offline, so after a controller restart or a driver update it starts over. A light that connects to the bridge over Wi-Fi or Matter rather than Zigbee never reports reachability, so its Online stays blank; that is harmless.

8.2 A light renamed in Hue, or a device renamed in Composer

Names are never changed behind your back on either side. When the Hue name and the Composer name of a device differ, Attention says so. For a light, the Hue name is the light's name as the Hue app shows it, the name the device was given when it was created (chapter 7), so a newly added light never differs. A device renamed in Composer is reported within a few seconds, because the bridge listens for Composer's rename. A light renamed in the Hue app is reported the next time the bridge refreshes its rows: at its next full read (after the feed reconnects), when something is added to or deleted from the bridge, or as soon as you run any action on the bridge's Actions tab; Room Audit is a harmless one to click for that. For a device renamed in Composer from Lamp 1 to Reading Lamp the line reads:

1 Hue name differs from the Composer names: Reading Lamp is Lamp 1 in Hue (Adopt Hue Names or Accept Names As They Are)

(With several differences it reads 2 Hue names differ from the Composer names: ….) You have three ways to answer:

  • Adopt Hue Names renames every differing device in Composer to its Hue name, accepted differences included; Last Action reads Renamed 1 device: Reading Lamp → Lamp 1 (device 1201) | Scenes: No Composer names differ from the Hue scene names | Zone lights: No Composer names differ from the Hue zone and room names | Motion: No Composer names differ from the Hue sensor and area names, or No Composer names differ from the Hue names when there is nothing to do. A device it could not rename is listed after a semicolon as not renamed 1: Reading Lamp (device 1201): no proxy id known or …: rename failed: …; try again after a minute, and after a controller restart if it persists.
  • Accept Names As They Are keeps the Composer names and stops Attention from mentioning these particular differences: Accepted 1 name difference: Reading Lamp (Hue: Lamp 1) | Scenes: No Composer names differ from the Hue scene names | Zone lights: No Composer names differ from the Hue zone and room names | Motion: No Composer names differ from the Hue sensor and area names. The driver remembers the Hue name it accepted: if that light is renamed again in the Hue app the difference is reported again, but a further rename of the device in Composer is not.
  • Adopt Hue Name on one Hue Light device (its own Actions tab) renames just that device: its Last Action reads Renamed device 1202 to Lamp 1 (the device's own actions use Composer's number for it, one higher than the bridge's, see chapter 5).

Scene devices, zone lights and motion devices join in: Adopt Hue Names, Accept Names As They Are and Link Devices apply to all of them, so their Last Action always has four parts, the lights' result, then | Scenes: and the scenes', then | Zone lights: and the zone and room lights' (8.10), then | Motion: and the motion devices' (chapter 14), for example No Composer names differ from the Hue names | Scenes: Renamed 1 scene device: Read → Accent Read (device 1215) | Zone lights: No Composer names differ from the Hue zone and room names | Motion: No Composer names differ from the Hue sensor and area names. The driver never renames anything in Hue.

The Attention row a few seconds after a device was renamed in Composer: 1 Hue name differs from the Composer names: …, naming the Composer name, the Hue name and the two actions that resolve it.

8.3 A light replaced or deleted in Hue

If a light is deleted from the bridge in the Hue app, or replaced by a new one (which Hue treats as a new light), then its Hue Light device stays in the project and reads Status Gone from the Hue bridge since 2026-10-22 09:15; re-link or Forget with Online Offline; its tile is disabled, the bridge's Lights counts it as 1 missing and Attention reads 1 light missing from the bridge: Lamp 5 #5 (device 1213, since 2026-10-22) (Re-link Device To Light or Forget Light). Programming, Advanced Lighting Scenes and Navigator pages still refer to the device, so nothing of yours is lost. The same is expected when a light is deleted by mistake in the app and added again a minute later (not yet tried on the house system): Hue should give it a new identity, so the device reads Gone and the re-added light appears as new with a hint, as below; re-link it, do not add it.

When the replacement light has been added in the Hue app it shows up as a new light, and the driver notices when it looks like the missing one (the same radio hardware, which a re-added light should keep, or the same name): 1 new light on the bridge: Lamp 5 [Bedroom] #9 (looks like Lamp 5 #5, device 1213; re-link?) (Add Light or Import All New Lights). Do not add it as a new device; re-point the old one:

  1. Open Re-link Device To Light. In Device, pick the gone device: Lamp 5 (device 1213, gone since 10-22) (a device whose connection was removed is listed as Lamp 4 (device 1211, unbound since 10-21); lists use the short date). In Light, which lists every light that has no device at the moment (likely replacements first with their hint, lights whose device you deleted in Composer last), pick the replacement.
  2. Click OK. Last Action reads Re-linked device 1213 (was Lamp 5 #5) to Lamp 5 [Bedroom] #9. The device keeps its name, room, connections and programming; its Status, Hue Light ID and capabilities now describe the new light and its tile works again. If the new light is named differently in Hue, Attention mentions the name difference (8.2). If the new light can do less than the old one (a white light in place of a colour one) the colour controls disappear from the tile, and programming that sets a colour on it does nothing.

The Re-link Device To Light dialog with its Device list open. With a gone device in the project the list reads Name (device number, gone since date); here there is none, so it holds only (no gone or unbound devices).

The same dialog with its Light list open: every light that has no device at the moment, likely replacements first with their (looks like …; re-link?) hint, lights whose device was deleted from Composer last, marked with the date.

If the light comes back under its old identity (for example you restored a Hue backup) then the driver notices and the device is simply linked again; you do nothing. If the light is gone for good and you do not want the device either, use Forget Light (8.5) and then delete the device in Composer. A gone device is never dropped by the driver itself. Refusals: Not re-linked: pick a device and a light (an empty row), Not re-linked: Lamp 5 #9 is already linked to device 1217 (the light you picked already has a device), Not re-linked: device 1213 is already linked to Lamp 5 #9 (the device you picked was re-linked meanwhile, usually a remembered choice; open the Device list again), Not re-linked: device 1213 is not a registered light (the device is not one the bridge manages) and Not re-linked: light … is not on the bridge (connect the event stream and try again) (the list was stale). The Device list says (no gone or unbound devices) when there is nothing to re-link.

8.4 A device deleted in Composer

If you delete a Hue Light device in Composer then the bridge is told at once: Lights ends in …, 1 deleted (after deleting most of them it reads, for example, 8 on bridge, 1 imported, 0 new, 0 missing, 0 unbound, 7 deleted) and Attention stays None, because deleting in Composer is a deliberate act. The driver remembers the deletion:

  • Import All New Lights skips that light on purpose and says so: skipped 1 deleted in Composer (Add Light re-creates one): Lamp 8 #8 (deleted 2026-10-22). A bulk import will therefore never quietly bring back a device you removed.
  • Add Light lists it at the end of its Light list as Lamp 8 [Bedroom] #8 (deleted from Composer 2026-10-22). Picking it creates a fresh device (a new device for Control4, so programming that pointed at the old one has to be redone) and clears the memory of the deletion.

If you deleted a device while the controller was off or the driver was not running then the driver finds out at its next start and treats it the same way, with one rare exception: when no Hue Light at all is left in the project at that moment the driver cannot tell a deletion from a missing answer and lists the device as 1 unbound device: … instead; Forget Light on it clears that.

8.5 Forget Light

Forget Light is the deliberate way to release a device from its Hue light without deleting anything. Its Device list holds every managed device as Lamp 1 #1 (device 1201); a device that is not in its normal state carries its state after the bracket, Lamp 5 #5 (device 1213) gone or Lamp 4 #4 (device 1211) unbound. On OK, Last Action reads Forgot Lamp 1 #1 (device 1201): unbound and left in the project for you to delete; the Hue light is listed under Add Light again. The device stays in the project with Status Forgotten by the bridge (was Hue light Lamp 1 #1). Delete this device in Composer, or bind it again and use Link Devices, its connection to the bridge removed and its tile disabled; delete it in Composer when you are ready. The Hue light is available in Add Light and counted as new again. The same can be started from the device itself with its Forget This Light action. An empty choice answers Not forgotten: pick a device first.

Which to use: delete the device without Forget when you want the light gone from Control4 for good (a bulk import will not bring it back); Forget and then delete when you want to import the light afresh, for example into another room; Re-link when you are replacing the light and want to keep the device.

The Forget Light dialog: every managed device as Name #number (device number); a gone or unbound device carries that word at the end of its entry. Zone and room lights follow as Name (zone light, device number) (the picture predates them).

8.6 Link Devices and the bare "Hue Light"

Occasionally a Hue Light device exists that the bridge does not manage: one added by hand from the Items pane, one that was Forgotten and that you want back, or every light after the bridge device itself was deleted and re-added (which this manual tells you never to do). Such a device reads Status Not bound to a Hue Bridge, or, if you forgot it, Forgotten by the bridge (…). (A device that is connected can read the same for up to a minute after a Director restart or a controller reboot; wait that minute first, 8.7.) The way in is a connection, and sometimes one click:

  1. Open the Connections view, select the device, and connect its Hue Bridge input (in the upper pane) to the bridge's Hue Lights output by dragging the bridge from the lower pane, HUE_BRIDGE Output Devices, onto that row. Scene devices use the same Hue Lights output.
  2. What happens next depends on whether the device remembers its light. A device that remembers which Hue light it was (one that was Forgotten, or one left over from a deleted bridge) announces that light the moment it is connected, and the bridge adopts it on the spot: its Status returns to Lamp 1 (Hue color lamp) and nothing needs clicking; Link Devices then only answers All 5 bound devices are registered | Scenes: All 2 bound scene devices are registered | Zone lights: All 2 bound zone light devices are registered | Motion: No motion devices are bound to this bridge (or … | Zone lights: No zone light devices are bound to this bridge | Motion: …), which is success. If that light was meanwhile imported as another device, the bridge adopts nothing and Attention reads 2 devices claim Hue light Lamp 1 #1: 1201 and 1219 (Forget Light on one of them); Link Devices says the same in its own words, … announces Lamp 1 #1, which device 1201 holds (Forget Light on one of them). Forget one of the two and delete it. If the remembered light is no longer on the bridge, Link Devices adopts the device anyway and ends its line with (not on the bridge: marked gone); it then reads Gone and 8.3 applies.
  3. A bare device, added by hand, remembers nothing and reads Bound; waiting for the bridge after the connection. On the bridge, click Link Devices: the device is matched by its Composer name to a Hue light that has no device yet (the light's name, or the name of its Hue device; capitals and spaces at the ends do not matter), and only a name that exactly one such light has counts. Last Action reads Linked 1 device: Lamp 3 (device 1221) to Lamp 3 [Kitchen] #3 by name | Scenes: All 2 bound scene devices are registered | Zone lights: All 2 bound zone light devices are registered | Motion: No motion devices are bound to this bridge; with nothing to do, the lights' part reads All 5 bound devices are registered, or No devices are bound to this bridge, and the parts of the scenes, the zone lights and the motion devices follow it (9.9, 8.10, 14.12). A device adopted by its remembered identity is reported in the same way, with by its rid (rid is the driver's word for the Hue identity) in place of by name.

If the name matches nothing then Last Action reads not linked 1: Hue Light (device 1221): no unimported Hue light has that name; rename the device in Composer to the Hue light's exact name and run Link Devices again: rename the device in the tree to the exact Hue name and click again. If two Hue lights share that name, … 2 unimported Hue lights have that name; rename the device in Composer to one Hue light's exact name … (rename one of them in the Hue app first). If two bound devices carry the same name, … 2 bound devices share that name; rename one in Composer and run Link Devices again. Not linked: the bound-device lookup failed (see the Lua output) means Composer's connection list was not available at that moment; try again. Linking never moves the device: it stays in whatever room it is in.

The Connections view of a Hue Light, upper pane Control & Audio Video Connections. The row to look at is the device's Hue Bridge input under Control Inputs, whose Connected To column reads Hue Bridge->Hue Lights: it is connected to the bridge's Hue Lights output. Devices the bridge creates come connected like this; a hand-added one gets this row filled by dragging the bridge from the lower pane, HUE_BRIDGE Output Devices, onto it. The picture predates the keypad feature: today the Control Outputs also list Top Button Link, Bottom Button Link and Toggle Button Link (chapter 11).

If you remove that connection on a managed device then the bridge counts it as 1 unbound, Attention reads 1 unbound device: Lamp 4 #4 (device 1211) (bind it to this bridge in Composer, or Forget Light) and the device reads Not bound to a Hue Bridge; connect it again and it resumes by itself, no click needed. A device connected to a second Hue Bridge device by mistake never hears from the bridge it belongs to (a bare one reads Bound; waiting for the bridge for ever); delete that second bridge rather than link anything to it.

If two devices both claim the same Hue light (possible after copying devices around) then neither is changed and Attention reads 2 devices claim Hue light Lamp 4 #4: 1211 and 1223 (Forget Light on one of them): Forget one of the two and delete it.

8.7 The bridge offline and back; a controller restart

Covered in chapter 4: the driver reconnects by itself and does a full read when the bridge returns. Expect Event Stream to read Disconnected: …. Reconnecting in … s meanwhile; nothing is needed from you unless the bridge's address changed. When the bridge itself ends the feed, Event Stream reads Disconnected: server closed the stream (HTTP 200). Reconnecting in 1 s and the driver reconnects when the bridge is back; a bridge that restarts or updates its firmware is the expected cause (not yet observed on the house system). A light that updates its firmware needs nothing either; a capability it gains (colour, say) is picked up at the next full read.

After a controller restart everything reconnects by itself: the lights, scenes and motion devices announce themselves to the bridge, Lights reads 5 imported alone until the first full read, and within seconds of Connected since every tile and colour control is back. Nothing to click. Right after a Director restart or a reboot the controller can be late in telling a device about its connection to the bridge, so a Hue Light, Hue Scene or Hue Motion Sensor may read Not bound to a Hue Bridge at first although Composer shows it connected. The device looks again 5, 15, 30 and 60 seconds after it starts and greets the bridge as soon as it sees the connection, so the text corrects itself within a minute. Only a device that still reads it after that needs its connection checked (8.6); a motion device then also acts as disconnected (14.12).

8.8 The bridge factory reset, restored or replaced

  • Factory reset. If the bridge is reset to factory settings then it forgets the pairing, and it refuses the next request the driver makes with it (the event feed reconnecting after the bridge's restart, or a command; after a refused command the driver asks the bridge once more to be sure): Status reads Key rejected by the bridge (HTTP 403): re-pair with Pair Bridge and Event Stream Stopped: key rejected (HTTP 403) (the number is the bridge's reply code: the Hue Bridge Pro answered 403 when this was tested on 2026-10-25; the driver treats a 401 the same way). From then on the driver sends nothing with the rejected pairing unless you click Test Connection: the feed stays stopped (Connect Event Stream does not reopen it) and commands from Control4 are dropped, not kept for later. Test Connection says Test failed: key rejected (HTTP 403). Re-pair with Pair Bridge. Press the link button and click Pair Bridge (it is the same bridge, so Forget Pairing is not needed); Status returns to Paired and the feed reconnects. The lights and scenes you then add back in the Hue app normally come with new identities, so after the first full read every Hue Light device reads Gone from the Hue bridge since … and you re-point each with Re-link Device To Light (the hints by name make each one quick, but it is one dialog per device) and each scene device with Re-link Scene. Your Control4 programming survives.
  • Restore from a Hue backup, or a bridge moved to new hardware with the Hue app's transfer. The bridge's identifier belongs to its hardware. Restored onto the same bridge, it keeps its identifier: if the restore brings the same lights back under their old identities then the driver sees nothing wrong and links them again by itself; only devices reading Gone from the Hue bridge … need Re-link Device To Light. If the restored bridge no longer accepts the pairing (Status reads Key rejected by the bridge …), press the link button and Pair Bridge. Moved to new hardware, it is a different bridge to the driver, as in the next case: Forget Pairing, then press the new bridge's link button and click Pair Bridge; lights and scenes that kept their identities in the transfer keep their links, and the first full read shows which did not. Expect few re-links, possibly none; read Lights and Attention rather than assume. Note that a Composer project backup does not back up the Hue bridge; use the Hue app's backup for that.
  • A different bridge at the same address. If a replacement bridge answers at the old address, the driver finds another identifier there at its next start or after a change of Bridge IP Address: Status reads Bridge at 10.0.2.143 reports ID 001788fffe456def, expected 001788fffe123abc; nothing is sent until the address is corrected, and the replacement receives nothing that carries the pairing. A bridge swapped while the driver runs refuses the pairing first, when the feed reconnects: Status reads Key rejected by the bridge (HTTP 403): re-pair with Pair Bridge, as after a factory reset. Either way, Pair Bridge refuses the replacement until you have clicked Forget Pairing: Pairing failed: Bridge at 10.0.2.143 reports ID 001788fffe456def, expected 001788fffe123abc; run Forget Pairing first to pair with another bridge, without asking it for a key. Click Forget Pairing, press the replacement's link button and click Pair Bridge; the devices then read Gone and you re-link each to its light as above. Taking a replacement bridge over automatically is not supported in this version.

8.9 A Control4 project restored from a backup, or a new controller

Where things are kept: the pairing key, the list of which device is which light or scene, the memory of deleted devices and the room mappings are all stored on the Hue Bridge device on the controller. Each Hue Light, Hue Scene and Hue Motion Sensor device also remembers its own Hue identity, name and model. Whether the bridge's store comes back when a project is restored from a Composer backup, or moved to a replacement controller, has not been verified; the devices' own memories travel with the project. So after a restore or a controller replacement:

  1. Read the bridge's Status. If it says Not paired: press the link button, then run Pair Bridge, press the link button and click Pair Bridge. If it says Paired, nothing was lost on that side.
  2. Wait for Event Stream to say Connected since and read Lights. If it shows all your devices as imported, you are done.
  3. Otherwise click Link Devices: every connected device that remembers its light or scene is adopted by that identity (most are adopted by themselves the moment the bridge starts, so expect All 5 bound devices are registered | Scenes: All 2 bound scene devices are registered | Zone lights: All 2 bound zone light devices are registered | Motion: No motion devices are bound to this bridge or a Linked … part). A device reading Not bound to a Hue Bridge needs its connection made again first (8.6).
  4. Check Room Mappings; if it reads None; use Map Rooms By Name, click Map Rooms By Name and fix the rest with Set Room Mapping. Programming, rooms and names are Composer's and come back with the project.

8.10 A zone or room renamed, emptied or deleted in Hue

A zone light or room light (chapter 7) lives through the same cases as a light, with the zone or room in place of the light. The rule is the same: the device stays, its link is re-pointed, and nothing is dropped by itself.

  • Renamed in the Hue app. The device's Hue Name and Status follow; its Composer name stays yours, and Attention reports it as an item of its own, after the lights' and the scenes': 1 zone light name differs from the Composer names: Accent is Sofa in Hue (Adopt Hue Names or Accept Names As They Are) after the zone Accent was renamed Sofa in the Hue app. Adopt Hue Names answers in its third part, Zone lights: Renamed 1 zone light device: Accent → Sofa (device 1203), Accept Names As They Are with Zone lights: Accepted 1 zone light name difference: Accent (Hue: Sofa), and the device's Adopt Hue Name as for a light; a room light's adopted name is the new room name with Lights added. 9. Scenes

A Hue scene is a saved look for one room or zone. The driver can show any scene as a Hue Scene device, a dimmer in a Control4 room: ON plays (recalls, in Hue's word) the scene on the bridge, OFF turns the scene's Hue room or zone off, the slider brightens or dims the room or zone while keeping the scene's look, and the device follows what happens in Hue. Hue creates about seven default scenes per room, so scenes are never imported automatically; you pick the ones you want. Hue's time-of-day scenes, which switch between ordinary scenes through the day, can become Hue Scene devices too; 9.11 says how they differ.

9.4 What the tile and slider do

  • ON (a tap on the tile, or programming turning it on) plays the scene on the bridge, the way the Recall As property says. The member lights take the scene within about a second and the tile shows ON with the room or zone's brightness. If the bridge does not confirm within two seconds the tile shows ON anyway; if the request then fails, the tile drops back, and the next full read corrects it in any case. A ramp time given by programming (or by an Advanced Lighting Scene) is passed to Hue as the time to fade into the scene (Hue accepts it; how long the fade takes has not been measured on the house system); a tap on a Navigator uses a short fade.
  • OFF turns the scene's whole Hue room or zone off, every light in it, imported or not, with one request to the bridge. The tile shows OFF. Remember that a zone can span several Control4 rooms, so OFF on a zone scene can turn off lights in rooms other than the one the scene device sits in. A zone that has no lights in it is reported off without any request.
  • The slider shows the brightness of the scene's Hue room or zone as Hue reports it (the average of the lights that are on, in whole percents), 0 % while the scene is off. Moving it while the scene is on brightens or dims the room or zone relative to where it is, keeping the scene's colours and the differences between its lights, as Hue's own dimmer switch does; 0 % turns the room or zone off. Moving it while the scene is off plays the scene at that brightness, all its lights at that one level; 100 % from off plays the scene exactly as designed, each light at its own level, as ON does. During a ramp (a keypad hold or a slow fade) the percentage on Navigators updates at most once a second.
  • When the tile reads on. The scene reads on from the moment it is played, from Control4, a keypad, the Hue app or a time-of-day scene's schedule (9.11), until its room or zone is turned off (from anywhere, including the Hue app or a Hue dimmer switch) or another scene of the same room or zone is played. Dimming or changing its lights afterwards (the slider, a keypad, a light's own tile, the Hue app, a Hue dimmer switch) does not turn it off, even when Hue itself no longer calls the scene active (Hue stops when a single light of the scene is changed; a dim of the whole room or zone leaves the scene active); the tile shows the new percentage instead. An ON tile therefore means "this was the last scene played here and its room or zone is on", and programming that fires "when this scene turns off" fires when the room or zone goes off or another of its scenes is played. The on state survives a controller restart. This is how the tile reads with On State Follows set to This scene (9.5).
  • With On State Follows set to Room/zone lights (the default), the scene also reads on whenever any light of its Hue room or zone is on, however it was turned on: another scene, the Hue app, a Hue switch or automation, or a Hue Light device in Control4. It reads off only when every light of the room or zone is off. The tile, the slider's percentage (the room or zone's brightness), the keypad LEDs and the on and off events all follow this one state. While only the lights are on, TOGGLE and the slider work on the room or zone as they do while the scene is on: TOGGLE turns it off, and the slider brightens or dims it instead of playing the scene. ON and a tap on a keypad's top button still play the scene. A keypad on a scene device therefore shows whether its room or zone is lit. Two scene devices of the same room show on together, so pick This scene where a keypad should show which scene is playing.

A time-of-day scene's device differs in a few points: it reads on exactly while Hue runs its schedule, and a level from off starts the schedule before moving to the level (9.11).

With a zone light of the same zone. If the zone also has a zone light (chapter 7), the two kinds of tile sit side by side and both follow the same reports from the bridge. Move the zone light's slider, or hold its keypad button, while a scene plays: both tiles show the new level and the scene stays on (its rule above). Turn the zone light off: Hue ends the scene, so the scene tile goes off and the zone light goes off. Turn the zone light on again: it comes on at 100 % on every lamp and the scene tile stays off, because on is a level, not a look. Play a scene, from anywhere: the zone light's tile goes on at the scene's average brightness and shows the colour of the first lamp the scene lit. A room light and the scenes of its Hue room behave the same way.

9.10 Programming with scenes

To Control4 a Hue Scene device is a dimmer. In programming, turn it on to play the scene, off to turn the room or zone off, or set a level to brighten or dim the room or zone (while the scene is off, a level plays it at that brightness, and 100 % plays it as designed); schedule it, put it behind a keypad button, or include it in an Advanced Lighting Scene next to other lights (the Hue lights themselves can be members too). Its on state follows the rule in 9.4, so a Hue scene played from the Hue app is visible to Control4 as the scene being on, and a dim in the Hue app as a new level. A time-of-day scene is a dimmer too: on starts its schedule, off stops it and turns the room or zone off, and its on and off events follow Hue's own word on whether the schedule is running (9.11). Chapter 11 has the details.

9.11 Time-of-day scenes

Besides ordinary scenes, Hue has scenes that follow the time of day, such as Golden hours and Natural light; Hue calls them smart scenes. Each belongs to one room or zone and is a schedule rather than a look: the day is divided into time slots, each slot plays an ordinary scene, and Hue moves on to the next slot's scene by itself, fading over about a minute. The Bedroom's Natural light, for example, may play Energize from 07:00 and Relax from 19:00; a slot can also begin at sunset. Once started, Hue keeps the schedule running, slot after slot, until something stops it. The driver lists time-of-day scenes next to the ordinary ones and makes each into a Hue Scene device; the rest of this chapter applies to them, with the differences below.

Adding one. Add Scene lists every time-of-day scene that has no device, marked (time-based) and placed before the ordinary scenes of its room or zone: Bedroom: Natural light (time-based), Accent (zone): Golden hours (time-based). An ordinary scene that a time-of-day scene plays in one of its time slots carries that scene's name in brackets, Bedroom: Relax (in Natural light), in Add Scene and in Re-link Scene; it need not be imported for the time-of-day scene to work. Add All Scenes Of Room creates a room's or zone's time-of-day scenes too, after its ordinary scenes, and its Hue Room list counts them separately from the ordinary scenes: Bedroom → Bedroom (8 scenes, 1 time-based), 8 ordinary and 1 time-of-day. The bridge's Scenes line counts them as well (9.1).

Names. On the bridge the Hue app can give a time-of-day scene a number after its name, Natural light 2, say. The driver leaves one such trailing number out, in its lists and in the device's name: the Bedroom's device is named Natural light, and a zone's carries the zone's name in front, as ordinary zone scenes do: Accent Golden hours. Adopt Hue Name, Adopt Hue Names and the name differences in Attention follow the same rule, so the missing number never counts as a difference.

Raise Level By and Restore Saved Level

Besides the standard commands of a Control4 dimmer (chapter 11), a Hue Light has two programming commands of its own, for a light that should be brighter for a while and then go back to the level it had: Raise Level By and Restore Saved Level. Composer's Programming view lists them under the device with its other commands. They act only when programming runs them, never by themselves, and they work the same on a light, a zone light and a room light.

CommandChoicesWhat it does
Raise Level ByPoints: 1 to 100, default 30.
Ramp (ms): 0 to 60000, default 1000 (one second).		Saves the light's current level (0 when it is off), then sets the light to the saved level plus the points, at most 100 %, fading over the ramp time. Points are added to the percentage: 40 % becomes 70 %, 80 % becomes 100 %, and a light that is off turns on at 30 %. While a level is saved, another Raise Level By saves nothing new and does not climb: it sets the light to the saved level plus the points again. If someone has changed the light by hand since the raise, that level becomes the new starting point instead, so a raise never lowers a light someone turned up.
Restore Saved LevelRamp (ms): as above.If the light still stands at the level the last raise set (within 1 point, as Hue reports it), fades it back to the saved level, or turns it off when the saved level was 0. If the light was changed since, to another level or off, leaves it as it is. Either way the saved level is then forgotten. With no saved level it does nothing.

Every outcome is one line in the light's Last Action, for example Raised from 40 % to 70 % (saved 40 %), Restored to 40 %, Not restored: changed to 55 % since the raise; saved level forgotten or Nothing to restore. A device whose light is gone from the bridge, or whose connection to the bridge was removed (chapter 8), refuses both commands, says so in Last Action and keeps its saved level. A saved level outlasts a driver update, a Director restart and a controller reboot, so a restore after one still works. Both commands go to the light as a slider move does, with the same pacing (chapter 11, "Whole-room and whole-house commands"). On a zone or room light the level is the zone's, the average of its lit lamps, and both commands set every lamp to one level, as the slider does. The update that brought the two commands changed no capability, so it needed no controller reboot.

An example: two lamps brighter while someone is there

The wish: when someone comes into the hall, its two lamps get 30 points brighter than they are, and 5 minutes after the last movement they go back to what they were. The motion comes from a Hue Motion Sensor device, which stands for a Hue motion sensor or a MotionAware area alike (chapter 14); here it is named Hallway sensor, and the lamps Lamp 1 and Lamp 2.

  1. The delay. Select Hallway sensor, open its Properties tab, set Occupancy Hold Time (s) to 300 and click the row's Set button. The device then shows motion until 5 minutes after Hue reports that the motion has ended (14.7), and Hue reports that itself about 10 seconds after the last movement (measured on a MotionAware area on 2026-10-26; a sensor keeps a time of its own, 14.6). So stops sensing motion fires about 5 minutes and 10 seconds after the last movement, and a movement before then starts the wait again.
  2. Brighter. In Programming, select the event senses motion of Hallway sensor. Among the actions select Lamp 1, choose Raise Level By, set Points to 30 and Ramp (ms) to 1000, and drag it into the event's script. Do the same for Lamp 2.
  3. Back. Select the event stops sensing motion of Hallway sensor and add Restore Saved Level, Ramp (ms) 1000, for Lamp 1 and for Lamp 2 the same way.

Then, with Lamp 1 at 40 % and Lamp 2 off: someone walks in, and within a second or two Lamp 1 fades up to 70 % and Lamp 2 comes on at 30 %. While they move about, nothing more happens: the sensor keeps showing motion, so the raise is not repeated. When they have left, Lamp 1 fades back to 40 % and Lamp 2 goes off, about 5 minutes and 10 seconds after the last movement. Each lamp's Last Action says what it did.

A lamp someone changed meanwhile is left alone. Say someone sets Lamp 1 to 55 % during those minutes, in the Hue app, on its tile or with a keypad. When the motion stops, Lamp 1 stays at 55 % (its Last Action reads Not restored: changed to 55 % since the raise; saved level forgotten) and Lamp 2 goes off as usual. The reason: the lamp no longer shows the level the raise gave it, so someone chose a new level after the raise, and putting back the old one would undo that choice. A lamp switched off meanwhile stays off for the same reason. Its old level is forgotten, and the next motion raises the lamp from where it now is, from 55 % to 85 %.

Two things to keep in mind. The hold time belongs to the motion device, so every program that uses Hallway sensor sees motion until those 5 minutes are over; when only these lamps should wait, a Timer agent can make the delay instead (14.7). And Hue's own automation for the sensor or area keeps running beside this program (14.1): if it moves the same lamps, the restore counts that as a change and leaves them where Hue put them. For a MotionAware area, 14.13 says how to make its automation do nothing, so that Control4 alone moves the lamps.

11. Using the lights from Control4

Once imported, a Hue Light is an ordinary Control4 dimmer to everything in the project, and a Hue Scene an ordinary dimmer whose level is its room or zone's brightness. This chapter says what that means in practice.

On Navigators

Each Hue Light and each Hue Scene appears on its room's Lighting page with a tile and a slider. For a Hue Scene a tap plays the scene or turns its room or zone off and the slider dims the whole room or zone (9.4); a time-of-day scene's tap starts or stops its schedule (9.11). The rest of this paragraph is about lights. Tap the tile for on and off; the slider sets brightness, and the light responds within about a second. A tap that turns a light on, and "Turn On" in programming, mean 100 %, not the level the light had before or the power-on level set in the Hue app; use the slider or a programmed level for anything else. (The driver turns a light on at 100 % for Control4's ON command, and for TOGGLE while the light is off; whether a tap on the tile and "Turn On" in programming arrive as these, and so ignore a changed On preset, one of the keypad settings below, has not been checked.) The slider settles on the level Hue reports back, in whole percents; 0 % is off and any other level is on. A tile is disabled while the light is unreachable (chapter 10, Online). Changes from the Hue app or a Hue dimmer switch show on the tile within a second or two.

In programming and on keypads

In Composer's Programming view a Hue Light offers the standard actions and events of any Control4 dimmer: turn on, turn off, toggle, set or ramp to a level, and events when it turns on or off or its level changes. It also offers two commands of its own, Raise Level By and Restore Saved Level, for a light that should be brighter for a while and then go back to the level it had (chapter 10, with an example for a motion sensor). The events fire for changes made in the Hue app, by a Hue dimmer switch or by a Hue automation too, because the device follows the bridge; a "when Lamp 3 turns on" program runs however the light was turned on. A keypad button connected to a Hue Light works as with any Control4 dimmer: a tap switches it, a hold dims it, and the LED follows the light's state, including changes made on the Hue side; the next section has the setup. A Hue Scene is a dimmer: on plays the scene, off turns the room or zone off, and a level brightens or dims the room or zone (or, while the scene is off, plays the scene at that level, 100 % as designed); its on event fires when the scene is played, from Control4, a keypad or the Hue app, its off event when its room or zone turns off or another scene of it is played, and its level events follow the room or zone's brightness, including dims made in the Hue app (9.4). A ramp time given to a scene is passed to Hue as the time to fade into the scene (Hue accepts it; how long the fade takes has not been measured on the house system). A time-of-day scene's on event fires when Hue starts its schedule and its off event when Hue stops it, and a ramp time does not apply to starting one (9.11).

Physical switches and keypads

A Hue bulb has to stay powered, so the wall switch that feeds it cannot switch its load. A Control4 Switch V2 or Essential Switch can instead run as a two-button keypad whose buttons drive the Hue Light:

  1. In the switch's Properties tick Use as 2 Button Keypad and leave Buttons Control Load unticked. The switch's own load is then set by programming only; for a Hue bulb set it to 100 % (a "when the project loads" program, for example) so the bulb keeps power. Hide From Nav hides the switch's own light from Navigators, leaving the Hue Light as the one tile for that lamp.
  2. In Connections, select the Hue Light (or Hue Scene) device: its Top Button Link, Bottom Button Link and Toggle Button Link are listed under its Control Outputs. Bind the switch's Top button to Top Button Link and its Bottom button to Bottom Button Link (a drag between the two lists). A single-button keypad goes to the Toggle Button Link instead.
  3. Tap and hold the buttons; the Hue Light answers within about a second, like a tap on its tile.

Keypad settings. A Control4 dimmer normally lists its own keypad settings on its Properties page: click and hold rates, an On preset and the colours of its keypad button LEDs. Showing these settings changed what the two device types can do in Control4, their capabilities, so after the update that brought them they appear only once the controller has been rebooted from System Manager (chapter 3, "Updating to a newer build"); until then the defaults apply.

The scene's tile and keypad LEDs follow its on state (9.4). A time-of-day scene on a keypad (9.11): a tap on top starts its schedule with the current time slot's look, a tap on bottom stops it and turns the room or zone off, and a toggle button alternates. Its LEDs follow Hue: they show the scene on while Hue runs the schedule and off as soon as Hue stops it, for whatever reason.

The keypad's LEDs: the driver tells every connected keypad button whether the light or scene is on, whenever that changes, including changes made in the Hue app. A top button is lit blue while the device is on, a bottom button is lit blue while it is off, and a toggle button is lit blue while it is on; blue is the default, and the colours are among the keypad settings (see the note above). The switch's own LED Behavior decides whether the button follows the device: Push/Release only lights it while pressed, so pick the switch's option that follows the connected device.

Advanced Lighting Scenes

Hue Lights and Hue Scene devices can be members of Control4's own Advanced Lighting Scenes (the Lighting Scenes agent). A lighting scene sets a Hue Light's level, and its colour or colour temperature where the light supports them, with the scene's ramp time; a Hue Scene is a dimmer member with a level: while the scene is off, a level above 0 plays the Hue scene at that brightness (100 % plays it as designed); while it is on, a level brightens or dims the room or zone to it, keeping the scene's look; 0 turns the Hue room or zone off. A time-of-day scene as a member starts its schedule for a level above 0 while it is off, and then moves the room or zone to the level (100 % from off simply starts it); 0 stops it and turns the room or zone off (9.11). A "stop ramp" from a lighting scene cannot stop a Hue light or a scene's room in mid-fade, because Hue cannot stop a fade to a set level; the driver reports the last level it knew, and the next report from the bridge corrects the tile.

Whole-room and whole-house commands

A room Off, a house Off or a lighting scene sends one request to the bridge per Hue light, and the driver paces them as Hue asks of every app: the first ten at once, then ten a second. A room with many Hue lights therefore takes a moment to finish, about a tenth of a second for every light beyond the first ten, the lights going off one after another rather than all at once; a room with a handful of lights is done within a second. The one-request way to switch a whole Hue room or zone is a room light or zone light (chapter 7): its on, off, level and colour each reach the bridge as one request for the whole room or zone, however many lamps it holds, so in programming, in Advanced Lighting Scenes and on keypads address the room light rather than the lamps one by one. (A Control4 room Off still sends its command to every light device in the room, the room light included, so it saves nothing there.) A Hue Scene device's OFF is one request for the whole room or zone as well (9.4). Several room lights or scene devices turned off at once send one request each: the first three at once, then about one a second.

After adding devices

After importing lights or adding scenes, choose File » Refresh Navigators in Composer so the touch screens and the app show the new tiles. Renaming or moving a device needs the same refresh to show on Navigators.

Things that look wrong but are not

  • Event Stream shows a new "Connected since" time now and then, and its reconnect count rises. The watchdog (after ten quiet minutes) and the stream timeout (once an hour) reopen the feed on purpose; every reopening is a full read. Every minute instead: the feed shares the commands' queue (chapter 3).
  • Lights shows "5 imported" with nothing else right after a restart. The bridge has not been read yet; the full line returns within seconds of Connected since.
  • Attention lists dozens of new lights after pairing. Those are the lights you have not imported yet. Import the ones you want and the rest stay listed; that is a reminder, not an error.
  • A property you changed jumped back. You did not click the row's Set button (chapter 4).
  • Events Received climbs once a second. Something on the Hue side keeps changing (a dynamic scene playing, for example), and every bundle of changes the bridge reports counts.
  • A scene tile stays ON after its lights were dimmed or changed in the Hue app. A scene reads on until its room or zone turns off or another of its scenes is played; the tile shows the new percentage instead (9.4).
  • A time-of-day scene's tile went off although its lights are still on. Hue stopped the schedule without turning the lights off, for example because another scene of its room or zone was played; the lights keep that scene's look (9.11).
  • An ordinary scene's tile turns on and off by itself during the day. A running time-of-day scene plays that scene in one of its time slots; its tile reads on while that slot plays (9.11).
  • A room dimmed during a time-of-day scene changed brightness by itself. Check whether a new time slot began then: Hue plays that slot's scene as it starts (9.11), and whether that also replaces a dimmed level had not been watched on the house system as this is written.
  • The tile shows the level you tapped but the light did not move. The bridge is unreachable (Event Stream reads Disconnected); the command is delivered if the bridge is back within 30 seconds and dropped otherwise, and the tile corrects itself when the feed returns (chapter 4).
  • Online is blank on a working light. A light connected over Wi-Fi or Matter never reports reachability.
  • A zone light shows a colour that only one of its lamps has. Hue reports no colour for a zone, so the tile shows the first lit lamp's colour (chapter 7).
  • A zone light's lamps lag a fast slider drag. Hue takes about one command a second for a zone or room; the lamps end where the slider ends (chapter 7).
  • ON on a zone light set every lamp to 100 % instead of their previous levels. That is what ON means on a Hue Light; use the slider, a scene or the Hue app for other levels (chapter 7).
  • A Hue Light, Hue Scene or Hue Motion Sensor reads Not bound to a Hue Bridge right after a restart. After a Director restart or a controller reboot this can last up to a minute while the device looks for its connection again (8.7).
  • A Hue Light or Hue Scene shows Control4's standard bulb icon. In a room's List View they always do; in the tree, a device created by a build without the Hue icons keeps the bulb (chapter 1).

The TLS check

The driver talks to the bridge over an encrypted connection (TLS) and tells the controller to accept only a bridge whose certificate comes from Signify, the maker of Hue: Signify's two root certificates ship with the driver. That is what keeps the pairing from being handed to a device that merely pretends to be your bridge. At every start of the driver and after a change of Bridge IP Address, once the bridge has confirmed its identity and before the first request that carries the pairing, the driver checks that the controller really applies this rule: it asks the bridge one question while telling the controller to trust only a decoy certificate made for this check, which no bridge's certificate matches, so the controller has to refuse the connection. While all is well you see nothing of it.

A refusal proves two things: the controller reads the driver's certificates, and it does not accept the bridge without them. It cannot prove that the controller has also stopped trusting the public certificates it keeps for websites, because those refuse the bridge's certificate too. On OS 3.4.2 the developer verified that it has, with a test against a public website that only a development build can make (Check CA Pinning), and repeats that test after a controller OS upgrade.

If the controller accepts the connection anyway, its software is not applying the driver's certificates, so that protection is not in effect. The driver then stops sending anything that carries the pairing, Status reads TLS check failed: nothing is sent (see Attention) and Attention reads TLS check failed: the controller accepted the bridge without its Signify roots; keyed requests stopped (see the manual): the lights and scenes no longer follow the bridge and their commands do not reach it, but nothing in the project is changed or lost. The likeliest cause is a controller OS version the driver has not been tested on (it was written and tested on OS 3.4.2). There is nothing to set: the check has no property and cannot be switched off. Report it as an issue at github.com/corytron/c4-hue-driver/issues, with the controller's OS version and the bridge's Lua output from the driver's start (with your own addresses replaced). The stop holds until the next start of the driver or a change of Bridge IP Address, when the check runs again; no check in between lifts it, and Test Connection sends nothing meanwhile.

If the check gets no answer at all (a timeout or a connection error), it proves nothing either way, and a device on the network that wanted to get past the check could always make it go unanswered. Whether the controller applies the driver's certificates depends on its software, not on the network, so the driver remembers on which controller software version (the Director version) the check last passed. On that version an unanswered check blocks nothing: the Lua output notes it and the driver checks again the next time it confirms the bridge's identity. On a version that has not passed it yet, as at the first start after an OS update, nothing that carries the pairing is sent: Status reads TLS check inconclusive (…): nothing is sent; checking again in 30 s with the reason in the brackets, the lights and scenes do not follow the bridge, and the driver asks again every 30 seconds. As soon as the bridge answers the check, everything resumes by itself. If it lasts, check that the bridge is on and reachable (chapter 4).

Diagnostics actions (normally not needed)

Test Connection asks the bridge one question over the normal connection and reports in Status (chapter 4). It is the first thing to click when lights do not respond: a Test OK means the controller reaches the bridge and the pairing is good. It sends the pairing only to a bridge whose identity is confirmed and only when the TLS check allows it, and makes those checks first when needed; a test that succeeds also ends a Key rejected stop (8.8).

Connect Event Stream and Disconnect Event Stream start and stop the event feed by hand. Disconnect leaves Event Stream at Stopped and the lights frozen at their last state until you connect again; the driver reconnects by itself at its next start in any case. Connect Event Stream does not reopen the feed while Status reads Key rejected or TLS check failed; Event Stream then reads Not connected: key rejected or Not connected: TLS check failed. Normally not needed.

Development builds add three diagnostic actions (Connection Test, Reload Credentials, Check CA Pinning) and two properties (Dev Server, Diagnostic Server); a release build carries none of them.

Debug Mode and Stream Address are the developer's; leave them as they are unless asked.

These are all the diagnostics a release build has. The developer's other diagnostic commands exist only in development builds; a release build removes them.

13. Limits, and a quick reference of all actions

Limits and what is not supported

  • One Hue bridge per project. A second bridge is not supported; a second Hue Bridge device should not be added (chapter 2).
  • A Hue zone or room becomes a controllable device only when you add it with Add Zone Light, one at a time; there is no "add all zones", and Hue's whole-home group ("all lights") is not offered (chapter 7). A zone light shows one lamp's colour, not a mixture, because Hue reports no colour for a group; its ON is 100 % on every lamp; a lamp that cannot do colour should ignore the colour it is sent (not seen on the house system as this is written, where every lamp has colour).
  • Hue buttons, dimmers and switches and entertainment areas are not represented. Hue motion sensors and MotionAware areas are, as a beta, with the limits of 14.16.
  • Hue cannot start a time-of-day scene at a chosen brightness or with a ramp time. A level below 100 % from off (the slider, programming, an Advanced Lighting Scene) therefore starts it with the current time slot's look and the driver then moves the room or zone to the level, so for a moment the lights show the slot's own brightness; a top button held while it is off starts it and brightens from there. A dim while it runs keeps the schedule running; when the next time slot starts, Hue plays that slot's scene (9.11), and whether it then also replaces the dimmed level had not been watched on the house system as this is written.
  • The bridge is found by its address only, IPv4 only. If the address changes you type the new one (chapter 4); a DHCP reservation avoids that. An address with no number below 8 works, but with the feed sharing the commands' queue (chapter 3).
  • Hue asks apps for no more than about ten light commands a second. Dragging a slider quickly sends many requests; the driver keeps only the latest value per light and sends at most ten a second across all lights (after a first ten at once), so the light lags the slider slightly during fast moves and ends where the slider ends. A whole-room command takes about a tenth of a second for every Hue light beyond the first ten (chapter 11); a whole-room OFF through a Hue Scene device or a room light counts as one request. For a zone or room as a whole Hue asks for about one command a second, so the driver sends the requests of zone and room lights, and a scene device's OFF, slider moves and keypad dims, at most one a second after a first three, keeping only the latest value: the tile moves smoothly meanwhile and a quick slider drag ends where the slider ends; a scene's ON is a request of its own and goes out at once (further ONs of the same scene within 0.3 s leave as one); a hold's start and stop go out at once.
  • Changes from Hue reach Control4 within one to two seconds: the bridge bundles its reports, about one bundle a second.
  • A command that cannot reach the bridge is retried for 30 seconds at most, then dropped; after a longer outage nothing old is replayed (chapter 4).
  • The driver never creates, renames, moves or deletes a Control4 device by itself. Importing, re-linking, forgetting, renaming and room mapping are all clicks; the only automatic housekeeping is the one-time Map Rooms By Name after installation, and linking a device again when its light or its connection comes back or when a connected device announces a light the bridge already knows (8.6).
  • Devices are never moved between rooms by the driver; Room Audit tells you what to drag.
  • Replacing the bridge itself is handled by pairing again and re-linking every device by hand (8.8).
  • Needs Control4 OS 3.3.0 or newer; tested on OS 3.4.2.
  • An update that changes a driver's capabilities takes effect only after a controller reboot from System Manager (chapter 3).
  • In a room's List View, Hue Lights and Hue Scenes show Control4's standard light icon; the Hue icons appear in the tree only (chapter 1).

A Hue motion sensor, and a MotionAware area of a Hue Bridge Pro, can each become a Hue Motion Sensor device: an ordinary Control4 motion sensor in a room, with Control4's own motion programming, fed by the bridge. It reads Open while Hue reports motion and Closed when Hue reports that the motion has ended, and it fires Control4's standard senses motion and stops sensing motion events as it changes. A sensor also brings its light level, temperature and battery. The bridge creates each device from an action you click, as it does for lights and scenes: one sensor or area at a time, or every new sensor at once (14.3).

A beta, written before the hardware. Motion devices come with release 1.1 beta 1, a prerelease for testers. The developer's house has a Hue Bridge Pro and no motion sensor, so this chapter describes what the drivers are built to do: the Control4 side can be tried without a sensor (14.6, the test actions), while what happens with a real sensor or area has not been seen yet. Where this chapter says expected, the testers' first reports will confirm or correct it (14.15).

14.1 What a motion device is

Each Hue motion sensor (indoor or outdoor) and each MotionAware area you choose becomes one Hue Motion Sensor device in a Control4 room. The device is the Control4 motion sensor: Control4 gives it the standard motion sensor's icons, its contact rows on the Properties tab (Invert Sensor, the debounce settings, State and Verified), its programming, and a place in the Control4 app under Security, Locks & Sensors (expected, with the history of its motion). There is nothing to connect: Control4 joins the motion sensor part to the driver by itself, and the bridge's action connects the device to the bridge. The driver's own rows follow Control4's on the Properties tab, under Advanced Properties.

Besides motion, a sensor's device shows the sensor's light level, temperature and battery, whether Hue can reach the sensor (Online) and whether it is switched on in the Hue app (Enabled), each as a row, a variable, events and a conditional of its own; its temperature also leaves on an output that a Temperature Display or a thermostat can use (14.8). An area senses motion only: its device hides those rows and leaves their variables at 0.

The driver only reads. It never switches a sensor or an area on or off, never changes a sensitivity, a name or an area, and never touches a Hue automation: all of that stays in the Hue app. Hue's own automations for a sensor or an area keep running beside Control4's programming; neither stops the other.

14.2 Installing the motion driver

Motion devices run a fourth driver file, hue_motion.c4z, which comes with the other three from 1.1 beta 1 on. Install all four at once through Driver » Add or Update Driver or Agent…, in place, as chapter 3 describes. The motion driver does not change what any device type can do in Control4, so Composer is not expected to ask Restart Director? (if it does, reboot the controller as chapter 3 says). As with lights and scenes, do not add a Hue Motion Sensor from the Items pane: the bridge creates each one, connected and linked (a device added by hand: 14.12).

After the install the bridge shows a new row, Motion Sensors (14.5), and six new actions: Add Motion Sensor, Add All New Motion Sensors, Add Motion Area, Re-link Motion Device, Forget Motion Device and Log Motion Diagnostics (chapter 13 has them all in one table).

14.6 What programming gets

From Control4's motion sensor, the same as on any other:

  • The events senses motion (the sensor turned Open) and stops sensing motion (it turned Closed).
  • The conditionals is sensing motion, is not sensing motion and State is Verified, and the loops built on them.
  • The variables ContactState, StateVerified and LastActionTime. Control4 counts a closed contact as true, so ContactState is expected to read true while there is no motion (reported by others, not yet seen here); the conditionals say it plainly.
Leave Invert Sensor unticked. The driver reports motion as Open, as Control4's own motion sensors do; ticking Invert Sensor on the device's Properties tab would turn motion into Closed and the two events round. The debounce settings next to it can stay as they are.

From the driver, on the same device:

Variables: Online and Enabled (true or false), Light Level (lux, a whole number, 0 when dark), Temperature C and Temperature F, Battery Level (percent) and Battery Low (true while Hue calls the battery low or critical). On an area the sensor-only ones stay 0, and Battery Low false. A variable keeps its last value when a reading is lost and is never set to a made-up one; until the bridge first reports, a new device's Online and Enabled are false and its readings 0. Conditionals: Online, Enabled in the Hue app and Battery Low (true or false), Light Level (lux) (0 to 100000), Temperature C (-40 to 60) and Battery Level (%) (0 to 100); the numeric ones compare whole numbers. Once released, none of these is removed, renamed or renumbered, so programming built on them keeps working through updates.

Timing. Hue reports the start of motion and its end separately. The end comes some seconds after the last movement, a time the sensor keeps itself (how long, per model, is one of the beta's first measurements); while someone keeps moving nothing new is reported and the sensor stays Open. The bridge bundles its reports, about one bundle a second, so motion reaches Control4 within a second or two, and a movement shorter than that may be reported as its end only, changing nothing.

After a restart. Right after a controller or driver start the device shows Waiting for the bridge, sends nothing, and Verified is expected to read No. When the bridge reports, the state is set quietly, without an event, if it is the one the device last reported; if it changed while nobody was watching (someone walked in during the restart), the event fires once, late rather than never. State is Verified lets programming leave a sensor alone until then. After a lost event feed the same rule applies: what changed meanwhile is reported when the feed is back, while a whole visit that began and ended in between is never replayed (the bridge's Lua output notes it only while the bridge's Debug Mode is On).

Trying it without a sensor. The device's Test: Motion and Test: No Motion actions make it read Open or Closed and fire senses motion or stops sensing motion as if Hue had reported it, so programming can be tried without walking past a sensor, or without a sensor at all; Last Action reads Test: motion sent; Hue's state returns within 30 s (Test: no motion sent; …). At the next motion report from the bridge, or after 30 seconds, whichever comes first, the sensor goes back to Hue's state, hold time included, and fires that state's event when it differs from the test's: 30 seconds after Test: Motion on a quiet sensor it reads Closed again and stops sensing motion fires. A device that knows no Hue state (no sensor, or not linked) goes back to what it showed before the first test, Closed if it showed nothing.

14.7 Occupancy Hold Time

Many installations want a room to count as occupied for a while after each movement, longer than Hue's own few seconds, which Control4 cannot change. Occupancy Hold Time (s), a property of each motion device, 0 to 3600 seconds and 0 by default, keeps the sensor Open that long after Hue reports the end of motion, and only then reports Closed and fires stops sensing motion. New motion during the hold cancels it; the next end of motion starts it again in full. The device's Hue Motion row always shows Hue's own state, so during a hold it reads, for example, No motion since 14:05 while the sensor still reads Open. Set the value and click the row's Set button; it applies from the next end of motion. A hold interrupted by a restart starts again in full at the first end of motion after it, never shortened. A deliberate end (14.10) closes the sensor at once, without the hold. A failure (14.10) does not stop a hold that is running: Hue had already reported the end of motion, so the sensor still closes when the hold time is over. The default 0 reports exactly what Hue reports. A lights-off delay is better made in programming, with a Timer agent, so it can differ per room and per time of day; the hold is for a sensor that every program should see as occupied for a while.

14.8 The temperature output

A sensor's device has an output named Temperature in Composer's Connections view, of the kind Control4 thermostats use for remote temperature sensors. Nothing needs it: the Temperature row, the two temperature variables and Temperature Changed work without it. Two uses:

  • On Navigators. Add Control4's Temperature Display driver to the room (find it with the Items pane's Search tab; it comes from Control4's online driver database), then in the Connections view connect its Temperature In input to the motion device's Temperature output, by dragging as for any connection (chapter 3). The display is then expected to show the sensor's temperature on Navigators.
  • In a thermostat. Connect the output to a thermostat's remote temperature input where the thermostat offers one; Control4's own thermostat is reported to accept such a sensor. A Control4 thermostat is also reported to ignore a reading older than 15 minutes, and the device sends a reading when Hue reports a new one (every few minutes, or on a change) and again at every full read (chapter 5), so whether a thermostat keeps the value through a steady temperature has not been tried. Treat it as an experiment in this beta and report what you see.

What the output sends: the temperature in Celsius and in Fahrenheit, with the time the reading reached the controller, on every reading the bridge passes on (a new temperature, and the last one again at every full read and Refresh From Bridge) and when something is connected to it; once, when the sensor goes offline, is switched off, deleted or forgotten, or the device is disconnected from the bridge, that the value is unavailable, which lasts until the bridge passes on a reading again (a changed temperature, a full read or Refresh From Bridge), not merely until the sensor is back; and to a connected device that asks, the last reading or that it is unavailable. After a start nothing is sent until the first reading. An area's device never sends on it. Connecting and disconnecting are yours; the driver never changes a connection.

14.9 Light level and battery

  • Light Level reads, for example, 123 lux, or Dark when it is too dark for the sensor to measure; the variable Light Level holds the lux, rounded, and 0 when dark. Hue is expected to report the light level every few minutes, and sooner on a large change. Light Level Changed fires only after a move of at least 5 lux or 10 %, whichever is more, so that small changes do not keep programming busy; for a threshold ("when it gets darker than 20 lux"), test the Light Level conditional in a program run by that event.
  • Battery reads, for example, 85 % (normal) or 12 % (low): the charge and Hue's own word for it, normal, low or critical (where Hue's "low" begins is not published). The variable Battery Low is true while Hue says low or critical, the events Battery Low and Battery Normal fire when Hue's word changes between normal and low or critical (14.6), and the bridge's Attention lists a low battery at once: 1 motion sensor battery low: Hallway sensor 12 % (1 motion sensor battery critical: … when Hue says critical).
  • A reading that is lost keeps its last value, and so does its variable. While the sensor is offline (not reachable, deleted, forgotten or disconnected from the bridge) the row adds the time it went offline, for example 123 lux (old, 14:02); a reading switched off in the Hue app, or flagged invalid by Hue, keeps its last value without a mark. A sensor without one of the three readings shows Not available on that row. After a re-link the old sensor's readings stay until the new one reports.

14.10 When a sensor or area stops reporting

A motion state is not like a light's level: a "no motion" that never arrives can leave programming waiting for hours. The driver therefore tells two kinds of ending apart.

  • Deliberate ends are someone's choice and nothing undoes them by itself: the sensor switched off, or the area paused, in the Hue app; the sensor or area deleted in the Hue app; the device forgotten by the bridge or disconnected from it. The device sends "no motion" once, at once and without the hold time, so stops sensing motion fires if the sensor was Open, and then reports nothing more.
  • Failures may clear by themselves: the sensor out of reach or its battery flat, the bridge away, an area not running. The device leaves the sensor as it is and says in Status that the motion is not current, so nothing in the house is switched off on stale information. Programming that must not act on an old state can check Online, and right after a restart State is Verified.

14.11 A sensor replaced, deleted or forgotten

The rule is the one of chapter 8: the Control4 device is what your programming refers to, so it is kept and re-pointed, never deleted and re-created to fix something. Deleting a motion device deletes the programming that uses it, and Add Motion Sensor then makes a new device with none.

A sensor replaced. A new sensor is new to Hue, with a new identity; the old one is usually deleted in the Hue app, or left there silent with a flat battery. When the new one looks like the old one (the same radio, after a reset sensor was paired again, or the same name in the same Hue room) the bridge says so: Attention reads 1 new motion sensor on the bridge: Hallway sensor [Hallway] #31 (looks like Hallway sensor, device 1251; re-link?) (Add Motion Sensor), or (same radio as Hallway sensor, device 1251; re-link?). Do not add it as a new device (Add All New Motion Sensors skips it for that reason); re-point the old one:

  1. Open Re-link Motion Device.   2. Pick the device and the replacement and click OK. Last Action reads, for example, Re-linked device 1251 (was Hallway sensor) to Hallway sensor [Hallway] #31. The device keeps its name, room, connections and programming and now reports the new sensor.

In this beta a sensor's device can be re-linked only to a sensor, and an area's device only to an area: Not re-linked: device 1251 is a motion sensor; pick a sensor (… is a motion area; pick an area). 14.12 Names, rooms, a device added by hand, restarts and restores

  • Names. A device is named after its sensor or area when it is created; the Composer name is then yours. When the two differ, Attention says so as for lights (8.2), and Adopt Hue Names and Accept Names As They Are answer for motion devices too, in a part of their Last Action of its own. A motion device has no Adopt Hue Name of its own. The driver never renames anything in Hue.
  • Rooms. Move a motion device to any Control4 room; nothing changes for the driver. Its Hue Room row follows the Hue app.
  • A device added by hand. A Hue Motion Sensor added from the Items pane is bare: it reads Not bound to a Hue Bridge, its test actions work, and nothing else does. Connected in the Connections view (its Hue Bridge input to the bridge's Hue Lights output, which lights, scenes and motion devices share), it reads Bound; waiting for the bridge; rename it to the exact name of a sensor or area that has no device, and Link Devices links it by that name when exactly one such sensor or area has it. A device that remembers its sensor (after a restore, say) is linked the moment it is connected. Two devices that claim one sensor are listed in Attention, 2 devices claim Hue motion sensor Hallway sensor: 1251 and 1257 (Forget Motion Device on one of them) (Hue motion area … for an area): forget one of them and delete it.
  • A Director restart or a controller reboot. Each device reads Waiting for the bridge until the bridge reports, then goes on as 14.6 describes; one may read Not bound to a Hue Bridge for up to a minute first, as lights do (8.7). Nothing to click. A device that still sees no connection a minute after its start treats it as a disconnection (14.10): Closed once if it was Open, Online No with Offline, the temperature output unavailable.
  • A project restored from a backup, or a new controller (8.9). Each device remembers its sensor or area and is linked again when the bridge starts, or by Link Devices; the first report then sets the true state, firing the event once where it changed since the backup.
  • The bridge factory reset or replaced (8.8). After a factory reset the sensors have to be paired again in the Hue app and come back with new identities, and the areas have to be set up again: every motion device then reads deleted, and Re-link Motion Device points each at its sensor or area again (a sensor paired again keeps its radio, so the hint finds it). A bridge replaced through the Hue app's transfer may keep the identities; read Attention rather than assume.
  • The motion driver updated in place: every device keeps its sensor or area, its hold time and its programming, and reads Waiting for the bridge for a moment.

14.13 MotionAware areas

MotionAware is the Hue Bridge Pro's way of sensing motion with Hue lights instead of a sensor. An area is set up in the Hue app, for a room, a zone or the whole home, with three or four compatible lights, and needs the room empty for a short calibration; a bridge has at most four. The driver cannot create, change, pause or resume an area; it only reports the area's motion, through a device made with Add Motion Area (14.4).

  • An area's device reads Kind MotionAware area and Model MotionAware area, and its Status names the area and its room or zone, for example MotionAware area Kitchen motion area in Kitchen, MotionAware area Upstairs motion in Upstairs (zone) or MotionAware area Home motion (whole home) (a sensor's reads, for example, Hue motion sensor Hallway sensor in Hallway). It has no light level, temperature or battery, and its Temperature output stays silent.
  • The area's lights must keep their power: one switched off at the wall leaves the area degraded (Status counts the lights that do not respond, for example Area degraded: 1 of its 4 lights not responding), and an area that stops running needs its lights powered and the area saved again in the Hue app (14.10).
  • Hue keeps two motion reports for an area, one for when it switches lights and one for when it is used for security, and both follow the same walk a few seconds apart; the device shows motion while either reports it, and no motion only when both report none, so changing the area's use in the Hue app needs nothing here. None of Hue's security features (alarms, alerts) is used.
  • Hue's own reaction to the area (lights that come on with motion, time slots) keeps running unless you stop it, and then Control4's programming acts beside it. To let Control4 alone decide what the lights do, make the area's Hue automation do nothing; the area keeps reporting motion to Control4 all the same (checked on a Hue Bridge Pro, 2026-10-26: the lights stayed as they were and the device showed motion within about a second). In the Hue app, open the area's automation (the app asks for one when the area is created); keep a single time slot for the whole day; set what happens on motion to Do nothing, and what happens when motion stops to Do nothing as well, since its default turns the room off after ten minutes; and save: the change reaches the bridge only once it is saved. Do not switch the area itself off, and do not remove its automation: either stops the area reporting motion.
  • A square Hue Bridge v2 has no MotionAware: the Motion Sensors row says MotionAware not supported by this bridge, Add Motion Area lists no area, and an area's device on such a bridge (after a change of bridge) reads This bridge offers no MotionAware areas; Forget Motion Device removes it and Online No, and Attention lists it: 1 motion area device on a bridge without MotionAware: Kitchen motion area (Forget Motion Device). It cannot be re-linked on such a bridge (Re-link does not list it, and there is no area to point it at): Forget Motion Device and delete it, or keep it for a later Bridge Pro.
  • A Bridge Pro with older software may report an area only in part, without its name, room or health: the device reads Hue reports this area only in part; update the bridge and still reports motion.

14.15 For testers: Log Motion Diagnostics and the Beta feedback form

Nobody on the developer's side owns a motion sensor, so a tester's report is how this beta learns what Hue really sends. The bridge's Log Motion Diagnostics action writes what the bridge reports about motion to the bridge's Lua output, whatever Debug Mode says: first one pass over every motion resource on the bridge (the resource types the bridge serves; every motion sensor with its light level, temperature, battery, radio and software update; room and zone motion and light level, and sensor groups; the MotionAware areas and their candidate lights; one line per room or zone with motion; the Hue automations; and the counts of cameras, contact sensors and tamper reports, which this beta does not use), then, for ten minutes, every report the bridge sends about them, at most 200 lines. Names are replaced by name#1, name#2 and so on, Hue identities are cut to their first four characters followed by …, radio (MAC) addresses, the bridge id, IP addresses and keys are replaced by <mac>, <bridge>, <ip>, <key> and the like, and no line carries a time of day: an automation's times read <clock>, and the reports count their seconds from the start of the pass (+12.40s). Last Action reads at once Motion diagnostics start in 20 seconds: open this bridge's Lua tab now. The pass and 10 minutes of events follow; copy them into the Beta feedback issue.; the Lua output ends the pass with Hue Bridge: diag: Motion diagnostics: pass done (3 sensors, 2 areas, 4 automations); capturing events for 10 minutes.; and when the ten minutes are over, Last Action and the Lua output read Motion diagnostics done: 57 items of events logged. Copy the Lua output into the Beta feedback issue. (Motion diagnostics stopped: the bridge did not answer (…). See the Lua output. when the bridge never answered; there is then no capture).

  1. Leave the bridge's Debug Mode Off: its extra lines carry the bridge's address, which the diagnostics' own lines never do. Select the bridge and open its Lua tab first. Composer shows a device's Lua output only while its Lua tab is open, and only the lines that arrive meanwhile.
  2. Open the Actions tab and click Log Motion Diagnostics. Last Action says the pass starts in 20 seconds: go back to the Lua tab before then and leave it open. (If the copied text starts in the middle, without the first lines about the bridge and what it serves, run the action again: a second click stops what the first one still does and starts over, with a new 20-second wait, the pass and ten minutes of events; the Lua output notes restarted: the pass starts again in 20 s.)
  3. During the ten minutes: walk past each sensor, wait until it reads Closed, walk past again; switch on a lamp next to a sensor, or cover it, to change its light level; walk through each MotionAware area.
  4. When the Lua output (or Last Action) reads Motion diagnostics done: …, about ten minutes after the pass, copy the lines from the Lua output.
  5. Read them before posting. They should hold no name, no full identity, no address, no bridge id and no time of day. If you see anything private, replace it with a placeholder or delete the line.

Then report on GitHub, at github.com/corytron/c4-hue-driver/issues: New issue » Beta feedback. The form asks for the prerelease version and the bridge's Driver Version, the Control4 OS and Composer Pro versions, the bridge's model and software (its Bridge Model and Bridge Firmware rows), the devices involved (for example the sensor models as the Hue app shows them), what you did and saw, and the Lua output. Replace your LAN addresses and the bridge id with placeholders (10.0.2.143 for the bridge, 001788fffe123abc for the bridge id) and never paste an application key; the driver never shows one. If you would rather not use GitHub, tell the person who sent you the beta.

What a first report answers best: which sensors and how many areas you have; whether a walk past turns the device Open and senses motion fires, and how many seconds after the last movement it turns Closed; whether the device appears in the Control4 app under Locks & Sensors with its history; whether a Temperature Display connected to the output shows the temperature; whether light level, temperature and battery agree with the Hue app; and, if a reboot suits you, whether the sensor comes back after it without a false event, with Verified Yes.

14.16 Not in this beta

  • No changes to Hue. Switching a sensor or an area on or off, its sensitivity, names, creating or pausing areas and Hue automations stay in the Hue app.
  • No room, zone or home motion (the motion Hue reports for a whole room from all its sensors), no Hue sensor groups: one device per sensor or area only. The diagnostics log them.
  • No Hue Secure cameras, contact sensors, buttons, dimmers or switches as devices. The diagnostics count cameras and contact sensors.
  • No "add all" for areas: Add All New Motion Sensors adds sensors only; areas come one at a time (14.4).
  • No re-link from a sensor to an area or back.
  • No history entries for battery or Online in the Control4 app; motion history, if the app shows one, is Control4's own.
  • Like the other drivers, the motion driver is meant for OS 3.3.0 and newer and was written against OS 3.4.2; it has not met a real sensor on any controller yet.

Glossary

Advanced Lighting Scene
Control4's own kind of lighting scene, made in the Lighting Scenes agent: a saved set of levels for several Control4 lights. Hue Lights and Hue Scene devices can be members (chapter 11). Not to be confused with a Hue scene.
Bound, bind, unbound
A device is bound when its Hue Bridge input is connected to the bridge's Hue Lights output in Composer's Connections view. The bridge's texts say bind it to this bridge in Composer for making that connection and unbound for a device whose connection was removed. Devices the bridge creates are bound from the start.
Linked, link, re-link
A device is linked when the bridge knows which Hue light or scene it stands for. Linking is the bridge's bookkeeping, binding is Composer's connection; a device needs both. Link Devices links bound devices the bridge does not manage yet; Re-link Device To Light and Re-link Scene point a linked device at a different light or scene. Not to be confused with the bridge's link button, which is pressed for pairing.
Button Link
The three connections of a Hue Light and a Hue Scene listed under the device's Control Outputs in Composer's Connections view, Top Button Link, Bottom Button Link and Toggle Button Link, to which the buttons of a Control4 switch or keypad are bound by a drag (chapter 11). The device then answers the buttons like a Control4 dimmer: tap for on and off, hold to dim.
On preset
The level a Hue Light goes to when its top keypad button is tapped, or its toggle button while the light is off: 100 % unless changed among the device's keypad settings (chapter 11). It applies to keypad buttons; Control4's ON command turns the light on at 100 %, and whether "Turn On" in programming arrives as ON, and so ignores a changed On preset, has not been checked (for a tap on the tile see chapter 11, "On Navigators").
Pairing
The one-time handshake in which the bridge issues a key to this controller after its link button was pressed (chapter 4). The driver stores the key on the Hue Bridge device and never shows it.
Hue identity
The long code the bridge gives every light and scene; the driver links devices by it, so names and rooms can change freely. Hue Light ID and Hue Scene ID show its start. A light deleted and re-added in the Hue app is expected to get a new one, which is why such a device reads Gone and has to be re-linked.
Gone, missing
The same state: a device whose light, scene, zone or room is no longer on the bridge. The device's Status says Gone from the Hue bridge (or Scene deleted in Hue, Zone deleted in Hue); the bridge's Lights, Scenes, Zone Lights and Attention count it as missing.
Mapping
The entry in Room Mappings that says which Control4 room a Hue room or zone stands for. It decides where newly created devices land and nothing else (chapter 6).
Room, zone
In Hue a light belongs to exactly one room; a zone is a second grouping that may span rooms, used mostly for scenes. In Control4 only rooms exist; a zone's scenes go to the room the zone is mapped to. Either can also be added as a single Hue Light, a zone light or room light (chapter 7).
Zone light, room light
A Hue Light device created by Add Zone Light that stands for a whole Hue zone or room rather than one light: one tile whose on, off, slider and colour go to every lamp of the zone or room in a single request; on while any lamp is lit, showing their average brightness and the first lit lamp's colour (chapter 7). A zone light is named after the zone, a room light Living Room Lights; the bridge's Zone Lights row counts them, and Re-link Zone Light re-points one whose zone was deleted (8.10).
Scene
A Hue scene is a saved look (levels and colours) for one Hue room or zone, created in the Hue app; Hue adds about seven defaults per room. The driver shows a scene as a Hue Scene device, a dimmer for its room or zone; playing it is what Hue calls recalling.
Time-of-day scene
One of Hue's scenes that follow the time of day, such as Golden hours and Natural light; Hue calls them smart scenes. It belongs to one room or zone and plays an ordinary scene in each time slot of the day, moving on to the next slot's scene by itself. Add Scene lists it as, for example, Bedroom: Natural light (time-based); its Hue Scene device starts and stops the schedule (9.11).
Navigator
Control4's word for the things you control the house from: touch screens, remotes and the Control4 app. A device appears there as a tile. File » Refresh Navigators in Composer makes them show newly added devices.
Director, System Manager
Director is the program on the controller that runs the project and every driver in it. System Manager is Control4's maintenance tool for the controller, opened from Composer's Tools menu with administrator rights; the controller is rebooted from it after an update during which Composer asked Restart Director? (chapter 3).
Event feed
The bridge's running report of everything that changes, which the driver keeps open all the time; the Event Stream property shows its state and the bridge's texts call it the event stream. Each report is a bundle the driver's texts call a container.
Full read
The driver reading every light, room, zone and scene from the bridge in one go: right after the feed connects and after every reconnection; Import All New Lights reads the lights afresh and Refresh Scenes the scenes. The Busy texts call it resyncing.
DHCP reservation
A setting in your router that makes it hand the bridge the same address every time it asks for one, so Bridge IP Address stays valid. The router's manual explains where; it is usually under the DHCP or LAN settings and needs the bridge's hardware (MAC) address.
Lua output
The driver's log, shown in the lower part of the device's Lua tab in Composer. A few texts in this manual say "see Lua output"; it is for the developer, you only need to look when asked.
Set button
The button that appears to the right of a property row the moment you change its value; only it hands the change to the driver (chapter 4). The keypad settings of chapter 11 take a change with Enter instead.

Hue Sync Box device

One Philips Hue Play HDMI Sync Box as a Control4 HDMI switch. Beta: this 2.0 beta does all that 2.0 plans for the box: it pairs with the box and shows its state, switches the box and starts and stops its sync with the room in each of the three ways a box can be wired, sets intensity and brightness by the time of day, and adds programming, four buttons for Navigator, the box's entertainment area, its name kept the same on both sides, a reversible switch-off of the box's own input switching and the take-over of another app's sync. Installing it needs one reboot of the controller (section 3). The Hue Bridge device's Documentation tab is the manual of the other Hue drivers.

1. What this device is, and what this beta does

A Hue Sync Box device stands for one Hue Play HDMI Sync Box: the box that sits between your sources (an Apple TV, a game console, a set-top box) and the TV or receiver, passes the picture through, and makes the Hue lamps of an entertainment area follow the picture or the music. In Control4 the box is an HDMI switch, with four HDMI inputs and one HDMI output, placed in the TV's room.

The device talks to the box directly over your home network, using the box's own local interface. It does not go through the Hue bridge, so it keeps working while the bridge is away. Nothing goes through the internet.

What this beta does

  • Pairs with one box (Pair Sync Box, then a hand on the box's button), and checks at every start that the box at the address is the paired one before it sends anything that carries the pairing (section 3).
  • Shows the box's state on its Properties page: its name, model, firmware, id and address; whether and how it syncs, and to which entertainment area; its inputs; its link to the Hue bridge; what it switches by itself; and how it is wired (section 14).
  • Switches the box with the room (section 5). With the box in line, picking a source in Watch or Listen makes Control4 send the box that source's input: the device wakes the box, switches it, starts syncing, and tells Control4 the input changed only once the box shows it. Behind a receiver Control4 routes every source through the box's HDMI 1, and the device takes the source from the room. On a receiver's second output Control4 sends the box nothing, and the device follows a room you choose, Follow Room.
  • Syncs in the mode the source calls for: Video, Game or Music, from the input's type in the Hue app, from the route (a Listen source is Music), or from the source's Control4 driver; set per input (HDMI 1 Sync Mode to HDMI 4 Sync Mode) or per source (Set Source Sync Mode), or Do not sync (section 5).
  • Sets intensity and brightness by the time of day: Day, Evening (from an hour before the sunset) and Night, each with its own; a change by hand holds until the source changes or the sync stops (section 6).
  • Room Off and a source that leaves the box: power save, or stop syncing and keep the picture, as At Room Off and When The Source Leaves The Box say.
  • Keeps the box's entertainment area, chosen in Composer from the box's own list, and takes over the area from another app's sync when the box starts syncing (section 7).
  • Four buttons for Navigator, Hue Sync, Sync Brighter, Sync Dimmer and Sync Intensity, whose icons show the box's state (section 8), and programming: commands, events, conditionals and variables (section 9).
  • Hands the choice of input to Control4 with one reversible click, Sync Box Settings: the box's own HDMI CEC and hot-plug switching, auto-sync and power save on the TV's standby turned off, and put back exactly as they were with another click. The same action sets the box's wiring setting, also reversibly (section 10).
  • Keeps the box's name the same in Composer and in the Hue app (section 11).
  • Goes offline and comes back as the other Hue devices do: after 5 minutes without an answer from the box, and at its first answer (section 13).
  • Works with the Hue Bridge device when connected to it: the bridge tells it when a sync starts or stops, stops another app's sync for it, and names it on its own rows and in the Status of the lights whose lamps sync (section 12).
  • Writes to the box only what its job needs: what routing, the periods, the buttons and programming change (wake and power save, the input, the sync, its mode, intensity, brightness and area), the box's name after a rename, and its input switching and its wiring setting only on a click of Sync Box Settings. Never its link to the Hue bridge or its picture settings.

The device's Composer frame (the HDMI switch, the four buttons and every connection) was fixed in the first 2.0 beta, and this beta adds the buttons' icons, the last change of that frame: the later releases add what they do without your deleting and adding the device again or making its connections again.

2. What you need

  • A sync box linked to a Hue bridge. Supported: the original Hue Play HDMI Sync Box (four inputs; Signify now also calls it "4K"), the Hue Play HDMI Sync Box 8K (four inputs) and the Hue Play HDMI Sync Box 4K of 2026 (one input), each set up in the Hue app and linked to a Hue bridge with an entertainment area. The driver tells the models apart by the type the box reports (HSB1, HSB2, HSB3), never by the name on the packaging. Not supported: the 2026 4K box running without a bridge (its standalone mode), and the Hue Play Screen Sync camera.
  • The box on the same network as the controller, preferably with a fixed address: a DHCP reservation in your router. The box connects over Wi-Fi. The controller reaches it over HTTPS on port 443; no internet access and no Hue account are involved.
  • Control4 OS 3.3.0 or newer and Composer Pro, as for the other Hue drivers.
  • The driver file hue_syncbox.c4z, from the same release as the other Hue drivers, installed with them through Driver » Add or Update Driver or Agent…. Updates go the same way and keep the pairing; never delete and re-add the device to update it (section 3, "Forget Pairing and deleting the device").
  • The Hue Bridge device is optional for the box device, but recommended (section 12).

This page says "the Hue app" for the app that set up the box: the Hue app, or the older Hue Sync app. The device's own texts say "Hue Sync app".

3. Adding and pairing

  1. Add the device. In System Design, select the TV's room. In the Items pane, open the Search tab and type Hue Sync Box. Pick the Hue Sync Box by manufacturer Philips Hue with the model text Hue Play HDMI Sync Box (local API) (the search may find other drivers for the box too) and double-click it. One device per box. The Hue Bridge never adds a box device: its Add From Hue and Import All New do not list boxes.
  2. Status reads Not paired: run Pair Sync Box. The device looks for sync boxes on the local network as soon as it is added, and then every hour until it is paired; once it found one, Status reads, for example, Not paired: 1 Hue sync box found (run Pair Sync Box).
  3. Run Pair Sync Box on the Actions tab. Under Box, pick the box from the boxes found, or pick Type an address and enter the box's address under Address (the Hue app shows it in the box's network information). If the list holds no box yet, the device searches again: open the action again a few seconds later, or type the address. Click OK.
  4. The device checks the box first. Before it asks for a pairing, it makes sure the address answers over HTTPS with a certificate from the sync boxes' own root certificate, which ships with the driver, and that the box reports its id. Then the pairing window opens for 2 minutes: Status reads Hold the button on the sync box for 3 seconds (1:58 left), counting down every 10 seconds, and Last Action says the same once.
  5. Hold the button on the box for about 3 seconds, until its light blinks green. The box should be on and not syncing while you pair it.
  6. Paired. Last Action reads, for example, Paired with Hue Sync Box (8K) #4E5F at 10.0.2.150 (#4E5F being the last four characters of its Box ID). The rows fill in, the device starts reading the box, and, when the device is connected to the Hue Bridge, it tells the bridge (section 12). When the device's name in Composer differs from the box's (a new device is named Hue Sync Box), Last Action says so a moment later and Sync Box Settings offers either name (section 11).

The box registers the pairing under the app name c4-hue and this device's Composer name (its first 32 characters). The device keeps the box's pairing key encrypted on the controller and never shows it: not in a property, not in the Lua output, not in Last Action.

Installing this beta: one reboot of the controller

This beta gives the four buttons their icons (section 8), which changes what the driver declares to Control4 about itself, and Control4 takes such a change only when the controller starts. So, once Add or Update Driver or Agent… has installed this beta:

  1. Composer may ask whether to restart Director. Answering Yes is not enough for the icons.
  2. Reboot the controller from System Manager (its reboot of the controller, not of Director). The pairing, the settings and the programming stay.
  3. Choose File » Refresh Navigators.

Until the reboot, everything else works, and the buttons show Control4's own button icon. This is the last change of this kind for the 2.0 box device: later updates take effect at once.

When pairing does not work

Last Action says why, in words. The reasons:

ReasonWhat to do
The 2 minutes passed without the button.Run Pair Sync Box again and hold the button within the 2 minutes.
Nothing answers at the address over HTTPS.Check the address, that the box has power and is on the network (the Hue app shows it), and that the controller can reach it (another VLAN, a firewall).
Something answers, but it is not a sync box.The address belongs to another device. Find the box's address in the Hue app.
It is a Hue Play Screen Sync camera.Not supported.
Another box than the paired one answers (when pairing again).The device pairs again only with the box it was paired with. To pair it with another box, run Forget Pairing first.
The controller's certificate check could not be confirmed.Before anything that carries the pairing is sent, the device checks that the controller really applies the driver's certificates, as the Hue Bridge does (the bridge's manual, chapter 12, "The TLS check"). If the controller accepts a certificate it should refuse, nothing that carries the pairing is sent; report it, with the controller's OS version. If the check got no answer, try again.
The box refused the registration with another error.Last Action keeps the box's own text. Make sure the box is on and not syncing, then try again.

A box of a type the driver does not know yet is paired all the same, with a warning; its rows show what the box reports.

At every start

At every start of the driver (an update, a Director restart, a controller reboot), the device checks the box at its address again before anything that carries the pairing is sent: the certificate, the controller's certificate check and the box's id. If another device answers at that address, Status reads Another device answers at 10.0.2.150 (expected #4E5F); Pair Sync Box to find it, nothing that carries the pairing is sent there, and the device searches the local network for the paired box. When it finds the box at a new address, and the box proves its id there, the device follows it and Last Action says so.

Forget Pairing and deleting the device

Forget Pairing, with Confirm set to Yes, removes this controller's registration from the box, clears the pairing on the device and stops reading the box. The rows clear, except Box Address, and the device tells the Hue Bridge it is gone (when it is connected to it, section 12). If the box does not answer, the pairing is cleared on the device all the same, and Last Action says the registration stays on the box until it is removed in the Hue app. Any other Confirm changes nothing.

Deleting the device in Composer does not remove the registration from the box: run Forget Pairing first. A box that was reset to its factory settings keeps its id but forgets every pairing: run Pair Sync Box again and hold the button.

4. The three wirings, and the connections for each

A sync box can be wired in three ways, and the box has a setting for each, made in the Hue app or with Sync Box Settings (below). The device reads that setting and shows it on its Wiring row. Make the HDMI connections in Composer's Connections view to match the cables. The box device has four HDMI inputs, HDMI 1 to HDMI 4, and one output, HDMI Out, each with a video and an audio connection; connect both alike.

WiringCablesThe box's setting, and its Wiring rowConnections in Composer
In lineSources → box → TV or receiverNeither ARC bypass nor solo mode: In line (sources → box → TV or receiver)Each source's HDMI output to one of the box's inputs, HDMI 1 to HDMI 4, as cabled; the box's HDMI Out to the receiver or the TV.
Behind a receiverSources → receiver → box → TVARC bypass on: Behind a receiver (ARC bypass on)The sources to the receiver as usual; the receiver's HDMI output to the box's HDMI 1, video and audio, as the cable carries both; the box's HDMI Out to the TV. Leave the room's endpoints as Composer sets them: the TV for the picture, the receiver for the sound.
From a receiver's second outputSources → receiver → TV, and the receiver's second HDMI output → box, with nothing on the box's outputSolo mode on: From a receiver's second output (solo mode)The sources, the receiver and the TV as usual, without the box. Connect nothing to the box's HDMI Out: that would give Control4 a path the cables do not have, and it may pick it. If the receiver's driver offers its second output as a connection, you may connect it to the box's HDMI 1; that changes no path.
  • The setting is the box's, changed only by a click. The driver never changes it by itself. To change it from Composer, run Sync Box Settings and pick, under What, Set the box to In line (ARC bypass and solo mode off), Set the box to Behind a receiver (ARC bypass on, solo mode off) or Set the box to From a receiver's second output (solo mode on, ARC bypass off); only the wirings the box is not in, and that its model and software have the setting for, are listed (solo mode needs a box at API level 14, as the Firmware row shows). Last Action then says what the box now does and how the connections in Composer must match the cables, for example Sent: the box is now Behind a receiver (ARC bypass on, solo mode off): every source reaches it from the receiver on HDMI 1. Control4's connections must match the cables: connect the receiver's HDMI output to the box's HDMI 1 and the box's HDMI Out to the TV (Sync Box Settings: Restore the box's wiring puts it back). Make those connections; the device does not change any. Restore the box's wiring puts back the setting the box had before the first such click. A change made in the Hue app works as well; the Wiring row follows at the next read either way.
  • A one-input box (the 4K box of 2026) shows four inputs in Composer too, as every model does; connect only HDMI 1.
  • No HDMI CEC. The driver relies on nothing the box learns over HDMI CEC: Control4 is meant to run the power, the signal path and the volume. The box's own CEC and hot-plug behaviour is shown on the Input Switching row, and Sync Box Settings turns it off with one reversible click (section 10).
  • Put the device in the TV's room. Behind a receiver and on a second output the device takes the source from a room, by default its own (Follow Room, section 5).
  • The connections decide what Control4 sends the box (section 5): in line, the input of each source watched through it; behind a receiver, HDMI 1 for every source that passes the receiver; from a second output, nothing.
  • The device shows only the settings of its wiring: HDMI 1 Sync Mode to HDMI 4 Sync Mode in line (only HDMI 1 on a one-input box); Follow Room and Source Sync Modes behind a receiver and on a second output; At Room Off and When The Source Leaves The Box in every wiring. When the box's setting changes, the device changes the rows it shows and how it drives the box, and says so in Last Action.

5. Switching and syncing with the room

Control4 owns the picture's path: which source a room watches, and through which devices. The device carries out what Control4 asks of the box and never steers it. What Control4 asks depends on the wiring (section 4), which the device reads from the box's own setting. In every wiring the box gets one change at a time: a burst of commands (a source picked while the room turns on, for example) becomes one change with the latest of each, and the device sends nothing the box already shows.

In line

  • Watching a source through the box. Control4 sends the box the source's input. The device wakes the box if it is in power save, switches it to that input and starts syncing in that input's mode (below), with the period's intensity and brightness (section 6), in one change. It tells Control4 the input changed only once a read of the box shows it. Picking another source on the box switches it again, without a power save in between.
  • Listening to a source through the box (Control4 routes only its sound): the box syncs to music, whatever the input's type.
  • Room Off: as At Room Off says, power save (the default) or stop syncing and keep the picture.
  • The room stays on, but its source no longer passes the box (the TV's own apps, a source on another input of the TV): as When The Source Leaves The Box says, stop syncing and keep the picture (the default) or power save.
  • When the box does not take a change (it does not answer, or a read does not show it), the device sends it again every 2 seconds, 4 times in all. Then Last Action says what did not happen, for example Not switched: the box did not change input, and Control4 is not told that the input changed.
  • A one-input box (the 4K box of 2026): a source routed to HDMI 2 to HDMI 4 is not switched, and Last Action reads Not switched: this box has 1 HDMI input. Connect only HDMI 1.
  • While another app syncs to the box's entertainment area, the box refuses to start a sync of its own. The device still switches the input, and then takes the area over through the Hue Bridge device (section 7). Where it cannot (no connection to the bridge, or the sync is another box's of this project), it leaves the other app's sync running and Last Action says why.
  • Changes made outside Control4 (in the Hue app, or by the box's own HDMI CEC and hot-plug switching) show on the device's rows and in its events and variables (section 9), never move a Control4 room, and are not switched back. The Watch screen can then show another source than the box: hand the choice of input to Control4 with Sync Box Settings (section 10).

Each input's sync mode (in line)

HDMI 1 Sync Mode to HDMI 4 Sync Mode say how the box syncs when Control4 routes a source through that input:

ValueThe box syncs
Automatic (default)To music for a Listen source (sound routed without a picture). Otherwise by the input's type in the Hue app: game for a game console (Xbox, PlayStation, Nintendo Switch or a game input), music for an AV receiver, a soundbar, speaker or music input, video for every other type.
Video, Game, MusicAlways in that mode on that input.
Do not syncNot at all: the box is switched to the input, and a sync that ran before stops.

Behind a receiver

  • Control4 routes every source that passes the receiver to the box's HDMI 1. The device wakes the box, switches it to HDMI 1 and starts syncing, and tells Control4 as in line.
  • Since every source arrives on the same input, the sync mode comes from the source the room is on: the device follows a room's power and current source (Follow Room, below), and each source has a mode (Source Sync Modes, below). A change of source changes the mode, even when Control4 sends the box nothing because its input stays the same.
  • Listen: Control4 takes the box off the picture's path, but the box keeps syncing, to music, while the room plays a Listen source, as long as the receiver sends the sound on to the box.
  • Room Off and a source that does not pass the box do what At Room Off and When The Source Leaves The Box say, as in line.

From a receiver's second output

  • Control4 sends the box nothing: no path that Control4 builds runs through it. The device follows the room named in Follow Room instead: Watch or Listen with a source that passes the receiver wakes the box and starts syncing in that source's mode; another source changes the mode; Room Off does what At Room Off says.
  • A source that does not pass the receiver (the TV's own apps) does what When The Source Leaves The Box says. The device can tell only when the receiver's second output is connected to the box's HDMI 1 in Composer (section 4). Without that connection, set such a source to Do not sync with Set Source Sync Mode, and it does the same.
  • Listen syncs to music only if the receiver sends the sound on its second output too. If the box sees no sound there, Last Action reads No sound on the second output: music sync did not start.
  • The device waits for the room to settle for a second, so quick changes of source (zapping) send the box only the last one.
  • At every start of the driver (an update, a Director restart, a controller reboot) the device takes the room as it finds it and sends the box nothing; only the room's next change acts. If the box was away while the room changed, the room's state is applied once when the box answers again, never the changes it missed.
  • Two boxes following one room each act on their own. Two boxes that sync the same entertainment area never take the sync from each other.

Follow Room

Behind a receiver and on a second output, Follow Room names the room whose power and source the device follows. Pick a value and click Set:

  • This device's room (Living Room), the default: the room the device is in, wherever it is moved. That is why the device belongs in the TV's room.
  • None (the box decides): no room. Behind a receiver the box then syncs in Video whatever the source; on a second output the device leaves the box to its own settings (its own auto-sync, as set in the Hue app).
  • Any room of the project by name, for a box placed elsewhere, such as in a rack room. The device keeps the room by its id: a renamed room is still followed under its new name. If the room is deleted, the device stops following, leaves the box as it is and says so in Status and Last Action, for example Follows a deleted room (Den, room 38): pick another in Follow Room; it never picks another room by itself.

When the room's variables cannot be read, Status reads, for example, Cannot follow Living Room: its variables do not answer, and the device tries again every hour and at each project load. Behind a receiver, when Control4 routes another room's picture through the box while the followed room's does not run through it, Last Action says so, for example Den's picture runs through this box (Follow Room): pick that room in Follow Room if it is the one the box serves. The device never changes Follow Room by itself.

Each source's sync mode (behind a receiver and on a second output)

Source Sync Modes lists the sources the device knows, each with its mode, for example Apple TV: Video; PS5: Game (guessed from its driver); FM Tuner: Music. The sources are those connected to the receiver ahead of the box and those seen in the followed room. A source's mode is, in this order:

  1. the one set with the action Set Source Sync Mode (section 15): Video, Game, Music or Do not sync; Automatic goes back to the rest of this list;
  2. Music for a Listen selection;
  3. Game for a source whose Control4 driver's file name names a game console (Xbox, PlayStation, Nintendo), shown as a guess: Control4 has no kind of device for game consoles, so check the guess and set the mode where it is wrong;
  4. Music for a tuner, CD player or disc changer;
  5. Video for every other source.

The device keeps each mode by the source's Control4 device. When a source with a mode of its own is deleted in Composer, the mode is kept and shown with (device gone). If one new device has the same name, the device offers it the old mode, once in Last Action and as an entry in the Source list of Set Source Sync Mode: picking that entry moves the mode. It never moves it by itself.

6. Intensity and brightness by the time of day

The box syncs with an intensity (how strongly the lights follow the picture: Subtle, Moderate, High or Intense) and a brightness (0 to 200 %; at 100 % the lights take the picture's brightness as it is). The device sets both by the time of day, in three periods, each with its own pair:

PeriodStartsIntensity (default)Brightness (default)
DayDay Starts, a clock time: 07:00Day Intensity: HighDay Brightness: 100
EveningEvening Starts, minutes from the sunset: -60, an hour before itEvening Intensity: ModerateEvening Brightness: 100
NightNight Starts, a clock time: 22:00Night Intensity: SubtleNight Brightness: 70
  • When they apply. The period's intensity and brightness go with every sync the device starts: a source routed through the box (section 5), Start Sync, Toggle Sync, the Hue Sync button. While the box syncs, they apply again at each change of period: at 22:00 a film that started in the Evening goes on at the Night's intensity and brightness. Nothing is sent while the box does not sync.
  • A change by hand holds. An intensity or a brightness set by hand (the Sync Brighter, Sync Dimmer and Sync Intensity buttons, a remote's or a keypad's button programmed with the commands of section 9, or programming itself) is never undone by a change of period. It holds until the source changes or the sync stops; the next sync starts with the period's values again. Only what was changed holds: after Sync Brighter the brightness holds, and the intensity still follows the periods. A restart of the driver keeps a hold.
  • The sunset. The device works out the sunset itself, on the controller, from the location set for the project in Composer (its latitude and longitude); it asks nothing of the internet. If the project has no location, if the location does not fit the controller's time zone, or on a day the sun does not set there, 19:00 stands in for the sunset, and the Periods row says so.
  • The Periods row shows the period now and when it ends, any value held by hand, and where the sunset comes from, for example Evening until 22:00. Sunset 16:08 today, from the project's location, or Night until 07:00; brightness set by hand. No location is set in the project: 19:00 stands in for sunset.
  • Changing a setting. Type a time as HH:MM (24-hour) and an offset as minutes, negative before the sunset and positive after it, then click Set. A value the device cannot read is replaced by the default, and Last Action says so, for example Day Starts: "7 o'clock" is not a 24-hour time like 07:00; 07:00 is used. A new setting counts at once: when it changes the period while the box syncs, the new period's values are sent at once (those held by hand left as they are), and Last Action names the period and what was sent, as at every change of period, for example Night: intensity subtle, brightness 70 %.
  • Odd settings. An Evening that would start before the Day (a large negative offset, a short winter day) starts with the Day, and there is no Day that day; one that would start after the Night does not happen that day. Night Starts may be after midnight (01:00). A change of the clocks for daylight saving moves no period: the Day still starts at 07:00.
  • After a restart of the driver the device takes the period it finds and sends nothing for a change of period it missed while it was down; the next change applies as usual.

7. The entertainment area, and another app's sync

Choosing the area

An entertainment area is the set of Hue lamps that a sync drives; it is made in the Hue app, on the bridge. The area belongs to the box: the box syncs to the one it is set to, and a Control4 room has none of its own. The Entertainment Area property lists the areas the box offers, by name; pick one and click Set. The areas whose lamps are in the box device's room come first in the list (they are found through the Hue Bridge device's room mappings, when the box device is connected to it, section 12), but none is chosen for you. Last Action reads, for example, Entertainment area set to Living Room TV. Programming changes the area with Set Entertainment Area or Apply Sync Settings (section 9).

  • Kept by its identity, not its name. An area renamed in the Hue app is still the box's area, under its new name. A change made in the Hue app (another area picked for the box) shows on the device as it is; the device never sets the area back.
  • An area deleted in the Hue app stays named on the device, marked (deleted in Hue) in the Entertainment Area row, and Last Action says so once: The box syncs to an entertainment area deleted in Hue (Living Room TV): pick another in Entertainment Area. The device never picks another area by itself, not even when only one is left.
  • An area made again under the same name. When the Hue app makes a new area with the name of the deleted one, the device offers it once in Last Action, Living Room TV is on the bridge again (made anew in Hue): pick it in Entertainment Area, and lists it in Entertainment Area; it never sets it by itself. Picking it re-points everything at once: the programming lines that named the old area use the new one from then on, and Last Action says so.
  • A programming line that names an area keeps the area it was written with. If that area was deleted and not re-pointed, the command is not carried out, and Last Action says why.

Another app's sync: taking over

Only one sync can run on an entertainment area at a time. When another app syncs to the box's area (Hue's sync for a PC, or for music, say), the box refuses to start its own. The device then takes over, as the owner of this project decided Control4 should: it asks the Hue Bridge device to stop that sync, and once the bridge has stopped it, the box starts its own. Last Action says so, for example Sync taken over: Hue Sync Box (8K) #4E5F took Living Room TV over from another app's sync, the event Sync Taken Over fires, and the area is the box's for as long as it syncs.

  • It needs the connection to the Hue Bridge (section 12): the bridge stops the other app's sync with its own pairing, which never leaves the bridge driver. Without the connection, or when the bridge cannot stop it, the device switches the input all the same, leaves the other app's sync running and says so in Last Action, for example Not syncing: another app streams to the Hue Bridge's entertainment area, so the box is busy.
  • Two boxes of this project never take a sync from each other. If the sync on the area is another Hue Sync Box device of this project (two boxes set to the same area), nothing is stopped: Last Action names the other box, for example Not syncing: Den Sync Box syncs to Living Room TV, and two sync boxes of this project never take a stream from each other, and this box does not sync until the other stops.
  • The device asks once per sync start; it does not keep trying. The next start (another source, Start Sync, the Hue Sync button) asks again.

8. The buttons in Navigator

The device comes with four buttons for a room's menus in Navigator (touch screens and the Control4 app). In Composer each is a device of its own, under the box device, named as below; rename them as you like.

10. The box's own input switching and its wiring: Sync Box Settings

A sync box can do things by itself, as set in the Hue app: switch to an input whose source wakes it over HDMI CEC, switch to an input when a source is plugged in or powered on (hot-plug), start syncing by itself when an input's picture appears (auto-sync), and go to power save when the TV goes to standby. The Input Switching row shows what this box does, for example By the box: HDMI CEC and hot-plug; auto-sync on HDMI 1, 2.

Control4 means to run the picture's path, the power and the volume itself, without HDMI CEC (section 4). A box that switches inputs by itself changes the picture behind Control4's back, and the room's Watch screen then shows another source than the TV. So, while Control4 routes through the box (in line, or behind a receiver) and the box switches inputs by itself, the Input Switching row is on Attention, for example Attention: The box switches inputs by itself (HDMI CEC switching on HDMI 1, 2) while Control4 routes through it: Control4 may show the wrong source (Sync Box Settings: Let Control4 choose inputs), and Status ends with ; Attention: the box switches inputs by itself (see Input Switching). Both go once the box no longer switches by itself. On a receiver's second output Control4 routes nothing through the box, and there is no Attention. It never changes the box's settings by itself: the action Sync Box Settings does, with one click, and undoes it with another.

11. The box's name

The box's name in the Hue app and the device's name in Composer stay the same, as for the Hue lights, scenes and motion sensors (the Hue Bridge device's manual, 8.2):

  • Renamed in Composer (select the device, F2, type, Enter): within seconds the box gets the same name, and Last Action reads, for example, Name sent to the box: Theater Sync Box (was Living Room Sync Box).
  • Renamed in the Hue app: the device follows in Composer at the next read of the box, and Last Action reads, for example, Name taken from the box: Den Sync Box (was Living Room Sync Box). The Sync Box row always shows the box's own name.
  • Renamed on both sides before the device saw either (while the box was offline, say): Control4 is the source of truth, so the Composer name wins and goes to the box: Both renamed: kept the Composer name Theater Sync Box and sent it to the box (the box had Den Sync Box).
  • The first time (after pairing, or after the update to this version): names that already agree are kept in step from then on. Names that differ are not changed by themselves. A new device is named Hue Sync Box, so this is the usual case after pairing: Last Action reads, for example, The box is named Living Room Sync Box and this device Hue Sync Box: Sync Box Settings offers either name, and a rename in Composer sends its name to the box, Status ends with an Attention: note saying the same, and Sync Box Settings lists Name the box after this device and Name this device after the box. One click settles it, and so does a rename in Composer.
  • A name the box does not take (longer than it allows, 32 characters as far as is known) is never cut by itself: the names wait, and Sync Box Settings offers the name cut to fit.
  • A device renamed every few seconds by both sides at once (two systems fighting over the name) stops syncing the name and says so; Sync Box Settings settles it.
  • The input names (Inputs, HDMI_INPUT_NAME) are the box's, set in the Hue app; the device only shows them.

12. The connection to the Hue Bridge

The box device has a control input named Hue Bridge, and the Hue Bridge device an output for sync boxes, Hue Sync Boxes (apart from Hue Lights, which the lights, scenes and motion sensors use). In the Connections view, select the box device and connect its Hue Bridge input to the bridge's Hue Sync Boxes output, by dragging the Hue Bridge from the lower pane onto that row. Several box devices can be connected to the one bridge.

The connection is optional: a box device that is not connected pairs, reads and shows its box all the same. Connected:

  • The device learns the bridge's entertainment areas. When it is connected, at every start and whenever its box's area or bridge changes, it greets the bridge; the bridge answers with its areas and whether the box is linked to this bridge, and sends them again after each read of the bridge.
  • It hears about syncs at once. When a sync to any area starts or stops, the bridge tells every connected box device, and the device reads its box at once instead of at its next regular read (at most once every 2 seconds).
  • The bridge stops another app's sync for the box. When the box is refused a sync because another app syncs to its area, the device asks the bridge to stop that sync, and the bridge does, with its own pairing, unless the sync is another box device's of this project (section 7). The bridge acts on such a request only for an area it has.
  • The bridge names the box. While an area syncs and this device's box has that area as its entertainment area, the bridge shows this device's Composer name instead of another app: on its Entertainment Areas row, for example Living Room TV (5 lamps, syncing: Living Room sync box), and in the Status and Last Action of the lights whose lamps sync, for example Syncing with the TV (Living Room sync box). Hue does not say who streams to an area, so the bridge goes by the area the box reports: if another app syncs to that area, the box's name shows too.

The bridge's messages are only hints: the device takes the box's state from the box alone. The bridge never passes its pairing key to the box device, and the box device never passes its own to the bridge. Removing the connection, or Forget Pairing, tells the bridge to forget the box. The Bridge Link row is something else: the box's own link to a Hue bridge, as set up in the Hue app (section 14).

13. How the device keeps up with the box

  • It asks the box. A sync box has no event feed, so the device reads the box's whole state every 5 seconds while the box is on, every 30 seconds while it is in power save, and every 60 seconds after three failed reads in a row; at once when the Hue Bridge reports a sync starting or stopping (section 12). Only one request goes to the box at a time. A change made in the Hue app or by the box itself shows within those times.
  • Offline after 5 minutes. When the box has not answered for 5 minutes, Online reads, for example, Offline since 21:04 and Status Offline since 21:04 (10.0.2.150 does not answer). The device keeps trying, and both rows come back at the box's first answer. A Hue bridge that is away never takes the box device offline.
  • The box refuses the pairing. If the box answers that it no longer knows this controller (after a factory reset, or when the registration was removed), the device stops reading it and says so in Status: run Pair Sync Box and hold the button again.
  • Debug Mode writes many more lines to the device's Lua output (the box's address, names and states; never the pairing key). It turns itself back Off 8 hours after it was set to On, and Last Action reads Debug Mode turned itself Off after 8 hours; a restart of the controller or the driver keeps the time that is left.

Not yet seen working

This beta was written against what other software that works with sync boxes shows about their local interface, and tested against an imitation of a box; it had not been run with a real box when this page was written. The first install with a box is expected to settle that the controller checks the box's certificate chain as it checks the bridge's, that the box answers the controller's encrypted connection by its address, and that the search finds boxes by mDNS. The texts in this page that depend on the box's answers, such as Inputs, Bridge Link and Input Switching, may change as real boxes are seen.

Routing was written from Control4's documentation and tested against an imitation of a box and of Control4. Still to be seen on a real system: in which order Control4 sends a box its commands at Room Off and when a source leaves it; what Control4 sends a box behind a receiver when the room changes source; and how a room reports its source and a Listen selection to a driver. Each may change details of section 5 in a later beta.

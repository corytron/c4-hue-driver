Hue Sync Box device

One Philips Hue Play HDMI Sync Box as a Control4 HDMI switch. Beta: in this 2.0 beta the device pairs with the box, shows its state, and switches the box and starts and stops its sync with the room, in each of the three ways a box can be wired. Intensity and brightness by the time of day, programming and the Watch-menu buttons come in the next beta. The Hue Bridge device's Documentation tab is the manual of the other Hue drivers.

1. What this device is, and what this beta does

A Hue Sync Box device stands for one Hue Play HDMI Sync Box: the box that sits between your sources (an Apple TV, a game console, a set-top box) and the TV or receiver, passes the picture through, and makes the Hue lamps of an entertainment area follow the picture or the music. In Control4 the box is an HDMI switch, with four HDMI inputs and one HDMI output, placed in the TV's room.

The device talks to the box directly over your home network, using the box's own local interface. It does not go through the Hue bridge, so it keeps working while the bridge is away. Nothing goes through the internet.

What this beta does

  • Pairs with one box (Pair Sync Box, then a hand on the box's button), and checks at every start that the box at the address is the paired one before it sends anything that carries the pairing (section 3).
  • Shows the box's state on its Properties page: its name, model, firmware, id and address; whether and how it syncs, and to which entertainment area; its inputs; its link to the Hue bridge; what it switches by itself; and how it is wired (section 8).
  • Switches the box with the room (section 5). With the box in line, picking a source in Watch or Listen makes Control4 send the box that source's input: the device wakes the box, switches it, starts syncing, and tells Control4 the input changed only once the box shows it. Behind a receiver Control4 routes every source through the box's HDMI 1, and the device takes the source from the room. On a receiver's second output Control4 sends the box nothing, and the device follows a room you choose, Follow Room.
  • Syncs in the mode the source calls for: Video, Game or Music, from the input's type in the Hue app, from the route (a Listen source is Music), or from the source's Control4 driver; set per input (HDMI 1 Sync Mode to HDMI 4 Sync Mode) or per source (Set Source Sync Mode), or Do not sync (section 5).
  • Room Off and a source that leaves the box: power save, or stop syncing and keep the picture, as At Room Off and When The Source Leaves The Box say.
  • Goes offline and comes back as the other Hue devices do: after 5 minutes without an answer from the box, and at its first answer (section 7).
  • Works with the Hue Bridge device when connected to it: the bridge tells it when a sync starts or stops, and names it on its own rows and in the Status of the lights whose lamps sync (section 6).
  • Writes to the box only what routing needs: wake and power save, the input, and sync on and off with its mode. The box's intensity, brightness and entertainment area stay as they are, and none of the box's settings is written: not its input switching, not its wiring.

What comes in the next 2.0 beta

As planned for 2.0; details may still change before the release:

  • Intensity and brightness by the time of day. Day, Evening and Night, each with its own intensity and brightness, applied when a sync starts and at each change of period; a change made by hand holds until the source changes or the sync stops. Until then a sync starts with the intensity and brightness the box already has.
  • Programming and Navigators. Commands, events, conditionals and variables, and four buttons for a room's Watch menu: Hue Sync, Sync Brighter, Sync Dimmer and Sync Intensity.
  • Settings and offers. The entertainment area chosen from Control4, the box's name kept the same on both sides, and one reversible click each to hand the choice of input to Control4 (the box's own HDMI CEC and hot-plug switching turned off) or to change the wiring setting. The driver never changes these by itself.
  • Taking over another app's sync. While another app syncs to the box's entertainment area (the Hue app's sync for a PC or for music, say), the box refuses to start its own. In this beta the device leaves the other app's sync running, still switches the box's input, and says so in Last Action.

The device already has its whole Composer frame: the HDMI switch, the four buttons above and every connection. They are fixed from this first beta on, so the later betas add what they do without your deleting and adding the device again or making its connections again.

2. What you need

  • A sync box linked to a Hue bridge. Supported: the original Hue Play HDMI Sync Box (four inputs; Signify now also calls it "4K"), the Hue Play HDMI Sync Box 8K (four inputs) and the Hue Play HDMI Sync Box 4K of 2026 (one input), each set up in the Hue app and linked to a Hue bridge with an entertainment area. The driver tells the models apart by the type the box reports (HSB1, HSB2, HSB3), never by the name on the packaging. Not supported: the 2026 4K box running without a bridge (its standalone mode), and the Hue Play Screen Sync camera.
  • The box on the same network as the controller, preferably with a fixed address: a DHCP reservation in your router. The box connects over Wi-Fi. The controller reaches it over HTTPS on port 443; no internet access and no Hue account are involved.
  • Control4 OS 3.3.0 or newer and Composer Pro, as for the other Hue drivers.
  • The driver file hue_syncbox.c4z, from the same release as the other Hue drivers, installed with them through Driver » Add or Update Driver or Agent…. Updates go the same way and keep the pairing; never delete and re-add the device to update it (section 3, "Forget Pairing and deleting the device").
  • The Hue Bridge device is optional for the box device, but recommended (section 6).

This page says "the Hue app" for the app that set up the box: the Hue app, or the older Hue Sync app. The device's own texts say "Hue Sync app".

3. Adding and pairing

  1. Add the device. In System Design, select the TV's room. In the Items pane, open the Search tab and type Hue Sync Box. Pick the Hue Sync Box by manufacturer Philips Hue with the model text Hue Play HDMI Sync Box (local API) (the search may find other drivers for the box too) and double-click it. One device per box. The Hue Bridge never adds a box device: its Add From Hue and Import All New do not list boxes.
  2. Status reads Not paired: run Pair Sync Box. The device looks for sync boxes on the local network as soon as it is added, and then every hour until it is paired; once it found one, Status reads, for example, Not paired: 1 Hue sync box found (run Pair Sync Box).
  3. Run Pair Sync Box on the Actions tab. Under Box, pick the box from the boxes found, or pick Type an address and enter the box's address under Address (the Hue app shows it in the box's network information). If the list holds no box yet, the device searches again: open the action again a few seconds later, or type the address. Click OK.
  4. The device checks the box first. Before it asks for a pairing, it makes sure the address answers over HTTPS with a certificate from the sync boxes' own root certificate, which ships with the driver, and that the box reports its id. Then the pairing window opens for 2 minutes: Status reads Hold the button on the sync box for 3 seconds (1:58 left), counting down every 10 seconds, and Last Action says the same once.
  5. Hold the button on the box for about 3 seconds, until its light blinks green. The box should be on and not syncing while you pair it.
  6. Paired. Last Action reads, for example, Paired with Hue Sync Box (8K) #4E5F at 10.0.2.150 (#4E5F being the last four characters of its Box ID). The rows fill in, the device starts reading the box, and, when the device is connected to the Hue Bridge, it tells the bridge (section 6).

The box registers the pairing under the app name c4-hue and this device's Composer name (its first 32 characters). The device keeps the box's pairing key encrypted on the controller and never shows it: not in a property, not in the Lua output, not in Last Action.

When pairing does not work

Last Action says why, in words. The reasons:

ReasonWhat to do
The 2 minutes passed without the button.Run Pair Sync Box again and hold the button within the 2 minutes.
Nothing answers at the address over HTTPS.Check the address, that the box has power and is on the network (the Hue app shows it), and that the controller can reach it (another VLAN, a firewall).
Something answers, but it is not a sync box.The address belongs to another device. Find the box's address in the Hue app.
It is a Hue Play Screen Sync camera.Not supported.
Another box than the paired one answers (when pairing again).The device pairs again only with the box it was paired with. To pair it with another box, run Forget Pairing first.
The controller's certificate check could not be confirmed.Before anything that carries the pairing is sent, the device checks that the controller really applies the driver's certificates, as the Hue Bridge does (the bridge's manual, chapter 12, "The TLS check"). If the controller accepts a certificate it should refuse, nothing that carries the pairing is sent; report it, with the controller's OS version. If the check got no answer, try again.
The box refused the registration with another error.Last Action keeps the box's own text. Make sure the box is on and not syncing, then try again.

A box of a type the driver does not know yet is paired all the same, with a warning; its rows show what the box reports.

At every start

At every start of the driver (an update, a Director restart, a controller reboot), the device checks the box at its address again before anything that carries the pairing is sent: the certificate, the controller's certificate check and the box's id. If another device answers at that address, Status reads Another device answers at 10.0.2.150 (expected #4E5F); Pair Sync Box to find it, nothing that carries the pairing is sent there, and the device searches the local network for the paired box. When it finds the box at a new address, and the box proves its id there, the device follows it and Last Action says so.

Forget Pairing and deleting the device

Forget Pairing, with Confirm set to Yes, removes this controller's registration from the box, clears the pairing on the device and stops reading the box. The rows clear, except Box Address, and the device tells the Hue Bridge it is gone (when it is connected to it, section 6). If the box does not answer, the pairing is cleared on the device all the same, and Last Action says the registration stays on the box until it is removed in the Hue app. Any other Confirm changes nothing.

Deleting the device in Composer does not remove the registration from the box: run Forget Pairing first. A box that was reset to its factory settings keeps its id but forgets every pairing: run Pair Sync Box again and hold the button.

4. The three wirings, and the connections for each

A sync box can be wired in three ways, and the box has a setting for each (made in the Hue app). The device reads that setting and shows it on its Wiring row. Make the HDMI connections in Composer's Connections view to match the cables. The box device has four HDMI inputs, HDMI 1 to HDMI 4, and one output, HDMI Out, each with a video and an audio connection; connect both alike.

WiringCablesThe box's setting, and its Wiring rowConnections in Composer
In lineSources → box → TV or receiverNeither ARC bypass nor solo mode: In line (sources → box → TV or receiver)Each source's HDMI output to one of the box's inputs, HDMI 1 to HDMI 4, as cabled; the box's HDMI Out to the receiver or the TV.
Behind a receiverSources → receiver → box → TVARC bypass on: Behind a receiver (ARC bypass on)The sources to the receiver as usual; the receiver's HDMI output to the box's HDMI 1, video and audio, as the cable carries both; the box's HDMI Out to the TV. Leave the room's endpoints as Composer sets them: the TV for the picture, the receiver for the sound.
From a receiver's second outputSources → receiver → TV, and the receiver's second HDMI output → box, with nothing on the box's outputSolo mode on: From a receiver's second output (solo mode)The sources, the receiver and the TV as usual, without the box. Connect nothing to the box's HDMI Out: that would give Control4 a path the cables do not have, and it may pick it. If the receiver's driver offers its second output as a connection, you may connect it to the box's HDMI 1; that changes no path.
  • The setting is the box's. The driver reads it and never changes it by itself. Change it in the Hue app; the Wiring row follows at the next read. A later 2.0 beta offers to change it with one reversible click.
  • A one-input box (the 4K box of 2026) shows four inputs in Composer too, as every model does; connect only HDMI 1.
  • No HDMI CEC. The driver relies on nothing the box learns over HDMI CEC: Control4 is meant to run the power, the signal path and the volume. The box's own CEC and hot-plug behaviour is shown on the Input Switching row.
  • Put the device in the TV's room. Behind a receiver and on a second output the device takes the source from a room, by default its own (Follow Room, section 5).
  • The connections decide what Control4 sends the box (section 5): in line, the input of each source watched through it; behind a receiver, HDMI 1 for every source that passes the receiver; from a second output, nothing.
  • The device shows only the settings of its wiring: HDMI 1 Sync Mode to HDMI 4 Sync Mode in line (only HDMI 1 on a one-input box); Follow Room and Source Sync Modes behind a receiver and on a second output; At Room Off and When The Source Leaves The Box in every wiring. When the box's setting changes, the device changes the rows it shows and how it drives the box, and says so in Last Action.

5. Switching and syncing with the room

Control4 owns the picture's path: which source a room watches, and through which devices. The device carries out what Control4 asks of the box and never steers it. What Control4 asks depends on the wiring (section 4), which the device reads from the box's own setting. In every wiring the box gets one change at a time: a burst of commands (a source picked while the room turns on, for example) becomes one change with the latest of each, and the device sends nothing the box already shows.

In line

  • Watching a source through the box. Control4 sends the box the source's input. The device wakes the box if it is in power save, switches it to that input and starts syncing in that input's mode (below), in one change. It tells Control4 the input changed only once a read of the box shows it. Picking another source on the box switches it again, without a power save in between.
  • Listening to a source through the box (Control4 routes only its sound): the box syncs to music, whatever the input's type.
  • Room Off: as At Room Off says, power save (the default) or stop syncing and keep the picture.
  • The room stays on, but its source no longer passes the box (the TV's own apps, a source on another input of the TV): as When The Source Leaves The Box says, stop syncing and keep the picture (the default) or power save.
  • When the box does not take a change (it does not answer, or a read does not show it), the device sends it again every 2 seconds, 4 times in all. Then Last Action says what did not happen, for example Not switched: the box did not change input, and Control4 is not told that the input changed.
  • A one-input box (the 4K box of 2026): a source routed to HDMI 2 to HDMI 4 is not switched, and Last Action reads Not switched: this box has 1 HDMI input. Connect only HDMI 1.
  • While another app syncs to the box's entertainment area, the box refuses to start a sync of its own. The device still switches the input, leaves the other app's sync running, and Last Action says that another app streams to the area. It does not try again by itself; taking over comes in the next beta.
  • Changes made outside Control4 (in the Hue app, or by the box's own HDMI CEC and hot-plug switching) show on the device's rows, never move a Control4 room, and are not switched back. The Watch screen can then show another source than the box: turn the box's own input switching off in the Hue app where Control4 should choose alone.

Each input's sync mode (in line)

HDMI 1 Sync Mode to HDMI 4 Sync Mode say how the box syncs when Control4 routes a source through that input:

ValueThe box syncs
Automatic (default)To music for a Listen source (sound routed without a picture). Otherwise by the input's type in the Hue app: game for a game console (Xbox, PlayStation, Nintendo Switch or a game input), music for an AV receiver, a soundbar, speaker or music input, video for every other type.
Video, Game, MusicAlways in that mode on that input.
Do not syncNot at all: the box is switched to the input, and a sync that ran before stops.

Behind a receiver

  • Control4 routes every source that passes the receiver to the box's HDMI 1. The device wakes the box, switches it to HDMI 1 and starts syncing, and tells Control4 as in line.
  • Since every source arrives on the same input, the sync mode comes from the source the room is on: the device follows a room's power and current source (Follow Room, below), and each source has a mode (Source Sync Modes, below). A change of source changes the mode, even when Control4 sends the box nothing because its input stays the same.
  • Listen: Control4 takes the box off the picture's path, but the box keeps syncing, to music, while the room plays a Listen source, as long as the receiver sends the sound on to the box.
  • Room Off and a source that does not pass the box do what At Room Off and When The Source Leaves The Box say, as in line.

From a receiver's second output

  • Control4 sends the box nothing: no path that Control4 builds runs through it. The device follows the room named in Follow Room instead: Watch or Listen with a source that passes the receiver wakes the box and starts syncing in that source's mode; another source changes the mode; Room Off does what At Room Off says.
  • A source that does not pass the receiver (the TV's own apps) does what When The Source Leaves The Box says. The device can tell only when the receiver's second output is connected to the box's HDMI 1 in Composer (section 4). Without that connection, set such a source to Do not sync with Set Source Sync Mode, and it does the same.
  • Listen syncs to music only if the receiver sends the sound on its second output too. If the box sees no sound there, Last Action reads No sound on the second output: music sync did not start.
  • The device waits for the room to settle for a second, so quick changes of source (zapping) send the box only the last one.
  • At every start of the driver (an update, a Director restart, a controller reboot) the device takes the room as it finds it and sends the box nothing; only the room's next change acts. If the box was away while the room changed, the room's state is applied once when the box answers again, never the changes it missed.
  • Two boxes following one room each act on their own. Two boxes that sync the same entertainment area never take the sync from each other.

Follow Room

Behind a receiver and on a second output, Follow Room names the room whose power and source the device follows. Pick a value and click Set:

  • This device's room (Living Room), the default: the room the device is in, wherever it is moved. That is why the device belongs in the TV's room.
  • None (the box decides): no room. Behind a receiver the box then syncs in Video whatever the source; on a second output the device leaves the box to its own settings (its own auto-sync, as set in the Hue app).
  • Any room of the project by name, for a box placed elsewhere, such as in a rack room. The device keeps the room by its id: a renamed room is still followed under its new name. If the room is deleted, the device stops following, leaves the box as it is and says so in Status and Last Action, for example Follows a deleted room (Den, room 38): pick another in Follow Room; it never picks another room by itself.

When the room's variables cannot be read, Status reads, for example, Cannot follow Living Room: its variables do not answer, and the device tries again every hour and at each project load. Behind a receiver, when Control4 routes another room's picture through the box while the followed room's does not run through it, Last Action says so, for example Den's picture runs through this box (Follow Room): pick that room in Follow Room if it is the one the box serves. The device never changes Follow Room by itself.

Each source's sync mode (behind a receiver and on a second output)

Source Sync Modes lists the sources the device knows, each with its mode, for example Apple TV: Video; PS5: Game (guessed from its driver); FM Tuner: Music. The sources are those connected to the receiver ahead of the box and those seen in the followed room. A source's mode is, in this order:

  1. the one set with the action Set Source Sync Mode (section 9): Video, Game, Music or Do not sync; Automatic goes back to the rest of this list;
  2. Music for a Listen selection;
  3. Game for a source whose Control4 driver's file name names a game console (Xbox, PlayStation, Nintendo), shown as a guess: Control4 has no kind of device for game consoles, so check the guess and set the mode where it is wrong;
  4. Music for a tuner, CD player or disc changer;
  5. Video for every other source.

The device keeps each mode by the source's Control4 device. When a source with a mode of its own is deleted in Composer, the mode is kept and shown with (device gone). If one new device has the same name, the device offers it the old mode, once in Last Action and as an entry in the Source list of Set Source Sync Mode: picking that entry moves the mode. It never moves it by itself.

6. The connection to the Hue Bridge

The box device has a control input named Hue Bridge, and the Hue Bridge device an output for sync boxes, Hue Sync Boxes (apart from Hue Lights, which the lights, scenes and motion sensors use). In the Connections view, select the box device and connect its Hue Bridge input to the bridge's Hue Sync Boxes output, by dragging the Hue Bridge from the lower pane onto that row. Several box devices can be connected to the one bridge.

The connection is optional: a box device that is not connected pairs, reads and shows its box all the same. Connected:

  • The device learns the bridge's entertainment areas. When it is connected, at every start and whenever its box's area or bridge changes, it greets the bridge; the bridge answers with its areas and whether the box is linked to this bridge, and sends them again after each read of the bridge.
  • It hears about syncs at once. When a sync to any area starts or stops, the bridge tells every connected box device, and the device reads its box at once instead of at its next regular read (at most once every 2 seconds).
  • The bridge names the box. While an area syncs and this device's box has that area as its entertainment area, the bridge shows this device's Composer name instead of another app: on its Entertainment Areas row, for example Living Room TV (5 lamps, syncing: Living Room sync box), and in the Status and Last Action of the lights whose lamps sync, for example Syncing with the TV (Living Room sync box). Hue does not say who streams to an area, so the bridge goes by the area the box reports: if another app syncs to that area, the box's name shows too.

The bridge's messages are only hints: the device takes the box's state from the box alone. The bridge never passes its pairing key to the box device, and the box device never passes its own to the bridge. Removing the connection, or Forget Pairing, tells the bridge to forget the box. The Bridge Link row is something else: the box's own link to a Hue bridge, as set up in the Hue app (section 8).

7. How the device keeps up with the box

  • It asks the box. A sync box has no event feed, so the device reads the box's whole state every 5 seconds while the box is on, every 30 seconds while it is in power save, and every 60 seconds after three failed reads in a row; at once when the Hue Bridge reports a sync starting or stopping (section 6). Only one request goes to the box at a time. A change made in the Hue app or by the box itself shows within those times.
  • Offline after 5 minutes. When the box has not answered for 5 minutes, Online reads, for example, Offline since 21:04 and Status Offline since 21:04 (10.0.2.150 does not answer). The device keeps trying, and both rows come back at the box's first answer. A Hue bridge that is away never takes the box device offline.
  • The box refuses the pairing. If the box answers that it no longer knows this controller (after a factory reset, or when the registration was removed), the device stops reading it and says so in Status: run Pair Sync Box and hold the button again.
  • Debug Mode writes many more lines to the device's Lua output (the box's address, names and states; never the pairing key). It turns itself back Off 8 hours after it was set to On, and Last Action reads Debug Mode turned itself Off after 8 hours; a restart of the controller or the driver keeps the time that is left.

Show the box's raw state in the Lua output writes the box's state, as the box reports it, to the device's Lua output for a report: it never holds the pairing key, but it does hold names and addresses from your network, so replace them with placeholders before you share it.
Forget PairingConfirm: No, YesRemoves the registration from the box and clears the pairing on the device; the device itself stays (section 3).

The driver adds no programming commands, events, conditionals or variables of its own in this beta. Its four buttons, Hue Sync, Sync Brighter, Sync Dimmer and Sync Intensity, are there already, but in this beta a press only writes Not done: the Hue Sync buttons come in a later 2.0 beta in Last Action; leave them out of the rooms' menus until the beta that gives them their job.

Not yet seen working

This beta was written against what other software that works with sync boxes shows about their local interface, and tested against an imitation of a box; it had not been run with a real box when this page was written. The first install with a box is expected to settle that the controller checks the box's certificate chain as it checks the bridge's, that the box answers the controller's encrypted connection by its address, and that the search finds boxes by mDNS. The texts in this page that depend on the box's answers, such as Inputs, Bridge Link and Input Switching, may change as real boxes are seen.

Routing was written from Control4's documentation and tested against an imitation of a box and of Control4. Still to be seen on a real system: in which order Control4 sends a box its commands at Room Off and when a source leaves it; what Control4 sends a box behind a receiver when the room changes source; and how a room reports its source and a Listen selection to a driver. Each may change details of section 5 in a later beta.

To report how a box behaves, open an issue on the project's GitHub page with the device's Driver Version, the Control4 OS version, the box's Model and Firmware, the texts of Status and Last Action, and, if asked, the output of Diagnostics » Show the box's raw state in the Lua output, with your addresses, names and ids replaced by placeholders.
